1. Master Formula Sheet

A. Forward and Futures Pricing (Cost of Carry Model)

1. Non-Income Paying Asset (Simple Compounding):

$$F_0 = S_0 \cdot \left(1 + r \cdot \frac{t}{12}\right)$$

2. Continuous Compounding:

$$F_0 = S_0 \cdot e^{rt}$$

3. Asset with Known Cash Dividend ($D$):

$$F_0 = (S_0 - D \cdot e^{-rt_d}) \cdot e^{rt} \quad \text{or} \quad F_0 = S_0 \cdot (1 + rt) - D$$

4. Asset with Continuous Dividend Yield ($y$):

$$F_0 = S_0 \cdot e^{(r - y)t}$$

5. Commodity with Storage ($u$) & Convenience Yield ($c$):

$$F_0 = S_0 \cdot e^{(r + u - c)t}$$
Arbitrage Condition Market Status Arbitrage Strategy Execution
$F_{\text{actual}} > F_{\text{fair}}$ Futures Overvalued Cash-and-Carry: Borrow money, buy stock in spot, sell (short) futures contract.
$F_{\text{actual}} < F_{\text{fair}}$ Futures Undervalued Reverse Cash-and-Carry: Short stock in spot, invest proceeds at risk-free rate, buy (long) futures.

2. Stock Index Futures & Beta Hedging

Number of Contracts to Trade ($N$):

$$N = \frac{V_p \cdot (\beta^* - \beta_p)}{V_f}$$

Where $V_f = \text{Futures Price} \times \text{Lot Size / Multiplier}$.

  • To reduce beta ($\beta^* < \beta_p$): Result is negative $\implies$ Sell (Short) $N$ Contracts.
  • To increase beta ($\beta^* > \beta_p$): Result is positive $\implies$ Buy (Long) $N$ Contracts.
  • For complete hedge ($\beta^* = 0$): $N = \frac{V_p \cdot \beta_p}{V_f}$ (Sell).
Daily Marking-to-Market (MTM) Margin Rules

$$\text{Initial Margin} = \mu + 3\sigma$$

$$\text{Maintenance Margin} = 75\% \text{ of Initial Margin (or specified %)}$$

If account drops below maintenance margin $\implies$ Margin Call for full deficit back to Initial Margin.

4. Binomial Model & Black-Scholes-Merton Formula

A. Binomial Model & Risk-Neutral Pricing

1. Hedge Ratio (Delta $\Delta$):

$$\Delta = \frac{C_u - C_d}{S_u - S_d}$$

2. Risk-Neutral Probability ($p$):

$$p = \frac{R - d}{u - d} \quad \text{where } R = 1 + r \cdot t \text{ (or } e^{rt}\text{)}$$

3. Single-Period Call Value:

$$C_0 = \frac{p \cdot C_u + (1-p) \cdot C_d}{R}$$

B. Black-Scholes-Merton Model

$$C_0 = S_0 \cdot N(d_1) - X \cdot e^{-rt} \cdot N(d_2)$$ $$d_1 = \frac{\ln(S_0 / X) + \left(r + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)t}{\sigma \sqrt{t}}; \quad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma \sqrt{t}$$

C. Put-Call Parity Theorem

$$C_0 + X \cdot e^{-rt} = P_0 + S_0 \implies P_0 = C_0 + X \cdot e^{-rt} - S_0$$

7. High-Frequency Exam Traps & Shortcuts

  • Continuous vs Simple Discounting: If exponential values ($e^{rt}$) are provided in question footnotes, use continuous compounding ($S e^{rt}$). Otherwise, use simple interest $S(1+rt)$.
  • Portfolio Beta with Cash: Cash always has a beta of zero ($\beta_{\text{cash}} = 0$). Remember to include cash in total portfolio value $V_p$ when weighting!
  • Sensex Multiplier vs Nifty Lot: Sensex multiplier is typically 50. Nifty lots in past exams are 25, 50, 100, or 200. Always multiply index price by lot size to obtain $V_f$!
  • Margin Call Top-up: The amount of money to deposit on margin call brings the balance back to Initial Margin, NOT just to the maintenance margin.
  • Option Payoff Tables: When constructing payoff tables for combinations (Straddle, Collar), always tabulate columns: $S_T$, Call Payoff, Put Payoff, Total Gross Payoff, Total Premium Paid, Net Profit/(Loss).
