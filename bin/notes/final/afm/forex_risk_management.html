1. Quotation Rules & Cross Currency Chain

1. Direct to Indirect Quote Inversion:

$$\text{Direct Bid} = \frac{1}{\text{Indirect Ask}}; \quad \text{Direct Ask} = \frac{1}{\text{Indirect Bid}}$$

2. Synthetic Cross Rates:

$$\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)_{\text{Bid}} = \left(\frac{A}{C}\right)_{\text{Bid}} \times \left(\frac{C}{B}\right)_{\text{Bid}} = \frac{(A/C)_{\text{Bid}}}{(B/C)_{\text{Ask}}}$$ $$\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)_{\text{Ask}} = \left(\frac{A}{C}\right)_{\text{Ask}} \times \left(\frac{C}{B}\right)_{\text{Ask}} = \frac{(A/C)_{\text{Ask}}}{(B/C)_{\text{Bid}}}$$

3. Forward Swap Points Rule:

  • Ascending Points ($\text{Bid} < \text{Ask}$) $\implies$ ADD Points (Premium)
  • Descending Points ($\text{Bid} > \text{Ask}$) $\implies$ SUBTRACT Points (Discount)

4. Annualized Premium / Discount:

$$\%\text{ Premium/Discount} = \frac{F - S}{S} \times \frac{12}{m} \times 100$$

2. International Parity Relationships

Theorem Direct Quote Formula ($\text{Domestic/Foreign}$) Core Takeaway
Interest Rate Parity (IRP) $$\frac{F}{S} = \frac{1 + r_D \cdot t}{1 + r_F \cdot t} \approx 1 + (r_D - r_F)t$$ Currency with lower interest rate trades at a forward premium.
Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) $$\frac{E(S_t)}{S_0} = \frac{1 + I_D}{1 + I_F} \approx 1 + (I_D - I_F)$$ Expected spot change equals inflation differential.
International Fisher Effect (IFE) $$\frac{E(S_t)}{S_0} = \frac{1 + r_D}{1 + r_F} \approx 1 + (r_D - r_F)$$ Higher nominal interest rate implies expected currency depreciation.

3. Covered Interest Arbitrage & Triangular Arbitrage

Covered Interest Arbitrage (CIA) Decision Rule:

  • If $\text{Forward Premium} > (r_D - r_F) \implies$ Foreign currency forward is Overvalued.
    Strategy: Borrow Domestic $\to$ Convert Spot $\to$ Invest Foreign $\to$ Sell Foreign Forward.
  • If $\text{Forward Premium} < (r_D - r_F) \implies$ Foreign currency forward is Undervalued.
    Strategy: Borrow Foreign $\to$ Convert Spot $\to$ Invest Domestic $\to$ Buy Foreign Forward.

4. Forward Cover vs. Money Market Hedge (MMH)

Parameter Exporter (Foreign Receivable) Importer (Foreign Payable)
Forward Contract Sell foreign currency at Forward Bid: $$\text{Inflow} = \text{Receivable} \times F_{\text{Bid}}$$ Buy foreign currency at Forward Ask: $$\text{Outflow} = \text{Payable} \times F_{\text{Ask}}$$
Money Market Hedge (MMH) 1. Borrow $FC = \frac{\text{Receivable}}{1 + r_{FC}^{\text{borrow}} \cdot t}$
2. Convert Spot at Bid.
3. Invest in Domestic at $r_D^{\text{deposit}} \cdot t$.		 1. Deposit $FC = \frac{\text{Payable}}{1 + r_{FC}^{\text{deposit}} \cdot t}$
2. Buy Spot at Ask.
3. Borrow Domestic at $r_D^{\text{borrow}} \cdot t$.
Currency Option Buy Put Option (Guaranteed floor with upside participation). Buy Call Option (Capped maximum cost with downside participation).

6. High-Frequency Exam Traps & Pointers

  • Bank Perspective: In every merchant transaction, the bank buys low (Bid) and sells high (Ask). Importers buy from bank at Ask; Exporters sell to bank at Bid.
  • Margin Direction: Bank always deducts margin from Bid (paying less to customer) and adds margin to Ask (charging more from customer).
  • Direct vs Indirect Formula: When quote is direct ($\text{Domestic/Foreign}$), domestic interest is in numerator: $\frac{F}{S} = \frac{1+r_D}{1+r_F}$. If quote is indirect ($\text{Foreign/Domestic}$), foreign interest is in numerator!
  • Exchange Position vs Nostro Cash: Forward contracts affect Exchange Position immediately, but DO NOT affect Nostro Cash until physical settlement.
