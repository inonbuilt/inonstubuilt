1. Quotation Rules & Cross Currency Chain

1. Direct to Indirect Quote Inversion:

$$\text{Direct Bid} = \frac{1}{\text{Indirect Ask}}; \quad \text{Direct Ask} = \frac{1}{\text{Indirect Bid}}$$

2. Synthetic Cross Rates:

$$\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)_{\text{Bid}} = \left(\frac{A}{C}\right)_{\text{Bid}} \times \left(\frac{C}{B}\right)_{\text{Bid}} = \frac{(A/C)_{\text{Bid}}}{(B/C)_{\text{Ask}}}$$ $$\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)_{\text{Ask}} = \left(\frac{A}{C}\right)_{\text{Ask}} \times \left(\frac{C}{B}\right)_{\text{Ask}} = \frac{(A/C)_{\text{Ask}}}{(B/C)_{\text{Bid}}}$$

3. Forward Swap Points Rule:

  • Ascending Points ($\text{Bid} < \text{Ask}$) $\implies$ ADD Points (Premium)
  • Descending Points ($\text{Bid} > \text{Ask}$) $\implies$ SUBTRACT Points (Discount)

4. Annualized Premium / Discount:

$$\%\text{ Premium/Discount} = \frac{F - S}{S} \times \frac{12}{m} \times 100$$
💡 Quotation Rules • Direct to Indirect Inversion
Question 1: A Mumbai foreign exchange dealer quotes the spot rate as $\text{INR/USD} = 82.40 / 82.60$. An American bank requests the equivalent indirect quote (USD per INR). What is the Bid and Ask rate for $\text{USD/INR}$?
A) Bid = 0.012107; Ask = 0.012136
B) Bid = 0.012136; Ask = 0.012107
C) Bid = 0.012107; Ask = 0.012107
D) Bid = 0.012000; Ask = 0.012200
Correct Answer: Option A (Bid = 0.012107; Ask = 0.012136)
Detailed Step-by-Step Working:
$$\text{Indirect Bid Rate} = \frac{1}{\text{Direct Ask Rate}} = \frac{1}{82.60} = \mathbf{0.012107}$$ $$\text{Indirect Ask Rate} = \frac{1}{\text{Direct Bid Rate}} = \frac{1}{82.40} = \mathbf{0.012136}$$ Hence, the two-way quote for $\text{USD/INR}$ is $\mathbf{0.012107 / 0.012136}$. Note that Bid < Ask is strictly maintained.
💡 Cross Rates • Chain Rule Derivation
Question 2: Given the following two-way market quotes: $\text{USD/INR} = 83.00 / 83.20$ and $\text{USD/EUR} = 0.9200 / 0.9220$. What is the cross-currency Bid and Ask quote for $\text{EUR/INR}$ (INR per 1 EUR)?
A) Bid = 90.02; Ask = 90.43
B) Bid = 90.20; Ask = 90.20
C) Bid = 76.36; Ask = 76.71
D) Bid = 89.80; Ask = 90.65
Correct Answer: Option A (Bid = 90.02; Ask = 90.43)
Detailed Step-by-Step Working:
$$\text{Bid}(\text{EUR/INR}) = \frac{\text{Bid}(\text{USD/INR})}{\text{Ask}(\text{USD/EUR})} = \frac{83.00}{0.9220} = \mathbf{90.0217 \approx 90.02}$$ $$\text{Ask}(\text{EUR/INR}) = \frac{\text{Ask}(\text{USD/INR})}{\text{Bid}(\text{USD/EUR})} = \frac{83.20}{0.9200} = \mathbf{90.4348 \approx 90.43}$$
💡 Swap Points • Broken Period Interpolation
Question 3: A bank quotes spot $\text{USD/INR} = 82.50 / 82.70$. 1-month swap points are quoted as $20 / 30$, while 2-month swap points are quoted as $45 / 60$. An importer requires a forward contract for 45 days (1.5 months). Using linear interpolation under FEDAI guidelines, what is the 45-day outright forward rate applicable for the importer?
A) ₹82.8250
B) ₹83.1500
C) ₹82.7450
D) ₹82.9500
Correct Answer: Option B (₹83.1500)
Detailed Step-by-Step Working:
• Importer needs to buy USD $\implies$ Bank sells USD at the Ask Rate.
• Swap points are ascending ($20/30$ and $45/60$), indicating USD is at a forward premium $\implies$ Add swap points to spot.
• 1-month Ask swap points = 30 points (₹0.30); 2-month Ask swap points = 60 points (₹0.60).
$$\text{Interpolated 45-day Ask Swap Points} = 30 + \frac{15}{30} \times (60 - 30) = 30 + 15 = 45\text{ points} = ₹0.45$$ $$\text{Outright 45-day Forward Ask Rate} = \text{Spot Ask} + \text{Swap Points} = 82.70 + 0.45 = \mathbf{₹83.1500}$$

2. International Parity Relationships

Theorem Direct Quote Formula ($\text{Domestic/Foreign}$) Core Takeaway
Interest Rate Parity (IRP) $$\frac{F}{S} = \frac{1 + r_D \cdot t}{1 + r_F \cdot t} \approx 1 + (r_D - r_F)t$$ Currency with lower interest rate trades at a forward premium.
Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) $$\frac{E(S_t)}{S_0} = \frac{1 + I_D}{1 + I_F} \approx 1 + (I_D - I_F)$$ Expected spot change equals inflation differential.
International Fisher Effect (IFE) $$\frac{E(S_t)}{S_0} = \frac{1 + r_D}{1 + r_F} \approx 1 + (r_D - r_F)$$ Higher nominal interest rate implies expected currency depreciation.
💡 Parity Relationships • Interest Rate Parity (IRP)
Question 4: Spot exchange rate is $\text{INR/USD} = 82.00$. The 1-year risk-free interest rate in India is 7.0% per annum, and in the USA is 4.0% per annum. According to Interest Rate Parity (IRP), what is the 1-year theoretical forward rate ($\text{INR/USD}$), and what is the forward premium/discount on USD?
A) Forward Rate = ₹84.37; USD at a forward premium of 2.89%
B) Forward Rate = ₹79.70; USD at a forward discount of 2.81%
C) Forward Rate = ₹84.46; USD at a forward premium of 3.00%
D) Forward Rate = ₹82.00; USD at par
Correct Answer: Option A (Forward Rate = ₹84.37; Premium = 2.89%)
Detailed Step-by-Step Working:
$$F = S \times \frac{1 + r_h}{1 + r_f} = 82.00 \times \frac{1 + 0.07}{1 + 0.04} = 82.00 \times \frac{1.07}{1.04} = \mathbf{₹84.365 \approx ₹84.37}$$ $$\text{Annualized Forward Premium on USD} = \frac{84.365 - 82.00}{82.00} \times 100 = \mathbf{2.885\% \approx 2.89\%}$$
💡 Parity Relationships • Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)
Question 5: According to the Relative Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) theorem, if the expected annual inflation rate is 6.0% in India and 2.0% in the UK, and the current spot exchange rate is $\text{INR/GBP} = 105.00$, what is the expected spot rate of GBP 1 year from now?
A) ₹109.12
B) ₹101.04
C) ₹111.30
D) ₹107.10
Correct Answer: Option A (₹109.12)
Detailed Step-by-Step Working:
$$E(S_1) = S_0 \times \frac{1 + I_{\text{India}}}{1 + I_{\text{UK}}} = 105.00 \times \frac{1 + 0.06}{1 + 0.02} = 105.00 \times \frac{1.06}{1.02} = 105.00 \times 1.039216 = \mathbf{₹109.12}$$

3. Covered Interest Arbitrage & Triangular Arbitrage

Covered Interest Arbitrage (CIA) Decision Rule:

  • If $\text{Forward Premium} > (r_D - r_F) \implies$ Foreign currency forward is Overvalued.
    Strategy: Borrow Domestic $\to$ Convert Spot $\to$ Invest Foreign $\to$ Sell Foreign Forward.
  • If $\text{Forward Premium} < (r_D - r_F) \implies$ Foreign currency forward is Undervalued.
    Strategy: Borrow Foreign $\to$ Convert Spot $\to$ Invest Domestic $\to$ Buy Foreign Forward.
💡 Arbitrage Mechanics • Triangular Currency Arbitrage
Question 6: A foreign exchange trader observes the following simultaneous spot quotes across three international centers: In Mumbai: $\text{USD/INR} = 82.50$; In London: $\text{GBP/USD} = 1.2500$; In New York: $\text{GBP/INR} = 104.00$. Does a Triangular Arbitrage opportunity exist for a trader starting with ₹1,00,00,000, and what is the net riskless arbitrage gain?
A) Arbitrage exists; Net Profit = ₹84,848
B) No arbitrage exists; cross rate matches quoted rate
C) Arbitrage exists; Net Loss = ₹50,000
D) Arbitrage exists; Net Profit = ₹12,500
Correct Answer: Option A (Arbitrage exists; Net Profit = ₹84,848)
Detailed Step-by-Step Working:
$$\text{Synthetic Cross Rate } (\text{GBP/INR}) = 82.50 \times 1.2500 = ₹103.125$$ Quoted market rate in New York is $₹104.00$ per GBP. Since Quoted > Synthetic, GBP is overvalued in New York.
1. Convert ₹1,00,00,000 to USD at $82.50 \implies \text{USD } 1,21,212.12$.
2. Convert USD to GBP at $1.2500 \implies \frac{1,21,212.12}{1.2500} = \text{GBP } 96,969.70$.
3. Sell GBP in New York for INR at $104.00 \implies 96,969.70 \times 104.00 = ₹1,00,84,848.48$.
$$\text{Net Riskless Profit} = ₹1,00,84,848.48 - ₹1,00,00,000 = \mathbf{₹84,848}$$
💡 Arbitrage Mechanics • Covered Interest Arbitrage (CIA)
Question 7: Spot $\text{INR/USD} = 82.00$. The 1-year forward rate quoted in the market is $\text{INR/USD} = 84.00$. 1-year interest rate in India is 6.0% p.a., and in USA is 3.0% p.a. A corporate treasurer wishes to execute Covered Interest Arbitrage (CIA) using a borrowing capacity of USD 1,00,000. Which transaction cycle must be executed, and what is the riskless arbitrage profit?
A) Borrow USD 1,00,000, convert to INR spot at 82.00, invest in India at 6.0%, and buy USD 1,03,000 forward at 84.00; Net Profit = ₹40,000
B) Borrow INR 82,00,000, convert to USD, invest in USA at 3.0%, and sell USD forward at 84.00; Net Profit = ₹40,000
C) Arbitrage is not possible because forward rate is within transaction cost bounds
D) Borrow USD 1,00,000 and invest in US treasury bills; Net Profit = USD 0
Correct Answer: Option A (Net Profit = ₹40,000)
Detailed Step-by-Step Working:
$$\text{Theoretical IRP Forward Rate} = 82.00 \times \frac{1.06}{1.03} = ₹84.388$$ Actual market forward rate is ₹84.00 (forward USD is underpriced relative to IRP, meaning forward INR is overpriced).
1. Borrow USD 1,00,000 at 3% in USA $\implies$ Repayment due in 1 year = USD 1,03,000.
2. Convert USD 1,00,000 to INR spot at $82.00 \implies ₹82,00,000$.
3. Invest ₹82,00,000 in India at 6% $\implies$ Maturity Inflow $= 82,00,000 \times 1.06 = ₹86,92,000$.
4. Cover USD liability: Buy USD 1,03,000 forward at ₹84.00 $\implies$ INR Cost $= 1,03,000 \times 84.00 = ₹86,52,000$.
$$\text{Net Riskless Arbitrage Profit} = ₹86,92,000 - ₹86,52,000 = \mathbf{₹40,000}$$
💡 Arbitrage Mechanics • Two-Point Space Arbitrage
Question 8: A foreign exchange dealer notices that in Frankfurt the quote is $\text{USD/EUR} = 1.0820 / 1.0825$, while in New York the quote is $\text{USD/EUR} = 1.0835 / 1.0840$. How can a trader execute two-point geographic (space) arbitrage starting with USD 10,00,000?
A) Buy EUR in Frankfurt at Ask 1.0825 and sell EUR in New York at Bid 1.0835; Net Profit = USD 924
B) Buy EUR in New York at 1.0840 and sell in Frankfurt at 1.0820; Net Profit = USD 1,847
C) Geographic arbitrage cannot generate profit after bid-ask spreads
D) Borrow EUR in London and deposit in Tokyo
Correct Answer: Option A (Net Profit = USD 924)
Detailed Step-by-Step Working:
• Frankfurt Bank sells EUR at Ask 1.0825.
• New York Bank buys EUR at Bid 1.0835.
Since New York Bid (1.0835) > Frankfurt Ask (1.0825), arbitrage is profitable:
$$\text{EUR purchased in Frankfurt} = \frac{\text{USD } 10,00,000}{1.0825} = \text{EUR } 9,23,787.53$$ $$\text{USD proceeds from sale in New York} = 9,23,787.53 \times 1.0835 = \text{USD } 10,00,923.79$$ $$\text{Net Arbitrage Profit} = 10,00,923.79 - 10,00,000 = \mathbf{\text{USD } 923.79 \approx \text{USD } 924}$$

4. Forward Cover vs. Money Market Hedge (MMH)

Parameter Exporter (Foreign Receivable) Importer (Foreign Payable)
Forward Contract Sell foreign currency at Forward Bid: $$\text{Inflow} = \text{Receivable} \times F_{\text{Bid}}$$ Buy foreign currency at Forward Ask: $$\text{Outflow} = \text{Payable} \times F_{\text{Ask}}$$
Money Market Hedge (MMH) 1. Borrow $FC = \frac{\text{Receivable}}{1 + r_{FC}^{\text{borrow}} \cdot t}$
2. Convert Spot at Bid.
3. Invest in Domestic at $r_D^{\text{deposit}} \cdot t$.		 1. Deposit $FC = \frac{\text{Payable}}{1 + r_{FC}^{\text{deposit}} \cdot t}$
2. Buy Spot at Ask.
3. Borrow Domestic at $r_D^{\text{borrow}} \cdot t$.
Currency Option Buy Put Option (Guaranteed floor with upside participation). Buy Call Option (Capped maximum cost with downside participation).
💡 Hedging Strategies • Money Market Hedge for Imports
Question 9: An Indian importer has an invoice payable of USD 5,00,000 due in 6 months. Relevant financial market data: Spot rate: $\text{INR/USD} = 82.50$; 6-month forward rate: $\text{INR/USD} = 83.20$; 6-month US deposit rate: 2.0% per half-year; 6-month Indian borrowing rate: 4.0% per half-year. What is the net rupee cost under a Money Market Hedge (MMH), and which hedging strategy is more cost-effective?
A) MMH Cost = ₹4,20,58,824; Forward Cover is cheaper (₹4,16,00,000)
B) MMH Cost = ₹4,29,04,412; MMH is cheaper
C) MMH Cost = ₹4,15,00,000; MMH is cheaper
D) Both strategies yield identical costs of ₹4,16,00,000
Correct Answer: Option A (MMH Cost = ₹4,20,58,824; Forward Cover is cheaper)
Detailed Step-by-Step Working:
• Forward Cover Cost: $5,00,000 \times 83.20 = \mathbf{₹4,16,00,000}$.
• Money Market Hedge:
1. Deposit USD today at 2.0%: $\frac{5,00,000}{1 + 0.02} = \text{USD } 4,90,196.08$.
2. Buy USD spot at 82.50: $4,90,196.08 \times 82.50 = ₹4,04,41,176.60$.
3. Borrow INR in India at 4.0%: $4,04,41,176.60 \times (1 + 0.04) = \mathbf{₹4,20,58,824}$.
Forward Cover saves ₹4,58,824 compared to MMH.
💡 Hedging Strategies • Money Market Hedge for Exports
Question 10: An Indian exporter expects to receive USD 2,00,000 in 3 months. Spot rate: $\text{INR/USD} = 82.00$; 3-month forward rate: $\text{INR/USD} = 82.60$; 3-month US borrowing rate: 1.5% per quarter; 3-month Indian deposit rate: 1.75% per quarter. Under a Money Market Hedge (MMH), what is the total rupee cash inflow realized at $t=3\text{ months}$, and how does it compare to the Forward Cover inflow?
A) MMH Inflow = ₹1,64,40,394; Forward Cover inflow = ₹1,65,20,000 (Forward Cover yields higher inflow)
B) MMH Inflow = ₹1,66,00,000; MMH yields higher inflow
C) MMH Inflow = ₹1,65,20,000; Both yield identical inflows
D) MMH Inflow = ₹1,61,57,635; Forward Cover yields higher inflow
Correct Answer: Option A (MMH Inflow = ₹1,64,40,394; Forward Cover is superior)
Detailed Step-by-Step Working:
• Forward Cover Inflow: $2,00,000 \times 82.60 = \mathbf{₹1,65,20,000}$.
• Money Market Hedge:
1. Borrow USD today: $\frac{2,00,000}{1 + 0.015} = \text{USD } 1,97,044.33$.
2. Convert to INR spot at $82.00: 1,97,044.33 \times 82.00 = ₹1,61,57,635.06$.
3. Invest INR at 1.75%: $1,61,57,635.06 \times 1.0175 = \mathbf{₹1,64,40,394}$.
Forward Cover yields ₹79,606 more than MMH.
💡 Internal Hedging • Multilateral Netting
Question 11: Which internal forex risk management technique involves an MNC coordinating intercompany settlement dates between subsidiaries so that cross-border fund transfers are executed only for the net balance of multilateral obligations?
A) Leading and Lagging
B) Multilateral Netting
C) Parallel Loan Matching
D) Currency Invoicing
Correct Answer: Option B (Multilateral Netting)
Detailed Explanation: Multilateral Netting consolidates intra-group transactions into a centralized treasury matrix. Instead of multiple subsidiaries wiring gross foreign exchange payments to each other (incurring bid-ask spreads, cross-border remittance fees, and float delays), only the net surplus or deficit is settled, drastically cutting transaction costs and currency exposure.

💡 FEDAI Rules • Forward Contract Cancellation Charges
Question 12: A customer booked a 3-month Forward Purchase contract with a bank to buy USD 1,00,000 at ₹82.50. On the due date, the customer requests cancellation of the contract. The prevailing spot market rate on the due date is $\text{INR/USD} = 81.80 / 81.90$. Under FEDAI guidelines, what cancellation charges or net gain/loss are debited or credited to the customer's account?
A) Customer account is credited with ₹60,000 profit
B) Customer account is debited with ₹70,000 loss
C) Customer account is debited with ₹60,000 loss
D) Customer account is credited with ₹70,000 profit
Correct Answer: Option B (Customer debited with ₹70,000 loss)
Detailed Step-by-Step Working:
• Customer had contracted to Buy USD at ₹82.50.
• To cancel on due date, the bank executes an opposite transaction at prevailing spot: the bank Buys USD from customer at prevailing Spot TT Buying Rate $= ₹81.80$.
$$\text{Loss per USD to customer} = 81.80 - 82.50 = -₹0.70$$ $$\text{Total Cancellation Loss Debited} = 1,00,000 \times ₹0.70 = \mathbf{₹70,000}$$
💡 FEDAI Rules • Overdue Forward Contract Cancellation
Question 13: Under Rule 8 of the Foreign Exchange Dealers' Association of India (FEDAI), if a customer fails to take delivery or give instructions on a forward exchange contract on or before the maturity date, what statutory action is mandated?
A) The contract is renewed automatically for another 1 year at the original contract rate
B) The contract is automatically cancelled by the bank on the 3rd working day following the maturity date at the prevailing TT buying or selling rate, with any resulting loss recovered from the customer
C) The bank files an immediate criminal complaint with the Reserve Bank of India
D) The customer forfeits all fixed deposits held across the domestic banking system
Correct Answer: Option B
Detailed Explanation: Under FEDAI Rule 8, in the absence of customer instructions on or before the due date, an overdue forward contract is automatically cancelled by the bank on the 3rd working day after maturity. The cancellation is executed at the prevailing TT rate (TT Buying for purchase contracts, TT Selling for sale contracts), and net exchange loss plus overdue interest/handling charges are recovered from the customer.

6. High-Frequency Exam Traps & Pointers

  • Bank Perspective: In every merchant transaction, the bank buys low (Bid) and sells high (Ask). Importers buy from bank at Ask; Exporters sell to bank at Bid.
  • Margin Direction: Bank always deducts margin from Bid (paying less to customer) and adds margin to Ask (charging more from customer).
  • Direct vs Indirect Formula: When quote is direct ($\text{Domestic/Foreign}$), domestic interest is in numerator: $\frac{F}{S} = \frac{1+r_D}{1+r_F}$. If quote is indirect ($\text{Foreign/Domestic}$), foreign interest is in numerator!
  • Exchange Position vs Nostro Cash: Forward contracts affect Exchange Position immediately, but DO NOT affect Nostro Cash until physical settlement.
💡 Interbank Accounts • Nostro, Vostro & Loro
Question 14: How are Nostro, Vostro, and Loro accounts correctly defined in interbank foreign exchange operations?
A) Nostro: Foreign bank's account with domestic bank; Vostro: Domestic bank's account with foreign bank; Loro: Customer savings account
B) Nostro ("Our account with you"): A domestic bank's account maintained in foreign currency with an overseas correspondent bank; Vostro ("Your account with us"): An overseas bank's account maintained in domestic currency with a domestic bank; Loro ("Their account with them"): Reference to a third party bank's account
C) Nostro: Non-resident external account; Vostro: Foreign currency non-resident account; Loro: Resident foreign currency account
D) Nostro, Vostro, and Loro are identical accounts with no operational distinction
Correct Answer: Option B
Detailed Explanation:
• Nostro Account ("Our account with you"): An account maintained by an Indian bank (e.g. SBI) in foreign currency (e.g. USD) with an overseas correspondent bank (e.g. Citibank, New York).
• Vostro Account ("Your account with us"): An account maintained by a foreign bank (e.g. Citibank) in Indian Rupees with an Indian bank (e.g. SBI, Mumbai).
• Loro Account ("Their account with them"): A bank referring to an account held by a third-party bank with another correspondent bank.
💡 Position Management • Exchange vs Cash Position
Question 15: What is the vital distinction between a foreign exchange dealer's "Exchange Position" and "Cash Position"?
A) Exchange Position tracks only physical paper currency; Cash Position tracks electronic funds
B) Exchange Position reflects the net commitment to buy or sell foreign currency (including spot, forward, and unexecuted customer orders) measuring exchange rate risk; Cash Position measures the actual cleared liquidity balance available in the foreign currency Nostro account
C) Exchange Position is audited annually; Cash Position is audited daily
D) Both positions must always remain exactly equal to zero at all times
Correct Answer: Option B
Detailed Explanation: The Exchange Position measures currency risk: it tracks all foreign currency contracts (spot, forward, bills purchased, outstanding commitments), indicating whether the bank is net Overbought (long) or Oversold (short). The Cash Position measures liquidity: it tracks actual cleared inflows and outflows of funds in the bank's Nostro account, ensuring there is sufficient foreign currency cash to settle immediate value-dated obligations.
