Chapter 13: Business Valuation

Comprehensive business valuation cheat sheet covering Discounted Cash Flow (DCF), Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF) vs Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE), Economic Value Added (EVA), Market Multiples, and Chop-Shop sum-of-the-parts.

📚 Standard Curriculum Study Material⏱️ Read Time: ~50 mins🎯 Exam Weightage: 15 – 25 Marks

1. Valuation Master Formula Bank

1. Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF) & Enterprise Value:
$$\text{FCFF} = \text{EBIT}(1 - T) + \text{Depreciation} - \text{Capex} - \Delta\text{Working Capital}$$ $$\text{Enterprise Value (EV)} = \sum_{t=1}^n \frac{\text{FCFF}_t}{(1 + \text{WACC})^t} + \frac{\text{Terminal Value}_n}{(1 + \text{WACC})^n}$$ $$\text{Terminal Value}_n = \frac{\text{FCFF}_{n+1}}{\text{WACC}_{\text{stable}} - g_{\text{stable}}}$$ $$\text{Equity Value} = \text{Enterprise Value} - \text{Debt} + \text{Cash & Liquid Investments}$$
2. Hamada Equation (Beta Re-leveraging):
$$\beta_L = \beta_U \left[ 1 + (1 - T)\frac{D}{E} \right]$$
3. Economic Value Added (EVA):
$$\mathbf{\text{EVA} = \text{NOPAT} - (\text{WACC} \times \text{Invested Capital})}$$ $$\text{NOPAT} = \text{EBIT}(1 - T) = \text{PAT} + \text{Interest}(1 - T)$$ $$\text{EVA Dividend per share} = \frac{\text{Total EVA}}{\text{Number of Shares}}$$
4. Market Value Added (MVA):
$$\text{MVA} = \text{Market Value of Equity} - \text{Book Value of Equity}$$
5. Profit Earning Capacity Value (PECV):
$$\text{PECV per share} = \frac{\text{Future Maintainable PAT} / \text{Capitalization Rate}}{\text{Number of Equity Shares}}$$
6. Regulatory Fair Value per Share:
$$\text{Fair Value per share} = \frac{\text{Net Asset Value (NAV) per share} + \text{PECV per share}}{2}$$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 1.1
In discounted cash flow valuation, which equation correctly expresses Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF) starting from Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE)?
A. FCFF = FCFE + Interest(1 - t) - Net New Debt Issued + Debt Principal Repayments
B. FCFF = FCFE - Interest(1 - t) + Net Borrowing
C. FCFF = FCFE × (1 - t)
D. FCFF = FCFE + Depreciation - Capex
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
$\text{FCFE} = \text{FCFF} - \text{Interest}(1 - t) + \text{Net New Debt Raised}$ Rearranging for FCFF:
$\text{FCFF} = \text{FCFE} + \text{Interest}(1 - t) - \text{Net Debt Raised}$ $\text{FCFF} = \text{FCFE} + \text{Interest}(1 - t) - \text{New Debt Issued} + \text{Debt Repaid}$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 1.2
A company has an Enterprise Value (EV) of INR 1,200 crores calculated via DCF. The balance sheet shows total interest-bearing debt of INR 350 crores, cash and marketable securities of INR 80 crores, and non-controlling interest of INR 30 crores. What is the value of common equity?
A. INR 900 crores
B. INR 930 crores
C. INR 850 crores
D. INR 1,550 crores
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
$\text{Equity Value} = \text{Enterprise Value} - \text{Total Debt} + \text{Cash} - \text{Non-Controlling Interest}$ $\text{Equity Value} = 1,200 - 350 + 80 - 30 = \text{INR } 900\text{ crores}$

2. FCFF vs FCFE Quick Comparison

Metric FCFF Model (Firm Level) FCFE Model (Equity Level)
Starting Point $\text{EBIT}(1 - T)$ (NOPAT) $\text{PAT} - \text{Preference Dividends}$
Debt Adjustment Zero (Debt flows included in overall capital) $+\text{New Debt Issued} - \text{Debt Repaid}$
Discount Rate WACC (Weighted Cost of Debt & Equity) $k_e$ (Cost of Equity via CAPM)
Direct Output Enterprise Value (Firm Value) Equity Value
To get Equity Value Subtract Debt and add Cash from EV Obtained directly from model
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 2.1
Net Block of fixed assets increased from INR 800 crores at the beginning of the year to INR 950 crores at year-end. Depreciation charged during the year was INR 120 crores. What was the Gross Capital Expenditure incurred during the year?
A. INR 270 crores
B. INR 150 crores
C. INR 30 crores
D. INR 120 crores
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
$\text{Gross Capex} = (\text{Closing Net Block} - \text{Opening Net Block}) + \text{Depreciation}$ $\text{Gross Capex} = (950 - 800) + 120 = 150 + 120 = \text{INR } 270\text{ crores}$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 2.2
A company projects FCFF for Year 5 to be INR 100 crores. From Year 6 onward, FCFF is expected to grow perpetually at a stable rate of 4% p.a. The firm's WACC is 12% p.a. What is the Terminal Value of the firm at the end of Year 5?
A. INR 1,300 crores
B. INR 1,250 crores
C. INR 833 crores
D. INR 1,040 crores
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
$\text{Terminal Value}_5 = \frac{\text{FCFF}_6}{\text{WACC} - g} = \frac{\text{FCFF}_5 \times (1 + g)}{\text{WACC} - g} = \frac{100 \times 1.04}{0.12 - 0.04} = \frac{104}{0.08} = \text{INR } 1,300\text{ crores}$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 2.3
An equity research report discounts Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE) using the firm's Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC). What is the primary conceptual flaw in this valuation approach?
A. Mismatch of cash flow and discount rate: FCFE is a post-debt cash flow belonging solely to equity holders and must be discounted at Cost of Equity (Ke), not WACC
B. FCFE must always be discounted at the risk-free rate
C. There is no error; FCFE and FCFF can both be discounted at WACC
D. WACC must be adjusted for flotation costs before discounting FCFE
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
• FCFF represents cash flows available to all capital providers and is discounted at WACC.
• FCFE represents residual cash flows available solely to equity shareholders after all debt obligations and must be discounted at the Cost of Equity ($K_e$). Discounting FCFE at WACC understates the discount rate and artificially inflates valuation.

📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 3.1
A company reports EBIT of INR 25,00,000, corporate tax rate of 40%, Capital Employed of INR 50,00,000, and WACC of 12% p.a. What is the Economic Value Added (EVA)?
A. INR 9,00,000
B. INR 19,00,000
C. INR 11,40,000
D. INR 6,00,000
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
$\text{NOPAT} = \text{EBIT} \times (1 - t) = 25,00,000 \times (1 - 0.40) = \text{INR } 15,00,000$ $\text{Capital Charge} = \text{Capital Employed} \times \text{WACC} = 50,00,000 \times 12\% = \text{INR } 6,00,000$ $\text{EVA} = 15,00,000 - 6,00,000 = \text{INR } 9,00,000$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 3.2
The historical book value of a firm's operating assets is INR 2,000 lakhs, while their current economic replacement cost is INR 6,000 lakhs. For computing true Economic Value Added (EVA), which asset base should be used for Capital Employed?
A. INR 6,000 lakhs (Replacement cost / economic value)
B. INR 2,000 lakhs (Historical book value)
C. INR 4,000 lakhs (Average value)
D. Zero (Operating assets are omitted)
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
Economic Value Added evaluates returns earned on the true economic value of capital committed. Therefore, capital employed must be calculated on the replacement cost basis (INR 6,000 lakhs) to reflect the opportunity cost of resources.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 3.3
What is the theoretical relationship between Market Value Added (MVA) and Economic Value Added (EVA)?
A. MVA equals the present value of all future expected EVAs of the firm discounted at WACC
B. MVA is the annual change in EVA divided by the corporate tax rate
C. MVA equals current year EVA multiplied by the price-to-earnings ratio
D. MVA always equals total historical retained earnings
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
$\text{MVA} = \text{Total Market Value of Capital} - \text{Capital Employed} = \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \frac{\text{EVA}_t}{(1 + \text{WACC})^t}$ MVA measures the cumulative shareholder wealth created by management above the capital entrusted to the enterprise.

4. Chop-Shop (Break-Up Value) 4-Step Protocol

  1. Segment Sales Capitalization: $\sum (\text{Segment Sales} \times \text{Industry } P/S \text{ multiple})$.
  2. Segment Assets Capitalization: $\sum (\text{Segment Assets} \times \text{Industry } P/\text{Asset} \text{ multiple})$.
  3. Segment Income Capitalization: $\sum (\text{Segment Operating Income} \times \text{Industry } P/E \text{ multiple})$.
  4. Synthesize & Average: Take simple average of the three totals $\implies$ Compare with current aggregate market price. If $\text{Chop-Shop Value} > \text{Market Price}$, conglomerate discount exists.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 4.1
A multi-industry conglomerate has two business segments: Consumer Goods (Sales = INR 600 cr, industry P/Sales multiple = 1.50x) and Industrial Machinery (Assets = INR 400 cr, industry EV/Assets multiple = 1.20x). If the conglomerate has debt of INR 250 cr and cash of INR 50 cr, what is its estimated equity value under the Chop-Shop approach?
A. INR 1,180 crores
B. INR 1,380 crores
C. INR 1,430 crores
D. INR 930 crores
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
1. Value of Consumer Goods: $600 \times 1.50 = \text{INR } 900\text{ cr}$.
2. Value of Industrial Machinery: $400 \times 1.20 = \text{INR } 480\text{ cr}$.
3. Total Enterprise Value $= 900 + 480 = \text{INR } 1,380\text{ cr}$.
4. Equity Value $= 1,380 - \text{Debt (250)} + \text{Cash (50)} = \text{INR } 1,180\text{ cr}$.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 4.2
If the actual market capitalization of a diversified conglomerate is INR 2,000 crores, but its sum-of-the-parts (Chop-Shop) theoretical break-up value is INR 2,400 crores, the conglomerate is trading at a:
A. Conglomerate Discount of 16.67%
B. Conglomerate Premium of 20.00%
C. Conglomerate Discount of 20.00%
D. Net Asset Premium of 16.67%
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
$\text{Conglomerate Discount} = \frac{\text{Break-up Value} - \text{Market Value}}{\text{Break-up Value}} = \frac{2,400 - 2,000}{2,400} = \frac{400}{2,400} = 16.67\%$

Valuation Method Swap Ratio Formula

The Net Asset Value (NAV) per share of an enterprise is INR 140, and the Profit Earning Capacity Value (PECV) per share is INR 180. Under the classic Dual Pricing (Fair Value) approach, what is the fair value per share?

6. Exam Traps & Pitfalls

âš ï¸ Pitfall 1: Stable Phase Reinvestment Assumption
In 2-stage DCF problems (like TYK PR-7 & PR-8), notice that during stable growth, Capital Expenditure equals Depreciation (Net capex = 0). Do not subtract the high-growth capex percentage in the terminal year!
âš ï¸ Pitfall 2: Discounting the Terminal Value
Remember that $TV_n = \frac{\text{FCFF}_{n+1}}{\text{WACC} - g}$ gives the value at Year $n$. You must multiply $TV_n$ by $\text{PVIF}_{\text{WACC}, n}$ to bring it back to Year 0.
âš ï¸ Pitfall 3: NOPAT Tax Rate on Interest
$\text{NOPAT} = \text{EBIT}(1 - T)$. If starting from PAT, add back post-tax interest: $\text{PAT} + \text{Interest}(1 - T)$, NOT pre-tax interest!
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 6.1
Why are traditional DCF valuation techniques largely inappropriate for valuing early-stage pre-revenue startup companies?
A. Startups report current losses/cash burn, lack historical operating performance, face high mortality rates, and hold primarily intangible assets
B. Startups are legally barred from preparing financial projection models
C. Discount rates cannot be computed for any firm less than 5 years old
D. Startup balance sheets are mandated to be evaluated strictly at book value
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
Early-stage startups do not possess steady-state cash flows, historical baselines, or predictable margins. DCF models are heavily dependent on terminal value assumptions that become speculative. Instead, venture capital methods, milestone traction metrics, and relative scorecards are utilized.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 6.2
When valuing a financially distressed entity facing imminent liquidation, which valuation methodology establishes the true economic floor price?
A. Liquidation (Net Realizable Break-Up) Value
B. Going-concern DCF Model
C. Historical Book Value of Equity
D. Capitalization of Past Earnings
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
When an entity is no longer a going concern, cash flow capitalization methods lose validity because ongoing operations generate negative returns. The true floor value is the Net Realizable Liquidation Value realized from disposing of individual assets and discharging liabilities.
