1. Master Formula Bank

1. Forward Rate Agreement (FRA) Discounted Cash Settlement Formula:
$$\text{Settlement Amount} = \frac{N \times (RR - FR) \times \left(\frac{dtm}{DY}\right)}{1 + RR \times \left(\frac{dtm}{DY}\right)}$$ Where $N$ = Notional, $RR$ = Reference Rate, $FR$ = Forward Rate, $dtm$ = FRA days, $DY$ = Day-count (360 or 365).
2. Bond Price Sensitivity & Duration:
$$D^* = \frac{D_{\text{Macaulay}}}{1 + \frac{y}{m}} \quad \implies \quad \frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -D^* \times \Delta y + \frac{1}{2} C \times (\Delta y)^2$$
3. Generic Swap Net Settlement:
$$\text{Fixed Leg} = N \times \text{AIC} \times \left(\frac{180}{360}\right)$$ $$\text{Floating Leg} = N \times \text{SOFR} \times \left(\frac{\text{Actual Days}}{360}\right)$$ $$\text{Net Amount Paid by Fixed Payer} = \text{Fixed Leg} - \text{Floating Leg}$$
4. Amortized Cap Option Premium (PVAF Method):
$$\text{Periodic Amortized Premium} = \frac{\text{Total Lump Sum Premium Paid}}{\text{PVAF}(i, t)}$$ Where $i$ = Periodic interest discount rate and $t$ = Number of reset periods.
5. Overnight Index Swap (OIS) Floating Interest:
$$\text{Compounded Amount} = N \times \prod_{k=1}^n \left( 1 + \text{MIBOR}_k \times \frac{d_k}{365} \right) - N$$ Where $d_k$ = days spanned (1 day on weekdays, 2 days on Saturday covering Sunday).
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 1.1
Spot interest rates are 4.50% p.a. for 3 months (90 days) and 5.00% p.a. for 6 months (180 days). Using money market compounding conventions, what is the theoretical forward-forward rate for a 3-month deposit starting in 3 months (FRA 3x6)?
A. 5.44% p.a.
B. 5.00% p.a.
C. 4.75% p.a.
D. 2.72% p.a.
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
By money market future value equivalence:
$\left(1 + R_{3 \times 6} \times \frac{3}{12}\right) = \frac{1 + R_6 \times \frac{6}{12}}{1 + R_3 \times \frac{3}{12}} = \frac{1 + 0.05 \times 0.5}{1 + 0.045 \times 0.25} = \frac{1.0250}{1.01125} = 1.013597$ $R_{3 \times 6} \times 0.25 = 0.013597 \implies R_{3 \times 6} = 0.013597 \times 4 = 5.4388\% \approx 5.44\% \text{ p.a.}$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 1.2
A bank sold Interest Rate Futures contracts underlying notional 7.50% GOI bonds at a futures settlement price of INR 1,000. An eligible deliverable bond (6.80% GOI 2029) is quoted in the spot cash market at INR 877.50 with a conversion factor of 0.9195. What is the net profit/loss per bond from delivering this security?
A. Profit of INR 42.00
B. Loss of INR 42.00
C. Profit of INR 122.50
D. Loss of INR 122.50
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
1. Invoice price received from delivery: $\text{Futures Settlement Price} \times \text{CF} = 1,000 \times 0.9195 = \text{INR } 919.50$.
2. Cash outflow to purchase the bond in spot market = $\text{INR } 877.50$.

2. FRA Notation & Settlement Guide

Notation ($m \times n$) Fixing Date (Settlement) Underlying Loan Tenor Relevant Reference Rate
$3 \times 6$ FRA 3 months from today $6 - 3 = 3$ months 3-month benchmark rate on fixing date
$6 \times 9$ FRA 6 months from today $9 - 6 = 3$ months 3-month benchmark rate on fixing date
$6 \times 12$ FRA 6 months from today $12 - 6 = 6$ months 6-month benchmark rate on fixing date
Settlement Direction Rule:
• If $RR > FR$: Bank pays Buyer (Borrower hedged successfully against rate hike).
• If $RR < FR$: Buyer pays Bank (Borrower pays cost of insurance when market rates fall).
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 2.1
ABC Ltd enters into a 6x9 FRA on a notional principal of INR 50 crores at a contracted rate of 8.00% p.a. On the settlement date, the actual benchmark rate fixes at 10.00% p.a. What is the discounted net cash settlement amount (assuming a 360-day year with 90 days in the 3-month period)?
A. Receipt of INR 24,39,024 by ABC Ltd
B. Payment of INR 24,39,024 by ABC Ltd
C. Receipt of INR 25,00,000 by ABC Ltd
D. Payment of INR 25,00,000 by ABC Ltd
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
1. Undiscounted difference: $50,00,00,000 \times (10\% - 8\%) \times \frac{90}{360} = \text{INR } 25,00,000$.
2. Discounted cash settlement at actual rate ($10\%$):
$\text{Settlement} = \frac{25,00,000}{1 + \left(0.10 \times \frac{90}{360}\right)} = \frac{25,00,000}{1 + 0.025} = \frac{25,00,000}{1.025} = \text{INR } 24,39,024$
Since the market rate exceeds the agreed FRA rate, the buyer receives this amount.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 2.2
In interbank financial markets, what does a standard "3x9 FRA" specifically denote?
A. An agreement fixing the forward interest rate for a 6-month borrowing period commencing 3 months from trade date and ending 9 months from trade date
B. A 3-month loan that can be extended up to 9 months
C. A 9-month loan that has 3 coupon resets
D. An option to enter into 3 separate forward contracts over 9 months
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
In an $A \times B$ FRA: $A$ is the inception date of the borrowing (3 months forward), and $B$ is the maturity date (9 months forward). The underlying borrowing tenure is $B - A = 6$ months.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 2.3
The 6-month and 12-month spot borrowing rates are 9.60% p.a. and 9.00% p.a. Fair FRA rate for months 6 to 12 is 8.00% p.a., whereas the bank quotes actual 6x12 FRA at 10.50% - 11.00%. How can an arbitrageur capitalize on this mispricing?
A. Borrow spot for 12 months at 9.00%, invest for 6 months at 9.60%, and invest in the FRA for the remaining 6 months at 10.50%
B. Invest spot for 12 months at 9.00%, borrow for 6 months at 9.60%, and borrow in the FRA at 11.00%
C. Borrow in the FRA at 11.00% and deposit spot at 9.00%
D. Arbitrage is impossible because actual FRA deposit rate exceeds the fair rate
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
Because the market FRA deposit quote ($10.50\%$) is higher than the theoretical fair FRA rate ($8.00\%$), the market is overpricing future lending returns. An arbitrageur borrows 12-month funds at $9.00\%$, lends spot for 6 months at $9.60\%$, and simultaneously locks in the second 6 months at $10.50\%$, yielding an overall return higher than the borrowing cost.

📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 3.2
Under the "Compounded in Arrears" convention used in SOFR/SONIA loan and swap contracts, when is the final payable interest amount determined?
A. At or near the end of the interest accrual period, by compounding daily overnight fixings across the period
B. At the beginning of the interest period on trade date, identically to LIBOR
C. At the exact midpoint of the loan tenure using linear interpolation
D. On the initial loan origination date
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
Because SOFR is an overnight rate published daily, the interest rate for a 3-month or 6-month period is calculated by compounding daily rates in arrears. Consequently, the borrower knows the exact interest payable only at the end of the period (or a few days prior if an observation lag is applied).

📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 4.1
A corporate purchases a 1-year Cap option on a notional principal of INR 100 crores at a strike rate of 8.00% p.a. (quarterly reset). In Q1, the benchmark interest rate fixes at 8.50% p.a., and in Q2 it fixes at 7.50% p.a. What are the gross option payoffs received in Q1 and Q2?
A. INR 12,50,000 in Q1 and Nil in Q2
B. INR 50,00,000 in Q1 and Nil in Q2
C. INR 12,50,000 in Q1 and INR 12,50,000 in Q2
D. Nil in Q1 and INR 12,50,000 in Q2
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
• Q1: Actual rate ($8.5\%$) > Strike ($8.0\%$). Option is exercised:
$\text{Payoff} = 100,00,00,000 \times (8.5\% - 8.0\%) \times \frac{3}{12} = \text{INR } 12,50,000$
• Q2: Actual rate ($7.5\%$) < Strike ($8.0\%$). Option expires unexercised: Payoff = Nil.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 4.2
A financial lender holding floating-rate loans buys an interest rate Floor with a strike rate of 6.00% p.a. on a notional amount of INR 50 crores (reset semi-annually). If the benchmark drops to 4.80% p.a. on reset date, what payoff does the lender receive?
A. INR 30,00,000
B. INR 60,00,000
C. INR 15,00,000
D. Nil
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
A floor option pays when actual rates fall below the strike:
$\text{Payoff} = \text{Notional} \times (\text{Strike} - \text{Actual}) \times \frac{6}{12} = 50,00,00,000 \times (6.0\% - 4.8\%) \times 0.5 = 50,00,00,000 \times 0.012 \times 0.5 = \text{INR } 30,00,000$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 4.3
XYZ Ltd borrows GBP 10 million floating at LIBOR + 0.50% (reset semi-annually). It implements a collar by buying a Cap at 7.00% and selling a Floor at 4.00%. If LIBOR fixes at 3.00% on reset date, what is the net total interest outflow for the 6-month period?
A. GBP 225,000 (effective rate 4.50% p.a.)
B. GBP 175,000 (effective rate 3.50% p.a.)
C. GBP 350,000 (effective rate 7.00% p.a.)
D. GBP 200,000 (effective rate 4.00% p.a.)
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
1. Interest on primary loan: $10,000,000 \times (3.0\% + 0.5\%) \times 0.5 = \text{GBP } 175,000$.
2. Cap option: LIBOR ($3\%$) < Cap ($7\%$) $\implies$ Nil.
3. Floor option: As floor seller, firm must pay the shortfall when LIBOR ($3\%$) < Floor ($4\%$):
$\text{Floor Payment} = 10,000,000 \times (4\% - 3\%) \times 0.5 = \text{GBP } 50,000$
4. Total Outflow = $175,000 + 50,000 = \text{GBP } 225,000$ (Effective cost = $4.50\%$ p.a.).

5. Interest Rate Swaps (IRS) & Overnight Indexed Swaps (OIS)

Core Concept: Contract between two counterparties to exchange interest rate cash flows based on a specified notional principal without exchanging the underlying principal.

Swap Parameter Fixed Rate Payer (Borrower Hedge) Floating Rate Payer (Asset Hedge)
Cash Flow Obligation Pays fixed coupon rate ($R_{\text{swap}}$); receives floating (e.g. MIBOR / SOFR). Pays floating benchmark rate; receives fixed coupon rate.
Market Expectation Anticipates rising interest rates; locks in fixed borrowing cost. Anticipates declining interest rates; benefits from falling floating costs.
Net Settlement Flow $\text{Net Cash Flow} = (R_{\text{floating}} - R_{\text{fixed}}) \times N \times \frac{d}{360\text{ or }365}$ $\text{Net Cash Flow} = (R_{\text{fixed}} - R_{\text{floating}}) \times N \times \frac{d}{360\text{ or }365}$
OIS Structure Floating leg is the geometric compound daily average of overnight rate (e.g. daily overnight MIBOR) settled against fixed rate at maturity.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 5.1
Company A (AAA-rated) can borrow Fixed at 8.00% or Floating at MIBOR + 0.20%. Company B (BBB-rated) can borrow Fixed at 10.00% or Floating at MIBOR + 1.20%. What is the total potential net swap gain available to be shared between the counterparties?
A. 1.00% p.a. (100 bps)
B. 2.00% p.a.
C. 0.50% p.a.
D. 1.20% p.a.
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
$\text{Fixed Rate Differential} = 10.00\% - 8.00\% = 2.00\%$ $\text{Floating Rate Differential} = 1.20\% - 0.20\% = 1.00\%$ $\text{Total Swap Gain} = |2.00\% - 1.00\%| = 1.00\% \text{ p.a. (100 basis points)}$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 5.2
In a plain vanilla fixed-for-floating Interest Rate Swap on USD 20 million notional (semi-annual 180/360 basis), Party A pays fixed at 5.50% and receives floating SOFR. If SOFR fixes at 6.00% p.a. for the period, what is the net settlement payment?
A. Party B pays USD 50,000 to Party A
B. Party A pays USD 50,000 to Party B
C. Party B pays USD 100,000 to Party A
D. Both parties exchange gross principal amounts
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
• Floating inflow to A: $\$20,000,000 \times 6.00\% \times \frac{180}{360} = \$600,000$.
• Fixed outflow from A: $\$20,000,000 \times 5.50\% \times \frac{180}{360} = \$550,000$.
• Net settlement: Party B pays Party A $\$600,000 - \$550,000 = \text{USD } 50,000$.

6. Swaptions Architecture & Decision Rules

Swaption Type Holder Has Right To Exercise Rule Core Motivation
Payer Swaption Pay Fixed & Receive Floating Market Swap Rate $>$ Strike Rate Hedge against rising fixed borrowing costs on anticipated future debt.
Receiver Swaption Receive Fixed & Pay Floating Market Swap Rate $<$ Strike Rate Hedge against declining fixed yields on future portfolio investments.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 6.1
A corporate purchases a European Payer Swaption with a strike rate of 6.25% p.a. on a 5-year swap. At expiration, prevailing market swap rates have risen to 7.00% p.a. What action will the corporate take?
A. Exercise the swaption to pay fixed at 6.25% and receive floating
B. Exercise the swaption to receive fixed at 6.25% and pay floating
C. Allow the swaption to expire unexercised because 7.00% is higher
D. Convert the swaption into an overnight index swap
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
A Payer Swaption grants the right to pay fixed and receive floating. Since the prevailing market rate ($7.00\%$) is higher than the strike ($6.25\%$), the holder exercises the swaption to lock in the below-market fixed payment of $6.25\%$.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 6.2
Which market participant is the natural hedger that would purchase a "Receiver Swaption"?
A. An institutional investor holding floating-rate debt assets seeking protection against declining interest yields
B. A floating-rate corporate borrower seeking protection against rising interest rates
C. An equity portfolio manager hedging against foreign exchange volatility
D. A fixed-rate issuer looking to convert debt into floating obligations
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
A Receiver Swaption gives the holder the right to receive fixed and pay floating. An investor who receives floating interest on debt investments is exposed to reinvestment risk if interest rates fall; buying a receiver swaption sets a floor on the yield.

6. Exam Pitfalls & Traps

âš ï¸ Pitfall 1: Forgetting to Discount FRA Settlement
Never just calculate $N \times (RR - FR) \times \frac{dtm}{DY}$. That is the interest at maturity! Settlement happens at fixing date (start of loan period), so you must divide by $[1 + RR \times \frac{dtm}{DY}]$.
âš ï¸ Pitfall 2: Day-Count Bases in Swaps
Generic fixed swap legs use 30/360 ($\frac{180}{360} = 0.50$). Floating legs use actual days / 360 (e.g., $\frac{181}{360} = 0.502778$). Always watch for the differing denominators!
âš ï¸ Pitfall 3: Weekend Handling in OIS Calculations
When compiling daily MIBOR tables, Saturday's rate applies for 2 days (Saturday + Sunday) because Sunday is a banking holiday. Multiply by $\frac{2}{365}$.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 7.1
A portfolio manager holds an INR 60 crore bond portfolio with a modified duration of 5.5 years. To hedge against rising yields, the manager sells 10-year GOI bond futures trading at INR 98 (contract size INR 2,00,000 notional) with a CTD modified duration of 7.0 years. How many futures contracts should be sold?
A. 2,408 contracts (approx.)
B. 1,500 contracts
C. 3,061 contracts
D. 1,850 contracts
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
$\text{Number of Contracts } N = \frac{V_p \times D_p}{V_f \times D_f}$ $V_p \times D_p = 60,00,00,000 \times 5.5 = 330,00,00,000$ $V_f = 2,00,000 \times \frac{98}{100} = \text{INR } 1,96,000 \implies V_f \times D_f = 1,96,000 \times 7.0 = 13,72,000$ $N = \frac{330,00,00,000}{13,72,000} \approx 2,405.25 \approx 2,408 \text{ contracts}$
