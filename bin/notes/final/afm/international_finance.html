1. Master Formula & Parity Cheat Sheet

1. Foreign Risk-Adjusted Discount Rate Formula (Fisher / Parity Relationship):
$$(1 + r_{\text{dom, risk-adjusted}}) = (1 + r_{\text{dom, rf}}) \times (1 + \text{Risk Premium})$$ $$1 + \text{Risk Premium} = \frac{1 + r_{\text{dom, risk-adjusted}}}{1 + r_{\text{dom, rf}}}$$ $$(1 + r_{\text{for, risk-adjusted}}) = (1 + r_{\text{for, rf}}) \times (1 + \text{Risk Premium})$$
2. Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) / Interest Rate Parity (IRP) for Future Exchange Rates:
$$F_t = S_0 \times \left( \frac{1 + I_{\text{foreign}}}{1 + I_{\text{domestic}}} \right)^t \quad \text{or} \quad F_t = S_0 \times \left( \frac{1 + r_{\text{foreign}}}{1 + r_{\text{domestic}}} \right)^t$$
3. Real to Nominal Cash Flow Adjustment:
$$\text{Nominal Cash Flow}_t = \text{Real Cash Flow}_t \times (1 + \text{Inflation Rate})^t$$
4. Modified Internal Rate of Return (MIRR):
$$\text{MIRR} = \left( \frac{\text{Terminal Inflow at Year } n}{\text{Initial Outlay at Year } 0} \right)^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1$$ Where Terminal Inflow = $\sum \left( \text{Cash Inflow}_t \times (1 + k)^{n-t} \right)$.
5. GDR Issue Share Requirement:
$$\text{GDR Issue Price} = \text{Domestic Market Price} \times (1 - \text{Discount}) \times \frac{\text{Domestic Currency}}{\text{Foreign Currency Rate}}$$ $$\text{Number of Shares per GDR} = \text{Fixed Ratio (e.g., 2 shares = 1 GDR)}$$ $$\text{Total Underlying Shares} = \text{Total GDRs Issued} \times \text{Shares per GDR}$$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 1.1
The required rate of return for an equity project in India is 12% per annum. The risk-free rate is 8% in India and 4% in the USA. Assuming the market risk premium remains constant across international currencies, what is the required rate of return for the same project denominated in USD?
A. 7.86%
B. 8.00%
C. 16.00%
D. 6.25%
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
1. Determine Risk Premium in INR:
$(1 + \text{Risky Rate}_{\text{INR}}) = (1 + R_{f,\text{INR}}) \times (1 + \text{Risk Premium})$ $(1 + 0.12) = (1 + 0.08) \times (1 + \text{Risk Premium}) \implies 1 + \text{Risk Premium} = \frac{1.12}{1.08} = 1.037037 \implies \text{Risk Premium} = 3.7037\%$ 2. Compute Required Risky Rate in USD:
$(1 + \text{Risky Rate}_{\text{USD}}) = (1 + R_{f,\text{USD}}) \times (1 + \text{Risk Premium}) = (1 + 0.04) \times 1.037037 = 1.078519$ $\text{Risky Rate}_{\text{USD}} = 7.8519\% \approx 7.86\%$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 1.2
An Indian corporate avails an External Commercial Borrowing (ECB) facility of GBP 10 million. The interest cost is 6-month GBP LIBOR (1.05%) plus a credit spread of 2.50% (total 3.55% p.a.). If the Average Loan Maturity of the facility is 3.4 years across an overall tenure of 5 years, what is the total expected interest cost?
A. GBP 1.207 million
B. GBP 1.775 million
C. GBP 0.8875 million
D. GBP 0.355 million
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
Total interest cost over the entire life of an amortizing or structured loan facility is calculated using Average Loan Maturity:
$\text{Total Interest Cost} = \text{Loan Principal} \times \text{Rate of Interest} \times \text{Average Loan Maturity}$ $\text{Total Interest} = \text{GBP } 10,000,000 \times (1.05\% + 2.50\%) \times 3.4 = 10,000,000 \times 0.0355 \times 3.4 = \text{GBP } 1,207,000 = \text{GBP } 1.207 \text{ million}$

2. Home Currency vs Foreign Currency Approach

Aspect Home Currency Approach Foreign Currency Approach
Step 1 Forecast cash flows in foreign currency. Forecast cash flows in foreign currency.
Step 2 Convert foreign CFs to home currency using expected forward rates (PPP/IRP). Derive foreign risk-adjusted discount rate using Fisher parity.
Step 3 Discount converted home currency CFs at the domestic risk-adjusted discount rate. Discount foreign CFs directly at foreign risk-adjusted rate to find Foreign NPV.
Step 4 Net Present Value in Home Currency obtained directly. Convert Foreign NPV to Home Currency at the current spot exchange rate ($S_0$).
Theoretical Equivalence Both approaches yield identical NPV if IRP/PPP holds strictly without market imperfections.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 2.1
A US MNC is evaluating an overseas capital investment project in India. In Indian Rupees (foreign currency), the project's NPV is computed as INR 10,000,000 discounted at the appropriate INR project cost of capital. The current spot rate is USD 1 = INR 50.00. Under frictionless international financial markets (CIRP and Fisher parity hold), what is the NPV in home currency (USD)?
A. USD 200,000
B. USD 500,000,000
C. USD 20,000
D. USD 50,000
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
• Under parity equivalence, the Home Currency Approach and Foreign Currency Approach yield identical results:
$\text{NPV}_{\text{Home (USD)}} = \frac{\text{NPV}_{\text{Foreign (INR)}}}{S_0} = \frac{\text{INR } 10,000,000}{50.00} = \text{USD } 200,000$ • Converting the foreign NPV at current spot rate $S_0$ produces the exact same result as converting each periodic foreign cash flow at forward rates and discounting at the domestic rate.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 2.2
An Indian company plans to set up a US venture requiring a total capital outlay of USD 5,000,000. It already holds surplus funds of USD 2,000,000 in a US bank account that cannot be repatriated to India due to host country exchange controls. If these blocked funds are redeployed into the new venture, what is the incremental initial cash outlay for evaluating the project from the Indian parent's perspective?
A. USD 3,000,000
B. USD 5,000,000
C. USD 7,000,000
D. USD 2,000,000
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
• Blocked funds already trapped overseas have an effective economic opportunity cost of zero in remitted home currency terms.
• When these blocked funds are utilized to partially finance the new project, they directly reduce the net fresh cash commitment from the parent:
$\text{Incremental Cash Outlay} = \text{Total Project Cost} - \text{Blocked Funds Deployed} = 5,000,000 - 2,000,000 = \text{USD } 3,000,000$

📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 3.1
A foreign subsidiary in India earns a Profit Before Tax (PBT) of INR 100 crores. India levies a 30% corporate income tax and a 10% withholding tax on remitted dividend distributions. Assuming taxes paid in India are NOT eligible for tax credit in the parent's home country, what is the net repatriated cash flow?
A. INR 63 crores
B. INR 70 crores
C. INR 60 crores
D. INR 77 crores
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
1. Profit Before Tax (PBT) = INR 100 crores
2. Less: Corporate Tax @ 30% = INR 30 crores $\implies \text{Profit After Tax (PAT)} = 100 - 30 = \text{INR } 70\text{ crores}$
3. Less: Withholding Tax @ 10% on remitted dividend $= 70 \times 10\% = \text{INR } 7\text{ crores}$
4. Net Repatriated Amount $= 70 - 7 = \text{INR } 63\text{ crores}$
Because foreign tax credits are unavailable, withholding tax acts as an outright deadweight leakage reducing cash inflows.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 3.2
A US corporation invests surplus cash in a German debt instrument yielding interest of EUR 120,190. German statutory domestic law imposes a 22% withholding tax on interest, but the Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) between USA and Germany prescribes a 10% withholding rate. What is the applicable withholding tax deducted in Germany?
A. EUR 12,019
B. EUR 26,441.80
C. EUR 38,460.80
D. EUR 14,422.80
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
Under international tax treaties, an investor is entitled to the beneficial rate between the domestic tax statute and the applicable DTAA treaty:
$\text{Applicable Rate} = \min(\text{Domestic Tax Rate } 22\%, \text{DTAA Rate } 10\%) = 10\%$ $\text{Withholding Tax Deducted} = \text{EUR } 120,190 \times 10\% = \text{EUR } 12,019$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 3.3
Which of the following creates a substantive divergence between Project Cash Flows (from the foreign subsidiary's perspective) and Parent Cash Flows (from the parent MNC's perspective)?

📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 4.1
An Indian company issues Global Depository Receipts (GDRs) in Luxembourg. Each GDR represents 2 underlying equity shares of the company. The market price of each equity share is INR 400. If the GDR is priced at a 5% discount to market and the prevailing exchange rate is USD 1 = INR 80, what is the issue price of 1 GDR in USD?
A. USD 9.50
B. USD 4.75
C. USD 10.00
D. USD 19.00
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
1. Value of underlying shares per GDR $= 2 \times \text{INR } 400 = \text{INR } 800$.
2. Discounted issue price in INR $= 800 \times (1 - 0.05) = \text{INR } 760$.
3. Issue price in USD $= \frac{\text{INR } 760}{\text{INR 80/USD}} = \text{USD } 9.50$.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 4.2
Which of the following statements accurately characterizes the distinction between American Depository Receipts (ADRs) and Global Depository Receipts (GDRs)?
A. ADRs are issued exclusively in the US market subject to SEC registration and trade on US exchanges in USD, while GDRs are issued globally outside the US (e.g. London or Luxembourg) and traded predominantly over-the-counter
B. ADR holders enjoy statutory voting rights in annual general meetings, whereas GDR holders are legally barred from receiving dividends
C. GDRs can only be denominated in Euro or British Pounds, never in US Dollars

Correct Answer: A

📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 4.3
What constitutes the primary financial and solvency risk for an issuing company raising debt via Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds (FCCBs)?
A. If the company's equity share price remains below the conversion strike price, bondholders will decline conversion, forcing the issuer to redeem the bonds in foreign currency at substantial cash outflow
B. FCCBs instantly dilute promoter shareholding by 50% on the date of allotment
C. The coupon rate on FCCBs is substantially higher than standard domestic non-convertible debentures
D. Interest paid on FCCBs is non-deductible under all corporate tax jurisdictions
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
• FCCBs feature low coupon rates because investors receive an equity call option.
• However, if the underlying equity stock fails to perform and trades below the conversion price ("underwater" options), investors will refuse conversion and demand cash redemption at maturity.

5. IFSC & GIFT City: Fast Facts

High-Yield Exam Points on GIFT IFSC:
  • Regulator: International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA) - unified statutory regulator established under IFSCA Act, 2019.
  • Jurisdiction Status: Deemed foreign territory for foreign exchange management and customs purposes; domestic companies inside IFSC enjoy foreign resident status.
  • Corporate Tax Incentive: 100% tax holiday on business income for any 10 consecutive years out of an initial block of 15 years.
  • Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT): Reduced rate of 9% (and nil for units opting for new tax regime).
  • Exemptions: No GST on services provided to IFSC units, no Securities Transaction Tax (STT) or Commodities Transaction Tax (CTT) on IFSC stock exchanges.

C. Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) exclusively

6. International Working Capital Techniques

Technique Operational Mechanism Primary Benefit
Multilateral Netting Centralized netting centre computes bilateral intra-group claims across worldwide subsidiaries and offsets balances to a single net payment. Drastically slashes cross-border banking transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, and FX conversion fees.
Centralized Cash Pooling Surplus cash across all operating subsidiaries is swept into a single master account at a global money center. Eliminates idle balances, maximizes interest yields, and minimizes external group borrowing requirements.
Leading & Lagging Accelerating (leading) payments in weak/depreciating currencies; delaying (lagging) payments in strong/appreciating currencies. Reduces currency exchange losses on intra-corporate trade credit without derivative hedging fees.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 6.1
In multinational working capital management, what is the core operational benefit derived from implementing a centralized Multilateral Netting system across global subsidiaries?
A. Drastically reduces the volume and frequency of intercompany cross-border funds transfers, saving transaction charges and bid-ask spread losses
B. Eliminates all local sovereign political risk across operating jurisdictions
C. Permanently avoids host country corporate income tax liabilities
D. Ensures immediate credit rating upgrade of all participating subsidiaries to AAA
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
• Multilateral Netting consolidates all reciprocal intra-group invoices into a central clearing matrix at designated intervals.
• Offsetting payables against receivables ensures that only net residual balances are remitted, slashing currency conversion fees, foreign exchange spread costs, banking transfer fees, and settlement float time.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 6.2
How does Notional Cash Pooling fundamentally differ from Physical Cash Sweeping in multinational liquidity management?
A. Notional Pooling offsets debit and credit balances virtually to calculate net interest without actual cross-border fund transfers, whereas Physical Sweeping physically moves cash into a single master account
B. Notional Pooling requires daily physical wire transfers between all subsidiary bank accounts
C. Physical Sweeping avoids creating any intercompany loans or transfer pricing documentation
D. Notional Pooling is restricted exclusively to domestic local currency accounts
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
• In Notional Pooling, participating accounts maintain separate legal balances; the bank virtually nets debit and credit interest without physical transfers, bypassing cross-border withholding tax and intercompany loan friction.
• In Physical Sweeping, cash is actually wired out of subsidiary accounts into a concentration account, creating formal intercompany loan balances that require arm's-length interest accounting.

An Indian corporate plans an overseas infrastructure venture in Nepal funded with 55% equity and 45% debt. The corporate tax rate is 30% in India and 20% in Nepal.
