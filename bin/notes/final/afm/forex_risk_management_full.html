Topic 1

FX Market Structure, Market Participants & Value Dates

1. Content in ICAI Book

The Foreign Exchange (Forex) Market: The international decentralized OTC market where currencies of different nations are bought, sold, and exchanged. It operates continuously 24 hours a day across global financial hubs (Tokyo, Singapore, London, New York, Zurich).

Market Hierarchy:

  1. Interbank / Wholesale Market: Major international commercial and investment banks trading massive volumes with tight bid-ask spreads.
  2. Merchant / Retail Market: Commercial banks quoting rates to retail clients, corporate importers, exporters, and individual travelers (carrying bank profit margins).
  3. Central Banks: Intervene to smooth exchange rate volatility, maintain foreign exchange reserves, and implement national monetary policy.

Settlement & Value Dates:

Transaction Type Value Date Terminology Settlement Timing
Cash / Ready Value Today Settlement takes place on the exact same day of deal agreement ($t+0$).
TOM Value Tomorrow Settlement takes place on the next working business day ($t+1$).
Spot Transaction Value Spot Settlement takes place on the second working business day ($t+2$) after the trade date.
Forward Transaction Value Forward Settlement takes place on a specified agreed date beyond the spot date ($> t+2$).
2. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Spot Date Standard: Always remember that in foreign exchange, Spot means $t+2$ business days. If Sunday or bank holidays intervene, the value date is postponed to the next valid banking day.
Topic 2

Nostro, Vostro & Loro Accounts, Exchange & Cash Positions

1. Content in ICAI Book

Correspondent banking relationships rely on three distinct accounts (Latin terminology):

Account Type Literal Meaning Operational Definition & Example
Nostro Account "Our account with you" A bank's account maintained in a foreign country in foreign currency with a foreign correspondent bank.
Example: State Bank of India (SBI) maintaining an account in USD with Citibank, New York.
Vostro Account "Your account with us" A foreign bank's account maintained in the domestic country in domestic currency with a domestic bank.
Example: Citibank New York maintaining an account in INR with SBI, Mumbai.
Loro Account "Their account with them" Referring to an account of a third party bank maintained with another bank.
Example: SBI referring to Bank of Baroda's account with Citibank New York.

Exchange Position vs. Cash Position (Illustration 2 & PQ 51):

  • Exchange Position: Measures the bank's net foreign currency commitment or market exposure, combining both spot and forward commitments.
    • Overbought / Long Position: $\text{Total Purchases (Spot + Forward)} > \text{Total Sales}$. Bank gains if foreign currency appreciates.
    • Oversold / Short Position: $\text{Total Sales (Spot + Forward)} > \text{Total Purchases}$. Bank gains if foreign currency depreciates.
    • Square / Balanced Position: Purchases equal Sales (Zero market currency risk).
  • Cash Position (Nostro Balance): Reflects the actual physical cash funds available in the Nostro account (only settled spot cash flows, remittances, and bank drafts; forward commitments are excluded).
2. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Theoretical Question 2: "What do you mean by Nostro, Vostro and Loro Accounts?" is a direct 4-mark question. Provide definitions and exact examples.
  • Squaring Positions: In Illustration 2, balancing Cash Position requires spot transactions, while balancing Exchange Position requires forward transactions.
Topic 3

Direct vs. Indirect Quotes & American vs. European Terms

1. Content in ICAI Book

Quotation Conventions: An exchange rate expresses the value of one currency (Base Currency) in units of another currency (Quote / Variable Currency):

$$\text{Exchange Rate} = \frac{\text{Quote Currency}}{\text{Base Currency}} \quad \text{e.g., } \text{USD/INR } = 83.00 \text{ (1 USD = 83 INR)}$$
Convention Definition Example in India
Direct Quote Units of Domestic Currency required to buy 1 unit of Foreign Currency.
Fixed = 1 Foreign Currency; Variable = Domestic Currency.		 $1\text{ USD} = \text{₹ } 83.20$
$1\text{ EUR} = \text{₹ } 90.50$
Indirect Quote Units of Foreign Currency obtained for 1 unit of Domestic Currency.
Fixed = 1 Domestic Currency; Variable = Foreign Currency.		 $\text{₹ } 1 = \text{USD } 0.01202$
$\text{₹ } 100 = \text{USD } 1.202$
American Terms Exchange rate expressed as units of US Dollar per unit of foreign currency (USD is quote currency). $\text{GBP/USD } = 1.2800$
$\text{EUR/USD } = 1.0900$
European Terms Exchange rate expressed as units of foreign currency per 1 US Dollar (USD is base currency). $\text{USD/INR } = 83.20$
$\text{USD/JPY } = 155.00$
Mathematical Inversion Rule
$$\text{Direct Quote} = \frac{1}{\text{Indirect Quote}}$$ $$\text{Bid (Direct)} = \frac{1}{\text{Ask (Indirect)}}; \quad \text{Ask (Direct)} = \frac{1}{\text{Bid (Indirect)}}$$
Topic 4

Two-Way Quotes, Bid-Ask Spread, Pips & Cross Rates

1. Content in ICAI Book

Two-Way Quote: In the forex market, quotes are given as $\text{Bid} / \text{Ask}$ (or $\text{Buy} / \text{Sell}$ from the bank's viewpoint):

  • Bid Rate: Price at which the market maker (bank) buys the base currency. (Customer sells at Bid).
  • Ask / Offer Rate: Price at which the market maker (bank) sells the base currency. (Customer buys at Ask).
  • Rule: The bank always buys low and sells high ($\text{Ask} > \text{Bid}$).
$$\text{Spread} = \text{Ask Rate} - \text{Bid Rate}; \quad \%\text{ Spread} = \frac{\text{Ask} - \text{Bid}}{\text{Ask}} \times 100$$

Pips: Price Interest Point, the smallest standard price increment (typically the 4th decimal place: $0.0001$, or 2nd decimal for JPY: $0.01$).

Cross Currency Rates: An exchange rate between two currencies calculated from their respective exchange rates against a common third currency (usually USD):

Desired Cross Rate Formula using Common Currency ($Z$)
Bid Rate for $\frac{X}{Y}$ $$\left(\frac{X}{Y}\right)_{\text{Bid}} = \left(\frac{X}{Z}\right)_{\text{Bid}} \times \left(\frac{Z}{Y}\right)_{\text{Bid}} = \frac{(X/Z)_{\text{Bid}}}{(Y/Z)_{\text{Ask}}}$$
Ask Rate for $\frac{X}{Y}$ $$\left(\frac{X}{Y}\right)_{\text{Ask}} = \left(\frac{X}{Z}\right)_{\text{Ask}} \times \left(\frac{Z}{Y}\right)_{\text{Ask}} = \frac{(X/Z)_{\text{Ask}}}{(Y/Z)_{\text{Bid}}}$$
2. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Always Think Bank's Perspective: When an importer customer wants foreign currency, the bank SELLS to them at the Ask rate. When an exporter receives foreign currency, the bank BUYS from them at the Bid rate.
  • Chain Rule Cross Rates: Cancel matching currencies: $\frac{\text{EUR}}{\text{INR}} = \frac{\text{EUR}}{\text{USD}} \times \frac{\text{USD}}{\text{INR}}$.
Topic 5

Forward Rates, Swap Points, Premium/Discount & Broken Periods

1. Content in ICAI Book

Forward Exchange Rate Quotations: Quoted either as an outright rate or as Forward Margins / Swap Points added/subtracted from the Spot rate:

Condition of Swap Points Status of Base Currency Rule to get Forward Rate
Ascending Order ($\text{Bid Points} < \text{Ask Points}$)
e.g., Spot 82.50/55, 1-m 20/30		 Base Currency is at a Premium ADD Swap Points to Spot:
$\text{Bid} = 82.50 + 0.20 = 82.70$
$\text{Ask} = 82.55 + 0.30 = 82.85$
Descending Order ($\text{Bid Points} > \text{Ask Points}$)
e.g., Spot 82.50/55, 1-m 30/20		 Base Currency is at a Discount SUBTRACT Swap Points from Spot:
$\text{Bid} = 82.50 - 0.30 = 82.20$
$\text{Ask} = 82.55 - 0.20 = 82.35$

Annualized Premium / Discount Formula:

$$\%\text{ Premium / Discount} = \frac{\text{Forward Rate} - \text{Spot Rate}}{\text{Spot Rate}} \times \frac{12}{\text{Tenure in Months}} \times 100$$

Broken Period Forward Rate: When the contract maturity falls on a date between standard monthly dates, use linear interpolation between the two adjacent forward quotes.

2. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Ascending = Add, Descending = Deduct: Remembering this single rule eliminates 90% of sign errors in forward quotation problems!
  • Decimals Alignment: Pay attention to whether swap points are given in paise (2 decimal places) or pips (4 decimal places).

Merchant Rates Architecture (FEDAI Guidelines)

1. Content in ICAI Book

Banks quote Merchant Rates to commercial customers by adjusting the interbank market rate with an exchange margin specified by FEDAI (Foreign Exchange Dealers' Association of India):

Rate Classification Applicability Interbank Base Exchange Margin Rule
TT Buying Rate Inward remittances, cancellation of outward TT, payment of demand drafts already credited. Clean transactions where foreign funds are already in Nostro account. Interbank Bid Rate Deduct Margin:
$\text{Rate} = \text{Interbank Bid} \times (1 - \text{Margin})$

$\text{Rate} = \text{Interbank Ask} \times (1 + \text{Margin})$

$\text{Rate} = \text{Interbank Ask} \times (1 + \text{Margin})$

Topic 7

International Parity Relationships (IRP, PPP, IFE)

1. Content in ICAI Book

The three fundamental parity theorems connect exchange rates, interest rates, and inflation rates:

Parity Theory Fundamental Equation (Direct Quote $\frac{D}{F}$) Economic Meaning & Equilibrium Condition
1. Interest Rate Parity (IRP) $$\frac{F_{D/F}}{S_{D/F}} = \frac{1 + r_D}{1 + r_F} \approx 1 + (r_D - r_F)$$ The forward premium/discount equals the nominal interest rate differential between the domestic and foreign economies. Eliminates Covered Interest Arbitrage (CIA).
2. Purchasing Power Parity (PPP - Relative) $$\frac{E(S_t)}{S_0} = \frac{1 + I_D}{1 + I_F} \approx 1 + (I_D - I_F)$$ The expected percentage change in the spot exchange rate equals the expected inflation rate differential between the two countries (Law of One Price).
3. International Fisher Effect (IFE) $$\frac{E(S_t)}{S_0} = \frac{1 + r_D}{1 + r_F} \approx 1 + (r_D - r_F)$$ Currencies with high nominal interest rates are expected to depreciate against currencies with low nominal interest rates over the investment horizon.

Covered Interest Arbitrage (CIA) Mechanics:

  • If $\frac{F}{S} > \frac{1 + r_D}{1 + r_F} \implies$ Forward rate is too high. Borrow Domestic, Convert Spot to Foreign, Invest in Foreign, Sell Foreign Forward.
  • If $\frac{F}{S} < \frac{1 + r_D}{1 + r_F} \implies$ Forward rate is too low. Borrow Foreign, Convert Spot to Domestic, Invest in Domestic, Buy Foreign Forward.
2. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Direct vs Indirect Form: When exchange rate is direct ($\text{INR/USD}$), domestic rate ($r_h$) is in the numerator: $\frac{F}{S} = \frac{1 + r_h}{1 + r_f}$. If quote is indirect ($\text{USD/INR}$), foreign rate is in the numerator!
Topic 8

Two-Point Geographic & Three-Point Triangular Arbitrage

1. Content in ICAI Book

Geographical (Two-Point) Arbitrage: Capitalizing on simultaneous price discrepancies for the same currency pair across two geographically separated financial centres (e.g., USD priced at ₹ 82.50 in Mumbai and ₹ 82.70 in London). Buy in the cheaper market and sell in the dearer market.

Triangular (Three-Point) Arbitrage: Exploiting misalignments among three mutually related currency pairs across three financial markets:

  1. Compute the Cross Rate between Currency A and B through common Currency C.
  2. Compare the synthetic Cross Rate with the direct market quote.
  3. If synthetic rate $\neq$ quoted rate, an arbitrage opportunity exists. Start with 1 unit of Currency A, execute consecutive conversions across the triangle, and end with $> 1$ unit of Currency A.
2. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Bid-Ask Spreads in Arbitrage: In realistic exam problems (e.g. PQ 13, PQ 47), banks buy at Bid and sell at Ask. Ensure your triangular path yields a net gain after paying the bid-ask spread at every stage!

Topic 10

Internal Hedging Techniques

1. Content in ICAI Book

Internal techniques mitigate forex risk within the multinational group without paying external bank fees or derivative margins:

  1. Invoicing in Home Currency: Passing the entire currency risk onto the foreign counterparty by denominating exports in home currency (e.g., INR).
  2. Netting (Bilateral & Multilateral): Consolidating intra-group cash inflows and outflows and settling only the net balance through a centralized treasury netting center.
  3. Leading and Lagging: Accelerating (leading) or delaying (lagging) payment dates based on expected currency movements (Lead payments in appreciating currencies; lag payments in depreciating currencies).
  4. Matching: Offsetting natural foreign currency inflows from exports against foreign currency outflows for imports in the same currency.
  5. Price Variation: Adjusting selling prices to offset anticipated currency devaluations.
Topic 11

External Hedging: Forward Cover vs. Money Market Hedge (MMH)

1. Content in ICAI Book

The Money Market Hedge (MMH) synthetic structure locks in certain domestic cash flows using domestic and foreign money markets:

Step Hedging Foreign Currency Receivable (Export Bill) Hedging Foreign Currency Payable (Import Bill)
Step 1: Borrowing Today Borrow foreign currency equal to its discounted present value: $$\text{Borrow } FC = \frac{\text{Receivable}}{1 + r_{FC}^{\text{borrow}} \cdot t}$$ Borrow domestic currency ($DC$) required to purchase the discounted foreign currency: $$\text{Borrow } DC = \text{Spot Ask} \times \frac{\text{Payable}}{1 + r_{FC}^{\text{invest}} \cdot t}$$
Step 2: Spot Conversion Sell borrowed $FC$ in spot market at Spot Bid to receive domestic currency ($DC$). Convert borrowed $DC$ into $FC$ at Spot Ask.
Step 3: Investment Invest realized $DC$ in domestic money market at $r_{DC}^{\text{invest}}$ for period $t$. Invest $FC$ in foreign money market at $r_{FC}^{\text{invest}}$ for period $t$.
Step 4: Settlement at Maturity Export proceeds received from customer repay the foreign currency loan plus interest. Domestic investment matures. Foreign investment matures to exactly pay the supplier invoice. Domestic loan is repaid with interest.
2. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Comparison Table Required: In questions asking "Which proposal is best: Forward or MMH?" (e.g. PQ 12, 22, 29, 37, 41), always compute effective net cash inflow (for exports) or net cash outflow (for imports) on the exact same date (either today or at maturity $T$) to enable an apples-to-apples comparison!
Topic 12

External Hedging: Currency Options & Zero-Cost Range Forwards

1. Content in ICAI Book

Currency Options: Unlike forward contracts which impose an absolute obligation, currency options provide asymmetric protection:

  • Importer (Hedging Payable): Buys a Call Option on foreign currency. Protects against depreciation beyond strike $X$, but allows participation in favorable appreciation.
  • Exporter (Hedging Receivable): Buys a Put Option on foreign currency. Guarantees a minimum floor exchange rate $X$, while allowing upside participation.

Zero-Cost Collar / Range Forward / Cylinder: An exporter buys an OTM Put option (paying premium) and simultaneously sells an OTM Call option (receiving premium) such that the two premiums exactly offset:

$$\text{Net Premium Outlay} = \text{Put Premium Paid} - \text{Call Premium Received} = \mathbf{0}$$

Locks the realized exchange rate within a defined corridor $[X_{\text{Put}}, X_{\text{Call}}]$.

Topic 13

Forward Contract Execution, Cancellation & Extension (FEDAI Rules)

1. Content in ICAI Book

When an existing forward contract cannot be executed as originally contracted, FEDAI guidelines govern the settlement charges (Illustrations 3 to 9 & PQ 32, 44, 45):

Lifecycle Event Operational Mechanism Charges / Cash Flows Recovered by Bank
1. Cancellation on Due Date Customer requests cancellation on maturity date. Bank enters into an offsetting spot transaction. Forward Purchase Cancellation: Cancel at prevailing Spot TT Selling Rate.
Forward Sale Cancellation: Cancel at prevailing Spot TT Buying Rate.
Difference = Cancellation Gain / (Loss) + Flat Cancellation Charges.
2. Cancellation Before Due Date (Early Cancellation) Customer requests cancellation prior to maturity. Bank enters into an offsetting forward contract maturing on original date. Cancel at prevailing Forward Selling / Buying Rate for the residual period.
Difference + Flat Cancellation Charges.
3. Early Delivery / Execution Before Due Date Customer wants to deliver currency prior to agreed maturity date. Bank delivers at original contracted rate, but executes an offsetting swap to cover cash mismatch. Swap Loss (if any) and interest on outlay of funds are recovered from customer.
4. Extension / Rollover Customer requests postponement of delivery date. Existing contract is first cancelled at prevailing market rates (settling cancellation gain/loss), and a new forward contract is booked at current forward market rates.
5. Automatic Cancellation Customer fails to appear or communicate instructions within 3 working days after maturity date. Bank automatically cancels the contract on the 3rd business day at prevailing TT Buying / Selling rate. Customer remains liable for all losses and interest.
2. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Swap Difference Rule: In early delivery problems (Illustration 7, 8), Swap Difference = Difference between original forward rate and swap cover rate. If bank incurs a cost, customer must bear it.
  • Interest on Outlay of Funds: Charged at Prime Lending Rate (PLR) on the actual cash outlay borne by the bank from spot date until the original forward date.
Topic 14

Currency Swaps & Cross-Currency Mechanics

1. Content in ICAI Book

Currency Swap: A financial contract in which two counterparties exchange principal amounts and periodic interest payments in two different currencies at inception, and reverse the principal exchange at a predetermined rate upon maturity.

Three Distinct Cash Flow Phases:

  1. Inception: Exchange of principal amounts at prevailing spot exchange rate ($S_0$).
  2. Interim Life: Periodic exchange of interest payments (one fixed/floating in Currency A, the other fixed/floating in Currency B).
  3. Maturity: Re-exchange of original principal amounts at the original inception exchange rate ($S_0$), completely immunizing both parties from exchange rate volatility.

Comparative Advantage in Currency Swaps: Companies exploit institutional market segmentations or domestic credit rating premiums to lower borrowing costs across foreign jurisdictions.

Corporate Hedging Policy & Risk Appetite Matrix

1. Content in ICAI Book

Corporate risk management frameworks categorize hedging strategies into four distinct risk-reward quadrants:

Quadrant Corporate Stance Recommended Hedging Tools
1. Low Risk - Low Reward Strict risk aversion; 100% hedge ratio. Eliminate all foreign exchange volatility. Outright Forward Contracts, Money Market Hedges, Fixed-to-Fixed Currency Swaps.
2. Low Risk - Reasonable Reward Downside protection guaranteed while retaining upside participation. Purchased Plain Vanilla Currency Options, Zero-Cost Collars / Range Forwards.
3. High Risk - Low Reward Unhedged open speculative positions with no downside protection. Flawed treasury strategy. Uncovered open positions in volatile emerging market currencies.
4. High Risk - High Reward Active selective treasury positioning based on quantitative proprietary forecasting. Dynamic delta hedging, structured barrier options, algorithmic currency trading.

Illustration 3

Forward Sale Contract Cancellation on Due Date (FEDAI Guidelines)

Problem Statement (ICAI Study Material)

On 1st June 2015, the bank enters into a forward contract for 2 months for selling US$ 1,00,000 at ₹ 65.5000. On 1st August 2015 (the due date), the customer requests the bank to cancel the contract. On this date, the spot interbank rates are:

$$\text{Spot USD/INR} = 65.1000 / 65.3000$$

The bank charges an exchange margin of 0.10% on buying, and a flat cancellation charge of ₹ 100.

Required: Determine the cancellation charges payable by or refund due to the customer.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Determine Offsetting Market Rate

The original contract is a Forward Sale Contract (Bank agreed to sell USD at ₹ 65.5000). To cancel on due date, the bank must execute an offsetting purchase from the customer at prevailing Spot TT Buying Rate.

$$\text{Interbank Spot Bid Rate} = 65.1000$$ $$\text{Deduct Exchange Margin (0.10%)} = 65.1000 \times 0.0010 = 0.0651$$ $$\text{Spot TT Buying Rate applied} = 65.1000 - 0.0651 = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 65.0349}$$

Step 2: Calculate Cancellation Difference

Theory Question 1

Risks Involved in Foreign Exchange Operations

Question (ICAI Study Material)

“Operations in foreign exchange are exposed to a number of risks.” Discuss.

Answer (Verbatim & Comprehensive ICAI Analysis)

Financial institutions, banks, and multinational corporations operating in the foreign exchange market face several interconnected categories of risk:

  1. Exchange Rate / Market Risk: The risk of financial loss resulting from adverse movements in currency exchange rates while holding an open (overbought or oversold) position. Unhedged open positions directly impact profitability when market values shift.
  2. Settlement / Herstatt Risk: Occurs when one counterparty delivers currency as contracted, but the other party defaults before delivering the offsetting currency. Named after the failure of Germany's Bankhaus Herstatt in 1974, this risk arises primarily from time zone differences between global clearing centers.
  3. Liquidity Risk: The risk that a bank or trader cannot quickly execute a large currency transaction or unwind an existing position at prevailing market prices due to lack of market depth or market disruption.
  4. Credit / Counterparty Default Risk: The risk that the counterparty to an OTC forward or swap contract defaults prior to maturity, forcing the non-defaulting bank to replace the contract at unfavorable prevailing market rates (replacement cost risk).
  5. Sovereign / Country Risk: The risk that a foreign government imposes exchange controls, currency moratoria, capital restrictions, or expropriation, preventing foreign currency funds from being remitted across borders.
  6. Interest Rate Risk: Fluctuations in benchmark interest rates alter forward swap points, the cost of carrying currency positions, and the market valuation of currency derivative portfolios.
  7. Operational Risk: Losses arising from inadequate internal controls, unauthorized trading (rogue traders), computer hardware or software failures, communication breakdowns, or documentation errors.
