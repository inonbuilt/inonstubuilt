1. M&A Master Mathematical Formulas

1. Post-Merger Combined Shares & EPS:
$$N_{AB} = N_A + (N_T \times \text{Swap Ratio})$$ $$\text{Combined EPS } (EPS_{AB}) = \frac{\text{PAT}_A + \text{PAT}_T + \text{Synergy}}{N_{AB}}$$
2. Post-Merger Combined Market Capitalization & Price:
$$V_{AB} = V_A + V_T + \text{PV of Synergy} - \text{Cash Consideration}$$ $$\text{Post-Merger MPS } (P_{AB}) = \frac{V_{AB}}{N_{AB}} \quad \text{or} \quad \text{EPS}_{AB} \times (P/E)_{AB}$$
3. Shareholder Wealth Gain/Loss Formulas:
$$\text{Gain to Acquirer Shareholder} = (P_{AB} - P_A) \times N_A$$ $$\text{Gain to Target Shareholder} = (P_{AB} \times \text{Swap Ratio} - P_T) \times N_T$$
4. Maximum Exchange Ratio for Acquirer without EPS Dilution:
$$\text{Max Swap Ratio (EPS)} = \frac{\text{EPS}_T}{\text{EPS}_A}$$
5. Maximum Acquisition Bid Consideration:
$$\text{Max Purchase Consideration} = \text{Target Standalone Market Value} + \text{PV of Synergies} + \text{Cost Savings}$$ $$\text{Max Price per Target Share} = \frac{\text{Max Purchase Consideration}}{N_T}$$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 1.1
Pre-merger annual cash flows of Firm A are INR 25 lakhs (cost of capital 10%), and Firm B are INR 20 lakhs (cost of capital 20%). The combined entity will generate cash flows of INR 60 lakhs per annum with a post-merger cost of capital of 8% due to scale synergies. What is the total synergy gain from the merger?
A. INR 400 lakhs
B. INR 750 lakhs
C. INR 350 lakhs
D. INR 150 lakhs
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
1. $V_A = \frac{25}{0.10} = \text{INR } 250\text{ lakhs}$.
2. $V_B = \frac{20}{0.20} = \text{INR } 100\text{ lakhs}$.
3. Combined Pre-merger value $= 250 + 100 = \text{INR } 350\text{ lakhs}$.
4. Combined Post-merger value $V_{AB} = \frac{60}{0.08} = \text{INR } 750\text{ lakhs}$.
5. $\text{Synergy Gain} = V_{AB} - (V_A + V_B) = 750 - 350 = \text{INR } 400\text{ lakhs}$.

2. Swap Ratio Cheat Sheet Across Methodologies

Valuation Basis Swap Ratio Formula Strategic Implication
Market Price (MPS) Basis $$\text{Swap Ratio} = \frac{\text{MPS}_T}{\text{MPS}_A}$$ Standard market-negotiated ratio. If acquirer P/E is higher, EPS increases automatically (EPS bootstrapping).
Earnings (EPS) Basis $$\text{Swap Ratio} = \frac{\text{EPS}_T}{\text{EPS}_A}$$ Guarantees zero post-merger EPS dilution for both acquirer and target shareholders.
Book Value (BVPS) Basis $$\text{Swap Ratio} = \frac{\text{BVPS}_T}{\text{BVPS}_A}$$ Favored in regulatory/bank mergers (e.g. RBI bank amalgamation schemes). Reflects historical capital base.
Composite / Fair Ratio $$\frac{w_1 \text{SR}_{\text{MPS}} + w_2 \text{SR}_{\text{EPS}} + w_3 \text{SR}_{\text{NAV}}}{w_1 + w_2 + w_3}$$ Dual/Triple method recommended by Court-sanctioned schemes of arrangement to protect minority shareholders.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 2.1
Acquirer A has PAT of INR 200 lakhs, 20 lakh shares (EPS = INR 10), and market price of INR 150. Target B has PAT of INR 60 lakhs, 10 lakh shares (EPS = INR 6), and market price of INR 75. If shares are swapped on market price ratio with zero synergies, what is the post-merger EPS?
A. INR 10.40 (Accretive by INR 0.40)
B. INR 8.67 (Dilutive)
C. INR 9.80
D. INR 11.55
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
1. Swap Ratio $= \frac{75}{150} = 0.50$.
2. New shares issued $= 10\text{ lakh} \times 0.50 = 5\text{ lakh shares}$.
3. Total post-merger shares $= 20 + 5 = 25\text{ lakh shares}$.
4. Combined PAT $= 200 + 60 = \text{INR } 260\text{ lakhs}$.
5. Post-merger EPS $= \frac{260}{25} = \text{INR } 10.40$ (Bootstrap effect: higher P/E acquirer acquires lower P/E target).
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 2.2
Target T shareholders receive 0.40 shares of Acquirer A for each share held. Following the merger, the combined firm reports an EPS of INR 15.00. What is the Equivalent EPS earned by Target T's original shareholders?
A. INR 6.00
B. INR 15.00
C. INR 37.50
D. INR 7.50
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
$\text{Equivalent EPS} = \text{Post-merger EPS} \times \text{Exchange Ratio} = 15.00 \times 0.40 = \text{INR } 6.00$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 2.3
Acquirer A (EPS = INR 8.00) acquires Target B (EPS = INR 3.20). In the absence of synergies, what exchange ratio must be set so that Target B shareholders maintain their pre-merger earnings per share exactly?
A. 0.40
B. 2.50
C. 0.80
D. 0.50
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
$\text{Exchange Ratio to preserve EPS} = \frac{\text{EPS}_B}{\text{EPS}_A} = \frac{3.20}{8.00} = 0.40$

3. Hostile Takeover Defensive Weapons

Tactical Weapon Timing Operational Mechanism
Poison Pill Pre-Bid Target shareholders receive rights to purchase new shares at deep discounts once raider crosses threshold, massively diluting hostile bidder.
Shark Repellents Pre-Bid Staggered boards (re-electing only 1/3 directors annually) or supermajority voting clauses (80% approval required for mergers).
Golden Parachutes Pre-Bid Extravagant contractual termination severance paid to executives if fired, increasing acquirer cash drain.
White Knight Post-Bid Target invites a friendly third party to launch a higher counter-bid to defeat the hostile raider.
White Squire Post-Bid Friendly investor buys a large strategic block of voting shares pledged to vote with incumbent management.
Pac-Man Defense Post-Bid Target company turns around and launches a hostile tender offer to acquire the original predator firm itself!
Crown Jewel Defense Post-Bid Target sells its most valuable, strategic operating division to make the rest of the company unattractive to the raider.
Greenmail Post-Bid Target repurchases the hostile raider's stock at a hefty premium over market price in exchange for a standstill pledge.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 3.1
In hostile takeover defense mechanics, what is the core operational distinction between a "Flip-in" and a "Flip-over" poison pill rights plan?
A. A Flip-in allows target shareholders (excluding the hostile bidder) to purchase target shares at a steep discount; a Flip-over allows target shareholders to purchase the hostile bidder's shares at a discount after a merger
B. A Flip-in liquidates target assets while a Flip-over raises long-term debt
C. A Flip-in is an offensive tactic by the bidder while a Flip-over is a defensive tactic by the target
D. A Flip-over triggers immediately upon filing of a tender offer, while a Flip-in applies only to bondholders
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
• Flip-in: Triggers when an acquirer accumulates a threshold stake (e.g., 20%). Target shareholders (except the acquirer) are granted rights to buy target shares at a massive discount (e.g., 50%), massively diluting the acquirer.
• Flip-over: Triggers if the acquirer completes a subsequent merger. Target shareholders are entitled to purchase shares of the acquirer at a deep discount, diluting the predator's existing shareholders.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 3.2
Which hostile takeover defense strategy involves the target company countering an unsolicited takeover bid by launching a hostile tender offer to purchase voting control of the bidder itself?
A. Pac-Man Defense
B. Crown Jewel Defense
C. Greenmail
D. Golden Parachute
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
In the Pac-Man defense, the target firm reverses the acquisition direction by making a tender offer for the shares of the original hostile bidder, seeking to gain controlling majority before the bidder can complete its takeover.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 3.3
How does a "White Squire" defense fundamentally differ from a "White Knight" rescue?
A. A White Knight acquires complete ownership or majority voting control of the target, whereas a White Squire buys only a friendly minority blocking stake without taking full control
B. A White Squire liquidates target core divisions while a White Knight provides debt financing
C. A White Knight is an affiliate of the hostile bidder while a White Squire is an impartial arbitrator
D. A White Squire defense requires statutory approval from the competition commission while a White Knight does not
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
• White Knight: A friendly firm that rescues the target by acquiring full control on negotiated friendly terms.
• White Squire: A friendly investor that acquires a strategic minority block (e.g. 15%–25%) to prevent the hostile bidder from attaining voting control, allowing current management to retain operational independence.

4. Restructuring & Demerger Comparison Guide

Technique Cash to Parent? Shareholder Distribution Corporate Separation
Sell-off Yes (Cash proceeds) None (Retained by parent) Division absorbed by third party buyer.
Spin-off No 100% shares distributed pro-rata for free New listed independent entity with identical ownership.
Split-off No Parent shares surrendered in exchange for new shares Ownership bifurcates into separate groups.

📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 4.1
What is the essential operational distinction between a corporate "Spin-off" and a "Split-off"?
A. In a Spin-off, shares of the divested unit are distributed pro-rata to existing shareholders without surrendering parent shares; in a Split-off, shareholders must surrender parent shares in exchange for subsidiary shares
B. A Spin-off is an outright asset sale to third-party competitors while a Split-off is an internal dissolution
C. A Split-off generates an immediate cash dividend to all shareholders
D. A Spin-off requires the parent company to delist from stock exchanges
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
• Spin-off: Pro-rata dividend of new subsidiary shares to all existing parent shareholders. No shares of the parent are surrendered.
• Split-off: Shareholders are offered subsidiary shares only if they voluntarily surrender their parent company shares, altering the parent's share capital.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 4.2
Which corporate restructuring strategy involves floating a minority equity stake in a subsidiary to the public through an Initial Public Offering (IPO), establishing a public valuation and generating cash while retaining majority control?
A. Equity Carve-Out
B. Spin-off
C. Leveraged Buyout
D. Slump Sale
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
An Equity Carve-out sells a partial stake (e.g. 20%) of a wholly-owned subsidiary to public investors in an IPO. The parent unlocks value, raises external cash, and retains operational majority control.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 4.3
In a standard Leveraged Buyout (LBO), what constitutes the primary financial mechanism for acquiring the target enterprise?
A. Financed predominantly with high debt (70%–90%) secured against the target company's assets and serviced by its operating cash flows
B. Financed 100% by internal cash reserves of the financial sponsor
C. Financed via non-voting equity crowdfunding without bank borrowing
D. Financed by interest-free loans granted by the target company's management
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
An LBO relies heavily on debt (senior bank debt, mezzanine debt, and high-yield bonds). The sponsor commits a small equity ticket (10%–30%), and the target's operating cash flows and assets are used to collateralize and service the debt over the holding period.

5. Maximum Bid Price & Synergies Master Formulas

Core Concept: Determining the absolute upper price ceiling an acquirer can pay without destroying shareholder value.

Metric / Valuation Parameter Formula & Arithmetic Representation Strategic Interpretation
Maximum Cash Price ($P_{\max}$) $P_{\max} = V_T + \Delta V$ Standalone Target value ($V_T$) + Present Value of all Synergies ($\Delta V$).
Maximum Share Exchange Ratio ($ER_{\max}$) $ER_{\max} = \frac{V_T + \Delta V}{V_A} \times \frac{N_A}{N_T}$ At $ER_{\max}$, all synergies transfer to Target shareholders; Acquirer gains 0.
Minimum Share Exchange Ratio ($ER_{\min}$) $ER_{\min} = \frac{P_T}{P_A} = \frac{V_T}{V_A} \times \frac{N_A}{N_T}$ Target shareholders receive standalone value; Acquirer retains 100% of synergies.
Combined Firm Value ($V_{AB}$) $V_{AB} = V_A + V_T + \Delta V - \text{Cash Outflow}$ Total post-merger economic value of the consolidated entity.
Acquirer Gain ($\Delta W_A$) $\Delta W_A = \left[V_{AB} \times \frac{N_A}{N_A + N_{\text{new}}}\right] - V_A$ Value creation accruing to Acquirer's existing shareholders.
Target Gain ($\Delta W_T$) $\Delta W_T = \left[V_{AB} \times \frac{N_{\text{new}}}{N_A + N_{\text{new}}}\right] - V_T$ Total takeover premium and synergy share enjoyed by Target shareholders.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 5.1
Acquirer A evaluates Target T (10 lakh shares outstanding). Pre-merger market value of A is INR 80 crores and T is INR 20 crores. Present value of operational synergies is INR 10 crores. What is the maximum cash price per share Acquirer A can pay without diluting its own pre-merger shareholder wealth?
A. INR 300 per share
B. INR 200 per share
C. INR 250 per share
D. INR 350 per share
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
$\text{Maximum Consideration} = V_T + \text{Synergies} = 20\text{ cr} + 10\text{ cr} = \text{INR } 30\text{ crores}$ $\text{Maximum Price per share} = \frac{\text{INR } 30\text{ crores}}{10\text{ lakh shares}} = \text{INR } 300\text{ per share}$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 5.2
If Acquirer A (pre-merger value INR 80 crores) acquires Target T (pre-merger value INR 20 crores, synergies INR 10 crores) for a total cash payment of INR 26 crores, what is the net present value (NPV) of the merger accruing to Acquirer A's shareholders?
A. INR 4 crores
B. INR 10 crores
C. INR 6 crores
D. INR 14 crores
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
$\text{Synergy Gain} = \text{INR } 10\text{ crores}$ $\text{Premium to Target} = 26 - 20 = \text{INR } 6\text{ crores}$ $\text{NPV to Acquirer} = \text{Synergy Gain} - \text{Premium Paid} = 10 - 6 = \text{INR } 4\text{ crores}$

5. Exam Pitfalls & Traps

âš ï¸ Pitfall 1: Gross NPA Ratio Direction in Bank Mergers
In bank merger problems (like TYK PR-29), when calculating the swap ratio based on Gross NPA, invert the ratio ($\frac{\text{Strong}}{\text{Weak}}$) because a lower NPA represents superior asset quality! For CAR, MPS, and BVPS, use $\frac{\text{Weak}}{\text{Strong}}$.
âš ï¸ Pitfall 2: Demerger Shares Entitlement
In Demerger calculations (like TYK PR-22), ensure you calculate the ratio of net worth between the divisions first. The shares issued by the demerged company must match the proportion of net worth transferred.
âš ï¸ Pitfall 3: Total Post-Merger Earnings
Do not forget to add annual operational synergies to combined earnings when calculating post-merger EPS: $\text{Combined PAT} = \text{PAT}_A + \text{PAT}_T + \text{Synergy}$.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 6.1
Acquirer A (PAT = INR 100 cr, Shares = 10 cr, MPS = INR 120, P/E = 12x) acquires Target T (PAT = INR 30 cr, Shares = 5 cr, MPS = INR 48) by issuing 2 crore new shares. Post-merger synergies will yield additional PAT of INR 10 cr. What combined P/E multiple must the market award to maintain Acquirer A's market price of INR 120?
A. 10.29x
B. 12.00x
C. 14.40x
D. 9.50x
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
1. Post-merger shares $= 10 + 2 = 12\text{ crore shares}$.
2. Desired Market Cap $= 12 \text{ cr} \times 120 = \text{INR } 1,440\text{ crores}$.
3. Combined PAT $= 100 + 30 + 10 = \text{INR } 140\text{ crores}$.
4. Required Combined P/E $= \frac{1,440}{140} \approx 10.29\text{x}$.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Section 6.2
When an acquisition is financed by raising new 8% debt (post-tax cost 5.6%) versus issuing new equity at a P/E multiple of 20x (earnings yield 5%), which financing mode delivers higher post-merger EPS?
A. Equity share offer, because the earnings yield of 5.0% is lower than the after-tax debt cost of 5.6%
B. Debt financing, because debt interest is tax-deductible
C. Both modes produce identical EPS results
D. Post-merger EPS is unaffected by the financing method
Correct Answer: A
Detailed Explanation:
The cost of new equity equals its earnings yield: $\frac{1}{\text{P/E}} = \frac{1}{20} = 5.0\%$. Since after-tax debt costs $5.6\%$, issuing equity dilutes earnings by only $5.0\%$ per rupee raised compared to $5.6\%$ for debt, resulting in higher post-merger EPS.
