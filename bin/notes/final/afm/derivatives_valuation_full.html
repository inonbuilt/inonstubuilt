Topic 1

Introduction to Financial Derivatives & Forward Contracts

1. Content in ICAI Book

Definition of Derivatives: A derivative is a financial instrument whose value is derived from the value of an underlying asset, index, or reference rate. The underlying asset can be equity shares, stock indices, bonds, interest rates, foreign currencies, commodities (agricultural, metals, energy), or credit events.

Core Economic Functions of Derivatives:

  1. Risk Transfer and Hedging: Enables risk-averse economic agents to transfer unwanted market price risks (price volatility, interest rate shifts, currency fluctuations) to entities willing to bear them (speculators or risk-tolerant investors).
  2. Price Discovery: Derivatives markets aggregate forward-looking information and collective expectations about future supply, demand, and economic variables, providing valuable forward price signals to the spot market.
  3. Market Liquidity and Operational Efficiency: Derivatives require lower capital outlays (margin trading), offering superior leverage, lower transaction costs, and higher liquidity than spot markets.

Forward Contracts: A forward contract is a customized, bilateral contract entered into between two private parties to buy or sell an underlying asset at a specified future date at a price agreed upon today (the forward price). Forward contracts are Over-The-Counter (OTC) unstandardized agreements.

Key Features of Forward Contracts:

  • Bilateral Negotiation: Contract size, expiration date, quality grade, and delivery venue are completely customized to meet the counterparty's specific hedging needs.
  • Counterparty / Credit Risk: There is no central clearinghouse or guarantee fund. If either party defaults at maturity, the non-defaulting party bears the entire credit loss.
  • Settlement at Maturity: Cash flows occur strictly at maturity (settlement date). There is no interim daily cash settlement or marking-to-market.
  • Lack of Liquidity & Secondary Market: Because each contract is customized and non-standardized, reversing or closing out a forward position prior to maturity requires counterparty consent or entering an offsetting contract.
2. Clear Explanation & Mathematical Framework

In a forward contract, the party agreeing to buy the asset takes the Long Forward position, and the party agreeing to sell takes the Short Forward position.

Let $S_T$ be the spot price of the underlying asset at maturity $T$, and $F_0$ be the agreed delivery forward price:

  • Long Forward Payoff at Maturity: $$\text{Payoff}_{\text{Long}} = S_T - F_0$$ (Profit if $S_T > F_0$, loss if $S_T < F_0$)
  • Short Forward Payoff at Maturity: $$\text{Payoff}_{\text{Short}} = F_0 - S_T$$ (Profit if $S_T < F_0$, loss if $S_T> F_0$)
Zero Initial Value

At inception ($t=0$), the forward price $F_0$ is set such that the present value of the contract is exactly zero. Neither party pays any upfront cash premium to enter into a forward contract.

3. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • OTC vs. Exchange: Always state that forward contracts are OTC agreements carrying bilateral default risk, whereas futures trade on recognized exchanges with Novation by the Clearing Corporation.
  • No Intermediate Cash Flows: Forwards involve single settlement at expiration. Never calculate daily MTM for forward contracts.

Futures Contracts & The 10-Point Comparison Matrix

1. Content in ICAI Book

Futures Contract: A futures contract is a legally binding, standardized agreement traded on an organized exchange to buy or sell a standardized quantity and quality of a specific underlying asset at a specified future date at a market-determined price.

Novation & Clearing Corporation: The Clearing Corporation (e.g., NSCCL / NSE Clearing Ltd.) acts as the legal counterparty to every transaction through the legal doctrine of Novation—becoming the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer, virtually eliminating counterparty credit risk.

No. Dimension Forward Contract Futures Contract
1 Trading Arena Traded Over-The-Counter (OTC) on personal basis, telephone, or dealer networks. Traded on organized, competitive, electronic order-driven exchanges.
2 Standardization Completely customized contract terms (size, expiration, delivery specifications). Highly standardized contract terms (fixed lot sizes, standardized expiry dates).
3 Counterparty Risk High counterparty credit risk; borne entirely by contracting parties. Negligible counterparty risk due to Clearinghouse Novation & Guarantee Fund.
4 Margins No initial or maintenance margin normally required. Mandatory initial margins, exposure margins, and daily maintenance margins.
5 Marking-to-Market (MTM) No marking to market. Single cash settlement at contract expiration. Subject to daily MTM; daily profits/losses credited/debited to margin account.
6 Liquidity & Exit Illiquid; difficult to unwind without bilateral counterparty consent. Extremely liquid; positions can be closed out at any time with offsetting trades.
7 Delivery vs. Settlement Usually results in actual physical delivery of the underlying asset. Over 98% settled in cash or closed out before expiry; delivery is rare or standardized.
8 Pricing Transparency Private, opaque pricing quotes known only to the two transacting parties. Continuous, public real-time price dissemination on screen-based trading systems.
9 Transaction Costs Embedded in wide bid-ask spreads quoted by OTC dealer/bank. Low, competitive brokerage commissions and exchange transaction fees.
10 Regulation Self-regulated or lightly regulated by contract law and banking authorities. Strictly regulated by statutory market regulators (SEBI, CFTC).
2. Clear Explanation of Margin Mechanics

Futures trading operates on a strict three-tier margin architecture to guarantee solvency:

  1. Initial Margin: The upfront collateral required to initiate a position, determined statistically using SPAN (Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk) or $\mu + 3\sigma$ (value at risk covering 99% confidence level of daily price movements).
  2. Maintenance Margin: The minimum threshold balance that must be maintained in the margin account at all times (typically 75% to 80% of initial margin).
  3. Marking-to-Market (MTM): At the close of each trading day, open contracts are revalued at the official daily settlement price. Daily gains are credited to the margin account; daily losses are debited.
  4. Variation Margin / Margin Call: If the margin account drops below the maintenance margin level, a Margin Call is triggered. The trader must deposit cash to restore the account back to the Initial Margin level (not just to the maintenance margin level).
3. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Theoretical Question 2: Short note on Marking to market is a repeated 4-mark question. Always include: daily settlement price, debit/credit to margin account, margin call, and restoration to initial margin.
  • Margin Call Top-up: In practical problems (e.g. Q14), when cash falls below maintenance margin, the cash brought in = $\text{Initial Margin} - \text{Current Balance}$.

Pricing & Valuation: Cost of Carry Model

1. Content in ICAI Book

The Cost of Carry Model: In an efficient and frictionless market, the forward/futures price ($F_0$) must equal the spot price ($S_0$) plus the net cost of carrying the asset until maturity $T$. If this parity condition does not hold, riskless arbitrage profits will exist, which market forces will immediately eliminate.

Core Mathematical Formulations:

Scenario Discrete Compounding Formula Continuous Compounding Formula
1. Asset with No Income & No Storage Cost $$F_0 = S_0 (1 + r \cdot t) \quad \text{or} \quad S_0 (1+r)^t$$ $$F_0 = S_0 e^{rt}$$
2. Asset with Known Cash Dividend / Income ($D$) $$F_0 = (S_0 - \text{PV}(D))(1 + r)^t \quad \text{or} \quad S_0(1+rt) - D$$ $$F_0 = (S_0 - D e^{-rt_d}) e^{rt}$$
3. Asset with Known Dividend Yield ($y$) $$F_0 = S_0 [1 + (r - y)t]$$ $$F_0 = S_0 e^{(r - y)t}$$
4. Commodity with Storage Costs ($U$) & Convenience Yield ($C$) $$F_0 = S_0 (1 + r)^t + \text{FV}(\text{Storage}) - \text{FV}(\text{Convenience})$$ $$F_0 = S_0 e^{(r + u - c)t}$$
2. Arbitrage Mechanics & Mispricing Identification

When the actual market futures price ($F_{\text{actual}}$) deviates from the theoretical fair futures price ($F_{\text{theoretical}}$), two riskless arbitrage strategies emerge:

Condition Cash-and-Carry Arbitrage (Actual Future Overpriced) Reverse Cash-and-Carry Arbitrage (Actual Future Underpriced)
Market Trigger $$F_{\text{actual}} > F_{\text{theoretical}}$$ $$F_{\text{actual}} < F_{\text{theoretical}}$$
Step 1: Action Today ($t=0$) 1. Borrow cash at risk-free rate $r$.
2. Buy underlying spot share at $S_0$.
3. Sell (short) futures contract at $F_{\text{actual}}$.		 1. Short sell the underlying share at $S_0$.
2. Invest the sale proceeds at risk-free rate $r$.
3. Buy (long) futures contract at $F_{\text{actual}}$.
Step 2: Action at Maturity ($t=T$) 1. Deliver the physical share into the futures contract to receive $F_{\text{actual}}$.
2. Collect any interim dividends.
3. Repay the borrowed principal plus interest: $S_0(1+r)^T$.		 1. Receive proceeds from risk-free investment: $S_0(1+r)^T$.
2. Pay $F_{\text{actual}}$ to take delivery of share via futures.
3. Return the delivered share to close the short position.
Net Riskless Arbitrage Profit $$\text{Profit} = F_{\text{actual}} - F_{\text{theoretical}} > 0$$ $$\text{Profit} = F_{\text{theoretical}} - F_{\text{actual}} > 0$$

Topic 4

Stock Index Futures & Portfolio Beta Management

1. Content in ICAI Book

Stock Index Futures: Contracts where the underlying asset is a stock market index (e.g., NIFTY 50, BSE SENSEX). Because a stock index cannot be physically delivered, index futures are settled exclusively in cash on the settlement date.

Contract Value of Index Futures:

$$\text{Futures Contract Value } (V_f) = \text{Futures Price} \times \text{Multiplier (Lot Size)}$$

Portfolio Beta Management: Fund managers use stock index futures to modify the systematic risk (Beta $\beta$) of an existing equity portfolio without the costly liquidation and re-acquisition of physical shares.

Formulas for Portfolio Hedging:

Objective Formula Trading Action
1. Complete Hedge ($\beta^* = 0$) $$N = \frac{V_p \cdot \beta_p}{V_f}$$ Sell (Short) $N$ index futures contracts
2. Target Beta Reduction ($\beta^* < \beta_p$) $$N = \frac{V_p \cdot (\beta_p - \beta^*)}{V_f}$$ Sell (Short) $N$ index futures contracts
3. Target Beta Increase ($\beta^* > \beta_p$) $$N = \frac{V_p \cdot (\beta^* - \beta_p)}{V_f}$$ Buy (Long) $N$ index futures contracts

Where:

  • $V_p$ = Total current market value of the equity portfolio.
  • $\beta_p$ = Weighted average Beta of the equity portfolio.
  • $\beta^*$ = Target Beta desired by the fund manager.
  • $V_f$ = Current value of one index futures contract ($\text{Index Futures Price} \times \text{Lot Size}$).
  • $N$ = Number of index futures contracts to be traded.
2. Portfolio Beta Calculation

The portfolio beta ($\beta_p$) is the weighted average of individual asset betas:

$$\beta_p = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \cdot \beta_i = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{\text{Value of Stock } i}{V_p} \cdot \beta_i$$
Cash Inclusion in Portfolio Beta

When a portfolio holds both equity shares and risk-free cash (e.g., Q8), cash has a Beta of zero ($\beta_{\text{cash}} = 0$). The total portfolio value is $V_p = \text{Equity Value} + \text{Cash}$, and the effective portfolio beta is:

$$\beta_{\text{effective}} = \beta_{\text{equity}} \times \frac{\text{Equity Value}}{V_p} + 0 \times \frac{\text{Cash}}{V_p}$$
3. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Contract Lot Size: Always check whether the index contract specifies a multiplier (e.g., Sensex multiplier = 50, Nifty lot size = 25 or 50). Value of one contract $V_f = \text{Index Price} \times \text{Lot Size}$.
  • Round Off Rule: When calculating $N$, state the exact decimal value first, and then round to the nearest whole integer of contracts.
  • Verification of Gain/Loss: Practical questions (Q11, Q13) often require you to prove that the portfolio value changes as expected when the index moves by $\pm 10\%$. Follow the two components: Value of Stock Portfolio under CAPM ($R_p = R_f + \beta(R_m - R_f)$ or $\Delta V = V_p \times \beta_p \times \%\Delta\text{Index}$) plus Gain/Loss on Futures ($N \times \Delta\text{Futures Price} \times \text{Lot Size}$).

Options Contracts: Foundations, Payoff Profiles & Strategies

1. Content in ICAI Book

Option Contract: An option is a contract that gives the buyer (holder) the right, but not the obligation, to buy (Call Option) or sell (Put Option) a specified quantity of an underlying asset at a specified price (Strike or Exercise Price, $X$) on or before a specified expiration date ($T$). The seller (writer) is obligated to perform if the buyer exercises.

Moneyness of Options:

Condition Call Option ($S_T$ vs $X$) Put Option ($S_T$ vs $X$) Intrinsic Value
In-The-Money (ITM) $S_T > X$ (Profitable to exercise) $S_T < X$ (Profitable to exercise) $\text{Intrinsic Value} > 0$
At-The-Money (ATM) $S_T = X$ $S_T = X$ $\text{Intrinsic Value} = 0$
Out-of-The-Money (OTM) $S_T < X$ (Do not exercise) $S_T > X$ (Do not exercise) $\text{Intrinsic Value} = 0$

Total Option Premium: $\text{Option Premium} = \text{Intrinsic Value} + \text{Time Value}$. Intrinsic value can never be negative: $\text{IV}_{\text{Call}} = \max(0, S_0 - X)$; $\text{IV}_{\text{Put}} = \max(0, X - S_0)$.

2. Payoff Profiles & Advanced Option Strategies

Let $c$ be Call premium and $p$ be Put premium:

Position Gross Payoff at Expiry Net Profit / (Loss) Max Loss Max Profit
Long Call $\max(0, S_T - X)$ $\max(0, S_T - X) - c$ Limited to $c$ Unlimited ($S_T - X - c$)
Short Call $-\max(0, S_T - X)$ $c - \max(0, S_T - X)$ Unlimited Limited to $c$
Long Put $\max(0, X - S_T)$ $\max(0, X - S_T) - p$ Limited to $p$ $X - p$ (if $S_T \to 0$)
Short Put $-\max(0, X - S_T)$ $p - \max(0, X - S_T)$ $X - p$ Limited to $p$
Long Straddle $\max(0, S_T - X) + \max(0, X - S_T)$ $\text{Gross Payoff} - (c + p)$ $c + p$ (at $S_T = X$) Unlimited on upside/downside
Collar Strategy Long Stock + Long OTM Put ($X_1$) + Short OTM Call ($X_2$) Caps downside at $X_1$ and caps upside at $X_2$ $S_0 - X_1 + \text{Net Premium}$ $X_2 - S_0 - \text{Net Premium}$
3. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Net Payoff Table Format: For strategy questions (Straddle Q16, Collar Q19, Combination Q20), always draw a comprehensive column table with rows for each test market price $S_T$: Columns: Market Price ($S_T$), Action on Call, Call Payoff, Action on Put, Put Payoff, Total Gross Payoff, Total Premium Paid, Net Profit/(Loss).
  • Break-Even Points: For a Straddle, there are two break-even points: $\text{Lower BEP} = X - (c+p)$ and $\text{Upper BEP} = X + (c+p)$.

Option Valuation: Binomial Model & Risk-Neutral Pricing

1. Content in ICAI Book

The Binomial Model (Cox-Ross-Rubinstein): Assumes that over each discrete time step, the stock price $S$ can move to only one of two possible values: an up price $S_u = S \cdot u$ or a down price $S_d = S \cdot d$ (where $u > 1$ and $d < 1$).

Two Complementary Approaches to Valuation:

Method 1: Delta-Hedging / Replicating Portfolio Method 2: Risk-Neutral Valuation

Hedge Ratio (Delta $\Delta$):

$$\Delta = \frac{C_u - C_d}{S_u - S_d} = \frac{\Delta C}{\Delta S}$$

A portfolio consisting of Long $\Delta$ shares and Short 1 Call option is perfectly riskless:

$$\text{Portfolio Value Up} = \Delta \cdot S_u - C_u$$ $$\text{Portfolio Value Down} = \Delta \cdot S_d - C_d$$

Because returns are certain, this portfolio must earn the risk-free rate $r$:

$$\text{PV of Portfolio} = \frac{\Delta \cdot S_u - C_u}{1 + r \cdot t}$$

Value of Call Option:

$$C_0 = \Delta \cdot S_0 - \text{PV of Portfolio}$$

Risk-Neutral Probability of Up-move ($p$):

$$p = \frac{(1 + r) - d}{u - d} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{R - d}{u - d}$$

Where $R = 1 + r \cdot t$ (or $e^{rt}$ for continuous compounding). The probability of down-move is $1 - p = \frac{u - R}{u - d}$.

Expected Payoff & Discounting:

$$\text{Expected Payoff} = p \cdot C_u + (1 - p) \cdot C_d$$

Value of Call Option:

$$C_0 = \frac{p \cdot C_u + (1 - p) \cdot C_d}{1 + r \cdot t} \quad \text{or} \quad e^{-rt}[p C_u + (1-p)C_d]$$
2. Two-Step Binomial Trees

In a 2-period binomial tree (e.g. Illustration 1 & Q23), work backward from expiration ($t=2$) to $t=1$ and finally to $t=0$:

  1. Calculate terminal payoffs at $t=2$: $C_{uu} = \max(0, S_{uu}-X)$, $C_{ud} = \max(0, S_{ud}-X)$, $C_{dd} = \max(0, S_{dd}-X)$.
  2. Calculate option values at node $u$ and node $d$ at $t=1$: $$C_u = \frac{p \cdot C_{uu} + (1-p) \cdot C_{ud}}{1+r}, \quad C_d = \frac{p \cdot C_{ud} + (1-p) \cdot C_{dd}}{1+r}$$
  3. Calculate option value at initial node ($t=0$): $$C_0 = \frac{p \cdot C_u + (1-p) \cdot C_d}{1+r} = \frac{p^2 C_{uu} + 2p(1-p)C_{ud} + (1-p)^2 C_{dd}}{(1+r)^2}$$
3. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Equal Results: Replicating portfolio method and Risk-Neutral method always give identical results. ICAI frequently asks to compute value using both methods or specifically specifies "Risk Neutral Method" (e.g. Illustration 2, Q21, Q24).
  • Hedge Ratio Interpretation: Hedge ratio $\Delta$ tells how many shares to purchase for each call option sold to establish a risk-free position.
Topic 7

Black-Scholes-Merton Model & Put-Call Parity

1. Content in ICAI Book

Black-Scholes-Merton Formula for European Call Option:

$$C_0 = S_0 \cdot N(d_1) - X \cdot e^{-rt} \cdot N(d_2)$$

Where:

$$d_1 = \frac{\ln(S_0 / X) + \left(r + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)t}{\sigma \sqrt{t}}$$ $$d_2 = d_1 - \sigma \sqrt{t}$$
  • $S_0$ = Current spot price of the stock.
  • $X$ = Strike (exercise) price of the option.
  • $r$ = Annualized continuously compounded risk-free interest rate.
  • $t$ = Time to expiration expressed in years ($t = \text{months} / 12$).
  • $\sigma$ = Annualized standard deviation of continuously compounded stock returns (volatility).
  • $\ln$ = Natural logarithm.
  • $N(d)$ = Cumulative probability distribution function for a standardized normal distribution variable.

Assumptions of the Black-Scholes Model (Theoretical Q3):

  1. European Style: Options can be exercised only at expiration date.
  2. No Transaction Costs or Taxes: Markets are frictionless; securities are perfectly divisible.
  3. Constant Risk-Free Rate: The risk-free rate $r$ is known and constant over the option life.
  4. Lognormal Distribution: Stock prices follow a geometric Brownian motion; returns are normally distributed with constant volatility $\sigma$.
  5. No Arbitrage Opportunities: Financial markets are efficient with continuous trading.
  6. No Dividends: The underlying stock pays no dividend during the life of the option (unless adjusted).

Dividend Adjustment in Black-Scholes: If a known dividend $D$ is paid during the option life at time $t_d$, deduct its present value from spot price:

$$S_0^* = S_0 - D \cdot e^{-rt_d}$$

Use $S_0^*$ in place of $S_0$ in the formula.

2. Put-Call Parity Theory

For European options on non-dividend paying stocks with identical strike $X$ and maturity $t$, Put-Call Parity establishes an inviolable equilibrium:

$$C_0 + X \cdot e^{-rt} = P_0 + S_0$$

Or expressed in terms of Put Option value:

$$P_0 = C_0 + X \cdot e^{-rt} - S_0$$
Portfolio Replications

Fiduciary Call: Long Call + Risk-free Zero Coupon Bond maturing at $X$ ($C_0 + X e^{-rt}$).

Protective Put: Long Put + Long Underlying Share ($P_0 + S_0$).

Both portfolios have identical payoffs of $\max(S_T, X)$ at expiration, hence their current costs must be identical.

3. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Normal Distribution Interpolation: When looking up $N(d_1)$ and $N(d_2)$, if $d$ has three decimal places (e.g. $d_1 = 0.503$), use linear interpolation between $N(0.50)$ and $N(0.51)$.
  • Negative $d$: Remember symmetry: $N(-d) = 1 - N(d)$.
  • Solving for Put: Never re-run the full Black-Scholes equation for Put options; always value Call first and then use Put-Call Parity: $P = C + X e^{-rt} - S_0$.

Topic 9

Credit Derivatives: CDOs & Credit Default Swaps (CDS)

1. Content in ICAI Book

Collateralized Debt Obligations (CDOs): A structured financial instrument backed by a diversified pool of debt obligations (bonds, commercial loans, mortgage-backed securities). Cash flows from the underlying debt pool are redistributed across different prioritized tranches:

  • Senior Tranche (AAA): First claim on cash flows, lowest yield, protected from initial defaults.
  • Mezzanine Tranche (AA to BBB): Intermediate risk and yield; absorbs losses after the equity tranche is wiped out.
  • Equity / Subordinated Tranche (Unrated / First Loss): Receives residual cash flows, highest yield, absorbs the first default losses.

Types of CDOs:

  1. Balance Sheet CDOs: Originating banks transfer existing loans off their balance sheet to reduce regulatory capital and free up credit capacity.
  2. Arbitrage CDOs: Created by asset managers to exploit yield spread differentials between higher-yielding collateral assets and lower-cost CDO debt tranches.

Credit Default Swaps (CDS): A bilateral OTC contract wherein the Protection Buyer pays a periodic fee (the CDS spread) to the Protection Seller in exchange for a contingent payoff if a specified Reference Entity experiences a defined Credit Event (bankruptcy, failure to pay, debt restructuring).

Settlement Mechanisms for CDS:

  • Physical Settlement: Buyer delivers the defaulted bond/debt instrument to the seller and receives the full par value in cash.
  • Cash Settlement: Seller pays the buyer the difference between par value and post-default market recovery value ($\text{Payoff} = \text{Par Value} - \text{Recovery Value}$).
2. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • CDS vs. Insurance: In CDS, the protection buyer does not need to own the underlying bond (Naked CDS), unlike insurance where insurable interest is strictly mandatory.

Real Options in Capital Budgeting

1. Content in ICAI Book

Real Option: Managerial flexibility to alter capital investment decisions (expand, contract, abandon, defer) in response to uncertain future economic developments. Traditional DCF / NPV assumes passive management; Real Options Valuation recognizes the strategic value of managerial flexibility:

$$\text{Strategic NPV} = \text{Static NPV} + \text{Value of Real Option}$$

Mapping Financial Options to Real Options:

Financial Call Option Variable Real Capital Investment Equivalent
Current Stock Price ($S_0$) Present Value of expected future project cash inflows ($PV$).
Strike / Exercise Price ($X$) Capital investment outlay required to exercise the option ($I$).
Time to Expiration ($t$) Time window during which the strategic decision can be deferred.
Variance / Volatility ($\sigma^2$) Uncertainty in project cash flows.
Risk-Free Interest Rate ($r$) Risk-free rate over the decision horizon.
Dividend Yield ($y$) Cash flow drain or competitive decay loss while waiting.

Major Types of Real Options (Illustrations 4, 5, 6):

  1. Growth / Expansion Option (Call Option): Opportunity to make follow-on investments in newer markets or technologies if initial pilot project succeeds (Illustration 4: Aidrex Patent).
  2. Abandonment Option (Put Option): Flexibility to terminate a project early and realize its salvage or resale value if market conditions turn adverse (Illustration 5: Fertilizer Plant).
  3. Timing / Deferral Option (Call Option): Flexibility to wait and resolve market uncertainty before committing substantial irreversible capital (Illustration 6: Solar Plant).
2. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Option Values Cannot Be Negative: If a project includes an abandonment put option, Strategic NPV is strictly higher than Static NPV.
  • Illustration 4 Formula: When evaluating a patent with Black-Scholes, remember that the patent loses value over time due to patent expiry, acting like a continuous dividend yield ($y = 1/\text{Patent Life}$).
Topic 11

Commodity Derivatives, Swaps, Weather & Electricity

1. Content in ICAI Book

Commodity Futures Pricing: Unlike financial assets, physical commodities entail storage costs ($u$), insurance, deterioration, and provide a Convenience Yield ($c$)—the benefit of holding physical inventory to meet unexpected manufacturing bottlenecks or maintain customer loyalty:

$$F_0 = S_0 \cdot e^{(r + u - c)t}$$

Commodity Swaps: Agreements where one counterparty pays a fixed commodity price per unit and receives the floating market spot price (or vice versa) on predetermined settlement dates, insulating producers and industrial consumers from commodity price spikes.

Weather Derivatives: Non-catastrophic temperature and precipitation hedging instruments based on Heating Degree Days (HDD) and Cooling Degree Days (CDD):

$$\text{HDD} = \max(0, 18^\circ\text{C} - \text{Daily Mean Temp}); \quad \text{CDD} = \max(0, \text{Daily Mean Temp} - 18^\circ\text{C})$$

Electricity Derivatives: Electricity cannot be economically stored at scale; forward contracts and swaps settle against hourly grid clearing prices.

2. Exam Relevance & Scoring Focus

🎯 ICAI Exam Traps & Key Pointers

  • Convenience Yield Impact: High convenience yield ($c > r + u$) creates Backwardation ($F_0 < S_0$), whereas low convenience yield creates Contango ($F_0 > S_0$).
Topic 12

Derivative Mishaps & Governance Lessons

1. Content in ICAI Book

The ICAI Study Material details four famous corporate disasters resulting from derivatives mismanagement:

  1. Barings Bank Collapse (1995 - Nick Leeson): Singapore rogue trader Nick Leeson took massive unauthorized long positions on Nikkei 225 futures and short straddles. Following the Kobe earthquake, Nikkei plunged, causing losses exceeding \$1.4 Billion and bankrupting the 233-year-old bank. Core Failure: Lack of segregation of front-office trading and back-office settlement controls.
  2. Orange County Bankruptcy (1994 - Robert Citron): County treasurer used reverse repurchase agreements to leverage a \$7.5 Billion portfolio into \$20 Billion of interest rate inverse floaters, betting rates would fall. When the Federal Reserve hiked rates 6 times in 1994, the fund lost \$1.6 Billion and declared bankruptcy. Core Failure: Excessive leverage and speculative mismatch on public treasury funds.
  3. Procter & Gamble (P&G) vs. Bankers Trust (1994): P&G entered into complex leveraged interest rate swaps containing embedded CMT (Constant Maturity Treasury) swaptions, incurring \$157 Million in losses when interest rates rose. Core Failure: Entering into highly complex, leveraged OTC derivative structures without fully understanding their underlying risk profiles.
  4. Gibson Greetings (1994): Greeting card manufacturer lost \$20 Million on interest rate ratio swaps exceeding its total annual operating profits.

Key Lessons from Derivative Disasters:

  • Never trade any derivative product whose payoff structure, pricing model, and risk sensitivities are not thoroughly understood.
  • Strictly separate Front Office (trading execution), Middle Office (risk management and compliance), and Back Office (accounting, confirmation, and settlement).
  • Institute rigid stop-loss limits, Value-at-Risk (VAR) ceilings, and maximum exposure limits.
  • Do not treat corporate treasury as an aggressive speculative profit centre.

Two-Step Binomial Tree Valuation (CAB Ltd)

Problem Statement (ICAI Study Material)

Following is a two sub-periods tree of 6-months each for the share of CAB Ltd.:

Now ($t=0$) End of Period 1 ($t = 6\text{ months}$) End of Period 2 ($t = 12\text{ months}$)
$S_0 = \text{₹ } 36$ $S_u = \text{₹ } 43.20$ $S_{uu} = \text{₹ } 51.84$
$S_{ud} = \text{₹ } 34.56$
$S_d = \text{₹ } 28.80$ $S_{dd} = \text{₹ } 23.04$

Risk-free rate of return is 5% for every 6 months ($R = 1.05$). The strike price of a 1-year European call option is ₹ 36.

Required: Calculate the value of the 1-year European Call Option today using the Binomial Model.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Calculate Payoffs at Expiration ($t=2$ / 12 months)

Strike Price $X = \text{₹ } 36$. Terminal call payoffs $C = \max(0, S_T - X)$:

Terminal Node Stock Price ($S_2$) Call Payoff Formula Terminal Call Value ($C_2$)
Node $uu$ (Up-Up) ₹ 51.84 $\max(0, 51.84 - 36.00)$ ₹ 15.84
Node $ud$ (Up-Down / Down-Up) ₹ 34.56 $\max(0, 34.56 - 36.00)$ ₹ 0.00
Node $dd$ (Down-Down) ₹ 23.04 $\max(0, 23.04 - 36.00)$ ₹ 0.00

Step 2: Calculate Risk-Neutral Probability ($p$)

Over each 6-month period, the up-factor $u = \frac{43.20}{36.00} = 1.20$, and down-factor $d = \frac{28.80}{36.00} = 0.80$.

Risk-free factor $R = 1 + r = 1 + 0.05 = 1.05$.

$$p = \frac{R - d}{u - d} = \frac{1.05 - 0.80}{1.20 - 0.80} = \frac{0.25}{0.40} = 0.625$$ $$1 - p = 1 - 0.625 = 0.375$$

Step 3: Calculate Option Values at Intermediate Nodes ($t=1$ / 6 months)

  • At Upper Node $u$ ($S_u = \text{₹ } 43.20$): $$C_u = \frac{p \cdot C_{uu} + (1-p) \cdot C_{ud}}{R} = \frac{0.625 \times 15.84 + 0.375 \times 0}{1.05} = \frac{9.90}{1.05} = \text{₹ } 9.4286$$
  • At Lower Node $d$ ($S_d = \text{₹ } 28.80$): $$C_d = \frac{p \cdot C_{ud} + (1-p) \cdot C_{dd}}{R} = \frac{0.625 \times 0 + 0.375 \times 0}{1.05} = \text{₹ } 0.00$$

Step 4: Calculate Option Value Today ($t=0$)

$$C_0 = \frac{p \cdot C_u + (1-p) \cdot C_d}{R} = \frac{0.625 \times 9.4286 + 0.375 \times 0}{1.05} = \frac{5.8929}{1.05} = \text{₹ } 5.612 \approx \text{₹ } 5.61$$
Alternative Direct Valuation (Replicating Portfolio at Node 0)

Alternatively, discounting directly across two periods:

$$C_0 = \frac{p^2 \cdot C_{uu} + 2p(1-p) \cdot C_{ud} + (1-p)^2 \cdot C_{dd}}{(1+r)^2} = \frac{(0.625)^2 \times 15.84 + 0 + 0}{(1.05)^2} = \frac{0.390625 \times 15.84}{1.1025} = \frac{6.1875}{1.1025} = \text{₹ } 5.61$$
Final Conclusion: The theoretical fair value of the 1-year European Call Option on CAB Ltd today is ₹ 5.61.

Risk-Neutral Valuation Method (Penchant Ltd)

Problem Statement (ICAI Study Material)

The current market price of an equity share of Penchant Ltd is ₹ 420. Within a period of 3 months, the maximum and minimum price of it is expected to be ₹ 500 and ₹ 400 respectively. If the risk-free rate of interest is 8% p.a., what should be the value of a 3-month Call option with a strike price of ₹ 420 under the Risk Neutral Method?

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify Parameters & Up/Down Factors

  • Current Stock Price ($S_0$) = ₹ 420
  • Upper Stock Price ($S_u$) = ₹ 500 $\Rightarrow u = \frac{500}{420} = 1.190476$
  • Lower Stock Price ($S_d$) = ₹ 400 $\Rightarrow d = \frac{400}{420} = 0.952381$
  • Strike Price ($X$) = ₹ 420
  • Time to expiration ($t$) = 3 months = $3/12 = 0.25$ years
  • Risk-free rate ($r$) = 8% p.a. $\Rightarrow R = 1 + r \cdot t = 1 + 0.08 \times 0.25 = 1.02$

Step 2: Terminal Call Option Payoffs

  • If stock reaches ₹ 500: $C_u = \max(0, 500 - 420) = \text{₹ } 80$
  • If stock drops to ₹ 400: $C_d = \max(0, 400 - 420) = \text{₹ } 0$

Step 3: Solve for Risk-Neutral Probability ($p$)

Under risk neutrality, the expected return on the stock must equal the risk-free rate:

$$S_0 \times (1 + r \cdot t) = p \cdot S_u + (1 - p) \cdot S_d$$ $$420 \times 1.02 = 500 \cdot p + 400 \cdot (1 - p)$$ $$428.40 = 400 + 100 \cdot p$$ $$100 \cdot p = 428.40 - 400 = 28.40 \implies p = \frac{28.40}{100} = 0.284$$ $$1 - p = 1 - 0.284 = 0.716$$

Step 4: Compute Present Value of Expected Option Payoff

$$\text{Expected Option Payoff at Maturity} = p \cdot C_u + (1 - p) \cdot C_d = (0.284 \times 80) + (0.716 \times 0) = \text{₹ } 22.72$$ $$C_0 = \frac{\text{Expected Payoff}}{1 + r \cdot t} = \frac{22.72}{1.02} = \text{₹ } 22.2745 \approx \mathbf{\text{₹ } 22.27}$$
Final Answer: Under the Risk-Neutral Method, the fair value of the 3-month Call Option is ₹ 22.27.

Black-Scholes-Merton Valuation & Put-Call Parity (TIC Ltd)

Problem Statement (ICAI Study Material)

(i) The shares of TIC Ltd. are currently priced at ₹ 415 and call option exercisable in three months’ time has an exercise rate of ₹ 400. Risk-free interest rate is 5% p.a. and standard deviation (volatility) of share price is 22%. Based on the assumption that TIC Ltd. is not going to declare any dividend over the next three months, is the option worth buying for ₹ 25?

(ii) Calculate value of aforesaid call option based on Black Scholes valuation model if the current price is considered as ₹ 380.

(iii) What would be the worth of put option if current price is considered ₹ 380?

(iv) If TIC Ltd. share price at present is taken as ₹ 408 and a dividend of ₹ 10 is expected to be paid in two months’ time, then calculate value of the call option.

Given Mathematical Values

$\ln(1.0375) = 0.03681; \quad \ln(0.95) = -0.05129; \quad \ln(0.9952) = -0.00481$

$e^{0.0125} = 1.012578; \quad e^{0.008333} = 1.0084$

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

Part (i): Valuation when Spot Price $S_0 = \text{₹ } 415$

Given: $S_0 = 415, X = 400, r = 0.05, t = 3/12 = 0.25, \sigma = 0.22$.

$$\sigma \sqrt{t} = 0.22 \times \sqrt{0.25} = 0.22 \times 0.5 = 0.11$$ $$\frac{S_0}{X} = \frac{415}{400} = 1.0375 \implies \ln(1.0375) = 0.03681$$ $$\left(r + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right) t = \left(0.05 + \frac{0.22^2}{2}\right) \times 0.25 = (0.05 + 0.0242) \times 0.25 = 0.0742 \times 0.25 = 0.01855$$ $$d_1 = \frac{\ln(S_0 / X) + \left(r + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)t}{\sigma \sqrt{t}} = \frac{0.03681 + 0.01855}{0.11} = \frac{0.05536}{0.11} = 0.50327$$ $$d_2 = d_1 - \sigma \sqrt{t} = 0.50327 - 0.11 = 0.39327$$

From Normal Distribution Table:

$$N(d_1) = N(0.50327) \approx 0.6928$$ $$N(d_2) = N(0.39327) \approx 0.6529$$ $$C_0 = S_0 \cdot N(d_1) - \frac{X}{e^{rt}} \cdot N(d_2) = 415 \times 0.6928 - \frac{400}{1.012578} \times 0.6529$$ $$C_0 = 287.512 - 395.031 \times 0.6529 = 287.512 - 257.916 = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 29.60}$$

Decision: Since the theoretical fair value of the call option is ₹ 29.60 and it is available in the market for ₹ 25.00, the option is underpriced in the market. Hence, it is worth buying.

Part (ii): Valuation when Spot Price $S_0 = \text{₹ } 380$

Given: $S_0 = 380, X = 400 \implies \frac{S_0}{X} = \frac{380}{400} = 0.95 \implies \ln(0.95) = -0.05129$.

$$d_1 = \frac{-0.05129 + 0.01855}{0.11} = \frac{-0.03274}{0.11} = -0.29764$$ $$d_2 = -0.29764 - 0.11 = -0.40764$$ $$N(d_1) = N(-0.29764) = 1 - N(0.29764) = 1 - 0.6170 = 0.3830$$ $$N(d_2) = N(-0.40764) = 1 - N(0.40764) = 1 - 0.6582 = 0.3418$$ $$C_0 = 380 \times 0.3830 - \frac{400}{1.012578} \times 0.3418 = 145.54 - 395.031 \times 0.3418 = 145.54 - 135.02 = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 10.52}$$

Part (iii): Valuation of Put Option when Spot Price $S_0 = \text{₹ } 380$

Using the Put-Call Parity Theorem:

$$P_0 = C_0 + \frac{X}{e^{rt}} - S_0$$ $$P_0 = 10.52 + \frac{400}{1.012578} - 380 = 10.52 + 395.031 - 380 = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 25.55}$$

Part (iv): Valuation with Expected Dividend ($S_0 = \text{₹ } 408$, $D = \text{₹ } 10$ in 2 Months)

Time to dividend $t_d = 2/12 = 0.1667$ years. Interest factor $r \cdot t_d = 0.05 \times 0.1667 = 0.008333$.

$$\text{PV of Dividend} = D \times e^{-rt_d} = \frac{10}{e^{0.008333}} = \frac{10}{1.0084} = \text{₹ } 9.9167$$ $$\text{Adjusted Spot Price } (S_0^*) = 408.00 - 9.9167 = \text{₹ } 398.0833$$ $$\frac{S_0^*}{X} = \frac{398.0833}{400} = 0.9952 \implies \ln(0.9952) = -0.00481$$ $$d_1 = \frac{-0.00481 + 0.01855}{0.11} = \frac{0.01374}{0.11} = 0.1249 \approx 0.125$$ $$d_2 = 0.125 - 0.11 = 0.015$$ $$N(d_1) = N(0.125) = 0.5498; \quad N(d_2) = N(0.015) = 0.5060$$ $$C_0 = 398.083 \times 0.5498 - \frac{400}{1.012578} \times 0.5060 = 218.866 - 395.031 \times 0.5060$$ $$C_0 = 218.866 - 199.886 = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 18.98}$$
Summary of Answers:
  • (i) Call Value = ₹ 29.60; Worth buying at ₹ 25? Yes (Underpriced).
  • (ii) Call Value at ₹ 380 = ₹ 10.52.
  • (iii) Put Value at ₹ 380 = ₹ 25.55.
  • (iv) Call Value with Dividend = ₹ 18.98.

Real Options: Patent Valuation as Growth Option (Aidrex)

Problem Statement (ICAI Study Material)

ABC Ltd. is a pharmaceutical company possessing a patent of a drug called ‘Aidrex’, a medicine for AIDS patients. Being an approach drug, ABC Ltd. holds the right of production of drugs and its marketing. The period of patent is 15 years after which any other pharmaceutical company can produce the drug with the same formula. It is estimated that company shall require to incur $ 12.5 million for development and marketing of the drug. As per a survey conducted, the expected present value of cash flows from the sale of the drug during the period of 15 years shall be $ 16.7 million. Cash flows from a previous similar type of drug have exhibited a variance of 26.8% ($\sigma^2 = 0.268$). The current yield on Treasury Bonds of similar duration (15 years) is 7.8%.

Required: Determine the value of the patent.

Given Mathematical Values

$\ln(1.336) = 0.2897; \quad e^{-1.0005} = 0.3677; \quad e^{-1.17} = 0.3104$

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Map Capital Budgeting Parameters to Black-Scholes Model

  • Current Present Value of Cash Flows ($S_0$) = $ 16.70 Million
  • Capital Outlay required to commercially exploit ($X$) = $ 12.50 Million
  • Time horizon ($t$) = 15 Years
  • Variance of cash flows ($\sigma^2$) = 0.268 ($\sigma = \sqrt{0.268} = 0.5177$)
  • Risk-free interest rate ($r$) = 7.8% p.a. = 0.078
  • Continuous cash flow loss / dividend yield ($y$): Each year delay causes loss of 1 year of exclusivity: $$y = \frac{1}{15} = 0.0667 \text{ (6.67% p.a.)}$$

Step 2: Calculate $d_1$ and $d_2$

$$\sigma \sqrt{t} = 0.5177 \times \sqrt{15} = 0.5177 \times 3.873 = 2.005$$ $$\frac{S_0}{X} = \frac{16.70}{12.50} = 1.336 \implies \ln(1.336) = 0.2897$$ $$\left(r - y + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right) t = \left(0.078 - 0.0667 + \frac{0.268}{2}\right) \times 15 = (0.0113 + 0.1340) \times 15 = 0.1453 \times 15 = 2.1795$$ $$d_1 = \frac{0.2897 + 2.1795}{2.005} = \frac{2.4692}{2.005} = 1.2315$$ $$d_2 = d_1 - \sigma \sqrt{t} = 1.2315 - 2.005 = -0.7735$$

From Normal Distribution Table:

$$N(d_1) = N(1.23) = 0.8907$$ $$N(d_2) = N(-0.77) = 1 - N(0.77) = 1 - 0.7794 = 0.2206$$

Step 3: Value of Patent as European Call Option with Dividend Yield

$$C = S_0 \cdot e^{-yt} \cdot N(d_1) - X \cdot e^{-rt} \cdot N(d_2)$$

Where $e^{-yt} = e^{-0.0667 \times 15} = e^{-1.0005} = 0.3677$ and $e^{-rt} = e^{-0.078 \times 15} = e^{-1.17} = 0.3104$.

$$C = (16.70 \times 0.3677 \times 0.8907) - (12.50 \times 0.3104 \times 0.2206)$$ $$C = (6.1406 \times 0.8907) - (3.8800 \times 0.2206) = 5.4694 - 0.8559 = \mathbf{\$ 4.6135 \text{ Million}}$$
Final Answer: The strategic fair value of the 'Aidrex' drug patent is approximately $ 4.61 Million (or $ 4.42 Million using exact continuous approximations).

Real Options: Project Abandonment Option (IPL Fertilizer)

Problem Statement (ICAI Study Material)

IPL, already in the production of Fertilizer, is considering a proposal of building a new plant to produce pesticides. Suppose the PV of the proposal is ₹ 100 crore without the abandonment option. However, if market conditions for pesticide turn out to be favourable, the PV of the proposal shall increase by 30%. On the other hand, if market conditions remain sluggish, the PV of the proposal shall be reduced by 40%. In case the company is not interested in continuation of the project, it can be disposed of for ₹ 80 crore.

If the risk-free rate of interest is 8% p.a., what will be the value of the abandonment option?

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Construct Decision Tree at Year 1

Market State Value Without Abandonment Salvage / Disposal Value Value With Abandonment Abandonment Option Payoff
Favourable (+30%) $100 \times 1.30 = \text{₹ } 130 \text{ Cr}$ ₹ 80 Cr $\max(130, 80) = \text{₹ } 130 \text{ Cr}$ $\max(0, 80 - 130) = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 0}$
Sluggish (-40%) $100 \times 0.60 = \text{₹ } 60 \text{ Cr}$ ₹ 80 Cr $\max(60, 80) = \text{₹ } 80 \text{ Cr}$ $\max(0, 80 - 60) = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 20 \text{ Cr}}$

Step 2: Solve for Risk-Neutral Probability ($p$)

Expected return on project under risk-neutral probability must equal risk-free rate (8%):

$$p \times (+30\%) + (1 - p) \times (-40\%) = 8\%$$ $$0.30p - 0.40 + 0.40p = 0.08$$ $$0.70p = 0.48 \implies p = \frac{0.48}{0.70} = 0.6857 \approx 0.686$$ $$1 - p = 1 - 0.6857 = 0.3143 \approx 0.314$$

The abandonment option is an American/European Put Option on project value with strike price $X = \text{₹ } 80 \text{ Cr}$:

Final Conclusion: The value of the abandonment option is ₹ 5.82 Crore. The total strategic present value of the pesticide project increases from ₹ 100 Crore to ₹ 105.82 Crore due to this managerial flexibility.

Real Options: Timing / Deferral Option (MIS Ltd Solar Plant)

Problem Statement (ICAI Study Material)

Suppose MIS Ltd. is considering installation of a solar electricity generating plant for lighting the staff quarters. The plant shall cost ₹ 2.50 crore and shall lead to saving in electricity expenses at the current tariff by ₹ 21 lakh per year forever.

However, after one year with a change in Government in the state, the rate of electricity is subject to change. Accordingly, the saving in electricity can be of ₹ 12 lakh or ₹ 35 lakh per year and forever.

Assuming WACC of MIS Ltd. is 10% and risk-free rate of return is 8%, decide whether MIS Ltd. should accept the project today or wait and see.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

Alternative 1: Invest Today ($t=0$)

Present value of perpetual annual savings of ₹ 21 Lakh discounted at WACC (10%):

$$\text{PV of Savings} = \frac{\text{Annual Saving}}{\text{WACC}} = \frac{\text{₹ } 21 \text{ Lakh}}{0.10} = \text{₹ } 210 \text{ Lakh} = \text{₹ } 2.10 \text{ Crore}$$ $$\text{Static NPV}_{\text{Today}} = \text{PV of Inflows} - \text{Initial Cost} = \text{₹ } 2.10 \text{ Cr} - \text{₹ } 2.50 \text{ Cr} = \mathbf{-\text{₹ } 0.40 \text{ Crore (Negative)}}$$

Based on static NPV alone, the project should be rejected today.

Alternative 2: Defer Investment by One Year ($t=1$)

At the end of Year 1, uncertainty about electricity tariff is resolved:

  • State A (High Tariff - Saving ₹ 35 Lakh forever): $$\text{PV of Savings at } t=1 = \frac{\text{₹ } 35 \text{ Lakh}}{0.10} = \text{₹ } 350 \text{ Lakh} = \text{₹ } 3.50 \text{ Crore}$$ $$\text{NPV at } t=1 = \text{₹ } 3.50 \text{ Cr} - \text{₹ } 2.50 \text{ Cr} = \mathbf{+\text{₹ } 1.00 \text{ Crore (Accept Project)}}$$
  • State B (Low Tariff - Saving ₹ 12 Lakh forever): $$\text{PV of Savings at } t=1 = \frac{\text{₹ } 12 \text{ Lakh}}{0.10} = \text{₹ } 120 \text{ Lakh} = \text{₹ } 1.20 \text{ Crore}$$ $$\text{NPV at } t=1 = \text{₹ } 1.20 \text{ Cr} - \text{₹ } 2.50 \text{ Cr} = \mathbf{-\text{₹ } 1.30 \text{ Crore (Do Not Invest / Abandon)}}$$ $$\text{Payoff at } t=1 = \max(0, -1.30) = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 0.00}$$

Step 3: Solve for Risk-Neutral Probability ($p$)

Current value of electricity savings portfolio is ₹ 210 Lakh. At $t=1$, value is either ₹ 350 Lakh or ₹ 120 Lakh:

$$210 \times (1 + r_f) = p \times 350 + (1 - p) \times 120$$ $$210 \times 1.08 = 350p + 120 - 120p$$ $$226.80 = 120 + 230p \implies 230p = 106.80 \implies p = \frac{106.80}{230} = 0.4643$$ $$1 - p = 1 - 0.4643 = 0.5357$$

Step 4: Calculate Present Value of Timing Option

$$\text{Expected Payoff at } t=1 = (0.4643 \times \text{₹ } 1.00 \text{ Cr}) + (0.5357 \times \text{₹ } 0) = \text{₹ } 0.4643 \text{ Crore}$$ $$\text{NPV of Waiting (Timing Option Value)} = \frac{\text{₹ } 0.4643 \text{ Cr}}{1.08} = \mathbf{+\text{₹ } 0.43 \text{ Crore (+₹ 42.99 Lakh)}}$$
Final Recommendation:
  • Investing today yields an NPV of -₹ 0.40 Crore.
  • Waiting for one year yields an expected NPV of +₹ 0.43 Crore.
  • Therefore, MIS Ltd. should definitely wait and see for one year before making the investment commitment.

Theory Question 1

Reasons for Popularity of Stock Index Futures over Stock Futures in India

Question (ICAI Study Material)

What are the reasons for stock index futures becoming more popular financial derivatives over stock futures segment in India?

Answer (Verbatim & Comprehensive ICAI Analysis)

Stock index futures have emerged as the most active and dominant segment of equity derivatives globally and in India due to several fundamental operational and economic advantages:

  1. Institutional Portfolio Hedging Flexibility: Index futures enable institutional investors, mutual funds, and pension funds to hedge entire diversified equity portfolios in a single transaction without disturbing or liquidating individual underlying share holdings.
  2. Cost Efficiency: Hedging a diversified portfolio using individual single stock futures requires executing dozens of separate contracts across multiple stocks, incurring substantial brokerage, exchange turnover fees, and securities transaction tax (STT). A single index futures contract provides comprehensive systematic risk hedging at a fraction of the transaction cost.
  3. Immunity from Individual Stock Price Manipulation: Individual stock prices can be cornered or manipulated by concentrated market participants or market operators. A broad-based index (like NIFTY 50 or S&P BSE SENSEX) reflects a diversified basket of liquid mega-cap stocks across sectors, making price manipulation virtually impossible.
  4. Cash Settlement vs. Physical Delivery: Individual stock futures in India are subject to mandatory physical delivery settlement at expiry for open positions. In contrast, stock index futures are settled strictly in cash against the official index closing value, completely avoiding the logistical burdens, stamp duties, and delivery default risks of physical share transfer.
  5. Substantially Lower Regulatory & Margin Complexity: Single stock futures require dynamic surveillance, circuit filters, price bands, and specific margin requirements (including delivery margins during expiry week). Index futures enjoy lower volatility, narrower Value-at-Risk margins, and simpler regulatory oversight.
  6. Systematic vs. Unsystematic Risk Management: In a well-diversified equity portfolio, unsystematic (company-specific) risk is largely diversified away. The remaining risk is systematic market risk, which is precisely what stock index futures hedge.

Short Note on Marking to Market (MTM)

Question (ICAI Study Material)

Write short note on Marking to market.

Answer (Verbatim & Comprehensive ICAI Analysis)

Marking to Market (MTM) is the daily settlement mechanism mandated by derivative exchanges to ensure financial integrity and eliminate counterparty credit risk across futures contracts:

  • Daily Settlement Process: At the end of each trading day, every open futures contract is revalued at the official closing settlement price established by the exchange.
  • Cash Debits & Credits:
    • If the daily settlement price rises: The buyer's (Long position) margin account is credited with the daily gain, and the seller's (Short position) margin account is debited with an identical loss.
    • If the daily settlement price falls: The buyer's margin account is debited with the daily loss, and the seller's margin account is credited with the gain.
  • Maintenance Margin & Margin Calls: When accumulated daily losses deplete a trader's margin account below the specified Maintenance Margin threshold, the broker/clearing member issues a Margin Call.
  • Variation Margin Obligation: The trader must immediately deposit additional funds (known as Variation Margin) before the next trading session commences to restore the account balance back to the Initial Margin level (not merely back to the maintenance level).
  • Default Prevention: By collecting losses and distributing gains every 24 hours, credit exposure is reset to zero daily, preventing large accumulated default losses that could threaten the clearinghouse.

Answer (Verbatim & Comprehensive ICAI Analysis)

The Black-Scholes-Merton option pricing model relies on the following fundamental structural assumptions (state any four for full marks):

  1. European Exercise Style: The option can be exercised only at its final expiration date $T$; early exercise is precluded.
  2. No Transaction Costs or Taxes: There are zero brokerage commissions, transaction levies, exchange fees, or tax frictions. Securities are completely liquid and infinitely divisible.

Theory Question 4

Definition of Option Greeks

Question (ICAI Study Material)

Define the term Greeks with respect to options.

Answer (Verbatim & Comprehensive ICAI Analysis)

The term Option Greeks refers to a collection of statistical sensitivity measures derived from partial derivatives of option pricing models (such as Black-Scholes). They quantify the expected rate of change in an option's market price in response to changes in key underlying market variables:

Greek Symbol Parameter Sensitivity Operational Significance
Delta ($\Delta$) Underlying Asset Price ($S$) Measures $\frac{\partial C}{\partial S}$. Indicates how much the option price moves per ₹ 1 move in underlying stock. Used to determine the hedge ratio for delta neutrality.
Gamma ($\Gamma$) Rate of Change of Delta ($\Delta$) Measures $\frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial S^2} = \frac{\partial \Delta}{\partial S}$. Measures the curvature or acceleration of Delta. High Gamma near ATM requires frequent portfolio rebalancing.
Theta ($\Theta$) Time to Expiry ($t$) Measures $\frac{\partial C}{\partial t}$. Represents the daily time decay of the option premium. Theta is almost always negative for long option positions.
Vega ($\mathcal{V}$ or $\nu$) Volatility of Underlying ($\sigma$) Measures $\frac{\partial C}{\partial \sigma}$. Quantifies option price sensitivity to a 1% shift in implied volatility. Highest for At-The-Money options.
Rho ($\rho$) Risk-Free Interest Rate ($r$) Measures $\frac{\partial C}{\partial r}$. Measures option price sensitivity to shifts in benchmark interest rates. Positively related to Call options; negatively related to Put options.

Delta Hedge Ratio & One-Period Option Valuation (Dayal / ABC Ltd)

Problem Statement (ICAI Study Material)

Mr. Dayal is interested in purchasing equity shares of ABC Ltd. which are currently selling at ₹ 600 each. He expects that price of share may go up to ₹ 780 or may go down to ₹ 480 in three months. The chances of occurring such variations are 60% and 40% respectively. A call option on the shares of ABC Ltd. can be exercised at the end of three months at a strike price of ₹ 630.

Required:

  1. What combination of share and option should Mr. Dayal select if he wants a perfect hedge?
  2. What should be the value of option today (risk-free rate is 10% p.a.)?
  3. What is the expected rate of return on the option?
Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Determine Terminal Option Payoffs ($X = 630$)

  • If stock rises to ₹ 780: $C_u = \max(0, 780 - 630) = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 150}$
  • If stock falls to ₹ 480: $C_d = \max(0, 480 - 630) = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 0}$

Part (i): Perfect Hedge Combination (Hedge Ratio $\Delta$)

$$\Delta = \frac{C_u - C_d}{S_u - S_d} = \frac{150 - 0}{780 - 480} = \frac{150}{300} = \mathbf{0.50}$$

Hedging Strategy: Purchase 0.50 shares of ABC Ltd. for every 1 Call option written (sold), or purchase 1 share and write 2 call options.

Part (ii): Value of Option Today

Terminal payoff of the hedged portfolio (Long 0.5 shares + Short 1 Call):

  • If stock rises to ₹ 780: Portfolio Value = $(0.50 \times 780) - 150 = 390 - 150 = \text{₹ } 240$.
  • If stock falls to ₹ 480: Portfolio Value = $(0.50 \times 480) - 0 = 240 - 0 = \text{₹ } 240$.

Since the portfolio is completely riskless, its present value discounted at the risk-free rate ($10\%$ for 3 months = $2.5\%$):

$$\text{PV of Hedged Portfolio} = \frac{\text{₹ } 240}{1 + 0.10 \times 0.25} = \frac{240}{1.025} = \text{₹ } 234.146$$

Current cost of hedged portfolio = $\Delta \cdot S_0 - C_0 = (0.50 \times 600) - C_0 = 300 - C_0$.

$$300 - C_0 = 234.146 \implies C_0 = 300 - 234.146 = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 65.85}$$

Part (iii): Expected Rate of Return on the Option

$$\text{Expected Option Payoff at Maturity} = (0.60 \times 150) + (0.40 \times 0) = \text{₹ } 90.00$$ $$\text{Expected 3-Month Return} = \frac{90.00 - 65.85}{65.85} = \frac{24.15}{65.85} = 36.67\%$$ $$\text{Annualized Expected Rate of Return} = 36.67\% \times \frac{12}{3} = \mathbf{146.70\% \text{ p.a.}}$$
Summary of Answers:
  • (i) Perfect Hedge Ratio = Buy 0.5 shares for each Call written (or 1 share for 2 calls).
  • (ii) Fair Value of Option Today = ₹ 65.85.
  • (iii) Expected Rate of Return on Option = 146.70% p.a.

Two-Period Binomial Tree Call Option Valuation (Strike ₹ 50)

Problem Statement (ICAI Study Material)

Consider a two-year call option with a strike price of ₹ 50 on a stock the current price of which is also ₹ 50. Assume that there are two time periods of one year each, and in each year the stock price can move up or down by an equal percentage of 20% ($u = 1.20, d = 0.80$). The risk-free interest rate is 6% p.a.

Required: Using the binomial option model, calculate the probability of price moving up and down. Also draw a two-step binomial tree and determine the value of the option today.

Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Compute Stock Prices in 2-Period Tree

  • Current Stock Price ($S_0$) = ₹ 50.00
  • Period 1: $S_u = 50 \times 1.20 = \text{₹ } 60.00; \quad S_d = 50 \times 0.80 = \text{₹ } 40.00$
  • Period 2:
    • $S_{uu} = 60 \times 1.20 = \text{₹ } 72.00$
    • $S_{ud} = 60 \times 0.80 = \text{₹ } 48.00$
    • $S_{dd} = 40 \times 0.80 = \text{₹ } 32.00$

Step 2: Terminal Call Payoffs at Year 2 ($X = 50$)

  • $C_{uu} = \max(0, 72.00 - 50.00) = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 22.00}$
  • $C_{ud} = \max(0, 48.00 - 50.00) = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 0.00}$
  • $C_{dd} = \max(0, 32.00 - 50.00) = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 0.00}$

Step 3: Risk-Neutral Probabilities

$$p = \frac{R - d}{u - d} = \frac{1.06 - 0.80}{1.20 - 0.80} = \frac{0.26}{0.40} = \mathbf{0.65}$$ $$1 - p = 1 - 0.65 = \mathbf{0.35}$$

Step 4: Roll Back to Value Option Today ($t=0$)

Using the two-period risk-neutral discounting formula:

$$C_0 = \frac{p^2 \cdot C_{uu} + 2p(1-p) \cdot C_{ud} + (1-p)^2 \cdot C_{dd}}{(1 + r)^2}$$ $$C_0 = \frac{(0.65)^2 \times 22.00 + 2(0.65)(0.35) \times 0 + (0.35)^2 \times 0}{(1.06)^2}$$ $$C_0 = \frac{0.4225 \times 22.00}{1.1236} = \frac{9.295}{1.1236} = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 8.2725 \approx \text{₹ } 8.27}$$
Final Answer:
  • Risk-neutral probability of up-move ($p$) = 0.65; down-move ($1-p$) = 0.35.
  • Value of the 2-Year Call Option today = ₹ 8.27.

Risk-Neutral Option Valuation with Strike ₹ 450 (Penchant Ltd)

Problem Statement (ICAI Study Material)

The current market price of an equity share of Penchant Ltd is ₹ 420. Within a period of 3 months, the maximum and minimum price of it is expected to be ₹ 500 and ₹ 400 respectively. If the risk-free rate of interest is 8% p.a., what should be the value of a 3-month Call option under the Risk Neutral method at the strike rate of ₹ 450? Given $e^{0.02} = 1.0202$.

Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Solve for Risk-Neutral Probability ($p$)

Using continuous compounding: $R = e^{rt} = e^{0.08 \times 0.25} = e^{0.02} = 1.0202$.

$$S_0 \cdot e^{0.02} = p \cdot S_u + (1 - p) \cdot S_d$$ $$420 \times 1.0202 = 500p + 400(1 - p)$$ $$428.484 = 400 + 100p \implies 100p = 28.484 \implies p = \mathbf{0.2848}$$ $$1 - p = 1 - 0.2848 = \mathbf{0.7152}$$

Step 2: Terminal Call Payoffs ($X = 450$)

  • If stock reaches ₹ 500: $C_u = \max(0, 500 - 450) = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 50}$
  • If stock drops to ₹ 400: $C_d = \max(0, 400 - 450) = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 0}$

Black-Scholes Call Valuation with Interpolation & Greeks Verification

Problem Statement (ICAI Study Material)

From the following data for a certain stock, find the value of a call option:

Parameter Value
Price of stock now ($S_0$) ₹ 80
Exercise price ($X$) ₹ 75
Standard deviation of continuously compounded annual return ($\sigma$) 0.40
Maturity period ($t$) 6 months (0.50 years)
Annual risk-free interest rate ($r$) 12% p.a. (0.12)
Given Mathematical Values

$\ln(1.0667) = 0.06456; \quad e^{-0.06} = 0.94176$

Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Compute Intermediate Terms

$$\frac{S_0}{X} = \frac{80}{75} = 1.06667 \implies \ln(1.0667) = 0.06456$$ $$\sigma \sqrt{t} = 0.40 \times \sqrt{0.50} = 0.40 \times 0.7071 = 0.28284$$ $$\left(r + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right) t = \left(0.12 + \frac{0.16}{2}\right) \times 0.50 = (0.12 + 0.08) \times 0.50 = 0.20 \times 0.50 = 0.10$$

Step 2: Calculate $d_1$ and $d_2$

$$d_1 = \frac{0.06456 + 0.10}{0.28284} = \frac{0.16456}{0.28284} = \mathbf{0.5818}$$ $$d_2 = d_1 - \sigma \sqrt{t} = 0.5818 - 0.28284 = \mathbf{0.2990}$$

Step 3: Cumulative Normal Probabilities $N(d_1)$ and $N(d_2)$

  • For $d_1 = 0.5818$: $N(0.58) = 0.7190$ and $N(0.59) = 0.7224$. $$N(0.5818) = 0.7190 + 0.18 \times (0.7224 - 0.7190) = 0.7190 + 0.0006 = \mathbf{0.7196}$$
  • For $d_2 = 0.2990 \approx 0.30$: $N(0.30) = \mathbf{0.6179}$

Step 4: Compute Call Option Value

$$C_0 = S_0 \cdot N(d_1) - X \cdot e^{-rt} \cdot N(d_2)$$ $$C_0 = (80 \times 0.7196) - (75 \times 0.94176 \times 0.6179)$$ $$C_0 = 57.568 - (70.632 \times 0.6179) = 57.568 - 43.643 = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 13.925 \approx \text{₹ } 13.93}$$
Final Answer: Under the Black-Scholes Formula, the fair value of the 6-month Call Option is ₹ 13.93 (or ₹ 13.96 using standard three-digit normal tables).
