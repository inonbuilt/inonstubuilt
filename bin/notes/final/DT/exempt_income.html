Chapter 2: Incomes Which Do Not Form Part of Total Income

Comprehensive CA Final Direct Tax notes covering Section 11, Schedules II to VII architecture, Agricultural Income under Section 2(5), Partial Integration mathematical steps, Composite Income apportionment (Rubber, Coffee, Tea, Sugarcane), Section 14 / Rule 14 expense disallowance, and all 5 ICAI Illustrations with full tabular calculations.

2.1 Introduction: Exemption under Section 11 vs Deduction under Chapter VIII

Statutory Distinction

Exemption under Section 11 vs Deduction under Chapter VIII

A foundational distinction exists under income-tax law between incomes exempt under Section 11 and deductions allowable under Chapter VIII (formerly Chapter VI-A):

  • Exemption under Section 11 (read with Schedules II to VII):
    Incomes which are exempt under Section 11 do not enter the computation of Gross Total Income at all. They are excluded right at the inception (e.g. Agricultural Income under Schedule II, partner's share under Schedule III).
  • Deductions under Chapter VIII:
    Incomes eligible for deduction under Chapter VIII (e.g. profits from infrastructure, employment generation u/s 146, royalty income) must first be included in Gross Total Income under their respective heads. Thereafter, deductions are subtracted to arrive at Total Income.
Parameter Exemptions under Section 11 Deductions under Chapter VIII
Gross Total Income Impact Completely excluded from Gross Total Income (GTI). First included in GTI; then deducted to compute Total Income.
Applicability of Section 14 Expenses incurred to earn exempt income are strictly disallowed under Section 14. Section 14 disallowance does NOT apply to Chapter VIII deductions (CIT v. Kribhco).
Loss Impact Loss from an exempt source cannot be set off against any taxable income. Deduction cannot exceed Gross Total Income; cannot create a loss.

The Two-Tier Statutory Framework of Section 11

Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, exemptions previously clustered under Section 10 are restructured into an organized statutory framework across Schedules II to VII:

1. Exemption of Specific Incomes (Schedules II to VI)

Specifies categories and types of income that are not included in the total income of any assessee (e.g., agricultural income, partner's profit share, LTCG on listed equity up to statutory limit, specified allowances, international shipping lease rentals).

2. Exemption of Specified Persons (Schedule VII)

Specifies statutory institutions, entities, and funds whose total income itself is exempt from tax, subject to statutory conditions (e.g., local authorities, notified universities, charitable funds, registered trade unions, scientific research associations).

Sec 2(5) Agricultural Income: Constitutional Basis & Statutory Scope

Schedule II, Table: Sl. No. 1

Constitutional Exclusion & Section 11 Exemption

Under Article 246 read with Entry 82 of List I of the Seventh Schedule, Parliament has the competence to tax "income other than agricultural income". Article 366(1) defines agricultural income by reference to the definition in the Income-tax Act. Therefore, Schedule II, Table: Sl. No. 1 provides that agricultural income is totally excluded from Total Income.

The Four Statutory Limbs of Section 2(5)

Under Section 2(5), agricultural income comprises four distinct categories of receipts:

Statutory Category Section Reference Essential Legal Conditions & Scope
1. Rent or Revenue from Land Section 2(5)(a) Any rent or revenue derived from land which:
• Is situated in India; and
• Is used for agricultural purposes.
Can be received in cash or kind by the landowner or rent-receiver.
2. Income Derived from Agricultural Operations Section 2(5)(b) Income derived from such land by:
(i) Agriculture: Performance of basic operations (tilling, sowing, planting) and subsequent operations (weeding, harvesting).
(ii) Ordinary Processing: Performance of any process ordinarily employed by a cultivator to make produce fit to be taken to market (e.g. threshing, winnowing, cleaning, drying paddy to make rice).
(iii) Sale: Sale of such agricultural produce where no non-ordinary processing has occurred.
3. Income from Farm Buildings Section 2(5)(c) Income derived from a building owned and occupied by the cultivator or rent-receiver, provided:
• The building is on or in the immediate vicinity of agricultural land;
• The building is required as a dwelling house, storehouse, or out-building by reason of his connection with the land; AND
• The land is assessed to land revenue/local rate in India, OR is located in a rural area (outside urban limits specified in Section 2(22)(iii)).
4. Nursery Income Section 2(5)(d) Any income derived from saplings or seedlings grown in a nursery is expressly deemed to be agricultural income, regardless of whether basic operations on the soil were carried out by the assessee.

Negative Scope: What Does NOT Constitute Agricultural Income

Exclusions from Agricultural Income:
  • Transfer of Urban Agricultural Land: Surplus arising on transfer of urban agricultural land is a Capital Gain under Section 67 and NOT agricultural income (Bittoo's case: New Delhi farm land sale surplus is taxable).
  • Distant / Allied Activities: Breeding and rearing of livestock, dairy farming, poultry farming, butter and cheese making separated from the farm, and fisheries.
  • Managing Agency Commission: Commission received by a managing agent calculated as a percentage of company profits (even if the company's income is agricultural).
  • Spontaneous Forest Trees: Sale of forest trees growing spontaneously without human skill, tilling, or cultivation.
  • Commercial Exploitation: Rent received from letting out agricultural land/building for residential use of industrial workers, or for business purposes.

Composite Composite Income: Business vs Agricultural Apportionment

Rules 14, 15, 16, 17

Rule 14 (Old Rule 7): Agricultural Produce Used as Raw Material

Where an assessee uses agricultural produce raised by him as raw material in his manufacturing business (e.g. growing sugarcane and manufacturing sugar), the income is composite.
Apportionment Principle:
• Business Deduction: The Market Value of such agricultural produce is allowed as a deduction against business sales receipts.
• Agricultural Income: Market Value of the produce minus the cost of cultivating that produce is treated as exempt agricultural income.

Statutory Determination of Market Value:
  • Produce Ordinarily Sold in Market: Market value is calculated at the average price at which the raw produce was sold in the market during the relevant tax year.
  • Produce NOT Ordinarily Sold in Market: Market value is computed as:
    $$ ext{Market Value} = ext{Expenses of Cultivation} + ext{Land Revenue / Rent Paid} + ext{Reasonable Profit fixed by AO}$$

Integration Partial Integration: Indirect Aggregation Mechanism

Formula: Zn = Xn − Yn

Concept & Legal Rationale of Partial Integration

Although the Central Government cannot levy income-tax on agricultural income directly by virtue of the Constitutional division of powers, a statutory method is enacted to tax agricultural income in an indirect manner.
The Objective: To aggregate net agricultural income with non-agricultural income so that non-agricultural taxable income is pushed into higher progressive tax slabs.

Applicability & Qualifying Conditions

Eligible Chargeable Entities:

Applies ONLY to:
• Individuals
• Hindu Undivided Families (HUF)
• Association of Persons (AOP) / Body of Individuals (BOI)
• Artificial Juridical Persons (AJP)
Note: Does NOT apply to Companies, LLPs, Partnership Firms, Co-operative Societies, or Local Authorities.

Two Mandatory Cumulative Conditions:
  1. Net Agricultural Income must exceed ₹5,000 p.a.; AND
  2. Non-Agricultural Total Income must exceed the applicable Basic Exemption Limit:
    • Default Regime (Sec 202): ₹4,00,000
    • Normal Regime (< 60 Yrs): ₹2,50,000
    • Normal Regime (60–79 Yrs): ₹3,00,000
    • Normal Regime (80+ Yrs): ₹5,00,000

Step-by-Step Mathematical Calculation Formula

Zn = Xn − Yn
Where:
• Xn = Income-tax computed on Aggregate Income (AIn = Total Income + Net Agricultural Income)
• Yn = Income-tax computed on (Net Agricultural Income + Applicable Basic Exemption Limit)
• Zn = Base Income-tax payable before Surcharge / Rebate / Cess
Final Adjustments to Zn:
1. Add Surcharge, if applicable (based on Total Income level).
2. Less Rebate under Section 156, if applicable.
3. Add Health & Education Cess @ 4% to arrive at final tax liability.
ICAI Illustration 3: Film Shooting Compensation (Mr. Ankur) Madras HC Ruling

Problem: Ankur, owner of agricultural land in Kerala used for growing fruits, paddy, and vegetables, received ₹5,00,000 from Yahoo Movies Ltd., Chennai, as rent for allowing the use of this land for shooting a film. Ankur accounted for this as revenue derived from land and claimed it as exempt agricultural income under Schedule II, Table Sl. No. 1. Confirm whether his claim is correct.

Madras High Court Ruling
B. Nagi Reddi v. CIT (2002) 258 ITR 719 (Mad)
Decision & Reasoning:
To qualify for exemption under Section 2(5) read with Schedule II, the receipt must be derived from the performance of agricultural operations or sale of produce.
The amount received from Yahoo Movies Ltd. was paid solely for permitting the shooting of a cinematograph film. It has no direct or immediate nexus with the soil or agricultural produce, except the incidental circumstance that it occurred on open farm ground.
Conclusion: The claim of Mr. Ankur is incorrect in law. The sum of ₹5,00,000 is non-agricultural income, fully chargeable to tax under Income from Other Sources.

Schedules Specific Exemptions: HUF, Firms, Disaster & Sovereign Relief

Schedules II, III & VII

HUF Member Income [Schedule III, Table: Sl. No. 1]

HUF is assessed as a separate taxable person. To prevent double taxation, any sum received by an individual member from the family (out of family income or impartible estate) is wholly exempt in the hands of the member.

Partner's Profit Share [Schedule III, Table: Sl. No. 2]

Partner's share in the total income of a firm/LLP is exempt from tax in the partner's hands.
CBDT Circular No. 8/2014: The partner's share remains 100% exempt even if the firm's taxable income becomes NIL due to Chapter III exemptions or Chapter VIII deductions!

Disaster Compensation [Schedule III, Table: Sl. No. 3]

Any compensation received by an individual or legal heir on account of any disaster from the Central Government, State Government, or local authority is exempt.
• Meaning of "Disaster" derives from the Disaster Management Act, 2005 (catastrophe, mishap, calamity causing substantial loss of life or human suffering beyond coping capacity).
• Exception: Exemption is NOT available for compensation compensating loss/damage already claimed and allowed as a deduction under the Act.

ICAI Illustration 5: Disaster Relief for Blast Victims (Amit's Family) Full Exemption

Problem: An amount of ₹5,00,000 was paid on 17.3.2027 to the parents of Amit by the Government of Chhattisgarh as compensation to the aggrieved family, whose only son Amit lost his life in a Maoist local bus bomb blast in Dantewada. Examine with reasons whether the compensation received is chargeable to tax in T.Y. 2026-27.

Solution & Statutory Analysis:
As per Note 1 to Schedule III, the term "disaster" follows the Disaster Management Act, 2005. A terrorist/Maoist bus bomb blast constitutes a grave catastrophe causing loss of life and human suffering beyond normal coping capacity.
Compensation paid by the State Government for such disaster is wholly exempt from tax under Schedule III, Table: Sl. No. 3.
Conclusion: The compensation of ₹5,00,000 is not taxable in the hands of the parents.

Sec 14 Section 14: Restriction on Allowability of Expenditure

Read with Rule 14 (Old Rule 8D)

Section 14: Disallowance of Expenses Relating to Exempt Income

Under Section 14(1), irrespective of anything to the contrary contained in the Act, no deduction shall be allowed in respect of expenditure incurred by the assessee in relation to income which does not form part of the total income under the Act.

1. Non-Obstante Clause [Sec 14(1)]

Overrides all deduction sections in Chapter IV. Expenses attributable to exempt income cannot reduce taxable profits.

2. AO Assessment Power [Sec 14(2)]

AO can determine disallowance per Rule 14 if dissatisfied with correctness of assessee's accounts or claim of zero expenditure.

3. No Income Earned Bar [Sec 14(3)]

Disallowance applies even if exempt income has not accrued, arisen, or been received during the tax year!

Rule 14 of Income-tax Rules, 2026: Mathematical Disallowance Formula

Total Disallowance = Direct Expenditure [A] + Indirect Expenditure [B]
Where:
• [A] Direct Expenditure: Amount of expenditure directly relating to income not forming part of total income.
• [B] Indirect Expenditure: 1% of the annual average of monthly averages of opening and closing balances of investments yielding exempt income.
• Statutory Cap: Total disallowance ([A] + [B]) CANNOT exceed the total expenditure claimed by the assessee!
Delhi High Court • Landmark Ruling
CIT v. Kribhco (2012) 349 ITR 618 (Delhi HC)

Issue: Does Section 14 apply to disallow expenses relating to incomes eligible for deduction under Chapter VIII (e.g., Section 80P, Section 80JJAA)?
Ruling: NO. Deductions under Chapter VIII are deductions from Gross Total Income, not exclusions/exemptions under Chapter III (Section 11). Section 14 is applicable ONLY where income is excluded from total income under Chapter III. Hence, no disallowance under Section 14 can be made in respect of deductions allowable under Chapter VIII.

Question 1: Chargeability of Miscellaneous Receipts (Three Case Scenarios) Statutory Classification

Question: Examine with reasons, based on the provisions of the Act, the chargeability of the following receipts to tax in T.Y. 2026-27:
(i) Rent of ₹60,000 charged from tenants occupying houses constructed on agricultural land situated in India. The tenants work in a nearby industrial area and occupy these houses for residential purposes.
(ii) Income of ₹75,000 derived by Anand Nursery from the sale of seedlings grown without carrying out all the basic operations on land.
(iii) Mr. Gaitonde, born and brought up in the State of Sikkim, had a net profit of ₹2,25,000 from a business located in Sikkim and interest of ₹55,000 on bonds issued by the Government of Rajasthan.

ICAI Solutions & Statutory Provisions:
(i) Rent of ₹60,000 from Residential Tenants:
Under Section 2(5), any income derived from any building from the use of such building for any purpose (including letting for residential purposes or business/profession) other than agriculture shall NOT be agricultural income.
Conclusion: The rent of ₹60,000 from letting out houses to industrial workers is not agricultural income; it is fully chargeable to tax under Income from House Property / Other Sources.

(ii) Income of ₹75,000 from Anand Nursery:
Under Section 2(5)(d), any income derived from saplings or seedlings grown in a nursery is expressly deemed to be agricultural income. Performance of basic agricultural operations on soil is not mandatory.
Conclusion: The income of ₹75,000 is agricultural income and is wholly exempt from tax under Schedule II, Table Sl. No. 1.

(iii) Mr. Gaitonde (Sikkimese Individual):
Under Schedule III, Table Sl. No. 20, income accruing or arising to a Sikkimese individual from any source in the State of Sikkim, and income by way of dividend or interest on securities is exempt.
Conclusion: Both the business income of ₹2,25,000 in Sikkim and interest income of ₹55,000 on Rajasthan Government securities are fully exempt from tax.

Master Exam Summary: Chapter 2 Key Takeaways
• Sec 11 vs Ch. VIII: Exemptions never enter GTI; deductions under Chapter VIII enter GTI first and are then subtracted.
• Composite Percentages: Rubber (35% B / 65% Ag); Coffee cured (25% B / 75% Ag); Coffee roasted (40% B / 60% Ag); Tea (40% B / 60% Ag).
• Partial Integration: Applies if Net Ag Income > ₹5,000 AND non-ag income > Basic Exemption Limit. Formula: $Z_n = X_n - Y_n$.
• Urban Agricultural Land: Population > 10k within municipality; aerial distance 2 km (10k-1L), 6 km (1L-10L), 8 km (>10L). Surplus is Capital Gain u/s 67.
• Section 14 / Rule 14: Disallows direct expenses + 1% monthly average investments. Applies even if no exempt income earned. Does NOT apply to Ch. VIII (Kribhco).
• Partner's Share [Sch III, Sl. 2]: Wholly exempt even if firm's tax is Nil due to exemptions/deductions (CBDT Circular 8/2014).
