Introduction to Transfer Pricing & Arm's Length Principle (ALP)

Economic Rationale, Objectives, Global Standards & Practical Difficulties

1. The Genesis of Transfer Pricing in Global Commerce

With the rapid globalization of business, multinational enterprises (MNEs) establish subsidiaries, branches, and joint ventures across multiple sovereign tax jurisdictions. In a corporate group, internal pricing of cross-border goods, intangibles, management services, and financial facilities is determined not by competitive market forces, but by internal corporate directives.

This gives MNEs an inherent incentive to engage in profit shifting: charging artificially high prices for expenses in high-tax countries (to inflate deductions) and shifting profits to low-tax or no-tax jurisdictions (tax havens). To protect national tax bases against erosion, tax administrations worldwide, spearheaded by the OECD, developed the Arm's Length Principle (ALP).

Meaning of Arm's Length Price (ALP) [Section 173(b)]

The Arm's Length Price (ALP) of a transaction between two Associated Enterprises (AEs) is the price that would have been negotiated, agreed upon, and charged in an uncontrolled transaction between independent, unrelated enterprises operating under similar commercial, market, and economic conditions.

2. Significance of ALP (OECD / ICAI)

  • Accurate Economic Contribution: Attributes profits to jurisdictions where real economic activity, value creation, functions, assets, and risks (FAR) reside.
  • Tax Base Protection: Safeguards source and resident countries from artificial profit deflation.
  • Level Playing Field: Places multinational enterprises on equal competitive footing with purely domestic enterprises.
  • Avoidance of Double Taxation: Provides a bilateral standard for tax treaty relief and Mutual Agreement Procedures (MAP).

3. Practical Difficulties in ALP

  • Absence of Identical Comparables: MNE transactions often involve proprietary intangibles, custom software, or secret recipes with no open market equivalent.
  • Information Asymmetry: Tax authorities lack real-time visibility into internal supply chains and pricing algorithms of multinational groups.
  • Massive Compliance Burden: Requires extensive search in commercial databases (Prowess, Capitaline) and specialized benchmarking studies.
  • Double Taxation Risks: Unilateral TP adjustments by one country without corresponding adjustment by the other lead to economic double taxation.

Sec 161

Charge of Transfer Pricing & Non-Reduction Rule

Scope of TP Computation, Expense Allocation, and the Golden Non-Reduction Principle
Core Charging Section

Section 161 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (Corresponds to Sec 92 of 1961 Act) Mandatory Rule

(1) Computation of Income having regard to Arm's Length Price: Any income arising from an international transaction or a specified domestic transaction shall be computed having regard to the arm's length price.

(2) Allocation or Apportionment of Expenses: Any allowance for an expenditure or interest arising from an international transaction or specified domestic transaction shall also be determined having regard to the arm's length price.

(3) The Golden Non-Reduction Rule: The provisions of this section shall not apply in a case where the computation of income having regard to the arm's length price has the effect of reducing the income chargeable to tax or increasing the loss, as the case may be, computed on the basis of books of account kept by the assessee in respect of the previous year in which the transaction took place.

(4) No Chapter VIII (Chapter VI-A) / SEZ Deductions on Enhanced Income: Where total income is increased due to transfer pricing adjustments made by the AO/TPO, no deduction under Chapter VIII (deductions from GTI, formerly Chapter VI-A) or Section 10AA (SEZ holiday) shall be allowed in respect of the enhanced income.

Key Dimensions of Section 161

Provision Statutory Mechanics Impact on Taxpayer
Income Computation
[Sec 161(1)]		 Replaces contracted transaction price with Arm's Length Price (ALP) if contracted price understates income. Increases taxable revenue for sales of goods, provision of services, or licensing of IP to foreign AEs.
Expense Disallowance
[Sec 161(2)]		 Disallows excessive expenditure, management fees, royalty, or interest paid to foreign AEs beyond arm's length levels. Disallowance of excess payment reduces allowable business deductions, increasing Net PBIT.
One-Way Street Principle
[Sec 161(3)]		 Transfer pricing is strictly a revenue-protection anti-avoidance provision. It is a one-way street favoring Revenue. If arm's length price would result in a lower profit or a higher tax loss than book profits, TP adjustments are ignored. Taxpayer cannot claim downward revision!
Denial of Tax Incentives
[Sec 161(4)]		 Section 10AA (SEZ unit profits) and Chapter VIII (Chapter VI-A: Sec 80-IA, 80-IB, etc.) apply only to book profits. The upward TP adjustment is taxed at regular corporate rates without benefit of any tax holidays or deductions.
Crucial Rule Tested in Exam: TYK Question 5 Application

If an Indian company sells goods to its foreign AE for ₹1,000 per unit, and the arm's length price determined under CUP method is ₹900 per unit, can the assessee substitute ₹900 to reduce its taxable income?
Statutory Answer: NO. By virtue of Section 161(3), transfer pricing provisions cannot be applied to reduce declared income. The returned income of ₹1,000 per unit stands intact!

Meaning of "Enterprise" [Section 162(2)]

An "Enterprise" means a person (including a permanent establishment of such person) who is, or has been, or is proposed to be, engaged in any activity relating to the production, storage, supply, distribution, acquisition or control of articles or goods, or the provision of services of any kind, or in investment, or providing loan or credit, or in the business of acquiring, holding, underwriting or dealing with shares, debentures or other securities.

Sec 163

International Transactions & Deemed International Transactions

Cross-Border Scope, Broadened Intangibles Definition, and Third-Party Tripartite Routing
Transaction Scope

Section 163 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (Corresponds to Sec 92B) Statutory Rule

(1) International Transaction: A transaction between two or more associated enterprises, either or both of whom are non-residents, in the nature of —

  • Purchase, sale, transfer, lease, or use of tangible property (buildings, plant, machinery, inventory);
  • Purchase, sale, transfer, licensing, or use of intangible property (patents, copyrights, trademarks, licenses, software, customer lists);
  • Provision of services (IT, technical, management, marketing, financial, consulting);
  • Lending or borrowing money, extending credit, issuing corporate guarantees;
  • Any other transaction having a bearing on the profits, income, losses, or assets of such enterprises.

(2) Deemed International Transaction [Tripartite / Interposed Routing]: A transaction entered into by an enterprise with an unrelated third party shall be deemed to be an international transaction entered into between two associated enterprises if —

  1. There exists a prior agreement in relation to the relevant transaction between such other person and the associated enterprise, OR
  2. The terms of the relevant transaction are determined in substance between such other person and the associated enterprise.

Crucial Statutory Note: This deemed provision applies even if the third party is an Indian resident, provided either the enterprise or its AE is a non-resident!

Visualizing Deemed International Transaction [Section 163(2)]

To bypass transfer pricing provisions, an Indian company (Ind Co) might not contract directly with its foreign AE (US Co). Instead, US Co enters into an arrangement with an independent third-party distributor in Singapore (Sing Co), and Sing Co contracts with Ind Co.

Leg of Transaction Parties Involved Legal Relationship Transfer Pricing Applicability
Leg 1 (Underlying Agreement) US Co (AE) ↔ Sing Co (Third Party) Prior agreement or terms fixed in substance Governs the commercial framework of the deal.
Leg 2 (Direct Transaction) Sing Co (Third Party) ↔ Ind Co (Assessee) Supply of goods / provision of services Deemed International Transaction u/s 163(2)! The transaction between Ind Co and Sing Co must be tested against Arm's Length Price!

Broad Scope of "Intangibles" under Transfer Pricing

Transfer pricing law adopts the widest OECD BEPS Action 8-10 definition of intangibles, covering:

1. Marketing Intangibles: Trademarks, trade names, brand names, logos, customer lists, distribution channels.
2. Technology Intangibles: Patents, patent applications, trade secrets, proprietary software, technical documentation, blueprints.
3. Human & Location Intangibles: Know-how, trained workforce, location-specific savings, assembled workforce, government licenses.
Sec 164

Specified Domestic Transactions (SDT > ₹20 Crores)

Application of Transfer Pricing to Purely Domestic Inter-Unit Transactions & Concessional Regimes
Domestic TP

Section 164 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (Corresponds to Sec 92BA) Threshold: ₹20 Crores

"Specified Domestic Transaction" (SDT) means any of the following transactions, not being an international transaction, where the aggregate value of such transactions exceeds ₹20 Crores in the relevant previous year:

  1. Inter-Unit Transfers of Goods or Services [Sec 80-IA(8) equivalent]: Transfer of goods or services between an eligible business undertaking (enjoying tax holiday deductions) and any other non-eligible business carried on by the assessee, where consideration does not correspond to market value.
  2. Close Connection More Than Ordinary Profits [Sec 80-IA(10) equivalent]: Transactions between the assessee carrying on an eligible business and any other person having a close connection, resulting in more than ordinary profits to the eligible business.
  3. Concessional Tax Regime Inter-Company Transactions: Any business transacted between an assessee opting for concessional tax under Section 115BAB / 115BAE (15% rate) and any other person having close connection, or inter-unit transfers with Section 115BAA (22% rate) entities.
  4. Any other domestic transaction as may be prescribed by the CBDT.

Crucial Mechanics of the ₹20 Crore Threshold

Aggregate Transaction Value Applicability of Transfer Pricing Compliance Requirements
Aggregate SDT ≤ ₹20 Crores Exempt from TP Normal market value rules under tax holiday sections apply; full TP code does NOT apply. No TP Documentation under Sec 171, no Form 3CEB audit report under Sec 172 required for domestic transactions.
Aggregate SDT > ₹20 Crores Full TP Applies All provisions of Sections 161, 165 (ALP methods), 166 (TPO), 171, and 172 apply from rupee one! Mandatory arm's length determination using 5 statutory methods, TP study documentation, and Form 3CEB filing by 31st October.
Tested in TYK Question 11: Beta Ltd SDT Case Study

Beta Ltd has Unit A (enjoying Section 115BAB 15% rate) and Unit B (normal corporate rate). Unit B transfers components to Unit A for ₹25 Crores. Since the aggregate value exceeds ₹20 Crores, it is a Specified Domestic Transaction u/s 164. The Transfer Pricing Officer can determine ALP using Comparable Uncontrolled Price (CUP) or Cost Plus Method (CPM) under Section 165.

Sec 165

Determination of Arm's Length Price (ALP) & The 5+1 Statutory Methods

CUP, RPM, CPM, PSM, TNMM, Other Method, Range Concept (35th-65th Percentile) & 3% Tolerance Band
Core Valuation Code

Section 165 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (Corresponds to Sec 92C) Methods & Range

(1) Prescribed Methods for ALP Determination: The Arm's Length Price in relation to an international transaction or specified domestic transaction shall be determined by any of the following methods, being the Most Appropriate Method (MAM):

  1. Comparable Uncontrolled Price (CUP) Method;
  2. Resale Price Method (RPM);
  3. Cost Plus Method (CPM);
  4. Profit Split Method (PSM);
  5. Transactional Net Margin Method (TNMM);
  6. Other Method as prescribed by the CBDT (Rule 79).

(2) Selection of Most Appropriate Method: The MAM shall be selected having regard to the nature of transaction, class of transactions, functions performed, assets employed, risks assumed (FAR analysis), availability and reliability of comparable data, and degree of comparability.

(3) Multiple Year Data & Range Concept: Where more than one price is determined by the MAM:

  • If dataset comprises 6 or more entries (under CUP, RPM, CPM, TNMM), the Arm's Length Range from the 35th percentile to the 65th percentile is constructed. If actual price is within range, actual price is ALP; if outside, median (50th percentile) is ALP.
  • If dataset has less than 6 entries, or if PSM / Other Method is applied, ALP is the Arithmetic Mean. A tolerance band of ±3% (±1% for wholesale traders) applies.

Comparative Analysis of the 6 Statutory Methods

Method Operational Mechanism Best Suited For Key Economic Adjustments
1. CUP Method
(Comparable Uncontrolled Price)		 Compares the price charged or paid in an AE transaction with price charged/paid in a comparable uncontrolled transaction. Direct price-to-price comparison. Commodities, standardized goods, financial loans/interest rates, foreign currency borrowings. Most direct and reliable method when data is available. Differences in freight, insurance, volume/quantity discounts, credit period, warranty obligations, and currency risks.
2. Resale Price Method (RPM) Starts with price at which property purchased from AE is resold to an unrelated party. Deducts normal gross profit margin earned by comparable independent distributors. Distributors, resellers, wholesalers, and marketing entities who buy from AE and resell without adding substantial value or altering goods. Warranty costs, customs duty differences, advertising/marketing expenses assumed, and inventory holding costs.
3. Cost Plus Method (CPM) Determines direct and indirect costs of production incurred by supplier. Adds an arm's length gross profit mark-up earned by comparable uncontrolled producers. Contract manufacturers, toll manufacturers, captive service providers, BPO/call centers, engineering/technical services. Differences in technology support received, marketing risk assumption, working capital/credit terms, and volume commitments.
4. Profit Split Method (PSM) Evaluates combined operating profit of AEs arising from the transaction. Allocates combined profit based on relative economic contribution (FAR) of each AE. Integrated cross-border operations, joint development of unique valuable intangibles, or where both AEs contribute unique non-routine assets. Two-tier split: Routine profit allocated based on market benchmarks; residual profit split based on IP ownership and risk capital.
5. TNMM
(Transactional Net Margin Method)		 Examines net operating profit margin realized by tested party relative to an appropriate base (costs, sales, or assets), compared to margins of comparable companies. Software development centers, IT-enabled services, contract R&D, complex manufacturing where gross margins cannot be reliably benchmarked. Working capital adjustments, capacity utilization adjustments, depreciation method differences, risk adjustments.
6. Other Method
(Rule 79)		 Allows any method which takes into account price charged or paid, or would have been charged/paid, for same/similar transaction under comparable conditions. Valuation of shares, acquisition of business/IP, standard quotation prices, valuation by certified valuer or merchant banker. Discounted Cash Flow (DCF), Net Asset Value (NAV), quotes from commodity exchanges, third-party expert valuations.

Illustration 1: CUP Method (US Ltd. & IND Ltd. - Computer Monitors)

Question: US Ltd., a US company, has a wholly-owned subsidiary, IND Ltd. in India. US Ltd. sells computer monitors to IND Ltd. for resale in India. US Ltd. also sells computer monitors to CMI Ltd., another independent computer reseller in India.

US Ltd. sells 50,000 computer monitors to IND Ltd. at ₹11,000 per unit. The price fixed for CMI Ltd. is ₹10,000 per unit.
The warranty in case of sale of monitors to IND Ltd. is handled directly by IND Ltd. However, for sale of monitors to CMI Ltd., US Ltd. is responsible for warranty for 3 months. Both US Ltd. and IND Ltd. offer extended warranty at a standard market rate of ₹1,000 per annum.
On these facts, analyze how the assessment of IND Ltd. is affected under Transfer Pricing provisions.

Statutory Analysis & ALP Computation:

1. Associated Enterprises: US Ltd. and IND Ltd. are Associated Enterprises under Section 162(1)(a) (holding-subsidiary relationship).

2. Most Appropriate Method: Since US Ltd. sells identical computer monitors to unrelated buyer CMI Ltd., open market comparable uncontrolled price is readily available. Hence, CUP Method is the Most Appropriate Method under Section 165.

3. Warranty Cost Adjustment: The price of ₹10,000 charged to CMI Ltd. includes 3 months warranty provided by US Ltd. In contrast, IND Ltd. bears its own warranty costs. Therefore, the value of 3 months warranty must be subtracted from the CMI price to make it comparable to IND Ltd.'s purchase:
Value of 3-month warranty = \( ext{₹}1,000 imes rac{3}{12} = ext{₹}250 ext{ per unit}\).

Particulars Computation Amount (₹)
Price charged by US Ltd. to unrelated buyer CMI Ltd. Base Uncontrolled Price 10,000

Illustration 4: TNMM & 51% Loan Deemed AE (Andes Inc. & Andes Ltd.)

Question: Andes Inc., Malaysia advanced a loan of MD 1,60,000 to Andes Ltd., India. The book value of total assets of Andes Ltd. was ₹125 Lakhs.
Andes Ltd. provides software backup support to Andes Inc. Andes Ltd. spent 50,000 man-hours during T.Y. 2026-27 for these services. Cost for Andes Ltd. is MD 75 per man-hour. Andes Ltd. billed Andes Inc. at MD 90.75 per man-hour. (Exchange rate: 1 MD = ₹45).

• Direct costs: ₹600 Lakhs; Indirect costs: ₹200 Lakhs; Operating profit: ₹200 Lakhs.

Sec 170

Secondary Adjustments in Transfer Pricing

Primary vs Secondary Adjustments, 90-Day Repatriation Rule, Imputed Interest & 20.9664% Additional Tax Option
Repatriation / 20.9664% Tax

Section 170 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (Corresponds to Sec 92CE) Statutory Mechanics

(1) Concept of Primary vs. Secondary Adjustment:

  • Primary Adjustment: An upward adjustment to the taxable income of the assessee made under Section 161 (or by assessee in return / APA / Safe Harbour / MAP) to reflect the Arm's Length Price.
  • Secondary Adjustment: An adjustment in the books of account of the assessee and its AE to reflect that the actual allocation of profits between the assessee and its AE is consistent with the primary adjustment, so that the excess cash lying with the foreign AE is either brought back into India or taxed as an advance/dividend.

(2) Conditions for Mandatory Secondary Adjustment: Secondary adjustment applies where a primary adjustment to transfer price —

  1. has been made suo motu by the assessee in his return of income;
  2. made by the Assessing Officer has been accepted by the assessee;
  3. is determined by an Advance Pricing Agreement (APA) entered into under Section 168;
  4. is made as per the Safe Harbour Rules framed under Section 167; or
  5. is arising as a result of resolution under the Mutual Agreement Procedure (MAP) in an agreement under Section 159/160 (DTAA).

Statutory Exemption Threshold: No secondary adjustment is required if the primary adjustment made in the previous year does not exceed ₹1 Crore.

(3) Repatriation of Excess Money within 90 Days:

The "excess money" (or part thereof) lying with the foreign AE must be repatriated into India in convertible foreign exchange within 90 days from the specified date. If not repatriated within 90 days, it is deemed to be an advance made by the assessee to such AE, and interest is imputed annually:

Denomination of Transaction Imputed Interest Rate per Annum
Indian Rupee (INR) One-year Marginal Cost of Lending Rate (MCLR) of SBI as on 1st April + 3.25% (325 bps)
Foreign Currency 6-month LIBOR / SOFR (Secured Overnight Financing Rate) as on 30th September + 3.00% (300 bps)

(4) Option to Pay Additional Income-tax @ 20.9664% [Section 170(2A)]:

The assessee has an option to pay additional income-tax @ 18% plus 12% Surcharge plus 4% Health & Education Cess (Effective rate = 20.9664%) on the excess money not repatriated.
Consequences of paying 20.9664% tax:
• No further interest is charged from the date of payment of such tax.
• Assessee is not required to carry out secondary adjustment.
• No deduction or credit is allowable under any provision of the Act for this tax paid.

Sec 176

Transactions with Notified Jurisdictional Area (NJA)

Tax Sanctions against Uncooperative Tax Havens, 30% Minimum Withholding & Deemed AE Fiction
Tax Sanctions

Section 176 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (Corresponds to Sec 94A) NJA Code

The Central Government may notify any country or territory outside India having a lack of effective exchange of information as a Notified Jurisdictional Area (NJA). Once notified, severe anti-avoidance measures automatically trigger:

  1. Deemed International Transaction between AEs: Any transaction entered into by an assessee with a person located in an NJA is deemed to be an international transaction, and both parties are deemed to be Associated Enterprises (full TP code applies).
  2. Disallowance of Expenditure / Depreciation: No deduction in respect of any expenditure or depreciation shall be allowed unless the assessee maintains prescribed documents and authorizes the CBDT to inspect books of account.
  3. Deemed Cash Credit [Sec 68 equivalent]: Any sum credited from a person in an NJA is treated as unexplained income unless the assessee offers a satisfactory explanation regarding the source of funds in the hands of the payer.
  4. TDS at Highest Rate of 30% [Sec 176(5)]: Tax is deductible at source on all sums payable to persons in NJA at the highest of: rate specified in the Act, rate in force, or 30%! All lower treaty rates or concessional rates are completely overridden!

2. Determination of ALP under CUP Method: The consideration of ₹42 Lakhs charged by A Ltd. to independent party PQR Inc. serves as an internal CUP. Hence, the Arm's Length Price of the service is ₹42 Lakhs.

Sec 177

EBITDA Interest Cap

Section 177 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (Corresponds to Sec 94B) 30% EBITDA Rule

(1) Limitation on Interest Deduction: Where an Indian company, or a permanent establishment of a foreign company in India, being the borrower, incurs any expenditure by way of interest or similar consideration in respect of any debt issued by its non-resident Associated Enterprise (AE), and such interest exceeds ₹1 Crore —

Interest Disallowance Formula
$$ ext{Disallowance} = ext{Actual Interest Paid/Payable to AE} - [30\% ext{ of EBITDA}]$$ $$ ext{Maximum Deduction Allowed} = \min( ext{Actual Interest to AE}, 30\% ext{ of EBITDA})$$

(2) Deemed Debt from AE: Where debt is issued by an unrelated third-party lender, but an Associated Enterprise provides an implicit or explicit guarantee to the lender, or deposits a matching amount of funds with the lender, the debt is deemed to have been issued by an AE, triggering Section 177!

(3) Carry Forward of Disallowed Interest: The disallowed interest can be carried forward for up to 8 assessment years immediately succeeding the assessment year in which the disallowance was first made, and set off against business profits subject to the 30% EBITDA limit in each subsequent year.

(4) Statutory Carve-Outs: Section 177 does not apply to an Indian company or PE engaged in banking, insurance, a finance company in an IFSC, or a notified NBFC under Section 2(71).

TYK Question 4: X Ltd. & Yen Ltd. (Japan) — Deemed AE & Tripartite Deal

Yen Ltd. enters into an agreement with Tokyo Ltd. (Japan) to supply specialized robotic arms to X Ltd., where Yen Ltd. dictates the prices and delivery schedule.
Analyze the transfer pricing implications in the hands of X Ltd.

Analysis & Legal Implications:

  1. Associated Enterprise Status: X Ltd. and Yen Ltd. are Associated Enterprises under both Section 162(1)(a) (holding 55% voting power) and Section 162(1)(d) (power to appoint 7 out of 11 directors, being more than half).
  2. Deemed International Transaction [Section 163(2)]: Although the purchase agreement is executed between X Ltd. (India) and Tokyo Ltd. TYK Question 6: Legislative Objective of Safe Harbour & APAs

Question: What is the legislative objective of bringing into existence the provisions relating to Safe Harbour Rules (Section 167) and Advance Pricing Agreements (Section 168)?

Four Primary Legislative Objectives:

  • 1. Mitigation of Transfer Pricing Disputes: Indian transfer pricing historically saw intense litigation over comparability, margins, and valuation methods. APAs and Safe Harbours provide upfront legal certainty, eliminating audit disputes.
  • 2. Long-Term Certainty & Tax Planning: An APA guarantees pricing methodology for up to 9 years (5 prospective + 4 rollback years), allowing MNEs to make large capital investments in India without tax unpredictability.
  • 3. Administrative Resource Optimization: Relieves tax administration and judiciary from thousands of protracted TP assessments, allowing resources to focus on high-risk evasion.
  • 4. Alignment with International Best Practices & FDI Promotion: Enhances India's ease of doing business rankings and signals an investor-friendly, non-adversarial tax regime aligned with OECD standards.

TYK Question 7: XE Ltd. & Zilla Inc. — Arithmetic Mean & Tolerance Band

Question: XE Ltd., an Indian Company, sells 1,00,000 units of an electronic item to its US Associated Enterprise Zilla Inc. (which holds 28% shares in XE Ltd.) at USD 82 per unit.
XE Ltd. also sells the same product to unrelated buyers in the international market:

• Buyer B: 15,000 units @ USD 92 per unit
• Buyer C: 25,000 units @ USD 88 per unit
XE Ltd. is not a wholesale trader.
Compute the Arm's Length Price and examine whether any transfer pricing adjustment is warranted.

Step 1: Check Number of Comparables & Applicable Concept

The dataset contains only 3 comparable uncontrolled prices (USD 90, USD 92, USD 88). Since the dataset has less than 6 entries, the Range Concept does NOT apply. The Arithmetic Mean governs: $$\text{Arithmetic Mean (ALP)} = \frac{\text{USD } 90 + \text{USD } 92 + \text{USD } 88}{3} = \mathbf{\text{USD } 90\text{ per unit}}$$

Step 2: Test the 3% Tolerance Band

Actual contracted price charged to Zilla Inc. = USD 82 per unit.
Variation from ALP = \(\frac{\text{USD } 90 - \text{USD } 82}{\text{USD } 82} \times 100 = \mathbf{9.76\%}\).
Since 9.76% far exceeds the maximum statutory tolerance limit of 3%, no tolerance band benefit is available!

TYK Question 10: Master File & Country-by-Country Reporting (MNO Ltd.)

Question: MNO Ltd., Mumbai, is a subsidiary of ABC Inc., Country A.
The consolidated group revenue of ABC Inc. for the preceding accounting year was USD 900 Million (equivalent to ₹7,500 Crores).
MNO Ltd.'s international transactions with ABC Inc. during T.Y. 2026-27 amounted to ₹120 Crores.
Examine the reporting obligations of MNO Ltd. in India regarding Master File [Section 171] and Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) [Section 511].

1. Master File Obligations [Section 171 read with Rule 10DA]:

  • Applicability Threshold: Master File Part B is mandatory if consolidated group revenue > ₹500 Crores AND aggregate international transactions > ₹50 Crores.
  • Application to MNO Ltd.: Consolidated revenue is ₹7,500 Cr (> ₹500 Cr) and international transactions are ₹120 Cr (> ₹50 Cr). Both thresholds are exceeded.
  • Compliance: MNO Ltd. must furnish Form 3CEAA (Part A & Part B) on or before 30th November 2027.

2. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) [Section 511 read with Rule 10DB]:

  • Applicability Threshold: CbCR is mandatory if consolidated group revenue in the preceding accounting year exceeds ₹6,400 Crores. ABC Inc.'s revenue of ₹7,500 Crores clearly exceeds this limit.
  • Primary Reporting Entity: Since the Ultimate Parent Entity (UPE) ABC Inc. is resident in Country A, ABC Inc. will file CbCR in Country A.
  • MNO Ltd.'s Obligation: MNO Ltd. must furnish an intimation in Form 3CEAC at least 2 months prior to due date of CbCR, stating that ABC Inc. is filing CbCR in Country A.
  • Secondary Filing in India: MNO Ltd. would be required to file CbCR in India in Form 3CEAD only if Country A has no agreement with India for automatic exchange of CbCR, or has systemic failure.
