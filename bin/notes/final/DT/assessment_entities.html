1 Chapter Overview & Statutory Architecture of Entity Taxation

The Architecture of Entity Assessment under Income-tax Act, 2025 Chapter IX: Sec 200 to 207 & 309 to 329

Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, every taxpayer is classified into distinct categories of 'Persons' defined under Section 2(77) [legacy Section 2(31)]. The computation of total income, admissible business deductions, applicability of minimum alternative taxes, surcharge schedules, and concessional tax regimes vary fundamentally across different taxable entities:

Companies
Assessed under general provisions (25%/30%) vs. Concessional regimes u/s 200 (22%) or 201 (15%). Subject to Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT) u/s 206 @ 15% on Book Profits.
Firms & LLPs
Assessed at flat 30%. Remuneration and interest deductible subject to strict statutory ceilings u/s 35(e) [legacy 40(b)]. Subject to AMT u/s 227 @ 18.5%. Partner share exempt u/s 11(2).
Co-operatives
Assessed at slab rates (10%-30%) with profit deductions u/s 149 [legacy 80P] vs. Concessional regimes u/s 203 (22%) or 204 (15%). Exemption from AMT under concessional regimes.
AOPs, BOIs & Mutuals
Assessed at individual slabs or Maximum Marginal Rate (MMR) depending on share determinacy and member income. Mutuality doctrine governs social and recreation clubs.

2 Taxation of Companies: Definitions, Base Rates & Surcharge

Statutory Meaning of Company [Section 2(28) / Legacy Section 2(17)] Sec 2(28) • Sec 2(42) • Sec 2(53) • Sec 2(46)

Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, the statutory definition of a ‘Company’ is broader than under the Companies Act, 2013:

  • Indian Company [Section 2(53)]: A company formed and registered under the Companies Act, 2013 or any earlier law, having its registered office in India.
  • Domestic Company [Section 2(42)]: An Indian company, OR any other company which, in respect of its income liable to tax under this Act, has made the prescribed arrangements for the declaration and payment of dividends within India.
  • Foreign Company [Section 2(46)]: A company which is not a domestic company.
  • CBDT Declared Company: Any institution, association or body (whether Indian or non-Indian, incorporated or not) declared by general or special order of the CBDT to be a company for specified tax year(s).

3 Deemed Dividend u/s 2(40)(e) & Share Buy-Back Taxation

Deemed Dividend on Loans & Advances [Section 2(40)(e) / Legacy 2(22)(e)] Section 2(40)(e)

Any payment by a company, other than a company in which the public are substantially interested (closely held private/unlisted company), of any sum by way of advance or loan to:

  1. A shareholder who is the beneficial owner of shares holding ≥ 10% of the voting power; OR
  2. Any concern (firm, LLP, HUF, company, AOP/BOI) in which such shareholder is a partner/member and has a substantial interest (≥ 20% share of profits/voting power); OR
  3. Any payment by such company on behalf of, or for the individual benefit of, any such shareholder,

is deemed to be a dividend in the hands of the recipient, to the extent to which the company possesses accumulated profits (whether capitalized or not) up to the date of payment.

Statutory Exceptions (Sum NOT treated as Deemed Dividend):
• Ordinary Course of Moneylending Business: Any advance or loan made to a shareholder or concern in the ordinary course of its business, where the lending of money is a substantial part of the business of the company.
• Subsequent Dividend Adjustment: Where a loan or advance has been treated as a deemed dividend, and the company subsequently declares and pays regular dividend to that shareholder and adjusts the payment against the loan, the adjusted regular dividend is not taxed again.
• Trade Advances: Commercial trade advances given towards purchase of goods/services in normal commercial intercourse do not constitute loans or advances (*CIT v. Creative Dyeing & Printing P. Ltd.*).
Radical Paradigm Shift in Share Buy-Back Taxation [Finance Act, 2024 / 2026] Sec 2(40)(f) • Former Sec 115QA

Under the statutory amendments enacted by the Finance Act, the taxation regime for buy-back of shares by domestic companies has been fundamentally overhauled:

Parameter Legacy Regime (Pre-Amendment / Sec 115QA) Modern Statutory Regime (Income-tax Act, 2025)
Taxing Point Company paid Buyback Tax @ 20% + 12% surcharge + 4% HEC = 23.296% on distributed income u/s 115QA. Taxed in the hands of the Shareholder as deemed dividend under Section 2(40)(f). No company-level buy-back tax!
Shareholder Treatment Income was fully exempt in shareholder's hands under legacy Section 10(34A). Full consideration received on buyback is taxable as Dividend Income under Income from Other Sources (IFOS) at normal applicable tax rates!
Cost of Acquisition Cost of shares surrendered in buyback was absorbed and had no tax utility. Cost of acquisition of the shares bought back constitutes a Capital Loss (STCL or LTCL) in the hands of the shareholder, eligible for set-off against other capital gains!
TDS Obligation No TDS; company discharged special distribution tax. Company must deduct TDS @ 10% u/s 194 on dividend paid on buyback exceeding ₹5,000 threshold.

4 Special Concessional Corporate Regimes: Section 200 & Section 201

Section 200: Concessional 22% Regime for All Domestic Companies Section 200 • Legacy Section 115BAA

Section 200 provides an elective, non-revertible statutory tax regime conferring a flat base tax rate of 22% for ANY domestic company, subject to statutory conditions under Section 205:

  • Effective Tax Rate: 22% + 10% flat surcharge + 4% HEC = 25.168% (~25.17%). Surcharge is strictly 10% irrespective of whether total income is ₹10 Lakhs or ₹100 Crores!
  • Exemption from MAT: Provisions of Section 206 (Minimum Alternate Tax) DO NOT APPLY to a company opting for Section 200 [Section 206(6)].
  • MAT Credit Lapse: The company CANNOT set off unabsorbed MAT credit brought forward from earlier years against tax payable under Section 200. Any accumulated MAT credit effectively lapses upon exercising the option!
  • Filing Requirement: Option must be exercised electronically in Form No. 10-IC on or before the due date specified under Section 263(1) [legacy 139(1)] for furnishing the return of income. Once exercised for any tax year, it cannot be withdrawn for that or any subsequent year!
Section 201: Ultra-Concessional 15% Regime for New Manufacturing Companies Section 201 • Legacy Section 115BAB

Section 201 offers a globally competitive base tax rate of 15% for newly incorporated domestic manufacturing companies:

  • Incorporation & Commencement Window: Must be set up and registered on or after 01.10.2019 and commenced manufacture or production of any article or thing on or before 31.03.2024.
  • Effective Tax Rate: 15% + 10% flat surcharge + 4% HEC = 17.16%.
  • Strict Anti-Splitting Conditions:
    • Not formed by the splitting up, or reconstruction, of a business already in existence.
    • Does not use any machinery or plant previously used for any purpose. Exception: Old machinery up to 20% of the total value of plant and machinery is permitted. Second-hand imported machinery previously used outside India and never depreciated under the Indian Income-tax Act is treated as brand new.
    • Does not engage in any business other than manufacture or production, and research/distribution related thereto.
  • Non-Manufacturing Income Taxation:
    • Incidental short-term capital gains on non-depreciable assets: Taxed @ 22% (+ surcharge + HEC = 25.17%).
    • Profits from closely connected transactions exceeding arm's length (transfer pricing adjustment): Taxed @ 30% (+ surcharge + HEC).
  • Procedural Mandate: Form No. 10-ID must be furnished on or before Section 263(1) return filing due date.

5 Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT) on Companies [Section 206 / Legacy Section 115JB]

The Charging Mandate of Minimum Alternate Tax [Section 206(1)] Section 206(1) • Legacy Section 115JB

Notwithstanding anything contained in any other provision of this Act, where in the case of an assessee, being a company, the income-tax, payable on the total income computed under this Act in respect of any tax year is less than 15% of its book profit, such book profit shall be deemed to be the total income of the assessee and the tax payable by the assessee on such total income shall be the amount of income-tax at the rate of 15%:

Statutory Determination of Tax Payable by a Company
Total Tax Liability = HIGHER of:
• Regular Tax Payable: Tax on Total Income computed under normal statutory provisions of the Act (25% / 30% + Surcharge + 4% HEC)
• MAT Payable: 15% of Book Profit (9% for units in IFSC) + Applicable Corporate Surcharge + 4% HEC
Statutory Exclusions from MAT [Section 206(6)]:
• Domestic companies opting for concessional regimes under Section 200 [115BAA] or Section 201 [115BAB].
• Any company carrying on life insurance business referred to in Section 190 [legacy 115B].
• A foreign company having no permanent establishment (PE) or place of business in India, or whose income consists entirely of capital gains, royalties, or FTS taxable at gross rates under DTAA.
Statutory Computation of Book Profit [Section 206(1) / Legacy 115JB(2)] Mandatory Statutory Additions & Deductions

Starting Point: Net Profit as per Statement of Profit & Loss prepared in accordance with Schedule III to the Companies Act, 2013 (or regulatory Act for banking/insurance/power). The Assessing Officer has no power to reopen or rewrite accounts audited by statutory auditors unless they do not conform to Schedule III (*Apollo Tyres Ltd. v. CIT (SC)*).

Ind AS Adjustments & Transition Amount Framework [Section 206(1) Second Proviso] Ind AS 115JB(2A) Equivalent

For companies preparing financial statements under Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS), Book Profit is adjusted for Other Comprehensive Income (OCI) and the Transition Amount:

  1. OCI Items that will NEVER be reclassified to P&L:
    • Remeasurement of Defined Benefit Plans: Included in book profit in the tax year in which it is recognized.
    • Equity Investments measured at FVTOCI: Cumulative gain or loss is included in book profit in the tax year in which the asset is retired, disposed of, or realized.
  2. Transition Amount Adjustments (Convergence to Ind AS):
    • Transition amount represents the aggregate of amounts adjusted in other equity (excluding capital reserve and revaluation reserve) on the convergence date.
    • One-fifth (20%) of the transition amount is included or deducted from book profit in each of the 5 consecutive tax years beginning from the convergence year.
Section 207: MAT Credit Carry Forward & Utilization Mechanism Section 207 • Legacy Section 115JAA
Determination of MAT Credit Generated
MAT Credit Entitlement = Tax Payable under Section 206 (MAT) − Regular Tax Payable on Total Income under Normal Provisions

Rules of Carry Forward & Set-Off under Section 207:

  • 15-Year Carry Forward: MAT credit can be carried forward for a maximum period of 15 Assessment Years (Tax Years) immediately succeeding the tax year in which such credit is generated.
  • Condition for Set-Off: Set-off is permitted ONLY in a subsequent tax year where Regular Tax > MAT Payable.
  • Quantum of Set-Off: The maximum allowable set-off in any tax year is restricted to:
    Max Set-off = Regular Tax Payable − MAT Payable of that Tax Year
  • No Interest on Refund: No interest is payable to the assessee by the Income-tax department in respect of any MAT credit.
Joint CIT v. Rolta India Ltd. [2011] 330 ITR 470 (SC) Landmark Supreme Court Precedent

Legal Principle Established: Advance tax provisions under Chapter XVII-C apply to companies assessed on book profits under Section 206 / 115JB. Consequently, where a company liable to MAT defaults in payment of advance tax installments, interest under Sections 234B and 234C is mandatorily leviable on the shortfall of MAT liability!

6 Tonnage Tax Scheme for Shipping Companies [Section 64 / Legacy Chapter XII-G]

Special Taxation Regime for Qualifying Indian Shipping Companies Section 64 • Legacy Sections 115V to 115VZC

The Tonnage Tax Scheme is an elective regime substituting presumptive tonnage income for normal business profits of eligible shipping companies:

  • Qualifying Ship: A sea-going ship or vessel of ≥ 15 net tonnage registered under the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 with a valid certificate. Excludes fishing vessels, factory ships, pleasure crafts, and river ferries.
  • Tonnage Income Computation: Calculated per qualifying ship on a daily slab rate based on its net tonnage, multiplied by the number of days operated in the tax year:
Net Tonnage of Qualifying Ship Daily Presumptive Tonnage Income per 100 Tons
Up to 1,000 tons ₹70 for each 100 tons
Exceeding 1,000 tons but ≤ 10,000 tons ₹700 + ₹53 for each 100 tons exceeding 1,000 tons
Exceeding 10,000 tons but ≤ 25,000 tons ₹5,470 + ₹42 for each 100 tons exceeding 10,000 tons
Exceeding 25,000 tons ₹11,770 + ₹29 for each 100 tons exceeding 25,000 tons
Key Operational Safeguards:
• Mandatory transfer of at least 20% of book profits to a Tonnage Tax Reserve Account, utilized solely for acquiring new qualifying ships within 8 years.
• Minimum training requirement for cadet officers on board.
• MAT Immunity: Book profit derived from core shipping activities of a tonnage tax company is excluded from MAT under Section 206.

7 Alternate Minimum Tax (AMT) on Non-Corporate Assessees [Sections 227 to 232 / Legacy 115JC to 115JF]

Statutory Scope & Liability of Alternate Minimum Tax [Section 227] Sec 227 • Sec 206(2) • Legacy Sec 115JC

Where the regular income-tax payable for a tax year by a person other than a company is less than the Alternate Minimum Tax (AMT), the Adjusted Total Income (ATI) shall be deemed to be the total income of that person, and he shall be liable to pay tax at the rate of 18.5%:

Determination of Alternate Minimum Tax Liability
Total Tax Payable by Non-Corporate Assessee = HIGHER of:
• Regular Tax Payable: Tax computed on Total Income under normal provisions of the Act
• AMT Payable: 18.5% of Adjusted Total Income (ATI) [9% for IFSC units] + Applicable Surcharge + 4% HEC
Assessee Category Applicability Threshold Status under Default / Concessional Regimes
Partnership Firms & LLPs No Threshold Limit!
Applies to ALL firms/LLPs claiming specified deductions, regardless of income level.		 Firms and LLPs have no concessional regime equivalent to Sec 200/202; AMT always applies if specified deductions claimed.
Individuals, HUFs, AOPs, BOIs & AJPs Applies ONLY IF Adjusted Total Income (ATI) > ₹20 Lakhs.
If ATI ≤ ₹20 Lakhs, AMT is totally inapplicable!		 EXEMPT: Does NOT apply to assessees paying tax under the Section 202(1) Default Tax Regime [Section 227(4)].
Co-operative Societies Subject to AMT under normal provisions. EXEMPT: Does NOT apply to co-operatives opting for Section 203 (22%) or Section 204 (15%).
Mathematical Computation of Adjusted Total Income (ATI) [Section 228 / Legacy 115JC(2)] Section 228 Formula
The Four-Step Adjusted Total Income (ATI) Algorithm
Adjusted Total Income (ATI) =
    Total Income computed under normal provisions of the Act
(+) Add: Deductions claimed under Chapter VIII Part C [Sections 138 to 152 / legacy 80-IA to 80RRB]
          [Note: Section 146 (80JJAA - New Employment) deduction is NOT added back!]

Audit Report Requirement [Section 230]: Every assessee liable to pay AMT must obtain a report from a Chartered Accountant in Form No. 29C certifying the computation of Adjusted Total Income and AMT, furnished electronically on or before the Section 263(1) return filing due date.
Section 231: AMT Credit Carry Forward & Utilization Mechanism Section 231 • Legacy Section 115JD
Determination of AMT Credit Entitlement
AMT Credit Generated = Alternate Minimum Tax Payable u/s 227 − Regular Tax Payable on Total Income

Statutory Rules of Set-Off & Carry Forward:

  • 15-Year Carry Forward: AMT credit is carried forward for up to 15 Assessment Years (Tax Years).
  • Permitted Set-Off: Allowed in a subsequent year only when Regular Tax > AMT. Maximum set-off is restricted to the excess of regular tax over AMT for that year.
  • Interaction with Section 202(1) Default Tax Regime: Where an assessee opts for the default regime u/s 202(1) in a future tax year, brought forward AMT credit cannot be set off against tax payable under Section 202(1). However, the credit does not perish — it remains alive and can be utilized in any subsequent year (within the 15-year window) if the assessee opts back into the normal tax regime!

8 Assessment of Partnership Firms & LLPs: General Scheme & Partner Taxability

Statutory Assessment of Firms as Separate Entities Flat 30% Base Rate • Sec 11(2)

Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, a partnership firm (including a Limited Liability Partnership — LLP) is assessed as a distinct taxable entity separate from its partners:

  • Tax Rates: Income of the firm is charged at a flat rate of 30%.
  • Surcharge: Surcharge is levied @ 12% if total income exceeds ₹1 Crore (subject to statutory marginal relief).
  • Health & Education Cess: 4% on tax plus surcharge.
  • Effective Tax Rate: 31.20% (income ≤ ₹1 Cr) or 34.944% (income > ₹1 Cr).
  • Exemption of Partner's Share of Profit [Section 11(2) / Legacy 10(2A)]: The share of profit received by a partner from the total income of the firm is completely exempt from tax in the hands of the partner, preventing double taxation of corporate/firm profits.
Section 35(e): Statutory Conditions for Partner Remuneration & Interest Section 35(e) • Legacy Section 40(b)

Remuneration (salary, bonus, commission) and interest paid by a firm to its partners are deductible in computing business profits ONLY IF all five statutory conditions under Section 35(e) are fulfilled:

Condition 1: Working Partner Only
Remuneration must be payable ONLY to an individual who is actively engaged in conducting the business/profession of the firm. Remuneration paid to non-working (sleeping) partners is strictly 100% disallowed.
Condition 2: Partnership Deed Authorization
Payment of remuneration and interest must be expressly authorized by and in accordance with the terms of the valid partnership deed.
Condition 3: Prospective Application Only
Remuneration and interest must relate to a period after the date of the partnership deed. No retrospective effect or clause can be given tax recognition!
Condition 4: Maximum 12% Interest Cap
Simple interest on capital or current account balances cannot exceed 12% per annum. Any excess interest over 12% is disallowed.
Section 35(e)(iii) Working Partner Remuneration Limit [Amended by Finance Act]
Maximum Allowable Remuneration to all working partners combined:
• On the first ₹6,00,000 of Book Profit (or in case of a Loss):
    ₹3,00,000 OR 90% of Book Profit, whichever is MORE
• On the balance of Book Profit (exceeding ₹6,00,000):
    60% of Book Profit
[Legacy comparison: Formerly ₹1,50,000 or 90% on first ₹3,00,000; 60% on balance].

Steps to Compute ‘Book Profit’ for Section 35(e):

  1. Start with Net Profit as per Statement of Profit and Loss.
  2. Exclude non-business incomes: Deduct Capital Gains, Income from House Property, and Income from Other Sources (Interest on refund, dividend).
  3. Add back disallowed interest: Add partner interest in excess of 12% p.a. or interest not authorized by deed.
  4. Add back partners' remuneration: Add back all salary, bonus, commission paid to partners debited to P&L.
  5. Depreciation: Allow current year depreciation and unabsorbed depreciation under Section 33.
  6. Brought forward loss restriction: Do NOT deduct brought forward business loss!
  7. Chapter VIII restriction: Do NOT deduct Chapter VIII deductions.
Taxability in Partner's Hands [Section 26(2)(d) / Legacy 28(v)]: Remuneration and interest allowed as a deduction to the firm are assessed as PGBP income in the hands of the partner. Any portion disallowed to the firm under Section 35(e) is NOT taxed in the partner's hands, preventing economic double taxation.

11 The Doctrine of Mutuality & Mutual Concerns

The Cardinal Common Law Principle of Mutuality Judicial Doctrine

The doctrine of mutuality is founded on the fundamental proposition that no person can trade with himself and generate a taxable profit out of himself. Three cumulative judicial tests must be satisfied:

  1. Identity Test: Complete identity between the contributors to the common fund and the participators entitled to the surplus.
  2. Mutual Mandate: The transactions must be in furtherance of the mutual objective of the association (not commercial trading with third parties).
  3. Impossibility of Profits: The fund must be returned to the contributors or held for their mutual benefit upon dissolution.
Secunderabad Club v. CIT [2023] 456 ITR 472 (SC) Landmark Supreme Court Full Bench Ruling

Facts & Dispute: Social and recreation clubs deposited surplus funds in fixed deposits with banks that were also corporate members of the club. The clubs claimed that interest earned from corporate member banks was exempt under the doctrine of mutuality.
Supreme Court Judgment: The Supreme Court firmly held that interest earned on fixed deposits with banks (even if such banks are members of the club) is NOT covered by the doctrine of mutuality and is FULLY TAXABLE as Income from Other Sources! The relationship between the club and the bank is purely that of a depositor and a debtor. There is no mutual identity in a commercial banking deposit.

ITO v. Venkatesh Premises Co-operative Society Ltd. [2018] 402 ITR 670 (SC) Supreme Court Precedent on Housing Societies

Legal Principle Established: Transfer fees, non-occupancy charges, and premium received by a co-operative housing society from incoming or outgoing members are utilized exclusively for the maintenance and improvement of common property for members. Consequently, transfer fees and non-occupancy charges satisfy all mutuality tests and are completely EXEMPT from income-tax!
