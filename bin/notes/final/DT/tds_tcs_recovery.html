Deduction, Collection and Recovery of Tax (TDS, TCS, Advance Tax, Recovery & Refunds)

An encyclopedic CA Final master compendium covering the entire tax collection and withholding machinery: general principles (Sections 390 & 391), comprehensive TDS tables under Section 393(1) (Salaries, EPF, Interest, Dividends, Online Gaming, Contractors, Rent, Professional Fees, Cash Withdrawals, Benefits/Perks u/s 194R, and Partner Remuneration u/s 194T), non-resident withholding u/s 394, comprehensive TCS on goods, LRS remittances, and motor vehicles u/s 398, lower deduction certificates u/s 395, quarterly compliance and penalties for default (Sections 398 & 401), Advance Tax estimation and interest (Sections 403 to 425), Tax Recovery Officer (TRO) powers, and Refunds with 0.5% interest under Section 437.

Overview & Architecture of Tax Deduction & Collection Machinery
Chapter 13 Architecture

The Indian income-tax collection system operates on the statutory doctrine of "Pay As You Earn" (PAYE). Under this scheme, taxes are collected at the earliest possible stage—either when the income accrues or arises, or at the point of receipt—rather than waiting for the end of the assessment year. The tax withholding and recovery architecture is governed by Chapters XIX-B, XIX-C, and XVII of the Income-tax Act, consolidated and modernized under the Income-tax Act, 2025 and Finance Act, 2026.

Structural Consolidation under Income-tax Act, 2025

Under the pre-2025 regime, tax deduction was fragmented across more than 35 disparate sections (Sections 192 through 194T). The Income-tax Act, 2025 systematically restructures this landscape into two core withholding anchors:

  • Section 392: Comprehensive withholding code for Salaries, Perquisites, and Accumulated Recognized Provident Fund (EPF) balances.
  • Section 393: The Unified Statutory Table covering all payments to residents, non-residents, and any person (interest, dividends, contracts, rent, professional fees, e-commerce, VDAs, partner remunerations).
General Statutory Principles: Sections 390 & 391
Statutory Base
1

Deduction or Collection at Source & Advance Payment [Section 390]

Section 390(1) establishes that notwithstanding the regular charge of income-tax under Section 4(1), tax on any income shall be deductible or collectible at source or payable by way of advance payment in accordance with the provisions of this Chapter.

Section 390(2) clarifies that the deduction, collection, or advance payment of tax shall be made without prejudice to any other mode of recovery. The payer is merely a collection agent of the State; the primary liability of the payee to be assessed on their total income remains intact.

2

Direct Payment by Assessee where No TDS is Deducted [Section 391]

Where tax is deductible at source under any provision of this Act, but the payer fails to deduct the whole or any part of the tax, or having deducted fails to remit it, the income-tax remains directly payable by the assessee (payee).

Landmark Statutory Shield: Interplay between Sec 391 and Sec 401(4)

While Section 391 permits the Revenue to recover tax directly from the payee if tax was never deducted, Section 401(4) strictly bars the Revenue from making any direct demand upon the payee to the extent tax has already been deducted from their income by the payer. Once tax is withheld from the payment, the payee cannot be coerced to pay it again, even if the deductor subsequently defaults in remitting it to the Central Government. The Revenue's sole remedy in such cases lies against the defaulting deductor as an assessee-in-default.

Deduction of Tax at Source from Salary & Accumulated PF [Section 392]
Section 392

Section 392(1) requires any person responsible for paying any income chargeable under the head ‘Salaries’ to deduct income-tax at the time of payment on the estimated total income of the employee at the average rate of income-tax computed on the basis of the rates in force for the tax year.

Average Rate
Computation Mechanism

Average Rate = (Total Estimated Tax on Salary + Surcharge + 4% HEC) / Total Estimated Income. The resulting monthly deduction is adjusted for any excess or deficiency in previous months.

Point of Deduction
Actual Payment Basis

Unlike other commercial TDS provisions that trigger on "credit or payment, whichever is earlier", TDS on salary triggers strictly at the time of actual payment (Section 392(1)).

1. Default Concessional Regime vs. Normal Provisions Choice [Section 392(1) Proviso]

Under the Income-tax Act, the default regime for individuals is the concessional tax regime under Section 115BAC. Under CBDT Circular instructions:

  • The employer must seek an intimation from each employee regarding their intended tax regime (Default Sec 115BAC vs. Opted Normal Regime).
  • If the employee does not make an intimation, the employer must deduct TDS under the default regime of Section 115BAC.
  • The employee is free to change their choice at the time of filing their Return of Income under Section 139(1).

2. Set-off of Losses & Consideration of Other Incomes [Section 392(2)]

The employee may declare any other income (interest, dividend, house property) and tax deducted thereon to the employer. Upon receiving such declaration:

3. Accumulated Balance of Recognized Provident Fund (EPF) [Section 392(3)]

Premature EPF Withdrawal Rules

Where accumulated balance in a Recognized Provident Fund becomes taxable because the employee has not rendered continuous service of 5 years (and the termination is not due to ill-health, contraction of employer's business, etc.):

  • Threshold Limit: Tax is deductible only if the payment is ₹50,000 or more.
  • Rate of TDS: 10% of the taxable component.
  • Non-furnishing of PAN: If PAN is not furnished, tax is deductible at the Maximum Marginal Rate (MMR), not the flat 20% under Section 396.

4. Multiple Employers & Relief under Section 89 [Section 392(4) & (5)]

  • Multiple Employers [Section 392(5)]: Where an employee is employed by two or more employers during the tax year, or has changed jobs, they may furnish details of salary and TDS from the other employer(s) in Form 12B to one employer of their choice, who will aggregate both incomes and deduct TDS accordingly.
  • Arrears of Salary [Section 392(4)]: The employer is statutorily authorized to grant relief under Section 89 upon receiving the required particulars in Form 10E from the employee.

TDS on Winnings: Lotteries, Horse Races & Online Gaming [Table Sl. 6 & 7]
Section 393(1) & (3)

Winnings from betting, gambling, lotteries, and modern online gaming platforms are subjected to the highest flat withholding tax rate of 30% under Section 393.

Sl. 8(i) • 30% Flat
Lotteries, Crossword Puzzles, Races

Applies where winnings exceed ₹10,000 in a transaction. No basic exemption limit or deductions allowed.

Sl. 8(ii) • 30% Flat
Online Gaming (Net Winnings)

TDS triggers on net winnings in user account. Zero threshold limit—taxable on every rupee of net winnings.

1. Winnings Wholly or Partly in Kind [Proviso to Sl. 8(i)]

Where winnings are wholly in kind (e.g., winning a luxury car or apartment), or partly in cash and partly in kind but the cash component is insufficient to satisfy the 30% tax liability:

Statutory Obligation of the Payer

The person responsible for paying must not release the prize until they ensure that the tax of 30% has been paid by the winner. The payer may either:

  1. Collect the 30% tax amount in cash/cheque from the winner and deposit it into the Government treasury; OR
  2. Verify the advance tax payment challan furnished by the winner proving that 30% tax on the market value of the prize has been duly deposited.

2. Online Gaming Mechanism [Sl. 8(ii) & Rule 133]

For online games, tax is deducted on the net winnings in the user account at:

  • Time of Withdrawal: Tax is deducted @ 30% on the net winnings comprised in the withdrawal amount.
  • End of Tax Year (31st March): Tax is deducted @ 30% on the remaining net winnings in the user account balance.
  • Net Winnings Formula (Rule 133):
    Net Winnings = (Total Withdrawals + Closing Balance) - (Total Deposits + Opening Balance)
  • No Threshold: Unlike lotteries (₹10,000 threshold), online gaming has no basic threshold; even a net winning of ₹100 attracts 30% TDS.

1. Comprehensive Statutory Definition of "Work" [Section 402(47)]

Under Section 402(47), "Work" expressly includes:

  • Advertising: Client payments to advertising agencies (Circular 715). However, payment by advertising agency to media companies (TV/print) for broadcasting space is subject to Circular 5/2016.
  • Broadcasting & Telecasting: Including production of programmes for such broadcasting or telecasting.
  • Carriage of Goods or Passengers: By any mode of transport other than railways.
  • Catering Services: Contractual provision of food and hospitality.
  • Job Work / Contract Manufacturing: Manufacturing or supplying a product according to the requirements or specifications of a customer by using material purchased from such customer or its associate.
Distinction: Contract for "Sale of Goods" vs. "Job Work" (Contract for Work)

As clarified in Section 402(47) and landmark judicial rulings (Bharathi Cements Ltd.):

  • Work (TDS u/s 393(1) Sl. 6(i)): Where the contractor manufactures goods using raw materials supplied/purchased from the customer or an associate of the customer. TDS applies on the invoice value of the contract (or excluding raw material cost if invoice is segregated).
  • Sale of Goods (No TDS u/s 393(1) Sl. 6(i)): Where the manufacturer produces custom goods according to customer specifications but purchases the raw materials from unrelated third parties. This remains a contract of sale; Section 393(1) Sl. 6(i) does not apply (subject only to Sec 398 TCS / Sec 194Q purchase TDS if applicable).

2. Transporter Exemption [Section 393(4) Table]

Relief for Small Goods Transport Operators

No tax is deductible under Sl. 6(i) on any sum credited or paid to a contractor in the business of plying, hiring, or leasing goods carriages, provided:

  1. The contractor owns not more than 10 goods carriages at any time during the tax year; AND
  2. The contractor furnishes a written declaration to that effect along with their Permanent Account Number (PAN) to the payer.

3. Payments by Individuals & HUFs Not Liable for Tax Audit [Sl. 6(ii)]

Individuals and HUFs who are not covered under business/profession tax audit are exempt from deducting TDS under Sl. 6(i). However, under Section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 6(ii)] (replaces Section 194M):

  • If an Individual or HUF pays any sum to a resident contractor, professional, or agent exceeding ₹50,00,000 in aggregate in a tax year for personal or business purposes;
  • Tax must be deducted @ 2% on the entire amount;
  • No TAN is required—compliance is executed via challan-cum-statement in Form 26QD using PAN.

Landmark Supreme Court Ruling: Principal-to-Principal Trade Discounts

In Bharti Cellular Ltd. / Idea Cellular, the Supreme Court ruled that where telecom operators sell SIM cards and recharge vouchers to independent distributors at a discounted price on a principal-to-principal basis (where title and risk pass to the distributor), the margin earned by the distributor is not "commission or brokerage" under Section 402(7). Consequently, no TDS is deductible under Sl. 1(ii).

1. Rent Paid by Specified Persons [Table Sl. 2(ii)]

  • Applicability: Any person, other than an individual or HUF (or an individual/HUF liable for tax audit) paying rent exceeding ₹2,40,000 per annum.
  • Separate Rates:
    • 2% for use of any machinery, plant, or equipment.
    • 10% for use of any land or building (including factory building) or land appurtenant thereto or furniture or fittings.
  • Municipal Taxes: TDS is deductible on the rent inclusive of municipal taxes if borne by the tenant, but exclusive of GST if GST is separately shown on the invoice (Circular 23/2017).
  • Cold Storage Facility: Customers paying charges to cold storage owners are paying for the provision of a cooling preservation service, not rent of building. Covered under Sl. 6(i) (Contractor @ 1%/2%), NOT under Sl. 2(ii) (Circular 1/2008).
  • Airport Landing & Parking Charges: Payment of landing and parking charges by commercial airlines to the Airports Authority of India is payment for the use of land/runway, falling under Sl. 2(ii) (Rent @ 10%) as settled by the Supreme Court in Japan Airlines Co. Ltd.

2. Rent Paid by Non-Audit Individuals & HUFs [Table Sl. 2(i)]

Simplified Tenancy Withholding

Where an individual or HUF (not subject to business tax audit) pays rent to a resident landlord exceeding ₹50,000 for a month or part of a month:

  • Rate of TDS: 2% (amended under ITA 2025).
  • Point of Deduction: Deducted once a year: either at the time of credit/payment for the last month of the tax year (March) or the last month of the tenancy (if vacated earlier).
  • Ceiling Safeguard: Where the landlord does not furnish PAN, tax is deductible @ 20% under Section 396; however, the total TDS cannot exceed the rent payable for that last month.
  • No TAN Requirement: The tenant uses PAN to file Form 26QC challan-cum-statement within 30 days from the end of the month of deduction.

3. Transfer of Immovable Property [Table Sl. 3(i)]

Any transferee (buyer) responsible for paying any sum to a resident transferor (seller) by way of consideration for transfer of any immovable property (other than agricultural land):

  • Threshold: Aggregate consideration or Stamp Duty Value (SDV) ≥ ₹50,00,000.
  • Rate of Tax: 1% on the higher of actual consideration or SDV of such property.
  • Clubbing of Incidental Charges: Consideration statutorily includes all charges incidental to transfer (car parking, electricity/water amenity fees, club membership fees, maintenance advances).
  • Multiple Buyers or Sellers: By virtue of the Finance Act amendment, where there are multiple buyers or multiple sellers for a single property, the ₹50,00,000 threshold applies to the aggregate consideration/SDV of the property, not on an individual co-owner basis.
  • Compliance: Form 26QB challan-cum-statement within 30 days from end of the month; buyer does not require TAN.

4. JDA Monetary Consideration & Compulsory Acquisition [Sl. 3(ii) & 3(iii)]

  • Joint Development Agreements [Sl. 3(ii)]: Any developer paying any monetary consideration (cash/cheque) to an individual or HUF landowner under a registered JDA referred to in Section 45(5A) must deduct tax @ 10% at the time of credit or payment.
  • Compulsory Acquisition [Sl. 3(iii)]: Any authority paying compensation or enhanced compensation for compulsory acquisition of immovable property (other than agricultural land) must deduct tax @ 10% where aggregate payment in a tax year exceeds ₹5,00,000.

1. Threshold Limits & Category-wise Aggregation

  • Separate ₹30,000 Threshold: The statutory threshold limit of ₹30,000 per annum applies independently to each of the four distinct statutory categories:
    (a) Fees for professional services,
    (b) Fees for technical services,
    (c) Royalty, and
    (d) Non-compete fees under Section 28(va).
    Example: If a company pays ₹25,000 as legal fees and ₹25,000 as technical fees to the same entity during a tax year, no TDS is deductible under either category since neither exceeds ₹30,000.
  • Zero Threshold for Director Remuneration: Payments of sitting fees, commission, or any other remuneration to a director (not being taxable as salary u/s 192/392) attract TDS @ 10% with NO threshold (even ₹1 is subject to TDS).

2. TPA Payments to Private Hospitals (Circular 8/2009 & Notification 88/2008)

Third Party Administrators (TPAs) Withholding Obligation

Under CBDT Notification No. 88/2008 and Circular No. 8/2009, payments made by Third Party Administrators (TPAs) on behalf of health insurance companies to private hospitals for medical services rendered to policyholders fall strictly within the ambit of Fees for Professional Services. TPAs are statutorily required to deduct tax at source @ 10% under Sl. 6(iii) on the gross payment made to hospitals.

3. Broadcasting / Telecasting Rights vs. Content Production (Circular 22/2016)

Where a television channel pays a production house:

  • Content produced to channel's specifications (Job Work): If the broadcaster retains the copyright, the transaction is a contract for "work" governed by Sl. 6(i) (Contractor @ 1%/2%).
  • Acquisition of Broadcasting / Telecasting Rights of pre-existing content: Where the copyright already exists with the producer and only telecasting rights are acquired, the payment represents Royalty / Professional Service governed by Sl. 6(iii) @ 2% or 10%.

TDS on Cash Withdrawals, E-Commerce Operators, Business Perks & VDAs
Anti-Evasion & Digital Codes

1. TDS on Cash Withdrawals from Banks & Post Offices [Table Sl. 8(xxii) / Old Sec 194N]

To discourage large cash transactions and foster a digital economy, every banking company, co-operative society engaged in banking, or post office must deduct tax at source on aggregate cash withdrawals:

2%
Regular Income-Tax Return Filers

Applies where aggregate cash withdrawals from all accounts maintained by the person with the banking institution exceed ₹1 Crore in a tax year. TDS @ 2% is deducted strictly on the amount exceeding ₹1 Crore.

2% & 5% Deterrent Rates
Non-Filers (Defaulting Taxpayers)

For payees who have not filed returns for all 3 preceding assessment years for which due date has expired:
• 2% on cash withdrawals exceeding ₹20 Lakhs up to ₹1 Crore.
• 5% on cash withdrawals exceeding ₹1 Crore.

Statutory Exemptions from Cash TDS

No tax is deductible under Sl. 8(xxii) on cash withdrawals made by:

  • The Government or Reserve Bank of India;
  • Any banking company, co-operative bank, or post office;
  • Business correspondents of banking companies or white-label ATM operators;
  • Authorised dealers and money changers licensed by RBI (Notification 70/2019);
  • Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) commission agents/traders (Notification 70/2019).

2. TDS on E-Commerce Platforms [Table Sl. 8(v) / Old Sec 194-O]

Every e-commerce operator facilitating the sale of goods or provision of services through its digital or electronic facility must deduct tax at source @ 0.1% on the gross amount of such sales or services at the time of credit or payment.

  • Deemed Credit: Any payment made directly by a purchaser of goods or recipient of services to an e-commerce participant is deemed to be an amount credited or paid by the operator to the participant.
  • Individual / HUF Participant Exemption: No TDS is deductible if the e-commerce participant is an Individual or HUF whose gross sales/services through the platform do not exceed ₹5,00,000 during the tax year and they have furnished their PAN/Aadhaar.
  • Interplay with Sec 194Q & Sec 206C(1H): Transactions subject to TDS under Sl. 8(v) (Sec 194-O) are expressly exempt from buyer TDS under Sec 194Q and seller TCS under Section 398(1) (Sec 206C(1H)). E-commerce withholding holds supreme statutory priority.

3. TDS on Business Benefits or Perquisites [Table Sl. 8(xxiii) / Old Sec 194R]

Any person responsible for providing to a resident any benefit or perquisite, whether convertible into money or not, arising from business or the exercise of a profession by such resident, must deduct tax at source @ 10% of the value or aggregate of value of such benefit/perquisite.

  • Threshold: Value or aggregate of value of benefits exceeds ₹20,000 during the tax year.
  • Non-applicability: Does not apply to an Individual or HUF whose business turnover does not exceed ₹1 Crore or profession turnover does not exceed ₹50 Lakhs in the preceding tax year.
  • Benefit in Kind (CBDT Circular 12/2022 & 18/2022): Where the benefit is wholly in kind, or partly in cash and partly in kind but the cash is insufficient, the provider must ensure tax has been paid before releasing the benefit (by receiving cash/cheque from the recipient or verifying their advance tax challan).
  • Social Media Influencers: If products (e.g. smartphones, cosmetics) are given to an influencer to review and are retained by them, it constitutes a perquisite subject to 10% TDS. If returned to the manufacturing company after review, it is not a perquisite.
  • Dealer Conferences: Expenses on dealer conferences held for business education/training, product launches, or dealer feedback are exempt from 194R; however, leisure trips, family accompaniment, or overstay days attract 10% TDS.

4. TDS on Virtual Digital Assets (VDAs) [Table Sl. 8(vi) / Old Sec 194S]

Any person responsible for paying to a resident any sum by way of consideration for transfer of a Virtual Digital Asset (crypto, NFT) must deduct tax @ 1% of such sum.

  • Thresholds:
    • ₹50,000 per tax year where the payer is a Specified Person (Individual/HUF without business income or with turnover ≤ ₹1 Cr business / ₹50L profession).
    • ₹10,000 for all other payers.
  • Crypto-for-Crypto Exchanges: Where a VDA is exchanged for another VDA, both parties are transferors and transferees. Both parties must deduct 1% TDS, or the crypto exchange facilitates withholding as clarified in CBDT Circular No. 13/2022.
Partner Remuneration & Interest, Non-Resident TDS & Deemed Income [Sections 393, 394 & 396]
Sections 393(1), 394 & 396

1. TDS on Salary, Remuneration, Bonus & Interest to Partners [Table Sl. 8(xxiv) / Sec 194T]

Landmark Reform: Withholding on Firm Payments to Partners

Historically, payments made by a partnership firm (or LLP) to its partners were completely outside the scope of TDS. Under Section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 8(xxiv)] (Section 194T):

  • Covered Payments: Any salary, remuneration, commission, bonus, or interest paid or credited to the account of a partner (including capital account or current account).
  • Rate of TDS: 10% (or 2% for specified categories) on the entire sum.
  • Statutory Threshold: Applies where the amount or aggregate of amounts of such payment/credit exceeds ₹20,000 during the tax year.
  • Point of Deduction: At the time of credit of such sum to the account of the partner or at the time of payment in cash/cheque/draft, whichever is earlier.

2. Withholding on Payments to Non-Residents [Section 394]

Under Section 394 (replaces Section 195 of the 1961 Act), any person responsible for paying to a non-resident or a foreign company any interest or any other sum chargeable under the provisions of this Act (not being salary) must deduct tax at source at the rates in force or at the Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) rate, whichever is more beneficial to the assessee under Section 90(2).

  • Chargeability Test (GE India Technology Centre): Deduction of tax is mandatory only if the payment contains an element of income chargeable to tax in India. If the sum is not chargeable to tax in India, no TDS is deductible.
  • Lower / Nil Withholding Application: Either the payer or the non-resident payee may apply to the Assessing Officer for a determination of the chargeable proportion or a certificate of lower/nil withholding under Section 395.
  • Compliance via Form 15CA & 15CB: Remittance information must be furnished electronically in Form 15CA (with Form 15CB certificate from an independent Chartered Accountant where remittance exceeds ₹5 Lakhs and is chargeable to tax).

3. Tax Deducted Deemed to be Income Received [Section 396]

Under Section 396, all sums deducted in accordance with the provisions of this Chapter shall, for the purpose of computing the income of an assessee, be deemed to be income received by the payee.

  • Grossing Up Principle: If a contractor receives ₹98,000 net of 2% TDS (₹2,000), their gross taxable income is ₹1,00,000.
  • Statutory Exceptions: Section 396 expressly excludes:
    • Tax paid by an employer under Section 392(2)(a) on non-monetary perquisites provided to employees (exempt under Section 10(10CC) / Schedule III).
    • Tax deducted on cash withdrawals under Section 393(3) [Table: Sl. No. 8(xxii) / Sec 194N] (since cash withdrawn is the assessee's own capital, not income).

4. Higher Rates of TDS for Non-Furnishing of PAN [Section 397(2)]

Mandatory Furnishing of PAN

Under Section 397(2) (replaces Section 206AA), any person entitled to receive any sum or income on which tax is deductible must furnish their Permanent Account Number (PAN) to the deductor. In the event of failure to furnish PAN:

  • Tax shall be deducted at the highest of the following three rates:
    (i) At the rate specified in the relevant provision of this Act;
    (ii) At the rate or rates in force; OR
    (iii) At the rate of 20%.
  • Concessional 5% Cap for Specified Sections: For payments under Section 194-O (e-commerce) and Section 194Q (purchase of goods), the higher rate for non-furnishing of PAN is capped at 5% instead of 20%.
  • Declaration Invalid: Any lower deduction certificate or Form 15G/15H declaration furnished without a valid PAN is statutorily invalid and void ab initio.

Illustration 1: Leave Travel Facility (LTC) & Employer Verification Duty [Section 392]

Problem Statement:

LL Limited paid leave travel facility to its employees and considered an exemption in respect of Leave Travel Concession (LTC) under Schedule III (Table: Sl. No. 8), based on the self-declaration furnished by the employees, who have exercised option to opt out of the default tax regime. The Assessing Officer held that the company as an employer ought to have verified the genuineness of the claim of exemption by obtaining from them, the proof of actual expenditure incurred by availing LTC. Accordingly, the Assessing Officer treated the assessee company as an assessee in default. Decide the correctness of action.

Statutory Analysis & Solution

Section 392 casts liability on the employer to deduct tax at source from salary paid to its employees. The employer has paid LTC to its employees, which is eligible for exemption under Schedule III (Table: Sl. No. 8) read with Rule 278.

As per Rule 278, the exemption shall be allowed subject to the condition of fulfillment of the prescribed conditions. Under CBDT Circulars and the Supreme Court ruling in CIT v. ITI Ltd., the employer is under a statutory obligation to collect evidence and proof of actual expenditure incurred by the employees before granting exemption for LTC while deducting tax under Section 392.

Conclusion: The action of the Assessing Officer in treating LL Limited as an assessee-in-default under Section 401 for failure to verify the documentary proof of actual travel expenditure is correct in law.

Illustration 2: Premature Withdrawal from Recognized Provident Fund (RPF) [Section 392(3)]

Problem Statement:

Mr. Sharma, an employee of M/s. ABC Ltd., since 10-04-2022, resigned on 31-03-2027 and withdrew ₹60,000, being the balance in his RPF account. Discuss with reasons whether the provisions of Chapter XIX-B are attracted, and if so, what is the net amount receivable by the payee, Mr. Sharma?

Statutory Analysis & Solution

Continuous Service Condition: Under Para 8 of Part A of Schedule XI, accumulated balance in an RPF is exempt only if the employee has rendered continuous service of at least 5 years with the employer. Here, Mr. Sharma served from 10-04-2022 to 31-03-2027 (less than 5 years). Hence, the accumulated balance is taxable.

TDS Liability under Section 392(3): Tax is deductible @ 10% where the accumulated balance payable is ₹50,000 or more.

  • Gross withdrawal amount: ₹60,000 (Exceeds ₹50,000 threshold).
  • TDS @ 10% under Section 392(3): ₹60,000 × 10% = ₹6,000.
  • Net Amount Receivable by Mr. Sharma: ₹60,000 − ₹6,000 = ₹54,000.
Illustration 3: Monthly Rent by Salaried Individual [Table Sl. 2(i) / Sec 194-IB]

Problem Statement:

Mr. X, a salaried individual, pays rent of ₹55,000 per month to Mr. Y from June, 2026. Is he required to deduct tax at source? If so, when is he required to deduct tax? Also, compute the amount of tax to be deducted at source.
• Would your answer change if Mr. X vacated the premises on 31st August, 2026?
• Also, what would be your answer if Mr. Y does not provide his PAN to Mr. X?

Statutory Analysis & Solution

Applicability under Section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 2(i)]: An individual not liable for tax audit paying rent exceeding ₹50,000 per month must deduct TDS @ 2%.

  1. Standard Case (Tenancy from June 2026 to March 2027 = 10 months):
    Total rent for 10 months = ₹55,000 × 10 = ₹5,50,000.
    TDS Amount: ₹5,50,000 × 2% = ₹11,000.
    Time of Deduction: Deducted in the last month of the tax year, i.e., March 2027.
  2. If premises vacated on 31st August, 2026 (Tenancy: June to August = 3 months):
    Total rent = ₹55,000 × 3 = ₹1,65,000.
    TDS Amount: ₹1,65,000 × 2% = ₹3,300.
    Time of Deduction: Deducted in the last month of tenancy, i.e., August 2026.
  3. If Landlord (Mr. Y) Does Not Furnish PAN:
    Under Section 397(2), tax is deductible at the higher rate of 20%.
    Computed TDS @ 20% on ₹5,50,000 = ₹1,10,000.
    Statutory Cap [Section 397(2)(e)]: The amount of TDS cannot exceed the rent payable for the last month (₹55,000).
    Therefore, the tax deduction is strictly restricted to ₹55,000.
Illustration 4: Transfer of Immovable Property & Agricultural Land [Table Sl. 3(i)]

Problem Statement:

Mr. X sold his house property in Bangalore as well as his rural agricultural land for a consideration of ₹60 lakh and ₹15 lakh, respectively, to Mr. Y on 1.8.2026. He had purchased the house property and the land in the year 2025 for ₹40 lakh and ₹10 lakh, respectively. The stamp duty value on the date of transfer, i.e., 1.8.2026, is ₹85 lakh and ₹20 lakh for the house property and rural agricultural land, respectively. Examine the tax implications in the hands of Mr. X and Mr. Y and the TDS implications, if any, in the hands of Mr. Y, assuming both are resident Indians.

Statutory Analysis & Solution

1. Tax Implications in hands of Seller (Mr. X):

  • House Property: Since SDV (₹85L) exceeds 110% of consideration (₹60L × 110% = ₹66L), deemed consideration u/s 78 is ₹85,00,000. Short-term Capital Gain = ₹85,00,000 − ₹40,00,000 = ₹45,00,000.
  • Rural Agricultural Land: Not a capital asset u/s 2(24). Capital gain is exempt.

2. Tax Implications in hands of Buyer (Mr. Y):

  3. Event Management Fee of ₹2,80,000:
    Event management in relation to sports activities is a notified profession under CBDT Notification 88/2008.
    Under Section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 6(iii)], fees for professional services exceeding ₹30,000 attract TDS @ 10%.
    TDS @ 10%: ₹2,80,000 × 10% = ₹28,000.

TYK Question 5: Airline Landing & Parking Charges [Contractor vs. Rent]

Question:

"Come Air Ltd." has paid a sum of ₹12 lakhs during the year ended 31-3-2027 to the Airports Authority of India (AAI) for landing and parking charges of its commercial aircraft. The company deducted tax @ 2% under Section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 6(i)] (Contractor). The Assessing Officer contends that landing and parking charges represent "Rent" of runway/land subject to 10% TDS under Section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 2(ii)]. Examine the correctness of the AO's contention.

Statutory Answer & Analysis

Supreme Court Precedent in Japan Airlines Co. Ltd. v. CIT (377 ITR 372):

The Supreme Court settled this controversy by analyzing the nature of landing and parking charges:

  • The wheels of the aircraft touching the runway upon landing and remaining on the tarmac constitutes "use of land".
  • The facility of runway and tarmac provided by the Airports Authority of India constitutes an agreement or arrangement for the use of land within the statutory definition of "Rent" under Section 402(29).
  • It is not merely a contract for services or work under Sl. 6(i).

Conclusion: The contention of the Assessing Officer is fully correct. Landing and parking charges are liable for TDS @ 10% as Rent under Section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 2(ii)]. Come Air Ltd. is an assessee-in-default for the 8% shortfall.

TYK Question 6: Property & Agricultural Land Sale [SDV Differential & TDS u/s 194-IA]

Question:

Mr. Harish, Vice President of ABC Bank, sold his house property in Chennai (Consideration: ₹85 lakhs; SDV: ₹95 lakhs) and rural agricultural land (Consideration: ₹15 lakhs; SDV: ₹22 lakhs) to Mr. Suresh on 15.11.2026. Both are resident Indians.
Examine:
(a) Tax implications in the hands of Mr. Harish and Mr. Suresh.
(b) TDS obligations of Mr. Suresh under Section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 3(i)].

Statutory Answer & Analysis
  1. Tax Implications for Seller (Mr. Harish):
    • House Property: Since SDV (₹95L) exceeds 110% of consideration (₹85L × 110% = ₹93.5L), full value of consideration u/s 78 is ₹95,00,000 for computing capital gains.
    • Rural Agricultural Land: Not a capital asset u/s 2(24). Capital gains are completely exempt.
  2. Tax Implications for Buyer (Mr. Suresh):
    • House Property: Difference between SDV (₹95L) and consideration (₹85L) = ₹10,00,000. Since it exceeds ₹50,000 and 10% of consideration, ₹10,00,000 is taxable as IFOS under Section 56(2)(x) / Sec 59.
    • Rural Land: Excluded from Section 56(2)(x) property definition. No tax.
  3. TDS Liability of Buyer (Mr. Suresh) under Section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 3(i)]:
    • Rural Land: Expressly exempt from TDS under Sl. 3(i).
    • House Property: Tax is deductible @ 1% on the HIGHER of consideration or SDV.
    Higher value = SDV of ₹95,00,000.
    TDS Amount: ₹95,00,000 × 1% = ₹95,000 (remitted via Form 26QB).
TYK Question 7: Third Party Administrator (TPA) Payments to Private Hospitals

Question:

Siddharth Hospitals Pvt. Ltd. has recently been accorded recognition by several insurance companies for providing cashless treatment. The TPAs making payments to Siddharth Hospitals Pvt. Ltd. on behalf of insurance companies deduct tax at source @ 10% under Section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 6(iii)]. The hospital contends that professional services are rendered by individual doctors, not by the private hospital company, and therefore TPA payments to the hospital company cannot be subjected to professional TDS. Decide the validity of this contention.

Statutory Answer & Analysis

CBDT Circular No. 8/2009 & Judicial Affirmation:

The CBDT clarified that payments made by TPAs on behalf of insurance companies to hospitals for medical treatment under health insurance policies fall within the scope of fees for professional services under Section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 6(iii)] (replaces Section 194J).

  • The statutory provision applies to payments made to any resident person (which includes a private hospital company).
  • There is no statutory requirement that the recipient of professional fees must be an individual or natural person. A corporate entity employing doctors to provide medical services is equally subject to withholding.

Conclusion: The contention of Siddharth Hospitals Pvt. Ltd. is incorrect in law. The TPAs are statutorily bound to deduct TDS @ 10% on all payments made to the hospital.

  3. Lottery Ticket Commission of ₹35,000:

TYK Question 10: Cash Withdrawal TDS u/s 194N & Filers vs. Non-Filers

Statutory Answer & Analysis
  1. PQR Ltd. (Regular Return Filer):

TYK Question 11: E-Commerce Operator (Sec 194-O) & Interplay with Sec 194Q

  1. ABC Ltd. (Corporate Participant):
    Under Section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 8(v)], the e-commerce operator must deduct TDS @ 0.1% on the GROSS amount of sales facilitated.

TYK Question 12: Business Benefits & Perquisites [Table Sl. 8(xxiii) / Sec 194R]
