Chapter 8: Deductions from Gross Total Income

Comprehensive CA Final Direct Tax notes covering Chapter VIII (Sections 122 to 154) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly Chapter VI-A, Sections 80C to 80U of the 1961 Act). Explains general statutory rules, payment-based deductions (Sections 123 to 137), profit-linked incentives (Sections 138 to 152), other income deductions (Sections 153 & 154), default tax regime (Section 202) eligibility matrix, all 19 ICAI Illustrations and all 5 Test Your Knowledge practical questions.

Statutory Framework

8.1 General Provisions Governing Deductions [Section 122]

Chapter VIII of Income-tax Act, 2025 • Sections 122 to 154 • Corresponding 1961 Act: Chapter VI-A (Sections 80A to 80U)

Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, taxable Total Income is computed by consolidating the net taxable income from all five heads into Gross Total Income (GTI), and then subtracting eligible deductions permissible under Chapter VIII (Sections 122 to 154).

Computation of Total Income
$$\text{Total Income} = \text{Gross Total Income (GTI)} - \sum \text{Deductions under Chapter VIII (Sections 122 to 154)}$$

Basic Statutory Rules Governing Chapter VIII Deductions [Section 122]

Rule 1 GTI as Maximum Ceiling [Section 122(2)]

The aggregate amount of deductions under Chapter VIII shall not, in any case, exceed the Gross Total Income of the assessee.

An assessee can never have a loss or negative income figure as a result of claiming Chapter VIII deductions! If GTI is ₹1,20,000 and eligible Section 123 investments are ₹1,50,000, the deduction is restricted strictly to ₹1,20,000, reducing Total Income to Nil.

Rule 2 No Deductions from Special Rate Incomes

Deductions under Chapter VIII are allowable only from normal incomes included in GTI. Deductions are strictly prohibited against:

  • Long-term capital gains taxable u/s 197 or u/s 198 [formerly 112 & 112A].
  • Short-term capital gains on STT-paid equity shares taxable u/s 196 [formerly 111A].
  • Winnings from lotteries, crossword puzzles, races, betting taxable u/s 94 [formerly 115BB].
  • Undisclosed income taxable u/s 199 [formerly 115BBE].
Rule 3 Double Deduction Bar [Section 122(3) & (4)]
  • Where a deduction of any profit or gain is allowed under any provision of Part C of Chapter VIII (Sections 138 to 152), deduction to that extent shall not be allowed again under any other provision of the Act!
  • The deduction shall not exceed the net profits and gains of the specific undertaking, unit, or enterprise.
  • If a member of an AOP/BOI is assessed, no double deduction is allowable in respect of his share of profit.
Rule 4 Inter-Unit Transactions at Market Value [Section 122(6) & (7)]

Where any goods or services held for an eligible business are transferred to any other business carried on by the assessee (or vice versa), and the transfer consideration does not correspond to the open market value, the profits of the eligible business shall be recomputed by the Assessing Officer taking the market value.

If it constitutes a Specified Domestic Transaction referred to in Section 164, market value means Arm's Length Price (ALP).

Mandatory Timely Return Filing for Profit-Linked Deductions [Section 122(5)]

Section 122(5) (formerly Section 80AC) mandates that no deduction under Part C of Chapter VIII (Sections 138 to 152) shall be allowed to an assessee unless he furnishes a return of income for that tax year on or before the due date specified under Section 263(1) [formerly 139(1)]!

If a return of income is filed belatedly under Section 263(4), the assessee completely forfeits the right to claim profit-linked tax holidays under Sections 138, 139, 140, 141, 142, 143, 144, 145, 146, 147, 148, 149, 150, 151 and 152!

ICAI Illustration 1

Mandatory Audit Report vs Timely Filing Deadline [Section 122(5)]

Problem Statement:

Examine the following statements with regard to the provisions of the Income-tax Act, 2025:

(a) For grant of deduction u/s 141, filing of audit report in prescribed form is a must for a corporate assessee, even if the return of income is filed after the due date specified under section 263(1).

(b) Filing of a belated return under section 263(4) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 will debar a company from claiming deduction with respect to its profits from housing projects under section 142.

Solution:

(a) The statement is NOT correct:

Section 122(5) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 explicitly stipulates compulsory filing of return of income on or before the due date specified under Section 263(1) as a mandatory pre-condition for claiming deduction under Section 141 (Part C of Chapter VIII). Therefore, if a corporate assessee fails to file its return within the Section 263(1) due date, mere filing of an audit report will NOT entitle the company to claim deduction u/s 141!

(b) The statement is CORRECT:

As per Section 122(5), an assessee has to furnish its return of income on or before the due date specified under Section 263(1) to be eligible for claiming deduction under Section 142 (profits from housing projects, falling under Part C of Chapter VIII). Consequently, filing a belated return under Section 263(4) will disentitle the company from claiming deduction under Section 142.

Section 123

8.2 Deduction in Respect of Investment in Specified Assets [Section 123]

Corresponding 1961 Act: Section 80C • Read with Schedule XV • Maximum Ceiling: ₹1,50,000

Section 123 read with Schedule XV (Para 2) provides a deduction from Gross Total Income to an Individual or a Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) in respect of amounts paid or deposited in specified financial assets, life insurance policies, provident funds, pension schemes, housing loans, and education fees during the tax year.

Eligible Assessees & Maximum Aggregate Ceiling [Section 123(1)]
  • Eligible Assessees: Available exclusively to Individuals and HUFs (Not available to Companies, Partnership Firms, LLPs, or AOPs/BOIs).
  • Regime Limitation: Deduction under Section 123 is available ONLY if the assessee exercises the option of shifting out of the default tax regime (Normal Regime). It is strictly disallowed under the default tax regime u/s 202(1)!
  • Maximum Monetary Limit: The total aggregate deduction under Section 123 shall not exceed ₹1,50,000 in any tax year.

1. Life Insurance Premium Limits [Schedule XV, Para 2]

Premiums paid on life insurance policies qualify for deduction. The policy must cover:

  • In case of an Individual: Self, spouse, and any child (child may be dependent or independent, minor or major, married or unmarried).
  • In case of a HUF: Any member of the family.
Date of Issue of Life Insurance Policy Category of Insured Person Maximum Permissible Premium (Cap on Deduction)
Issued on or before 31.03.2012 Any person (Self, Spouse, Child, Member) Up to 20% of the actual capital sum assured
Issued on or after 01.04.2012
(General Rule)		 Any person other than disabled Up to 10% of the actual capital sum assured
Issued on or after 01.04.2013
(Special Disability Carve-Out)		 Person with disability / severe disability u/s 154 or suffering from disease/ailment specified u/s 128 Up to 15% of the actual capital sum assured
Meaning of "Actual Capital Sum Assured": It means the minimum amount assured under the policy on the happening of the insured event at any time throughout the term of the policy, excluding: (i) the value of any premiums agreed to be returned; or (ii) any benefit by way of bonus or otherwise over and above the sum actually assured.

2. Comprehensive List of Other Eligible Specified Assets [Schedule XV]

Provident Funds & Social Schemes
  • Public Provident Fund (PPF): Subscribed in the name of self, spouse, or any child (Individual); or any member (HUF). Maximum deposit ₹1,50,000 per year.
  • Statutory Provident Fund (SPF) & Recognized Provident Fund (RPF): Employee's contribution.
  • Approved Superannuation Fund (ASF): Employee's contribution.
  • Sukanya Samriddhi Account Scheme: Deposits made in the name of any girl child (or child for whom individual is legal guardian). Interest and maturity amounts are 100% exempt under Schedule II.
Small Savings & Deposits
  • National Savings Certificates (NSC VIII Issue): Subscription amount and accrued interest deemed reinvested in years 1 to 4 (5th year interest is not reinvested).
  • Senior Citizens Savings Scheme (SCSS) Rules, 2004: 5-year lock-in deposit.
  • Post Office 5-Year Time Deposit: Deposit in an account under Post Office Time Deposit Rules, 1981.
  • Scheduled Bank 5-Year Term Deposit: Fixed deposit for ≥ 5 years under notified scheme.
  • NABARD Notified Bonds: Subscription to rural development bonds.
Housing Loan Principal & Transfer Costs
  • Repayment of Housing Loan Principal: Borrowed for purchase/construction of a residential house whose income is taxable under "Income from house property", from Govt, bank, LIC, NHB, or public housing finance company. Construction must be completed!
  • Stamp Duty & Registration Charges: Paid for transfer of the residential house property (eligible in the year of payment even if loan is not taken).
  • Inadmissible: Loans borrowed from relatives, friends, or non-approved entities; admission fees/shares; cost of alterations/repairs.
Education, Mutual Funds & Equities
  • Tuition Fees for Children: Paid at admission or thereafter for full-time education to any school, college, university in India for up to 2 children. (Development fees, transport, coaching, or foreign education fees are strictly ineligible).
  • Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS): Units of notified mutual funds (3-year lock-in).
  • Unit Linked Insurance Plan (ULIP): ULIP of UTI or LIC (Self, spouse, child).
  • Eligible Issue of Capital: Shares/debentures of infrastructure public companies approved by CBDT.
Lock-in Periods & Recapture of Deduction upon Premature Transfer
  • Life Insurance: If policy is terminated or premium not paid for 2 years (single premium 2 years), deduction allowed earlier is deemed income of the year of termination.
  • Residential House Property: If house is transferred before the expiry of 5 years from the end of the financial year in which possession was obtained, all aggregate deductions claimed for principal repayment and stamp duty in earlier years are recaptured as taxable income of the year of sale!
  • Senior Citizens Savings Scheme / Post Office TD: Withdrawal before 5 years is taxable in year of receipt.
  • ELSS Mutual Funds / Shares: Minimum lock-in of 3 years.

Section 125

8.2C Contribution to Agnipath Scheme [Section 125]

Corresponding 1961 Act: Section 80CCH • Agniveer Corpus Fund Deductions

Section 125 provides deductions in respect of contributions made to the Agniveer Corpus Fund under the Agnipath Scheme launched by the Central Government for Indian Armed Forces recruitment:

Section 125(1) Agniveer's Own Contribution
  • Each Agniveer contributes 30% of his monthly customized package to the Agniveer Corpus Fund.
  • Deduction is allowed for the full amount so paid or deposited by the individual.
  • Regime Limitation: Available ONLY if the individual shifts out of the default tax regime (Normal Regime).
Section 125(2) Central Government's Matching Contribution
  • The Central Government makes an equal matching contribution of 30% to the Agniveer Corpus Fund account.
  • This matching contribution is first included in the gross salary of the individual under Section 16, and then a 100% deduction is allowed under Section 125(2)!
  • Crucial Rule: Deduction u/s 125(2) is available irrespective of whether the individual pays tax under normal regime or default regime u/s 202!
Exemption of Seva Nidhi Package on Exit [Schedule II, Table S. No. 17]

Any payment received from the Agniveer Corpus Fund (the final Seva Nidhi package along with accrued interest) by a person enrolled under the Agnipath Scheme or his nominee upon completion of service is 100% EXEMPT FROM TAX under Schedule II.

8.2K Deduction for House Rent Paid [Section 134]

Corresponding 1961 Act: Section 80GG • For Assessees Not Receiving House Rent Allowance (HRA)

  • Assessee must not be in receipt of any HRA from any employer.
  • The individual, spouse, minor child, or HUF of which he is a member must not own any residential accommodation at the place where he ordinarily resides or performs duties of office/employment.

8.5 Deduction in Case of a Person with Disability [Section 154]

Corresponding 1961 Act: Section 80U • Self-Disability • Flat Statutory Deduction

Section 154 provides a flat statutory deduction to a resident Individual who, at any time during the previous year, is certified by a medical authority to be a person with disability.

Normal Disability Disability ≥ 40% and < 80%
₹ 75,000

Flat statutory deduction. The assessee is NOT required to submit proof of actual medical expenditure!

Severe Disability Disability ≥ 80%
₹ 1,25,000

Flat statutory deduction of ₹1,25,000 for a person with severe disability (80% or more disability or suffering from autism, cerebral palsy, or multiple disabilities).

Key Distinction: Section 127 vs Section 154
  • Section 127 [80DD]: Claimed by an assessee for the maintenance/medical care of a dependant disabled relative.
  • Section 154 [80U]: Claimed directly by the disabled individual himself against his own total income!
  • Rule against double benefit: If an individual claims deduction for his own disability u/s 154, no other person can claim deduction u/s 127 in respect of that individual!

TYK Question 2

X Ltd. Windmill Power Unit: Loss Set-Off vs Section 138 Standalone Rule

Problem Statement:

X Ltd. has two units: Unit 'N' (engaged in power generation, eligible for deduction u/s 138) and Unit 'Y'. Unit 'Y' had a profit of ₹ 100 lakhs in Tax Year 2026-27.

X Ltd. claimed depreciation of ₹ 120 lakhs on windmills of Unit 'N' against the profit of Unit 'Y'. The Assessing Officer was of the view that depreciation relating to an eligible business under Section 138 cannot be set off against non-eligible business income.

Requirement: Examine the correctness of the action of the Assessing Officer.

Judicial Analysis & Decision:
Application of High Court Precedent: CIT v. Swarnagiri Wire Insulations Pvt. Ltd. (2012) 349 ITR 245 (Kar.)

In CIT v. Swarnagiri Wire Insulations Pvt. Ltd., the Karnataka High Court resolved the identical controversy regarding the interplay between inter-unit set-off and standalone deduction provisions:

  • Step 1: Normal PGBP Computation: The profits and losses of an assessee under the head "Profits and gains of business or profession" must first be computed by aggregating all units in accordance with the general provisions of the Act.
  • Step 2: Section 108(1) Inter-Source Set-Off: Under Section 108(1) [formerly 70(1)], an assessee is statutorily entitled to set off the loss (including depreciation loss) of one source against the profit of another source under the same head of income in the same tax year.
  • Step 3: Section 138 Standalone Fiction: The deeming fiction treating an eligible undertaking as a separate source for Section 138 operates only for determining the quantum of deduction available under Section 138. It does NOT debar or nullify the mandatory intra-head set-off under Section 108(1)!
  • Since Unit 'N' suffered a net loss after depreciation (profit was less than ₹120L), no deduction is claimed under Section 138. The unabsorbed depreciation of ₹120L is fully eligible to be set off against the business profits of Unit 'Y' under Section 108(1).
Conclusion

The action of the Assessing Officer in not permitting set-off of the loss/depreciation of the eligible business against the non-eligible business profit is NOT CORRECT in law.

TYK Question 4

Navi Limited: Standalone Basis & Trade Interest Eligibility

Problem Statement:

Following issues have been raised by Navi Limited regarding its deduction eligibility:

(i) It operates two separate undertakings. One undertaking is eligible for deduction u/s 141 and the other is not. How should profits be computed?

(ii) Its profit from an undertaking in North Eastern States eligible u/s 143 includes interest income derived on delayed collection of sale proceeds from customers. Is this interest eligible for deduction u/s 143?

Statutory Solution:

(i) Standalone Profit Computation [Section 141 read with Section 122]:

As per Section 141 read with the overriding statutory provisions, the profits and gains of an eligible industrial undertaking must be computed as if such eligible undertaking were the only source of income of the assessee during the previous year. Consequently, losses of the non-eligible undertaking cannot be set off against the standalone profits of the eligible undertaking for the purpose of computing deduction under Section 141!

(ii) Interest on Delayed Customer Receivables [Section 143]:

Under Section 143, 100% deduction is allowed on profits "derived from" eligible business. Interest income earned on delayed collection of sale proceeds from trade debtors has a direct first-degree nexus with the business of the industrial undertaking. It represents part of the sales realization and is directly derived from the eligible industrial activity.

Therefore, interest income on delayed sale proceeds IS ELIGIBLE for 100% deduction under Section 143!
