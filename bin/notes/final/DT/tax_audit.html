Statutory Foundation & Audit Landscape

1. Overview, Legislative Objectives & Other Audit Reports under the 2025 Act

Tax Audit was originally introduced into Indian tax jurisprudence with effect from 1st April, 1985 (under Section 44AB of the 1961 Act) to assist revenue authorities in computing taxable income from books maintained in the ordinary course of business. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, the statutory framework of tax audit is codified under Section 63 read with Rule 47 and the newly unified Form No. 26.

Core Legislative Objectives of Tax Audit
  • Proper Bookkeeping: Ensures that books of account and other statutory records are properly maintained, audited, and faithfully reflect taxable income.
  • Verification of Deductions & Claims: Enables systematic verification of claims for allowances, deductions, and tax exemptions, curbing artificial inflation of expenditure.
  • Checking Tax Evasion: Deters fraudulent transactions, cash manipulations, unrecorded sales, and sham loans/deposits.
  • Facilitating Administration: Minimizes routine routine inquiry and verification during assessment, saving administrative time and preventing repetitive inquiries.
Judicial Faith in Chartered Accountants

Indian courts have consistently recognized the paramount sanctity of tax audit reports issued by Chartered Accountants:

G.M.E. Association v. CIT (1994) 205 ITR 356 (Bom.)

The Bombay High Court held that it is quite competent for the income-tax authorities not only to accept the tax auditor’s report, but also to place immense reliance upon it for framing assessments.

Swadeshi Cotton Mills & Goodyear India Ltd.

Where the original books of account were destroyed in a fire, the Court held that assessment framed on the basis of the auditor’s report was completely valid in law.

Comprehensive Matrix of Statutory Audits & Accountant Reports under the Income-tax Act, 2025

In addition to Section 63, the Income-tax Act, 2025 incorporates multiple specific provisions requiring mandatory audits, certificates, or reports by a Chartered Accountant:

Statutory Mandate & Applicability

2. Tax Audit under Section 63 & Statutory Threshold Limits

Under Section 63(1) of the Income-tax Act, 2025, every person carrying on business or profession is mandatorily required to get his accounts of the tax year audited by an accountant (Chartered Accountant in practice) and furnish by the specified date the report of such audit in the prescribed form:

Category of Assessee Statutory Threshold Limit / Trigger Event Key Statutory Conditions & Explanations
Business Assessee [Section 63(1)(a)] Total sales, turnover, or gross receipts in business exceed:
₹ 1,00,00,000 (₹ 1 Crore)
Elevated to ₹ 10 Crores if cash receipts and cash payments are ≤ 5% each! 		General threshold is ₹ 1 Crore. However, where non-cash transactions prevail (cash receipts ≤ 5% AND cash payments ≤ 5%), the threshold is elevated tenfold to ₹ 10 Crores under the safe harbour proviso.
Professional Assessee [Section 63(1)(b)] Gross receipts in profession exceed:
₹ 50,00,000 (₹ 50 Lakhs)
Elevated to ₹ 75 Lakhs if cash receipts are ≤ 5%! 		Applies to notified professions under Section 62(1) (Legal, medical, engineering, architectural, accountancy, technical consultancy, interior decoration, film artists, authorised representatives).
Presumptive Business Opt-Out [Section 63(1)(c)] Breach of 5-Year Lock-in under Section 57(4) (Old Sec 44AD(4)), where profits declared are lower than 8% / 6% AND total income exceeds basic exemption limit. If an eligible assessee opts out of presumptive taxation under Section 57 during the 5-year lock-in period, he cannot opt back into Section 57 for the subsequent 5 tax years, and must mandatorily get accounts audited under Section 63!
Presumptive Profession Lower Profits [Section 63(1)(d)] Resident professional claiming profits lower than the deemed 50% under Section 58(1) (Old Sec 44ADA) AND total income exceeds basic exemption limit. If a professional’s gross receipts are ≤ ₹ 50L (or ₹ 75L), but he declares profits less than 50% of gross receipts and his total income exceeds the basic exemption limit, tax audit is mandatory!
Goods Carriage Operators [Section 63(1)(e)] Assessee engaged in plying, hiring, or leasing goods carriages claiming profits lower than the presumptive rates under Section 59(1) (Old Sec 44AE). Presumptive profit is ₹ 1,000 per ton of gross vehicle weight per month for heavy goods vehicles (> 12 MT), and ₹ 7,500 per month for other vehicles. Declaring lower profits triggers mandatory Section 63 audit.
Guidance on Computing "Total Sales, Turnover, or Gross Receipts"

The terms "turnover" and "gross receipts" are commercial terms. The ICAI Guidance Note on Tax Audit lays down precise rules for their determination:

Inclusions in Turnover / Receipts:
  • Gross sales value of goods and professional service billings;
  • Sale of scrap, by-products, and manufacturing residues;
  • Export incentives, duty drawbacks, and cash assistance;
  • Excise duty, sales tax, or GST if the assessee follows the inclusive method of accounting;
  • Advance received and forfeited in the course of business;
  • Speculative Transactions: Aggregate of all positive and negative differences arising from speculative transactions represents speculative turnover!
  • Derivatives / Futures & Options (F&O): Total of favorable and unfavorable differences, plus premium received on sale of options!
Exclusions from Turnover / Receipts:
  • Trade Discounts & Turnover Discounts: Deducted in sales invoice or credited through credit notes;
  • Sales Returns: Return of goods outward/inward;
  • Sale proceeds of fixed capital assets (plant, machinery, cars, land);
  • Sale proceeds of investments (shares, securities, mutual funds) unless held as stock-in-trade;
  • Rental income, interest income, or dividends (chargeable under other heads);
  • Goods sent on consignment (until actually sold by the consignee);
  • Reimbursement of out-of-pocket expenses incurred on behalf of clients.
Specified Date for Furnishing Tax Audit Report:
Under Section 63, the tax audit report must be furnished electronically on or before the "specified date", which is defined as one month prior to the due date of furnishing the return of income under Section 263(1).
Example: For corporate assessees and business assessees whose return due date is 31st October, the specified date for furnishing the tax audit report is 30th September of the assessment year.
Cashless Economy Incentive

3. The ₹ 10 Crore Safe Harbour Scheme for Non-Cash Businesses [≤ 5% Cash]

To promote digital payments and reduce compliance burden on digital businesses, the Proviso to Section 63(1)(a) elevates the tax audit turnover threshold tenfold from ₹ 1 Crore to ₹ 10 Crores, provided the assessee satisfies the Twin 5% Non-Cash Conditions.

The Twin Cumulative 5% Conditions for ₹ 10 Crore Threshold
Condition 1: Aggregate Cash Receipts ≤ 5%
$$ rac{ ext{Aggregate of All Receipts in Cash}}{ ext{Aggregate of All Receipts (Cash + Non-Cash)}} \le 5\%$$

Crucial Scope: "Aggregate of all receipts" is NOT restricted to turnover! It encompasses every single rupee received during the tax year—including sales turnover, debtors' realizations, loans taken, advances, capital introduction, sale of fixed assets, interest, and gifts!

Condition 2: Aggregate Cash Payments ≤ 5%
$$ rac{ ext{Aggregate of All Payments in Cash}}{ ext{Aggregate of All Payments (Cash + Non-Cash)}} \le 5\%$$

Crucial Scope: "Aggregate of all payments" encompasses every single rupee paid during the tax year—including trade purchases, creditors' payments, operating expenses, asset purchases, loan repayments, capital drawings, and taxes!

CRITICAL TRAP — Bearer Cheques & Crossed Cheques are Treated as CASH!

Under the Explanation to Section 63, the payment or receipt by a cheque drawn on a bank or by a bank draft which is NOT an account payee cheque or account payee bank draft SHALL BE DEEMED TO BE A PAYMENT OR RECEIPT IN CASH!

  • Account Payee Cheque / Draft, ECS, UPI, NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, BHIM: Treated as non-cash (electronic banking modes).
  • Bearer Cheques & Crossed Cheques: Treated as CASH for computing both the numerator and denominator!
Audit Reporting Framework

4. Form No. 26 Structure: Part A, Part B & Part C [Auditor Opinions]

Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, the reporting architecture for tax audits has been streamlined into a single, unified document: Form No. 26. It replaces the erstwhile tripartite structure of Forms 3CA, 3CB, and 3CD, providing a standardized reporting interface for both corporate and non-corporate assessees.

Feature Part A of Form No. 26 (Old Form 3CA) Part B of Form No. 26 (Old Form 3CB)
Applicability In a case where the accounts of the business or profession of a person have been audited under any other law prior to the tax audit. In a case where the accounts of the business or profession of a person have NOT been audited under any other law.
Target Assessees Companies governed by the Companies Act, 2013; Banking Companies; Insurance Companies; Electricity Companies; Co-operative Societies. Sole Proprietorships; Partnership Firms; LLPs (not subject to statutory audit under LLP Act); Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs); Trusts.
Scope of Auditor's Opinion The auditor does not audit the financial statements anew. He certifies that the statutory audit has been conducted under the relevant Act, and expresses an opinion solely on whether the particulars in Part D are "True and Correct". The auditor conducts a full-scope financial audit. He expresses a dual opinion:
  1. Financial statements exhibit a "True and Fair View"; and
  2. The particulars given in Part D are "True and Correct".
Annexures Attached Copy of the statutory audit report along with audited Balance Sheet, Profit & Loss Account, and Notes to Accounts. Audited Balance Sheet, Profit & Loss Account, and Notes prepared and certified directly by the tax auditor.
"True and Fair View" (Financial Statements)

"True and Fair View" is an accounting concept governed by Standards on Auditing (SAs) and Accounting Standards. It implies that:

  • The financial statements are free from material misstatement;
  • Accounting policies are consistently applied;
  • Transactions are recorded on accrual, going concern, and prudence principles;
  • It permits reasonable estimation and materiality judgements.
"True and Correct" (Part D Particulars)

"True and Correct" implies factual precision and exact mathematical and legal accuracy. There is no leeway for materiality or subjective discretion:

  • The amounts reported (e.g., disallowances u/s 40(a), cash payments > ₹ 10,000, hundi loans) must be exact;
  • Dates of statutory deposits (PF/ESI) must match bank challans exactly;
  • Any divergence or approximation without qualification is an audit failure!
Paragraph 5 of Part A & Part B: Auditor Qualifications, Reservations & Disclaimers

Paragraph 5 of Part A and Part B provides the primary statutory channel for the tax auditor to state his observations, qualifications, comments, or disclaimers:

  • Where the tax auditor is unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence (e.g., cash vouchers unverified, stock records missing);
  • Where the tax auditor disagrees with the treatment or interpretation adopted by the management regarding tax disallowances;
  • Where conflicting legal interpretations exist (e.g., thin capitalization reporting or MSME disallowances), the auditor must report both viewpoints;
  • Crucial Disciplinary Rule: Marking "No" or leaving a clause blank based solely on a Management Representation Letter (without independent verification or qualification in Paragraph 5) constitutes gross negligence and professional misconduct under the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949!
Part C of Form No. 26: General Information [Clauses 1 to 8]
Clause No. Field Description Reporting Requirements & Guidance
Clause 1 Name of the Assessee Name as per PAN database. Full name for individuals (no abbreviations). Trade names and branch names to be stated. Name changes during the tax year must be disclosed.
Clause 2 Address Address of registered office or principal place of business.
Clause 3 Permanent Account Number (PAN) 10-character alphanumeric PAN of the assessee.
Clause 4 Status Individual, HUF, Firm, Company, AOP, BOI, Local Authority, Artificial Juridical Person.

High-Yield Statutory Compliance

7. Deep-Dive on MSME Delayed Payments Reporting [Clause 33 & Section 43B(h)]

The insertion of Clause 33 in Form No. 26 and Section 44(h) / Section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act has revolutionized commercial settlements in India. To protect Micro and Small Enterprises from crippling liquidity crunches, the Legislature has mandated strict tax disallowance for delayed payments.

7.1 Statutory Mandate of Clause 33(a) & Clause 33(b)
Clause 33(a): Inadmissible Interest u/s 23 of MSMED Act

Under Section 16 of the MSMED Act, 2006, where a buyer fails to pay a supplier within the statutory period, he is liable to pay compound interest with monthly rests at three times the Bank Rate notified by RBI.

Absolute Disallowance: Section 23 of the MSMED Act explicitly provides that interest paid or payable to MSMEs SHALL NOT BE ALLOWED as a deduction in computing taxable income under the Income-tax Act!
Clause 33(b): Principal Disallowance u/s 43B(h) / Section 44(h)

Under Section 43B(h), any sum payable by the assessee to a Micro or Small Enterprise beyond the time limit specified in Section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006:

Mandatory Add-Back: Shall be allowed as a deduction ONLY in the previous year in which such sum is actually paid, notwithstanding that the liability was incurred during the tax year!
7.2 Statutory Time Limits under Section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006

Section 15 of the MSMED Act prescribes strict, non-negotiable payment deadlines from the "appointed day" (date of acceptance or deemed acceptance of goods/services):

Contractual Scenario Statutory Payment Time Limit Tax Consequences of Default
Case 1: No Written Agreement Payment must be made within 15 DAYS from the date of delivery/acceptance. If unpaid on the 16th day and outstanding as on 31st March: Disallowed in current tax year.
Case 2: Written Agreement with Term ≤ 45 Days Payment must be made within the agreed period (e.g., 30 days). If unpaid beyond the agreed days and outstanding on 31st March: Disallowed in current tax year.
Case 3: Written Agreement with Term > 45 Days Statutory ceiling of 45 DAYS strictly applies! (Any clause exceeding 45 days is void ab initio). Payment made beyond 45 days is legally delayed. If outstanding on 31st March, it is disallowed.
CRITICAL TAX DISTINCTION — Proviso to Section 43B Does NOT Apply to Section 43B(h)!

For all other statutory liabilities under Section 43B (taxes, PF, bonus, bank interest), deduction is allowed if payment is made on or before the due date of furnishing the return of income under Section 263(1).
HOWEVER, Section 43B(h) is deliberately excluded from the Proviso! Paying an MSME after the 15/45-day statutory window (even if paid before the return filing due date) will NOT save the deduction in the year of accrual! It is disallowed in the current year and allowed only in the next tax year when actually paid.

7.3 Enterprise Classification & Auditor Verification Checklists
Who Qualifies as an MSME Supplier?
  • Micro Enterprise: Investment in Plant & Machinery ≤ ₹ 1 Crore AND Turnover ≤ ₹ 5 Crores;
  • Small Enterprise: Investment ≤ ₹ 10 Crores AND Turnover ≤ ₹ 50 Crores;
  • Medium Enterprises are EXCLUDED: Section 43B(h) does NOT apply to sums payable to Medium enterprises (> ₹ 50 Cr turnover);
  • Mandatory Udyam Registration: The supplier must possess a valid Udyam Registration Certificate on the date of supply.
Exclusion of Wholesale & Retail Traders

As per Office Memorandum issued by the Ministry of MSME, wholesale and retail traders are eligible for Udyam registration solely for Priority Sector Lending (PSL) benefits.

Legal Position: The benefits of Section 15 of the MSMED Act do not extend to traders. Consequently, Section 43B(h) disallowance does NOT apply to outstanding dues payable to traders! It applies strictly to suppliers engaged in manufacturing or provision of services.

8. Form 26 Part D: Detailed Reporting Requirements [Clauses 41 to 53]

The concluding segment of Part D (Clauses 41 to 53) addresses specialized corporate and international tax compliances—including Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR), interest limitation under thin capitalization, cash loan restrictions, quantitative inventory reconciliations, and comprehensive TDS/TCS audits.

Clause Reporting Domain & Statutory Subject Detailed Reporting Obligations & Audit Verification
Clause 41 Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) Expenditure:
Disallowance under Section 37(1) Explanation 2. 		Report all expenditure incurred by the company towards CSR activities under Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013 debited to P&L. By virtue of Explanation 2 to Section 37(1), CSR expenditure is statutorily deemed as NOT incurred for business purposes, and is fully disallowable.
Clause 42 Thin Capitalization / Interest Limitation u/s 177 / 94B:
Excess interest paid to Associated Enterprises (AEs).
  • Applies to Indian companies and permanent establishments paying interest on debt to non-resident AEs exceeding ₹ 1 Crore;
  • Deduction of interest is capped at 30% of EBITDA;
  • Deemed AE Debt [Sec 177(2)]: Where debt is issued by an unrelated lender (e.g. Indian branch of foreign bank) but guaranteed by a non-resident AE, or where AE deposits matching funds, the debt is deemed to be issued by an AE! (Tested in TYK 3).
Clause 43 Foreign Remittances (Form 145 / 15CA / 15CB):

  • 45(a): Taking or accepting loan, deposit, or specified sum ≥ ₹ 20,000 in contravention of Section 493;
  • 45(b): Repaying loan, deposit, or specified advance ≥ ₹ 20,000 in contravention of Section 494;
  • 45(c): Receiving cash ≥ ₹ 2,00,000 in contravention of Section 495 (single day, single transaction, or relating to one event).
  • Auditor must report name, PAN, address of person, amount, and mode of transaction.
Clause 46 Statement of Financial Transactions (SFT):

Clause 47 Shares Issued at Premium (Angel Tax):

Decriminalized Administrative Charges

9. Mandatory Administrative Fees for Tax Audit Failure [Section 428(c)]

Under modern tax administration, routine delays in filing the tax audit report have been decoupled from subjective, litigious penalty proceedings (erstwhile Section 271B) and converted into automatic, mandatory administrative late fees under Section 428(c).

Statutory Fee Schedule under Section 428(c)

As per Section 428(c) of the Income-tax Act, 2025, if any person fails to get his accounts audited in respect of any tax year or fails to furnish the tax audit report by the specified date under Section 63, he shall be liable to pay the following mandatory administrative fee:

Period of Delay in Furnishing Tax Audit Report Mandatory Administrative Fee Payable
Delay up to ONE MONTH from the specified date ₹ 75,000
Delay BEYOND ONE MONTH ₹ 1,50,000
Statutory Turnover Cap:
The fee leviable under Section 428(c) is subject to an overall ceiling of 0.75% of total sales, turnover, or gross receipts, or ₹ 1,50,000, whichever is lower! Furthermore, because Section 428(c) is an administrative fee, the defense of "reasonable cause" under Section 470 cannot be invoked to waive it.
Professional Standards & Disciplinary Code

10. Ethical Compliances & Professional Misconduct of Chartered Accountants

A tax audit is a public-trust statutory engagement. The Chartered Accountant acts not merely as an agent of the assessee, but as an independent quasi-judicial certifying officer assisting the Sovereign Republic in revenue determination. Failure to discharge professional duties with integrity, independence, and due diligence invites severe disciplinary proceedings under the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949.

10.1 Maximum Ceiling on Tax Audit Assignments [Council Guidelines No. 1-CA(7)/02/2008]

To maintain audit quality and prevent commercial over-solicitation, the Council of ICAI has prescribed strict quantitative ceilings:

Individual Member & Partner Limit:
  • A Chartered Accountant in practice, whether in individual capacity or as a partner of a firm, cannot accept more than 60 tax audit assignments under Section 63 in a financial year.
  • In a partnership firm, the limit applies at 60 audits per partner (e.g. a firm with 4 partners can accept a maximum of 240 tax audits).
Statutory Exclusions from Ceiling:
  • Audits conducted under presumptive taxation schemes (Section 57 / 44AD, Section 58 / 44ADA, Section 59 / 44AE) are excluded from the 60-audit ceiling;
  • Audits conducted for branches do not count towards the parent entity ceiling.
10.2 Professional Misconduct Provisions under the Second Schedule to the CA Act, 1949

The Disciplinary Directorate of ICAI actively prosecutes tax auditors under the following fundamental clauses of the Second Schedule to the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949:

Disciplinary Provision Nature of Professional Default Practical Tax Audit Application & Precedents
Clause (7) of Part I, Second Schedule Failure to Exercise Due Diligence or Gross Negligence:
A CA in practice is guilty of misconduct if he "fails to exercise due diligence, or is grossly negligent in the conduct of his professional duties".
  • Marking "No" for cash payments > ₹ 10,000 u/s 40A(3) relying solely on management representation without verifying cash books (TYK 5);
  • Certifying Section 146 (80JJAA) deduction for employees whose salary exceeds ₹ 25,000 p.m. without checking payroll registers (TYK 6);
  • Failure to report non-deduction of TDS on transport charges in Clause 50 (TYK 4).
Clause (8) of Part I, Second Schedule Failure to Obtain Sufficient Information:
A CA in practice is guilty of misconduct if he "fails to obtain sufficient information which is necessary for expression of an opinion or its exceptions are sufficiently material to negate the expression of an opinion".
  • Issuing Form 146 payee certificate without inspecting books of account or verified tax returns (TYK 7);
  • Signing Form 26 without examining bank statements or loan agreements;
  • Blindly accepting client summaries without independent corroboration.
Clause (1) of Part II, Second Schedule Contravention of Act, Regulations, or Guidelines:
Contravenes any of the provisions of this Act, regulations made thereunder, or any guideline issued by the Council. 		Accepting more than 60 tax audit assignments in violation of Council Guidelines No. 1-CA(7)/02/2008.
Clause (8) of Part I, First Schedule Non-Communication with Previous Auditor:
Accepts a position as auditor previously held by another chartered accountant without first communicating with him in writing. 		Tax auditor must send written communication via Registered Post with Acknowledgement Due (RPAD) or traceable official email to the previous tax auditor prior to accepting appointment.
Disciplinary Sanctions:
Under Section 21B of the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949, where the Disciplinary Committee finds a member guilty of misconduct under the Second Schedule, it may: (i) Reprimand the member; (ii) Remove the member’s name from the Register permanently or for a specified period; and/or (iii) Impose a fine which may extend up to ₹ 5,00,000!

Question 1: Business Tax Audit & 5% Cash Threshold (Sunlight & Co.) TYK 1

Sunlight & Co., a partnership firm engaged in trading of electronic goods, has a turnover of ₹ 2,65,00,000 for the T.Y. 2026-27. Its financial particulars are as under:

Particulars Amount (₹)
(i) Total turnover of T.Y. 2026-27 2,65,00,000
(ii) Aggregate of all receipts during the year (including turnover) 2,90,00,000
(iii) Cash receipts out of (i) above 14,00,000
(iv) Cash receipts out of (ii) above (inclusive of figure in (iii)) 16,00,000
(v) Aggregate of all payments during the year 1,35,00,000
(vi) Cash payments out of (v) above 6,95,000

(a) Is Sunlight & Co. required to get its accounts audited under section 63?
(b) Would your answer change if the cash receipts indicated in (iii) is ₹ 13 Lakh instead of ₹ 14 Lakh?

Statutory Analysis & Step-by-Step Solution:
1. Applicable Legal Provisions [Section 63(1)(a)]:
  • Under Section 63(1)(a), every person carrying on business is required to get his accounts audited if his total sales, turnover or gross receipts exceed ₹ 1 Crore.
  • However, the threshold is elevated to ₹ 10 Crores if:
    1. Aggregate cash receipts in the tax year ≤ 5% of aggregate receipts; AND
    2. Aggregate cash payments in the tax year ≤ 5% of aggregate payments.
Test Numerator (Cash) Denominator (Total) Percentage Threshold Limit Status
Receipts Test ₹ 16,00,000 ₹ 2,90,00,000 5.52% ≤ 5% FAILED (> 5%)
Payments Test ₹ 6,95,000 ₹ 1,35,00,000 5.15% ≤ 5% FAILED (> 5%)

Conclusion for Part (a): Since both cash receipts (5.52%) and cash payments (5.15%) exceed 5%, Sunlight & Co. is NOT eligible for the ₹ 10 Crore safe harbour. Since its turnover of ₹ 2.65 Crore exceeds ₹ 1 Crore, the firm is MANDATORILY REQUIRED to get its accounts audited under Section 63.

Presumptive Taxation Note: Sunlight & Co. cannot declare profits under Section 57 (Old 44AD) because its turnover exceeds ₹ 2 Crores and its cash receipts exceed 5% of turnover (₹ 14L / ₹ 265L = 5.28% > 5%).

Part (b): If Cash Receipts from Turnover are ₹ 13 Lakh instead of ₹ 14 Lakh:
  • Cash receipts out of turnover as a percentage of turnover = ₹ 13,00,000 / ₹ 2,65,00,000 = 4.91% (≤ 5%).
  • Under Section 57(1), the turnover limit for presumptive taxation is elevated from ₹ 2 Crores to ₹ 3 Crores if cash receipts from turnover do not exceed 5%!
  • Since turnover is ₹ 2.65 Crores (within ₹ 3 Crores), Sunlight & Co. can opt for presumptive taxation under Section 57 and declare profits ≥ 8% / 6%.
  • If the firm declares profits as per Section 57, it will NOT be required to get its accounts audited under Section 63!
Question 2: Bearer Cheques & Safe Harbour Disqualification (Mr. Abhinav Ahuja) TYK 2

Mr. Abhinav Ahuja runs a travel agency business since 2010. His total turnover for T.Y. 2026-27 is ₹ 2,90,57,400. Receipts and payments details:

Transaction & Date Amount (₹) Mode of Receipt / Payment
Receipts via UPI, NEFT & A/c Payee Cheques (15.4.26 to 17.3.27) 1,29,58,400 BHIM UPI, NEFT, A/c payee cheques
Receipt on 07.05.2026 13,35,000 Bearer Cheque
Other aggregate receipts (≤ ₹ 2,000 per person) 1,44,21,600 A/c payee cheques, NEFT, UPI (Except ₹ 52,500 in cash)
Total Receipts 2,87,15,000
Aggregate of all payments made during T.Y. 2026-27 2,58,00,000 Electronic & banking modes
Cash payments incurred for expenditure (≤ ₹ 10,000 per person) 20,58,000 Cash

Mr. Abhinav contended that he is not required to get his accounts audited since his turnover is less than ₹ 10 Crores and cash receipts are less than 5%. Examine his contention.

Statutory Analysis & Cash Calculation:
1. Legal Rule on Bearer Cheques:

Under the Explanation to Section 63, receipt or payment by a cheque which is NOT an account payee cheque SHALL BE DEEMED TO BE A RECEIPT OR PAYMENT IN CASH. Therefore, the bearer cheque of ₹ 13,35,000 must be treated as CASH!

  • Total Cash Receipts = Bearer cheque (₹ 13,35,000) + Cash (₹ 52,500) = ₹ 13,87,500.
  • Total Receipts = ₹ 2,87,15,000.
  • Percentage of Cash Receipts = ₹ 13,87,500 / ₹ 2,87,15,000 = 4.83% (≤ 5%). (Receipts test is satisfied).
2. Verification of Cash Payments:
  • Total Cash Payments = ₹ 20,58,000.
  • Total Payments = ₹ 2,58,00,000.
  • Percentage of Cash Payments = ₹ 20,58,000 / ₹ 2,58,00,000 = 7.98% (> 5%!).
  • Payments Test FAILED! The cash payments exceed the 5% statutory limit.
3. Ineligibility for Presumptive Taxation & Conclusion:
  • Under Section 57(6) (Old 44AD(6)), a person carrying on an agency business is explicitly excluded from the presumptive taxation scheme. Since Mr. Abhinav runs a travel agency, he cannot opt for Section 57.
  • Because cash payments exceed 5%, the ₹ 10 Crore safe harbour is NOT available.
  • Since his turnover of ₹ 2.90 Crore exceeds the general threshold of ₹ 1 Crore, Mr. Abhinav Ahuja is MANDATORILY REQUIRED to get his accounts audited under Section 63!
  • His contention is therefore INCORRECT.
Question 3: Thin Capitalization Reporting & Deemed AE Debt (X Ltd.) TYK 3

X Ltd., an Indian company, paid interest of ₹ 95 Lakhs to X Inc., a non-resident associated enterprise (AE). X Ltd. also took a loan of ₹ 5 Crores from Y Inc. (Indian branch of a foreign bank, an unrelated lender) and paid interest of ₹ 50 Lakhs. X Inc. had provided a guarantee to Y Inc. and deposited matching funds of ₹ 3 Crores.

The tax auditor is of the view that interest paid to Y Inc. is also subject to thin capitalization reporting under Section 177 / Clause 42. The management contends that since Y Inc. is an unrelated bank, its interest is not covered. Discuss the auditor's reporting responsibility.

Statutory Analysis & Guidance Note Reporting:
1. Relevant Statutory Law [Section 177(2) / Old Sec 94B(2)]:

Under Section 177(2), where debt is issued by a lender which is not an associated enterprise, the debt shall be deemed to have been issued by an associated enterprise if: (a) an associated enterprise provides an implicit or explicit guarantee to such lender; or (b) deposits a corresponding and matching amount of funds with the lender.

2. Scope of Deemed AE Debt:

In this case, since X Inc. (AE) provided a guarantee and deposited matching funds of ₹ 3 Crores against the ₹ 5 Crore loan from Y Inc., the debt issued by Y Inc. is deemed to be issued by an AE. However, a debate arises whether the entire loan of ₹ 5 Crores is deemed to be from an AE, or only the guaranteed/deposited portion of ₹ 3 Crores.

3. Reporting Obligation under Clause 42 / 41(a) of Form 26:
  • As per Para 18.6 of the ICAI Guidance Note on Tax Audit, where there is a difference of opinion between the management and the tax auditor regarding the interpretation of a legal provision, the tax auditor must NOT simply accept management's contention without qualification.
  • The tax auditor must state both viewpoints in Clause 42 of Part D of Form 26;
  • Furthermore, the auditor must record the difference of opinion as an observation in Paragraph 5 of Part A / Part B of Form 26, quantifying the financial impact on interest disallowance!
Question 4: Transport Contractor TDS Reporting & CA Ethics (ABC Ltd.) TYK 4

ABC Ltd. is engaged in transportation of building materials. It paid truck hiring charges of ₹ 45 Lakhs to individual truck owners (holding less than 10 goods carriages) without deducting TDS, as they furnished PAN declarations under section 393(4) / 194C(6).

The tax auditor is of the view that since no tax was deductible, these transactions need not be reported in Form 26. What is the reporting responsibility of the tax auditor and the consequent ethical implications?

Reporting Responsibility & Disciplinary Code:
1. Reporting Mandate under Clause 50(a) of Form 26:

Under Clause 50(a) of Form 26, the tax auditor is required to report whether the assessee is required to deduct or collect tax as per the provisions of Chapter XIX-B. Payments made to transport contractors under Section 194C are squarely liable to TDS, unless exempted by furnishing PAN under Section 194C(6).
The tax auditor is legally required to report these payments in Clause 50(a), showing the gross amount paid, the fact that PAN was obtained, and that tax was deducted at NIL rate!

2. Ethical Implications under the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949:
  • Clause (7) of Part I of Second Schedule: If the tax auditor omits to report these transactions under Clause 50(a), he fails to exercise due diligence and is grossly negligent in the conduct of his professional duties.
  • Clause (8) of Part I of Second Schedule: If the auditor failed to obtain and verify the declarations and PANs of the truck operators, he is guilty of failing to obtain sufficient information necessary for expression of an opinion.
  • Consequently, the tax auditor is liable to disciplinary action under Section 21B of the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949!
Question 5: Management Representation vs Due Diligence in Cash Payments (LMN Ltd.) TYK 5

A search under section 247 on LMN Ltd. (engaged in jewellery business) revealed that the company made unrecorded cash payments exceeding ₹ 10,000 each to customers. In Form 26, the tax auditor had marked "No" in response to Clause 30 (cash payments u/s 40A(3)), stating that he relied on a Management Representation Letter that no cash payments exceeded ₹ 10,000. Examine the ethical implications.

Professional Misconduct Evaluation:
1. Duty of Tax Auditor under Clause 30 & ICAI Guidance Note:

Under Clause 30 of Form 26, the tax auditor must report cash payments exceeding ₹ 10,000 disallowable under Section 40A(3). The ICAI Guidance Note on Tax Audit clarifies that while practical difficulties exist in verifying cash transactions, the tax auditor must examine primary cash vouchers, cash book records, bank statements, and bearer cheque leaves.

2. Nature of Business & Reliance on Management Representation:

Considering the high-risk, cash-intensive nature of the jewellery trade, the tax auditor was legally bound to conduct test checks and independent verification. A Management Representation Letter is corroborative evidence; it cannot substitute for primary audit procedures! If the auditor was unable to verify cash payments due to inadequate records, he was required to qualify his report in Paragraph 5 of Part A / Part B, which he failed to do.

3. Disciplinary Finding:
The tax auditor failed to exercise due diligence. He is deemed guilty of professional misconduct under Clause (7) of Part I of the Second Schedule to the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949 (gross negligence / failure to exercise due diligence) and Clause (8) (failure to obtain sufficient information).
Question 6: Employee Emoluments Ceiling u/s 146 & Clause 38 Reporting (XYZ & Co.) TYK 6

XYZ & Co., an interior decoration firm, employed 20 new employees on 01.04.2026 at a monthly salary of ₹ 28,000 (Basic Salary ₹ 24,000 + Transport & Special Allowance ₹ 4,000). The firm claimed deduction under section 146 (Old 80JJAA) of ₹ 18 Lakhs (30% of ₹ 60 Lakhs).

The Chartered Accountant certified Form 25 and reported the deduction in Clause 38 of Form 26, contending that basic salary was less than ₹ 25,000 p.m. Is his contention correct? Examine the ethical implications.

Statutory Analysis & Disciplinary Code:
1. Statutory Ceiling under Section 146 / Section 80JJAA:

Under the Explanation to Section 146, an "additional employee" does NOT include an employee whose total emoluments exceed ₹ 25,000 per month!

"Emoluments" means any sum paid or payable to an employee in lieu of his employment by whatever name called (including allowances). Since total emoluments are ₹ 28,000 per month, the 20 new employees do NOT qualify as additional employees. Hence, XYZ & Co. is NOT eligible for deduction under Section 146! The contention of the CA is completely INCORRECT.

2. Ethical Implications under CA Act, 1949:
  • The Chartered Accountant certified an inadmissible claim in Form 25 and wrongly confirmed it in Clause 38 of Form 26;
  • This failure to apply clear statutory definitions constitutes gross negligence in the conduct of professional duties;
  • The CA is guilty of professional misconduct under Clause (7) of Part I of the Second Schedule to the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949!
Question 7: False Certification in Form 146 & Second Schedule Misconduct TYK 7

The Income-tax Department collected documents from ABC Bank revealing that a Chartered Accountant had issued Form 146 certificates (payee certificate for non-deduction of TDS u/s 393(1)) for multiple housing loan borrowers. During inquiry, the CA admitted that he issued the certificates based merely on invoices produced by borrowers, without examining their books of account or verified tax returns, and collected fees partly in cash. Examine the ethical implications.

Professional Misconduct Analysis:
1. Statutory Significance of Form 146:

Form 146 is a statutory certificate wherein an accountant certifies that he has examined the books of account and documents of the payee, verified that the payee furnished return of income u/s 263, took into account such sum for computing taxable income, and paid the tax due thereon.

2. Disciplinary Findings under CA Act, 1949:
  • Clause (7) of Part I, Second Schedule: Issuing statutory certificates without examining books of account or tax returns constitutes gross negligence and absolute failure to exercise due diligence.
  • Clause (8) of Part I, Second Schedule: Certifying compliance without obtaining sufficient audit evidence and verified returns attracts misconduct for failure to obtain sufficient information necessary for expression of an opinion.
  • The Chartered Accountant is liable for disciplinary inquiry before the Disciplinary Committee, leading to reprimand, cancellation of certificate of practice, and removal of name from the Register of Members.
