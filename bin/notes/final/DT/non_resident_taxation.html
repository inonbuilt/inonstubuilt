Section 2(72)

Definition of Non-Resident & Jurisdictional Principles

Statutory Definition Source vs Residence
Statutory Definition • Income-tax Act, 2025 [Corresponding to Section 2(30) of 1961 Act]

Section 2(72) defines a “non-resident” as a person who is not a “resident”, and for the purposes of sections 92, 93 and 168, includes a person who is not ordinarily resident within the meaning of clause (6) of section 6.

Two Core Pillars of Cross-Border Taxation

International taxation of income across cross-border transactions universally rests on two foundational doctrines:

1. Residence-Based Taxation (Worldwide Scope)

Asserts that sovereign tax jurisdictions hold worldwide fiscal allegiance over persons who establish their personal residence, domicile, or permanent home (in case of individuals) or seat of incorporation / Place of Effective Management (POEM in case of companies). Residents are taxable on global income irrespective of where the economic activity takes place.

2. Source-Based Taxation (Territorial Nexus)

Asserts that a country has the primary right to tax income that originates within its geographic borders or economic territory, because the state provides legal infrastructure, capital markets, labor, natural resources, and consumer demand. Source jurisdiction applies regardless of the taxpayer's residential status or nationality.

Core Triad Governing Indian Tax Liability

Under Section 5 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, total income chargeability is determined by three interdependent factors:

  • The Residential Status of the assessee in the relevant tax year under Section 6.
  • The Place of Accrual or Receipt of income (whether actual or deemed under Section 7, 8, or 9).
  • The Timing of Accrual or Receipt during the previous tax year.

Section 6(1) Basic Conditions for Individuals

An individual is deemed to be a Resident in India in any tax year if he satisfies EITHER of the following basic conditions:

Statutory Basic Conditions under Section 6(1)
$$\mathbf{Condition\ [A]:}\quad ext{Physical presence in India for } \ge \mathbf{182\ days} ext{ during the relevant tax year.}$$
— OR —
$$\mathbf{Condition\ [B]:}\quad ext{Physical presence in India for } \ge \mathbf{60\ days} ext{ in relevant tax year } \mathbf{AND} \ge \mathbf{365\ days} ext{ during 4 preceding tax years.}$$
Mandatory Rule for Day Counting

In computing the period of stay in India, the day of arrival into India as well as the day of departure from India are both counted as days of presence in India, unless specific passport stamp entry/exit hours establish otherwise under continuous transit rules.

Section 6(3) Exceptions where Condition [B] (60-day rule) is INAPPLICABLE • Indian Ship Crew & Employment Abroad

In the following exceptional circumstances, Condition [B] is completely overridden and only Condition [A] (182 days) applies to establish residency:

1. Leaving India for Employment

An Indian citizen who leaves India during the previous year for the purposes of employment outside India (including self-employment or business relocation abroad).

2. Indian Crew Member on Indian Ship

An Indian citizen who leaves India as a member of the crew of an Indian ship as defined in clause (18) of section 3 of the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958.

Rule 126 • Computation of Stay Period for Crew of Foreign-Bound Ships

For an eligible voyager (Indian citizen, crew member on a foreign-bound ship), the period to be EXCLUDED from his stay in India is:

$$\mathbf{Excluded\ Period} = ext{Date entered in Continuous Discharge Certificate (CDC) for joining the ship}$$ $$ ext{through to the Date entered in the CDC for signing off from the ship.}$$

Both the joining date and the sign-off date recorded in the Continuous Discharge Certificate (CDC) are excluded from the individual's stay in India.

Where an Indian citizen or a Person of Indian Origin (PIO) who resides outside India comes on a visit to India during the previous year:

Meaning of "Income from Foreign Sources"

"Income from foreign sources" means income which accrues or arises outside India (except income derived from a business controlled in or a profession set up in India) and which is not deemed to accrue or arise in India.

Section 6(7) Deemed Resident Rule for Stateless Individuals [Section 6(1A) of 1961 Act]

An individual, being an Indian citizen, shall be deemed to be a resident in India in any tax year if:

  1. His total income, other than income from foreign sources, exceeds ₹15,00,000 during the previous year; AND
  2. He is not liable to tax in any other country or territory by reason of his domicile or residence or any other criteria of similar nature.
Statutory Status of Deemed Resident

Under Section 6(13)(d), an individual who becomes a resident by virtue of Section 6(7) is ALWAYS a Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR). Foreign source income not connected to an Indian business/profession is completely shielded from Indian taxation!

Section 6(9) Residential Status of HUF, Partnership Firms, LLPs & AOPs

The residential status of non-corporate non-individual entities depends on the seat of Control and Management of its affairs:

Entity Resident Non-Resident RNOR Conditions
Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) If control and management of its affairs is situated wholly or partly in India. If control and management is situated wholly outside India. HUF is RNOR if the Karta is a non-resident in 9 out of 10 prior years OR stay of Karta $\le$ 729 days in 7 prior years.
Partnership Firm / LLP / AOP / BOI If control and management is situated wholly or partly in India. If control and management is situated wholly outside India. Cannot be RNOR (Only Resident or Non-Resident).
Meaning of "Control and Management"

Refers to the controlling power, the head and brain which directs and governs business policies, financial approvals, and key operational decisions. It does not mean merely the place where trading operations are conducted, but where high-level managerial decisions are taken.

Section 6(10) Residential Status of Companies & Place of Effective Management (POEM)

A company is resident in India in any tax year if:

  • It is an Indian company (always resident, irrespective of management location); OR
  • Its Place of Effective Management (POEM) in that year is in India.
Definition of Place of Effective Management (POEM)

POEM means a place where key management and commercial decisions that are necessary for the conduct of the business of an entity as a whole are, in substance, made.

CBDT Guiding Principles for POEM Determination (Circular No. 6/2017)

POEM is tested sequentially based on whether the foreign company satisfies the Active Business Outside India (ABOI) test:

Four Mandatory Criteria for ABOI Test
$$\mathbf{1.\ Passive\ Income\ Test:}\quad ext{Passive income is } \le \mathbf{50\%} ext{ of total income.}$$ $$\mathbf{2.\ Assets\ Test:}\quad ext{Value of assets situated in India is } < \mathbf{50\%} ext{ of total assets.}$$ $$\mathbf{3.\ Employees\ Test:}\quad ext{Number of employees situated/resident in India is } < \mathbf{50\%} ext{ of total employees.}$$ $$\mathbf{4.\ Payroll\ Expense\ Test:}\quad ext{Payroll expense incurred on Indian employees is } < \mathbf{50\%} ext{ of total payroll expense.}$$
If ABOI Test is SATISFIED

POEM is presumed to be OUTSIDE INDIA if the majority of Board of Directors meetings are held outside India. However, if the Board is merely rubber-stamping decisions taken by an Indian parent company/de facto director, POEM will be in India.

If ABOI Test is NOT SATISFIED

Two-stage identification: First, identify the persons who actually make key commercial/managerial decisions. Second, determine the physical place where those decisions are de facto taken (head office location, executive committee seat).

Safe Harbour Turnover Threshold

CBDT Circular No. 8/2017 clarifies that POEM provisions do NOT apply to companies having turnover or gross receipts of ₹50 Crores or less in a financial year.

Section 6(13) Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) Conditions

An individual or HUF is classified as RNOR if they satisfy any of the statutory exclusionary gateways:

Gateway Statutory Condition Scope of Indian Taxation
Clause (a) Non-Resident in India in 9 out of the 10 preceding tax years. Shielded: Foreign income is exempt.
Taxed:
1. Income received/accrued in India.
2. Income deemed received/accrued in India.
3. Foreign income derived from a business controlled in or profession set up in India.
Clause (b) Stay in India for $\le$ 729 days during the 7 preceding tax years.
Clause (c) Indian citizen / PIO visiting India whose total income (excl. foreign sources) > ₹15 Lakhs and stay is $\ge$ 120 days but < 182 days.
Clause (d) Deemed resident under Section 6(7) (stateless Indian citizen).
ILLUSTRATION 1 • Residential Status of Foreign Sportsman Visiting India

Facts: Mr. Keshav Maharaj (KM), a South African cricketer, visits India for 100 days every financial year since FY 2018-19. Determine his residential status for Assessment Year 2026-27 (Previous Year 2025-26).

Statutory Analysis under Section 6(1):

  1. Condition [A] (182 days): Stay in PY 2025-26 is 100 days, which is < 182 days. (Condition [A] NOT satisfied).
  2. Condition [B] (60 days + 365 days):
    • Stay in PY 2025-26 = 100 days ($\ge$ 60 days).
    • Stay in 4 preceding years (2021-22 to 2024-25) = $100 imes 4 = 400 ext{ days}$ ($\ge$ 365 days).
    • KM is NOT an Indian citizen leaving for employment or crew of an Indian ship. Hence Condition [B] applies!
    • Therefore, KM is a Resident under Section 6(1).
  3. Ordinary Residence Test under Section 6(13):
    • Preceding 7 years stay (2018-19 to 2024-25) = $100 imes 7 = 700 ext{ days}$.
    • Since stay in 7 preceding years is 700 days (which is $\le$ 729 days), KM satisfies the condition in Section 6(13)(b).

Conclusion: For AY 2026-27, Mr. Keshav Maharaj is a Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR).

Section 9

Income Deemed to Accrue or Arise in India & Economic Nexus

Statutory Mandate • Section 9(1) of the Income-tax Act, 2025

All income accruing or arising, whether directly or indirectly, through or from:

  • Any business connection in India;
  • Any property in India;
  • Any asset or source of income in India; or
  • The transfer of a capital asset situated in India,

shall be deemed to accrue or arise in India, regardless of where the payment is made or where the contract is concluded.

Business Connection, DAPE & Significant Economic Presence (SEP)

Under Indian jurisprudence and statutory provisions, a Business Connection involves an intimate, real, and continuous relation between the trading activity carried on outside India and the business activity within India.

1. Dependent Agent PE (DAPE)

A business connection includes any business activity carried out through an agent acting on behalf of the non-resident who:

  • Habitually exercises an authority to conclude contracts in India, or habitually plays the principal role leading to the conclusion of contracts that are routinely approved without material modification;
  • Habitually maintains a stock of goods or merchandise in India from which he regularly delivers goods on behalf of the non-resident; or
  • Habitually secures orders in India, mainly or wholly for the non-resident or other non-residents controlling or controlled by the same principal.

2. Significant Economic Presence (SEP) • Digital Economy Nexus

To capture modern cross-border digital transactions that operate without a physical presence, Significant Economic Presence (SEP) constitutes a statutory business connection in India:

Condition [A]: Revenue Threshold
$$\mathbf{Aggregate\ Payments} \ge \mathbf{₹2\ Crores}$$

Any transaction in goods, services, or property carried out by a non-resident with any person in India, including provision of download of data or software in India, where aggregate payments exceed ₹2 Crores in the previous year.

Condition [B]: User Base Threshold
$$\mathbf{Indian\ Users} \ge \mathbf{3,00,000\ (3\ Lakhs)}$$

Systematic and continuous soliciting of business activities or engaging in interaction with 3 lakh or more users in India through digital platforms, websites, apps, or social media networks.

Complete Elimination of Physical Nexus Requirements

An SEP constitutes a business connection in India irrespective of whether:

  • The agreement for the transaction or activity is entered into in India;
  • The non-resident has a residence or place of business in India; or
  • The non-resident renders services in India.
Statutory Exclusions from Business Connection

In the following cases, no income is deemed to accrue or arise in India through or from business operations:

Exclusion Category Statutory Conditions & Scope
1. Purchase of Goods for Export In case of a non-resident, no income shall be deemed to accrue or arise in India to him through or from operations which are confined to the purchase of goods in India for the purpose of export.
2. Collection of News & Views No income deemed in India from operations confined to the collection of news and views in India for transmission out of India by/on behalf of a non-resident running a news agency, newspaper, magazine, or journal.
3. Shooting Cinematograph Films Shooting of films in India by a non-resident who is: (a) an individual not a citizen of India; (b) a firm not having any partner who is a citizen/resident; or (c) a foreign company not having any shareholder who is a citizen/resident of India.
4. Display of Rough Diamonds in SNZ Display of uncut and unassorted rough diamonds in any Special Notified Zone (SNZ) by a foreign company without carrying on any active sales operations in India.
Section 9(2) Income Accruing or Arising Outside India

Income which accrues or arises outside India shall not be deemed to be received or to accrue or arise in India solely by reason of the fact that it is taken into account in a balance sheet prepared in India or remitted to India.

Section 9(3) Salaries Deemed to Accrue or Arise in India

Salaries are deemed to accrue or arise in India in two principal situations:

  1. Service Rendered in India: Income chargeable under the head “Salaries” is deemed to accrue or arise in India if it is earned in India (i.e. service is rendered in India). Rest periods or leave periods forming part of the employment contract and preceded/succeeded by Indian service are also treated as earned in India.
  2. Government Salaries Paid Abroad: Income chargeable under “Salaries” payable by the Government of India to a citizen of India for service outside India is deemed to accrue or arise in India.
Section 10(7) Exemption for Foreign Allowances

While the basic salary of an Indian citizen employed abroad by the Government of India is taxable in India under Section 9(3), all allowances or perquisites paid or allowed outside India by the Government to such citizen are wholly exempt from tax under Section 10(7)!

Section 9(4) Dividends Paid by an Indian Company Outside India

All dividends paid by an Indian company outside India are deemed to accrue or arise in India. Since dividends are taxable in the hands of shareholders under Section 56(2)(i), dividend received by a non-resident from an Indian company is taxable in India and subject to TDS under Section 195 (or Section 196D for FPIs).

Section 9(5) Interest Income Deemed to Accrue or Arise in India

Interest is deemed to accrue or arise in India depending on the category of the borrower/payer:

Payer / Borrower Deemed Accrual in India? Exceptions / Conditions
1. Central Government or State Government Always Deemed No exception. Sovereign debt interest is always Indian source.
2. Resident Person Deemed to Accrue EXCEPTION: Not deemed if the money borrowed is used for a business or profession carried on outside India OR for making/earning any income from any source outside India.
3. Non-Resident Person Conditional Deemed to accrue in India ONLY IF the money borrowed is used for a business or profession carried on by him in India. (Not deemed if used for earning other Indian income).
Section 9(6) Royalty Income & Software Licensing

Royalty is deemed to accrue or arise in India if payable by Government, or by a Resident (except for overseas business/source), or by a Non-Resident (where right/property is used for business/source in India).

Scope of Royalty under Explanations to Section 9(1)(vi)
  • Explanation 2: Consideration for transfer of rights in patent, invention, design, trademark, copyright of literary/artistic/scientific work, films/tapes, or imparting of information/know-how.
  • Explanation 4 (Computer Software): Transfer of all or any rights for use or right to use a computer software (including granting of a licence) irrespective of the medium through which such right is transferred.
  • Explanation 5 (No Possession/Control Needed): Royalty includes consideration whether or not possession or control is with payer, whether used directly by payer, and whether the location of right/property is in India.
  • Explanation 6 (Process): Includes transmission by satellite (uplinking/downlinking), cable, optic fibre, or similar technology.
Landmark Precedent: Engineering Analysis Centre of Excellence Pvt. Ltd. v. CIT [2021] (SC)

Supreme Court Ruling on Shrink-Wrapped / Off-the-Shelf Software:

The Supreme Court held that the purchase of standard copyrighted software (shrink-wrapped software / off-the-shelf software) by Indian distributors or end-users does NOT constitute Royalty under the DTAA (Article 12):

  • There is a vital distinction between the transfer of a “copyright” (which gives the right to commercially exploit, reproduce, or modify) and the transfer of a “copyrighted article” (a user license to utilize the finished software product).
  • Since DTAA definitions take precedence over domestic law under Section 90(2), non-resident software suppliers residing in treaty partner countries are not liable to Indian TDS/tax as royalty, but represent business profits (taxable only if they have a Permanent Establishment in India).
Section 9(7) Fees for Technical Services (FTS)

Fees for Technical Services (FTS) means any consideration (including lump sum) for rendering of any:

Managerial Services
Technical Services
Consultancy Services

Statutory Exclusions from FTS: Does NOT include consideration for any construction, assembly, mining or like project undertaken by the recipient, or consideration which falls under the head “Salaries”.

Section 9(8) Monetary Gifts to Non-Residents

Any sum of money exceeding ₹50,000 paid on or after 5th July, 2019 by an Indian resident to a non-resident or a foreign company is deemed to accrue or arise in India under Section 9(8). Such gift is chargeable to tax under Section 56(2)(x) in the hands of the non-resident recipient.

Section 9(11) Royalty & FTS Taxed Irrespective of Territorial Nexus (Situs of Service Rule)

Under Section 9(11) [corresponding to Explanation to Section 9 of the 1961 Act], interest, royalty, or fees for technical services shall be deemed to accrue or arise in India to a non-resident whether or not:

  • The non-resident has a residence or place of business or business connection in India; OR
  • The non-resident has rendered services in India!
Utilization Test Prevails

Even if 100% of the technical or architectural services are executed sitting outside India, if the service/intellectual property is utilized for a business or property in India, the income is deemed to accrue or arise in India!

ILLUSTRATION 4 • Technical Fees Paid to US Architect for Properties

Facts: Miss Vivitha, a resident in India, pays technical fees of USD 25,000 to Mr. Robert, an architect based in New York (non-resident), for drawing architectural designs for:

  2. A commercial shopping mall being constructed by Vivitha in Mumbai, India.

Mr. Robert performed all design work entirely from his office in New York and never visited India. Determine whether the fees are deemed to accrue or arise in India.

Statutory Analysis under Section 9(7) and Section 9(11):

  • Transaction 1 (New York House): Fees paid by a resident to a non-resident for services utilized for a source of income / property situated outside India. Under Section 9(7), this falls directly within the statutory exception. Hence, USD 25,000 for the NY property is NOT deemed to accrue or arise in India.

Under Section 9(11), since the property is in India, the fees are deemed to accrue or arise in India notwithstanding the fact that Mr. Robert rendered all services in New York and has no place of business in India!

Section 60

Head Office Expenditure Deduction • The 5% Cap

Statutory Mandate • Section 60 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 [Corresponding to Section 44C of 1961 Act]

In the case of a non-resident having an Indian branch or PE, the deduction allowable in respect of Head Office Expenditure in computing business income is capped at the LEAST of the following:

$$\mathbf{Allowable\ HO\ Expenditure} = \mathbf{MINIMUM}\ \text{of}:$$ $$\text{(a) Actual Head Office Expenditure attributable to the Indian business; OR}$$ $$\text{(b) An amount equal to } \mathbf{5\%} \text{ of the } \mathbf{Adjusted\ Total\ Income}.$$
Computation of "Adjusted Total Income"

Adjusted Total Income means the total income of the assessee computed before giving effect to:

  • Deduction in respect of Head Office expenditure under this section;
  • Deductions under Chapter VI-A;
  • Unabsorbed depreciation under Section 32(2);
  • Brought forward business losses under Section 72; and
  • Family pension or investment allowances if any.
Treatment when Adjusted Total Income is a Loss

If the Adjusted Total Income of the Indian branch is a Loss, 5% of Adjusted Total Income is treated as NIL. Consequently, the allowable deduction for Head Office expenditure for that year is NIL!

TYK QUESTION 3 • Interest Charged between Non-Residents for Indian Share Purchase

Facts: Mr. A, a citizen of India, left for USA for the purposes of employment on 1.5.2026. He has not visited India thereafter. Discuss the taxability of the interest charged @ 10% in B's hands, if the said interest has been received in New York.

Statutory Analysis under Section 9(5):

  1. Residential Status of Mr. A and Mr. B: Both left India for employment abroad during PY 2026-27. Mr. A stayed for only 30 days and Mr. B stayed for 23 days (both < 182 days). Under Section 6(3), both are Non-Residents.
  2. Deemed Accrual Rule for Interest u/s 9(5)(c): Interest payable by a non-resident is deemed to accrue or arise in India ONLY IF the borrowed funds are utilized for the purposes of a business or profession carried on by him in India.

Conclusion: The interest of ₹1,00,000 ($10,00,000 \times 10\%$) received by Mr. B in New York is NOT deemed to accrue or arise in India and is therefore NOT taxable in India in the hands of Mr. B.

TYK QUESTION 4 • Off-shore Technical Services for Indian Fertilizer Plant

Statutory Analysis under Section 9(7) and Section 9(11):

  • Under Section 9(7), fees for technical services payable by an Indian resident company are deemed to accrue or arise in India, except where the services are utilized in a business or profession carried on outside India or for earning income from a source outside India.
  • Here, the services are utilized for setting up a fertilizer plant in Orissa, India (an Indian business source).

TYK QUESTION 8 • Atlant France SAS • Software Supply vs Treaty Characterization

Facts: Atlant France SAS, a company incorporated in France, manufactured computer software and licensed it to Indian distributors and end-users. The Indian tax authorities sought to treat the consideration as Royalty under Section 9(6) read with Explanation 4 and demanded tax with interest. The company contends that under the India-France DTAA, the income represents business profits not taxable in India as it has no PE in India. Discuss the validity of the contention in light of judicial precedents.

Judicial Analysis • Engineering Analysis Centre of Excellence (SC):

  1. Under Section 90(2), an assessee has the legal right to be governed by the provisions of the domestic Act or the Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA), whichever is more beneficial.
  2. Under Article 12 of the India-France DTAA, royalty is defined as payment received for the use of or the right to use any copyright of literary, artistic, or scientific work.
  3. In the landmark ruling of Engineering Analysis Centre of Excellence Pvt. Ltd. v. CIT [2021] (SC), the Supreme Court settled this controversy:
    • Distributors or end-users purchasing packaged software acquire only a license to use the copyrighted product (a “copyrighted article”).
    • They do not acquire any right to reproduce, sub-license, reverse-engineer, or commercially exploit the underlying “copyright”.
    • Therefore, such consideration is NOT Royalty under the DTAA.
  4. Such income constitutes Business Profits under Article 7, taxable in India only if Atlant France SAS has a Permanent Establishment (PE) in India.

Conclusion: Since Atlant France SAS has no PE in India, the consideration is NOT taxable in India. The contention of the company is VALID.
