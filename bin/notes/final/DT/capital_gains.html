Sec 66 Scope & Year of Chargeability of Capital Gains

Charging Section

Three Statutory Prerequisites for Chargeability [Section 66 (Old Sec 45)]

Under Section 66 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, any profits or gains arising from the transfer of a capital asset effected in the tax year shall be chargeable to income-tax under the head "Capital gains" and shall be deemed to be the income of the tax year in which the transfer took place.

  1. There must be a Capital Asset as defined under Section 2(14).
  2. There must be a Transfer of such capital asset within the meaning of Section 2(47).
  3. There must be Profits or Gains arising from such transfer (or loss).

Definition of "Capital Asset" [Section 2(14)] & Statutory Exclusions

Category Statutory Inclusions & Exclusions
Inclusions [Sec 2(14)] • Property of any kind held by an assessee, whether or not connected with business or profession.
• Any securities held by a Foreign Institutional Investor (FII / FPI).
• Any Unit Linked Insurance Policy (ULIP) where aggregate annual premium exceeds ₹2,50,000.
• High-premium Life Insurance Policies (issued on or after 01.04.2023) where annual premium exceeds ₹5,00,000.
Statutory Exclusions (NOT Capital Assets) 1. Stock-in-trade: Consumable stores or raw materials held for business or profession.
2. Personal Effects: Movable property held for personal use by the assessee or family. Exceptions (Capital Assets): Jewellery, archaeological collections, drawings, paintings, sculptures, or any work of art.
3. Rural Agricultural Land in India: Agricultural land not situated within municipal limits (pop ≥ 10,000) or notified aerial distances (2 km / 6 km / 8 km).
4. Notified Gold Bonds: 6.5% Gold Bonds 1977, 7% National Defence Gold Bonds 1980, Special Bearer Bonds 1991, and Gold Deposit Bonds / Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs).

Rationalized Holding Periods: 12-Month & 24-Month Regimes

Under the amended statutory architecture of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (Tax Year 2026-27), the holding period for classifying assets as Short-Term Capital Assets (STCA) versus Long-Term Capital Assets (LTCA) has been streamlined into only two distinct thresholds (the former 36-month holding period has been abolished):

Holding Period Threshold Eligible Classes of Capital Assets Classification
≤ 12 Months • Listed equity shares in a company (traded on recognized stock exchange).
• Units of an equity-oriented mutual fund.
• Units of a business trust (REITs / InvITs).
• Zero Coupon Bonds (ZCBs). 		STCA if ≤ 12 mos
LTCA if > 12 mos
≤ 24 Months • All other capital assets, including:
- Immovable property (land, building, residential house, commercial premises).
- Unlisted shares of public and private companies.
- Unlisted debentures, bonds, government securities.
- Jewellery, paintings, sculptures, works of art.
- Units of debt mutual funds (acquired before 01.04.2023). 		STCA if ≤ 24 mos
LTCA if > 24 mos

Scope of "Transfer" under Section 2(47)

  • Sale, Exchange, or Relinquishment: Absolute alienation or surrender of rights in a capital asset.
  • Extinguishment of Any Rights: Complete destruction, forfeiture, or cancellation of contractual rights.
  • Compulsory Acquisition: Acquisition of an asset under any Central, State or local statute.
  • Conversion into Stock-in-Trade: Assessee converts an investment capital asset into business stock-in-trade.
  • Maturity/Redemption of Zero Coupon Bonds: Payment on redemption treated as transfer.
  • Part Performance [Sec 53A of Transfer of Property Act]: Handing over possession of immovable property in part performance of contract for consideration, even without formal registration.
  • Co-operative Housing Society Transactions: Transfer of shares/allotment letters conferring title to enjoy an apartment or building.

Sec 70 Transactions Not Regarded as Transfer (Statutory Exemptions)

Tax Neutrality

Under Section 70 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly Section 47), the following transactions are expressly declared NOT to be transfers, and consequently, no capital gains tax liability arises at the time of the transaction:

Clause Nature of Transaction Statutory Conditions & Cost/Period Treatment
Sec 70(1)(a) Total or Partial Partition of HUF Any distribution of capital assets on the total or partial partition of a Hindu Undivided Family. Cost to coparcener/member is cost to the HUF [Sec 73(1)].
Sec 70(1)(b) Gift, Will, or Irrevocable Trust Any transfer of a capital asset under a gift or will or an irrevocable trust. (Except gifts of shares/debentures allotted under ESOP to employees).
Sec 70(1)(d)/(e) Holding & 100% Subsidiary Companies Transfer between a parent company and its 100% subsidiary, provided the transferee company is an Indian company.
Sec 70(1)(g)/(h) Amalgamation of Companies • Transfer of capital assets by amalgamating company to amalgamated Indian company.
• Transfer of shares by a shareholder in amalgamating company in consideration of allotment of shares in amalgamated Indian company.
Sec 70(1)(j)/(k) Demerger of Undertakings Transfer of capital assets from demerged company to resulting Indian company, and issue of shares by resulting company to shareholders of demerged company.
Sec 70(1)(m)/(n) Conversion of Bonds / Preference Shares • Conversion of bonds, debentures, or deposit certificates into equity shares of the company.
• Conversion of preference shares into equity shares of that company.
Sec 70(1)(p)/(q) Succession of Firm / Proprietorship into Company All assets and liabilities transferred; partners/proprietor receive consideration only in equity shares; aggregate shareholding is not less than 50% of voting power for at least 5 years.
Sec 70(1)(zh) Reverse Mortgage Scheme Any transfer of a capital asset in a transaction of reverse mortgage made under a scheme notified by the Central Government.

Reverse Mortgage Scheme for Senior Citizens [Sec 70(1)(zh) & Sec 10(43)]

• Eligible Borrowers: Senior citizens (individuals aged 60 years or above, or married couples where at least one spouse is 60+).
• Tax Exemption of Loan Inflows [Sec 10(43)]: Any amount received by the senior citizen as a loan, either in lump sum or in monthly installments, is completely exempt from income-tax.
• Alienation of Property: The mortgaging of the residential house with the approved lending institution does not constitute a transfer under Section 70(1)(zh). Capital gains arise only if the lending institution sells the property to recover loan dues upon the borrower's death (heirs can redeem the mortgage by repaying loan without sale).

Withdrawal of Exemption in Certain Cases [Section 71 (Old Sec 47A)]:
  • Holding-Subsidiary Violation: If the 100% shareholding relationship ceases or the capital asset is converted into stock-in-trade within 8 years from the date of transfer, the exemption granted under Section 70 is deemed withdrawn. The capital gains are charged to tax in the hands of the transferor company in the year of violation.
  • Firm/Proprietorship Succession Violation: If the partners/proprietor fail to maintain ≥ 50% voting power for 5 years, the capital gains exempt at succession are taxed in the hands of the successor company in the year of default.

Sec 72 Mode of Computation & Removal of Indexation

Section 72 Formula

Statutory Computation Formula under Section 72 (Old Sec 48)

$$\text{Full Value of Consideration (FVOC)} - \text{Expenses wholly & exclusively incurred on transfer} = \text{Net Sale Consideration}$$ $$\text{Net Consideration} - \text{Cost of Acquisition (COA)} - \text{Cost of Improvement (COI)} = \text{Capital Gains}$$

• Removal of Indexation Benefit: For transfers of long-term capital assets effected on or after 23rd July 2024 (Tax Year 2026-27), the indexation benefit under the second proviso to Section 48 has been withdrawn across all asset classes.
• Special Grandfathering for Real Estate [Resident Ind/HUF]: Where land or building was acquired prior to 23rd July 2024, resident Individuals and HUFs are given the statutory option to pay tax at:
  (a) 12.5% without indexation, OR
  (b) 20% with indexation (indexed up to FY 2024-25 / CII 363),
  whichever results in lower tax liability!

Determination of Cost of Acquisition [Section 73 / Old Sec 49 & 55]

Asset Category Statutory Cost Determination Rule
Assets Acquired before 01.04.2001 Higher of actual cost of acquisition or Fair Market Value (FMV) as on 01.04.2001.
Proviso for Land/Building: FMV as on 01.04.2001 cannot exceed the Stamp Duty Value (SDV) of the property as on 01.04.2001.
Cost of Improvement [Sec 55(1)(b)] • Any capital expenditure incurred on or after 01.04.2001 to increase the value of the asset.
• Any cost of improvement incurred prior to 01.04.2001 is completely ignored.
Gifts, Wills, Partition [Sec 73(1)] Cost of acquisition is deemed to be the cost to the previous owner who acquired it otherwise than by gift/will, plus any cost of improvement incurred by the previous owner and the assessee on or after 01.04.2001.
Bonus Shares • Allotted before 01.04.2001: FMV as on 01.04.2001.
• Allotted on or after 01.04.2001: Cost is NIL.
Right Shares & Renunciation • Original Rights: Amount actually paid to company.
• Renouncer of Right: Cost of right entitlement is NIL; entire sale consideration is Short-Term Capital Gain.
• Purchaser of Right: Purchase price of right paid to renouncer + subscription price paid to company.

Treatment of Advance Money Received & Forfeited [Section 81 / Old Sec 51]

  • Forfeited BEFORE 01.04.2014 [Section 81 / 51]:
    The forfeited advance money is deducted from the cost of acquisition (or FMV as on 01.04.2001) while computing capital gains upon subsequent transfer of the capital asset.
  • Forfeited ON OR AFTER 01.04.2014 [Section 56(2)(ix)]:
    The forfeited advance money is taxable as Income from Other Sources (IFOS) in the year of forfeiture. It is NOT deducted from the cost of acquisition of the asset!

Sec 74 & 75 Capital Gains on Depreciable Assets, MLDs & Debt Funds

Always STCG

Depreciable Assets Capital Gains [Section 74 / Old Sec 50]

Capital gains on assets forming part of a block of assets on which depreciation has been allowed under Section 33 are ALWAYS treated as Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG), irrespective of the period of holding. Capital gains arise only in two situations:

  1. When Moneys Payable Exceeds Block Value: Where the full value of consideration (moneys payable) for assets sold/discarded during the year exceeds the aggregate of [Opening WDV + Actual additions during the year + Transfer expenses]. The excess is chargeable as STCG under Section 74, and the closing WDV of the block becomes NIL.
  2. When Entire Block Ceases to Exist: Where all assets in the block are transferred during the tax year, and the sale proceeds are less than the WDV base. The shortfall is allowable as a Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL), and no depreciation is allowable.

Market Linked Debentures & Specified Mutual Funds [Section 75 / Old Sec 50AA]

Under Section 75, any capital gain arising from the transfer, redemption, or maturity of:

  • Market Linked Debentures (MLDs): Securities having underlying components of financial derivatives.
  • Specified Mutual Funds: Mutual funds acquired on or after 01.04.2023 where not more than 35% of total proceeds are invested in equity shares of domestic companies (debt mutual funds).
Mandatory Short-Term Treatment: Notwithstanding anything contained in the Act, capital gains on MLDs and specified debt mutual funds are deemed to be Short-Term Capital Gains taxable at applicable slab rates, irrespective of whether they were held for more than 24 months. No indexation benefit is allowable.

Computation of Capital Gains in Slump Sale [Section 76 / Old Sec 50B]

A slump sale means the transfer of one or more undertakings as a result of the sale for a lump sum consideration without values being assigned to individual assets and liabilities.

Component Statutory Computation Rules under Section 76
Full Value of Consideration (FVOC) Fair Market Value (FMV) of the capital assets as on the date of transfer, calculated in the prescribed manner under Rule 11UAE (higher of monetary consideration or FMV of assets transferred).
Cost of Acquisition (COA) Net Worth of the undertaking transferred, without indexation.
$$\text{Net Worth} = \text{Aggregate Value of Total Assets} - \text{Total Liabilities of Undertaking}$$
Asset Valuation in Net Worth • Depreciable Assets: Written Down Value (WDV) computed under Section 43(6).
• Section 35AD Assets: Value taken as NIL.
• All other assets: Book values appearing in books of account.
• Revaluation Effect: Any revaluation of assets is strictly ignored!
Holding Period Classification If the undertaking was owned and held for more than 24 months, the capital gain is treated as Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG); otherwise, Short-Term Capital Gain.

📌 Chapter 4 Statutory Master Summary & Exam Keypoints

  • Charging Section 66: 3 prerequisites (Capital asset, Transfer, Gains). Personal effects exclude jewellery, paintings, archaeological collections, sculptures, works of art.
  • Streamlined Holding Periods: 12 Months for listed equity shares, equity funds, business trust units, and ZCBs; 24 Months for all other assets including land, building, and unlisted shares. (36-month period eliminated).
  • New Capital Gains Tax Rates:
    • Section 196 (STCG on STT equity): 20%.
    • Section 198 (LTCG on STT equity): 12.5% on gains exceeding ₹1,25,000 (with 31.01.2018 grandfathering).
    • Section 197 (Other LTCG): 12.5% without indexation (Grandfathering option for resident Ind/HUF for pre-23.07.2024 land/building: lower of 12.5% unindexed or 20% indexed).
  • Depreciable Assets & MLDs: Section 74 (depreciable assets) and Section 75 (MLDs & specified debt mutual funds) are ALWAYS treated as Short-Term Capital Gains.
  • Slump Sale [Sec 76]: Gain is LTCG if held > 24 months. Cost of acquisition is Net Worth (WDV for depreciable assets, NIL for Sec 35AD, book values for others; revaluation ignored).
  • Real Estate 110% Safe Harbor [Sec 77]: SDV accepted as actual consideration if within 110% of sale price. Agreement date SDV valid if advance received by banking channel on/before agreement date.
  • Advance Money [Sec 81]: Forfeited before 01.04.2014 deducted from cost; forfeited on/after 01.04.2014 taxed under IFOS u/s 56(2)(ix).
  • Statutory Exemptions: Section 82 (Residential house - ₹10 Cr cap); Section 85 (Bonds - ₹50L cap within 6 months); Section 87 (Proportionate formula on non-residential asset - ₹10 Cr cap).
