1. Statutory Architecture & Legislative Scheme
#sec-overview

2. Penalty for Under-Reporting & Misreporting of Income [Section 439]
Sec 439 (Old Sec 270A)

Section 439 provides that the Competent Authority (Assessing Officer, Joint Commissioner (Appeals), Commissioner (Appeals), Commissioner, or Principal Commissioner) may, during the course of any proceedings under the Act, direct that any person who has under-reported his income shall pay by way of penalty a sum in addition to tax.

Six Statutory Cases of Under-Reported Income [Section 439(2)]

A person shall be considered to have under-reported his income if Condition (A) is greater than Condition (B) in the following cases:

Case Assessment Scenario Amount (A) Amount (B)
(i) Return of income has been filed Income assessed Income determined in return processed u/s 270(1)(a) [Old 143(1)(a)]
(ii) No return filed or furnished for first time u/s 280 [Old 148] Income assessed Maximum amount not chargeable to tax (Basic Exemption Limit)
(iii) Reassessment / Recomputation Income reassessed or recomputed Income assessed or reassessed immediately before such reassessment
(iv) Return filed and assessed under MAT / AMT provisions (Sec 206) Deemed total income assessed u/s 206(1) or 206(2) Deemed total income determined in return processed u/s 270(1)(a)
(v) No return filed (or first time u/s 280) and assessed under MAT/AMT Deemed total income assessed u/s 206(1) or 206(2) Maximum amount not chargeable to tax (for individual/HUF/AOP/BOI)
(vi) Reassessment under MAT / AMT provisions (Sec 206) Deemed total income reassessed u/s 206(1) or 206(2) Deemed total income assessed immediately before such reassessment
Loss Cases — Reducing Loss or Converting Loss into Income:
Under Section 439(3), a person is deemed to have under-reported his income where the assessment or reassessment has the effect of:
• Reducing the loss declared in the return; or
• Converting that loss into positive income.
In such cases, the under-reported income is computed as:
Under-Reported Income = Loss Claimed (−) Income or Loss Assessed/Reassessed.

Manner of Computation of Under-Reported Income [Section 439(4)-(7)]

Assessment Scenario Computation Formula for Under-Reported Income
Return Filed & First Assessment Assessed Income − Income determined under Section 270(1)(a)
No Return Filed & First Assessment (Company / Firm) Entire Assessed Income
No Return Filed & First Assessment (Other Persons) Assessed Income − Basic Exemption Limit
Reassessment / Recomputation Income reassessed − Income assessed in immediately preceding order
MAT / AMT Adjustment Cases [Sec 439(5)] (A − B) + (C − D) where:
• A = Total income assessed under general provisions;
• B = Total income if reduced by under-reported income under general provisions;
• C = Deemed total income assessed under Section 206 (MAT/AMT);
• D = Deemed total income under Section 206 reduced by under-reported income.

Safe Harbours: What is NOT Under-Reported Income [Section 439(8)]

Under-reported income shall NOT include the following amounts, and no penalty shall be levied thereon:

1
Bona Fide Explanation with Full Disclosure
The assessee offers an explanation and the authority is satisfied that the explanation is bona fide and all material facts have been disclosed to substantiate it.
2
Estimation where Accounts are Correct & Complete
The under-reported income was determined on an estimate because the method employed by the assessee prevents proper deduction of income, but accounts are correct and complete.
3
Assessee's Own Lower Estimate Disclosed
The assessee on his own estimated a lower addition/disallowance on the same issue, included it in his return, and disclosed all material facts.
4
Transfer Pricing Additions with Documented Compliance
Additions made in conformity with Arm's Length Price determined by TPO, where the assessee maintained prescribed documentation u/s 171 and declared the transaction under Chapter X.

Aggravated Category: Cases of Misreporting [Section 439(10)]

Where under-reporting of income results from any of the following misreporting circumstances, penalty is levied at the severe rate of 200% of tax payable:

  • (i) Misrepresentation or suppression of facts;
  • (ii) Failure to record investments in the books of account;
  • (iii) Claim of expenditure not substantiated by any evidence;
  • (iv) Recording of any false entry in the books of account;
  • (v) Failure to record any receipt in the books of account having a bearing on total income;
  • (vi) Failure to report any international transaction, deemed international transaction, or specified domestic transaction under Chapter X;
  • (vii) Unexplained cash credits, investments, or monies under Sections 102 to 106 (Section 195(1)(b) / Old 115BBE) determined by the AO and not reflected in the return furnished u/s 263.

Quantum of Penalty & Tax Calculation Methodology

Section Nature of Default Quantum of Penalty
Section 439(9) Under-reporting of income (standard) 50% of tax payable on under-reported income
Section 439(10) Under-reporting in consequence of Misreporting 200% of tax payable on under-reported income
Manner of Calculating Tax Payable on Under-Reported Income:
• No Return Filed / First Time u/s 280: Tax calculated on under-reported income as increased by the basic exemption limit as if it were total income.
• Where Preceding Income is a Loss: Tax calculated on under-reported income as if it were the total income.
• In Any Other Case: (X − Y) where:
  • X = Tax calculated on (Under-reported income + Total income determined u/s 270(1)(a) or preceding order);
  • Y = Tax calculated on Total income determined u/s 270(1)(a) or preceding order.

Mr. Ram, a resident individual aged 55, has not furnished his return of income for Tax Year 2026-27. Total income assessed under section 271 (Best Judgment Assessment) is ₹14,00,000. Is penalty u/s 439 attracted, and what is its quantum?

Solution & Computation:

Since Mr. Ram did not file a return, his under-reported income is the entire assessed income of ₹14,00,000. His tax is computed under the default new tax regime under Section 202:

  • Up to ₹4,00,000: Nil
  • ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 @ 5%: ₹20,000
  • ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 @ 10%: ₹40,000
  • ₹12,00,001 – ₹14,00,000 @ 15%: ₹30,000
  • Total Base Tax = ₹90,000 + HEC @ 4% (₹3,600) = ₹93,600

Penalty leviable @ 50% of tax payable = 50% × ₹93,600 = ₹46,800.

Statutory Immunity & Executive Discretion

3. Reduction or Waiver of Penalty by PCIT / CIT [Section 469]

While the imposition of penalties under Chapter XXI of the Income-tax Act, 2025 is primarily compensatory and deterrent, the Legislature recognises that genuine, contrite taxpayers who proactively disclose concealed income or experience severe financial catastrophe should not be subject to ruinous penalties. Section 469 vests discretionary power in senior revenue administrators to grant relief.

3.1 Exercise of Power to Reduce or Waive Penalty u/s 439 [Section 469(1) & (2)]

Under Section 469(1), the Principal Commissioner of Income-tax (PCIT) or Commissioner of Income-tax (CIT) is empowered to reduce or waive the amount of penalty imposed or imposable on a person under Section 439 (under-reporting or misreporting of income).

Nature of Power: The exercise of power under Section 469 is purely discretionary. The PCIT/CIT may initiate action suo motu (on his own motion) or upon receiving an application from the assessee.

Statutory Conditions to be Fulfilled [Section 469(2)]:

The Principal Commissioner or Commissioner can reduce or waive penalty under Section 439 only if he is satisfied that the applicant has met both of the following cumulative conditions:

Condition Statutory Requirement & Legal Tests
(a) Full & True Disclosure Voluntarily and in Good Faith
  • The assessee must, before the Assessing Officer detected any concealment of particulars of income or inaccuracy of particulars furnished, have made a full and true disclosure of such particulars voluntarily and in good faith.
  • Statutory Deeming Fiction: A person shall be deemed to have made full and true disclosure of his income or particulars relating thereto if the difference between the assessed and returned income does not attract penalties under Section 439.
  • If the disclosure is triggered by search, survey, third-party information notices, or requisition of books, it is not "voluntary" and relief is barred.
(b) Co-operation & Tax Settlement
  • The assessee has co-operated in any enquiry relating to the assessment of his income.
  • The assessee has paid or made satisfactory arrangements to pay any tax or interest payable in consequence of an order passed under this Act in respect of the relevant tax year.
3.2 Prior Approval of Higher Authorities [Section 469(3)]

Where an application under Section 439 falls into high-value brackets, the PCIT/CIT cannot act unilaterally. Prior approval must be obtained from the Principal Chief Commissioner (PCCIT) or Chief Commissioner (CCIT) or Principal Director General (PDGIT) or Director General (DGIT) if:

  • The amount of income in respect of which penalty is imposed/imposable for the relevant tax year (or aggregate income if disclosure relates to multiple tax years) exceeds ₹ 5,00,000; OR
  • The penalty imposed or imposable under Section 439 for the relevant tax year (or aggregate penalty for multiple tax years) exceeds ₹ 1,00,000.
3.3 Relief Available Only Once in a Lifetime [Section 469(4)]

Section 469(4) incorporates a strict once-in-a-lifetime restriction:

Where an order reducing or waiving penalty has been made under Section 469(1) or under Section 469(5) in favour of any person for any tax year, such person shall not be entitled to any relief under this section for any other tax year subsequently!

This ensures habitual tax evaders cannot repeatedly exploit executive mercy to shield systematic evasion.

3.4 Reduction or Waiver in Cases of Genuine Hardship [Section 469(5)] & CBDT Circular No. 784

Under Section 469(5), the Principal Commissioner or Commissioner may, on an application made by an assessee and after recording reasons in writing, reduce or waive any penalty payable under the Act if satisfied that:

  1. Payment of such penalty would cause genuine hardship to the assessee; and
  2. The assessee has co-operated in any enquiry relating to assessment or any proceeding for recovery of any amount due from him.
Key Facets of Section 469(5):
  • Scope: Unlike Section 469(1) which applies only to Section 439 penalties, Section 469(5) covers any penalty payable under the Act.
  • High Value Approval: If the penalty imposable exceeds ₹ 1,00,000, the order can only be passed with the prior approval of the Principal Chief Commissioner or Chief Commissioner.
  • Strict Time Limit [Section 469(6)]: Every order accepting or rejecting the application under Section 469(5) must be passed within 12 months from the end of the month in which the application is received by the PCIT/CIT.
CBDT Circular No. 784 (Genuine Hardship Guidelines):
  • "Genuine hardship" denotes severe financial stringency, commercial insolvency, destruction of business assets by natural disaster, or debilitating personal catastrophe.
  • Mere inconvenience or difficulty in liquidating investments does not amount to genuine hardship.
  • The financial inability must be genuine, established through audited balance sheets, asset-liability statements, and bank records.
Landmark Ruling K.S.N. Murthy v. Chairman, CBDT (2001) 252 ITR 741 (AP)

Issue: What is the legal standard governing the exercise of power by the Commissioner under the waiver provisions? Can an application be rejected summarily without giving objective reasons?
Held: The High Court held that the power vested in the Commissioner is a quasi-judicial discretion. It must be exercised fairly, reasonably, objectively, and not arbitrarily or mechanically. The Commissioner is legally bound to examine whether the twin statutory conditions—(i) genuine hardship and (ii) cooperation—are met. If they are satisfied, relief should normally be granted. Any rejection order must record lucid, rational findings reflecting proper application of mind to the taxpayer's financial condition.

5. Mandatory Fees in Lieu of Penalties [Sections 427, 428 & 429]

Under modern direct tax administration, routine procedural delays are decoupled from subjective, litigious penalty proceedings and converted into automatic, mandatory administrative fees. These fees are payable prior to filing the return, report, or statement, and cannot be defended on grounds of "reasonable cause" under Section 470!

Section 427: Late Return Filing Fee

Leviable for default in furnishing return of income under Section 263(1) within the prescribed statutory due date:

Standard Late Fee: ₹ 5,000
Small Taxpayer Relief:
(Total Income ≤ ₹ 5,00,000) Max ₹ 1,000

Note: Fee is mandatory and payable before furnishing the belated return under Section 263(4).

Section 428: Statements & Reports Fee

Automatic daily or fixed fees for statement / audit non-compliances:

  • 428(a) TDS/TCS Statement: ₹ 200/day of delay u/s 397(3)(b)/(e) (capped at total tax deductible/collectible).
  • 428(b) SFT Default: ₹ 200/day of delay in furnishing SFT u/s 507.
  • 428(c) Tax Audit Delay u/s 63: ₹ 75,000 or 0.75% of turnover/gross receipts, whichever is lower (Subject to maximum ceiling of ₹ 1,50,000).
  • 428(d) Accountant Report u/s 172: ₹ 50,000 or ₹ 1,00,000 (Form 3CEB).
  • 428(e) Aadhaar Intimation: ₹ 1,000 fee for delayed intimation.

Leviable on charitable trusts, scientific research associations, political parties, and exempt entities required to file returns under Section 349 or 350:

Daily Failure Fee: ₹ 200 / day for every day during which the failure continues!

Failure to file return by the due date also triggers forfeiture of exemption under Sections 337 to 348 for that tax year.

Statutory Immunity for Civil Penalties

6. Penalty Not Leviable in Certain Cases — Reasonable Cause [Section 470]

Under Section 470, the Legislature establishes a fundamental statutory shield against civil penalties where the taxpayer's failure is not deliberate, contumacious, or fraudulent, but stems from a bona fide, reasonable cause.

6.1 Statutory Architecture of Section 470

Section 470 provides that no penalty shall be leviable on a person or assessee under:

Sections 441, 442, 446, 448, 449, 450, 451, 452, 453, 454, 455, 456, 457, 458, 459, 460, 461, 462, 463, 465(1)(c), 465(1)(d), 465(2), 466, 467, or 468

...if the person or assessee proves that there was a reasonable cause for the said failure.

CRITICAL EXCLUSIONS — Sections where Reasonable Cause is NOT a Defense:
  • Section 439: Under-reporting and misreporting of income (Safe harbours are strictly codified in Section 439(8); general "reasonable cause" cannot override Section 439).
  • Section 444: False entries, forged invoices, or omission of entries in books to evade tax (Fraud and deliberate fabrication cannot coexist with "reasonable cause").
  • Section 445: Trust income applied for personal benefit of related persons.
  • Sections 427, 428, 429: Mandatory administrative fees (Automatic statutory levies).

6.2 Landmark Judicial Precedent on Reasonable Cause

Karnataka High Court (2025) CIT v. Jindal Tractebel Power Co. Ltd. (2025) 474 ITR 77 (Karn.)
Issue for Determination:

Whether reliance on a bona fide professional / legal advice from an expert tax counsel can be treated as a "reasonable cause" for failure to deduct tax at source (TDS), and consequently, whether penalty under Section 448 is leviable for such non-deduction?

High Court's Findings & Ratio Decidendi:

1. The High Court observed that a cause is "reasonable" when it is a cause which prevents a reasonable man of ordinary prudence, acting under normal circumstances, from doing a statutory duty.
2. Where an assessee, acting in good faith, obtains an opinion from a qualified legal or tax expert on a complex, debateable issue of law (e.g., whether a payment constitutes "fees for technical services" or reimbursement of expenses) and acts in accordance with such opinion, such reliance constitutes a valid "reasonable cause" within the meaning of Section 470.
3. In the absence of deliberate defiance, contumacious conduct, or dishonest concealment, penalty under Section 448 cannot be imposed!

Procedure & Limitation

7. Procedure for Assessment of Penalties [Section 471] & Time Limits [Section 472]

Penal proceedings are quasi-criminal in nature. The Income-tax Act strictly mandates strict adherence to the principles of natural justice and statutory supervisory approvals under Section 471, as well as definitive limitation periods under Section 472.

7.1 Procedure for Assessment of Penalties [Section 471]

Natural Justice & Show Cause Notice [Sec 471(1)]:

No order imposing a penalty under Chapter XXI shall be made unless the assessee has been heard, or has been given a reasonable opportunity of being heard. A formal Show Cause Notice (SCN) specifying the exact statutory default is a mandatory condition precedent.

Supervisory Approval of Joint Commissioner [Sec 471(2)]:

No order imposing penalty shall be passed without prior approval of the Joint Commissioner if:

  • Order passed by an Income-tax Officer (ITO) and penalty exceeds ₹ 10,000;
  • Order passed by an Assistant Commissioner (ACIT) or Deputy Commissioner (DCIT) and penalty exceeds ₹ 20,000.
MAJOR PROCEDURAL REFORM
Single Assessment-cum-Penalty Order Effective 01.04.2027 [Section 471(4) & (5)]

Section 471(4) & (5) introduce a paradigm procedural shift in Indian tax administration effective 1st April, 2027:

  • No Separate Penalty Order: Irrespective of anything contained in any other provision, penalty under Section 439 (under-reporting or misreporting) shall NOT be imposed by a separate order!
  • Unified Order: The penalty under Section 439 shall be proposed as part of the Draft Assessment Order and imposed directly in the Assessment Order or Reassessment Order itself!
  • Elimination of Dual Appeals: This landmark unification eliminates parallel, protracted litigation where quantum and penalty proceeded on separate appellate paths, fusing them into a single appeal before CIT(Appeals) and the ITAT!

Vicarious & Culpable Criminal Liability

9. Corporate & Entity Liability [Sections 487, 488 & 490]

Because artificial legal entities act through human agents, criminal law must delineate exactly which individuals bear personal criminal culpability when a company, firm, or HUF commits an offence under the Act.

9.1 Offences by Companies & Key Management Personnel [Section 487]

Under Section 487(1), where an offence under this Act has been committed by a company, every person who at the time the offence was committed was in charge of, and was responsible to, the company for the conduct of the business of the company, as well as the company itself, shall be deemed to be guilty of the offence and shall be liable to be proceeded against and punished accordingly.

Statutory Defense of In-Charge Person [Proviso to Sec 487(1)]:

The person in charge shall not be liable to punishment if he proves:

  1. That the offence was committed without his knowledge; OR
  2. That he had exercised all due diligence to prevent the commission of such offence.
Direct Liability of Directors & Officers [Sec 487(2)]:

Where an offence has been committed by a company and it is proved that the offence has been committed with the consent or connivance of, or is attributable to any neglect on the part of, any director, manager, secretary, or other officer of the company, such person shall also be deemed guilty!

Statutory Explanation: For the purposes of Section 487:
  • "Company" means any body corporate and includes a firm or other association of individuals; and
  • "Director", in relation to a firm, means a partner in the firm.

9.2 Offences by Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) [Section 488]

Under Section 488(1), where an offence under this Act has been committed by a Hindu Undivided Family (HUF), the Karta thereof shall be deemed to be guilty of the offence and shall be liable to be proceeded against and punished accordingly.

Statutory Defense of Karta [Proviso to Sec 488(1)]:

The Karta shall not be liable to any punishment if he proves:

  1. That the offence was committed without his knowledge; OR
  2. That he had exercised all due diligence to prevent the commission of such offence.
Liability of Other Family Members [Sec 488(2)]:

Where an offence has been committed by an HUF and it is proved that the offence has been committed with the consent or connivance of, or is attributable to any neglect on the part of, any coparcener or member of the HUF, such member shall also be deemed to be guilty of that offence!

9.3 Presumption as to Culpable Mental State [Section 490]

In general criminal jurisprudence, the prosecution bears the burden of proving mens rea (guilty mind) beyond reasonable doubt. Section 490 creates a statutory reversal of the burden of proof in tax prosecutions:

Statutory Principle Legal Operation under Section 490
Presumption of Mens Rea [Sec 490(1)] In any prosecution for an offence which requires a culpable mental state on the part of the accused, the Court SHALL PRESUME the existence of such mental state. It shall be a defense for the accused to prove that he had no such mental state with respect to the act charged.
Scope of Culpable Mental State [Sec 490(2)] "Culpable mental state" includes intention, motive, knowledge of a fact, or belief in, or reason to believe, a fact.
Strict Standard of Proof [Sec 490(3)] A fact is said to be proved only when the court believes it to exist beyond reasonable doubt and NOT merely when its existence is established by a preponderance of probability! The assessee cannot discharge this burden by vague assertions or plausible excuses.
Supreme Court Precedent Union of India v. Bhavecha Machinery

Ratio Decidendi: The Supreme Court affirmed that once the physical act (actus reus) constituting a default or false entry is proved by the Department, Section 490 immediately shifts the entire burden upon the accused to establish absence of criminal intent. The accused must present concrete, documented circumstances showing that the failure occurred due to factors beyond his control and without any deliberate evasion. Mere silence or denial is legally fatal.

Settlement, Pardon & Compounding

10. Immunity, Waiver & Compounding Framework [Sections 486, 440, 519, 491, 492, 520 & 521]

To prevent prolonged prosecution of remediable defaults and encourage voluntary compliance, the Income-tax Act contains four distinct statutory escape corridors: (i) reasonable cause immunity for TDS/TCS; (ii) waiver of penalty and prosecution immunity u/s 440; (iii) executive compounding; and (iv) Central Government pardon u/s 519.

10.1 Reasonable Cause Immunity for TDS/TCS Prosecution [Section 486]

Under Section 486, no person shall be punishable with any punishment for any failure under:

  • Section 476: Failure to pay tax deducted at source (TDS); OR
  • Section 477: Failure to pay tax collected at source (TCS),

...if he proves that there was reasonable cause for such failure. If the deductor/collector was prevented by bank strikes, sudden freezing of accounts, or bona fide uncertainty regarding taxability backed by professional advice, criminal courts will acquit the accused under Section 486.

10.2 Application for Waiver of Penalty & Prosecution Immunity [Section 440]

Section 440 provides a powerful fast-track dispute settlement window whereby an assessee accepts an assessment order, waives the right of appeal, and secures statutory immunity from Section 439 penalty and Section 478/479 prosecution.

Mandatory Conditions for Application [Sec 440(1)]:
  • Tax & Interest Payment: Pays the tax and interest payable as per the assessment order u/s 270(10) or reassessment order u/s 279 within the period specified in the notice of demand;
  • Payment of Additional Income-Tax:
    • 100% of tax on under-reported income where penalty is leviable u/s 439(11)(a) to (f) (routine under-reporting);
    • 120% of tax on under-reported income where penalty is leviable u/s 439(11)(g) (reassessment after search/survey);
  • No Appeal Filed: No appeal has been filed against the assessment/reassessment order, or any appeal filed has been withdrawn;
  • Strict Time Limit: Application must be made to the Assessing Officer within one month from the end of the month in which the assessment order is received.
Procedure & Finality of Order [Sec 440(2)-(5)]:
  • Order within 3 Months: The Assessing Officer shall, within a period of 3 months from the end of the month in which the application is received, pass an order accepting or rejecting the application.
  • Misreporting Exception: Immunity CANNOT be granted if the penalty proceedings have been initiated for misreporting of income u/s 439(10)!
  • Opportunity of Being Heard: No order rejecting the application shall be passed unless the assessee has been given an opportunity of being heard.
  • Absolute Finality: The order of the Assessing Officer granting or rejecting immunity is final and non-appealable! (No appeal lies to CIT(A) or ITAT).

10.3 Power of Central Government to Tender Immunity / Pardon [Section 519]

Under Section 519, the Central Government is vested with sovereign authority to grant a tender of immunity (pardon) to obtain state evidence:

If the Central Government is of opinion that with a view to obtaining the evidence of any person appearing to have been directly or indirectly concerned in or privy to the concealment of income or evasion of tax, it is necessary or expedient so to do, it may tender to such person:
  • Immunity from prosecution for any offence under this Act, or under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, or under any other Central Act; AND
  • Immunity from imposition of any penalty under this Act.

Condition Precedent: Full and true disclosure of the whole circumstances relating to concealment or evasion. If the person fails to comply with the condition or conceals anything, the tender of immunity is automatically revoked and he is prosecuted!

10.4 Sanction, Compounding & Cognizance [Sections 491, 492, 520 & 521]

Sanction for Prosecution [Sec 491(1)]:

Prosecution under Sections 473 to 484 can be initiated only after previous sanction of the Principal Commissioner or Commissioner or JCIT(A) or CIT(A).

Immunity where Penalty Waived [Sec 491(3)]:

No prosecution for Section 478 (evasion) or Section 482 (false verification) can be initiated in relation to an assessment where penalty u/s 439 has been waived or reduced u/s 469.

Compounding of Offences [Sec 492]:

Any offence under Chapter XXII may be compounded, either before or after the institution of proceedings, by the Principal Chief Commissioner, Chief Commissioner, Principal Director General, or Director General upon payment of compounding fee.

Cognizance & Disqualification [Sec 520 & 521]:

Sec 520: No court inferior to Judicial Magistrate First Class (JMFC) or Metropolitan Magistrate shall try offences.
Sec 521: The Probation of Offenders Act, 1958 and Section 401 of BNSS 2023 do not apply to any person convicted under this Act (unless under 18 years of age)!

Special Foreign Tax Regime

11. The Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income & Assets) and Imposition of Tax Act, 2015

The Black Money Act, 2015 is a specialized, draconian, standalone enactment designed to unmask, tax, and penalize undisclosed foreign income and assets stashed abroad by Indian residents. It completely supersedes the regular machinery of the Income-tax Act, imposing a flat 30% tax, zero deductions, zero loss set-offs, a mandatory 300% penalty, and up to 10 years of rigorous imprisonment.

11.1 Basis of Charge [Chapter II – Sections 3 to 5]

Scope of Charge [Section 3(1)]:
  • Flat Tax Rate: Undisclosed foreign income and assets are chargeable to tax at a flat rate of 30%. No slab benefits, exemptions, or basic exemption limits apply.
  • Year of Charge: The tax is charged in the previous year in which such undisclosed asset comes to the notice of the Assessing Officer! It is completely irrelevant when the foreign asset was actually acquired (whether 10, 20, or 40 years ago).
  • Assessee Definition: Applies to any person who is a Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR) in India in the tax year of detection, OR who was a resident in India in the previous year in which the undisclosed foreign asset was acquired!
Scope of Undisclosed Foreign Income & Asset [Sec 4]:

Total undisclosed foreign income and asset includes:

  1. Income from a source located outside India which has not been disclosed in the return of income filed u/s 263(1), 263(4), or 263(5);
  2. Income from a foreign source where no return of income was filed; and
  3. The fair market value of any undisclosed asset located outside India.

Non-Double Inclusion [Sec 4(2)]: Any amount taxed under the Black Money Act shall not form part of total income under the Income-tax Act, 2025.

11.2 Valuation Rules for Foreign Assets [Rule 3 of Black Money Rules, 2015]

Under Section 3(2) read with Rule 3, the value of an undisclosed foreign asset is its Fair Market Value (FMV) determined on the valuation date (1st April of the previous year in which the asset comes to the notice of the AO):

Class of Foreign Asset Statutory Method for Determining Fair Market Value (FMV)
(a) Bullion, Jewellery or Precious Stones [Rule 3(1)(a)] Higher of:
  1. Its cost of acquisition; and
  2. The price which it would ordinarily fetch if sold in the open market on the valuation date (backed by a valuation report from an authorized valuer).
(b) Archaeological Collections, Art, Drawings, Sculptures [Rule 3(1)(b)] Higher of:
  1. Its cost of acquisition; and
  2. The price which it would ordinarily fetch if sold in the open market on the valuation date.
(c) Quoted Shares & Securities [Rule 3(1)(c)(I)] Higher of:
  1. Its cost of acquisition; and
  2. The average of the lowest and highest price of such share/security quoted on an established recognized stock exchange on the valuation date.
(d) Unquoted Equity Shares [Rule 3(1)(c)(II)] Higher of:
  1. Its cost of acquisition; and
  2. The value determined under the formula:
    $$ ext{Value per share} = (A + B - L) imes \left( rac{PV}{PE} ight)$$
    Where:
    • $A$ = Book value of all assets (other than bullion, jewellery, shares, art, immovable property);
    • $B$ = Fair market value of bullion, jewellery, shares, art, and immovable property;
    • $L$ = Book value of liabilities (excluding paid-up equity capital, reserves, and provisions);
    • $PE$ = Total amount of paid-up equity share capital as shown in the balance sheet;
    • $PV$ = Paid-up value of the equity shares held by the assessee.
(e) Foreign Bank Accounts [Rule 3(1)(e)] Sum of ALL deposits made in the bank account from the date of opening of the account until the valuation date!
(Withdrawals are completely ignored; every deposit ever credited is aggregated to establish peak undisclosed value).
(f) Immovable Property [Rule 3(1)(f)] Higher of:
  1. Its cost of acquisition; and
  2. The price which the property would fetch if sold in the open market on the valuation date (supported by a report from an authorized valuer).
(g) Transferred Assets Prior to Valuation Date [Rule 3(2)] Where an asset was transferred before the valuation date: Higher of its cost of acquisition and the sale price / consideration received. If transferred for inadequate consideration, FMV on date of transfer applies.
Foreign Currency Conversion Rules [Rule 3(4) & (5)]:

The FMV computed in foreign currency is converted into Indian Rupees ($ ext{INR}$) using the telegraphic transfer buying rate (TTBR) adopted by the State Bank of India (SBI) on the valuation date (1st April of the relevant previous year).

If the asset is in a non-permitted currency, it is first converted into US Dollars (USD) at the central bank rate of that country, and thereafter converted into INR at the SBI rate on the valuation date.

In-Text Illustration: Acquisition of New Asset Out of Transferred Old Asset [Rule 3(3)]

Facts: House A located in Country X was bought in 1995 for ₹ 15 Lakh. It was sold in 2000 for ₹ 22 Lakh, and the sale proceeds were deposited into a foreign bank account in Country X. In 2004, ₹ 35 Lakh was withdrawn from this bank account to purchase House B for ₹ 35 Lakh. In 2026-27, these assets come to the notice of the AO. On 01.04.2026, the sum of all deposits in the bank account is ₹ 60 Lakh, and the open market value of House B is ₹ 48 Lakh.

Asset Valuation Basis under Rule 3(1), 3(2) & 3(3) Taxable Value
House A Higher of cost (₹ 15L) or sale price (₹ 22L) = ₹ 22L. Less consideration reinvested in foreign bank account (₹ 22L). ₹ NIL
Foreign Bank Account Sum of all deposits (₹ 60L) less consideration invested in House B (₹ 35L) ₹ 25 Lakh
House B Higher of cost of acquisition (₹ 35L) or open market value on 01.04.2026 (₹ 48L) ₹ 48 Lakh
Total Value of Undisclosed Foreign Assets Chargeable to Tax ₹ 73 Lakh

11.3 Computation Rules & Permissible Deductions [Section 5]

  • No Deductions or Allowances [Sec 5(1)]: No deduction in respect of any expenditure or allowance or set-off of any loss shall be allowed against undisclosed foreign income or assets!
  • Deduction for Previously Assessed Income [Sec 5(2)]: Where the assessee proves to the satisfaction of the AO that the foreign asset was acquired out of income that was already assessed to tax under the Income-tax Act in any prior year, the value of the asset shall be proportionately reduced!
Proportionate Deduction Formula for Immovable Property [Section 5(2)]:
$$ ext{Taxable Value } (Z) = X - Y$$ $$ ext{Where } Y = X imes \left( rac{ ext{Assessed Income Inflow}}{ ext{Total Cost of Acquisition}} ight)$$
  • $X$ = Fair market value of the undisclosed immovable property on the valuation date;
  • $Y$ = Proportion of FMV attributable to previously assessed income;
  • $Z$ = Net undisclosed value chargeable to tax under the Black Money Act.
In-Text Illustration: Computation of Net Undisclosed Asset Value u/s 5(2)

Facts: A foreign house property was acquired by Mr. A in P.Y. 2011-12 for ₹ 60 Lakh. Out of this investment of ₹ 60 Lakh, ₹ 35 Lakh was earned from income assessed to tax in India in earlier years. The asset comes to the notice of the AO in P.Y. 2026-27, when its FMV is ₹ 120 Lakh.

  • $X$ = Fair market value on 01.04.2026 = ₹ 120 Lakh
  • $Y$ = Previously taxed proportion = ₹ 120 Lakh × (₹ 35 Lakh / ₹ 60 Lakh) = ₹ 70 Lakh
  • $Z$ = Taxable Undisclosed Foreign Asset Value = ₹ 120 Lakh - ₹ 70 Lakh = ₹ 50 Lakh
• Tax Payable under Section 3 @ 30% of ₹ 50 Lakh = ₹ 15,00,000
• Mandatory Penalty under Section 41 @ 300% of Tax = ₹ 45,00,000
• Total Statutory Exaction = ₹ 60,00,000 (Equal to 120% of net undisclosed value)!

Question 7: Prosecution for Belated Return & De Minimis Safe Harbour (KLM Firm) TYK 7

The Assessing Officer lodged a complaint against M/s. KLM, a partnership firm, under section 479 for wilful failure to furnish return of income. The tax payable as per original assessment order was ₹ 10,200. On appeal, the tax liability was reduced by the CIT(Appeals) to ₹ 8,900.

Is the complaint lodged against the firm maintainable? Would your answer change if the person was KLM Ltd., a company?

Statutory Analysis & Supreme Court Precedent:
A. Position for M/s. KLM (Partnership Firm):
  • Under Section 479(1), wilful failure to furnish return of income is punishable with imprisonment or fine.
  • However, Section 479(2)(b) creates an explicit statutory safe harbour: No prosecution proceedings shall be maintainable against a person, NOT BEING A COMPANY, if the tax payable on total income determined on regular assessment (reduced by advance tax and TDS/TCS) does not exceed ₹ 10,000!
  • In Guru Nanak Enterprises v. ITO (2005) 279 ITR 30, the Supreme Court held that the "tax payable on regular assessment" refers to the final tax liability determined after giving effect to the order of the appellate authority!
  • Since the tax liability of M/s. KLM was reduced to ₹ 8,900 (which does not exceed ₹ 10,000), the statutory safe harbour under Section 479(2) applies. Hence, the prosecution complaint is NOT maintainable and must be quashed!
B. Position if the Person was KLM Ltd. (A Company):
  • Yes, the answer would change! The de minimis statutory safe harbour of Section 479(2)(b) specifically excludes companies ("the tax payable by such person, not being a company...").
  • Therefore, a company cannot claim immunity under Section 479(2)(b), even if its final tax liability is less than ₹ 10,000.
  • However, since the amount of tax sought to be evaded is ₹ 8,900 (does not exceed ₹ 10 Lakh), the punishment against KLM Ltd. would be fine only (no custodial sentence).

Statutory Scheme of Section 488:
  • Deemed Culpability of Karta [Section 488(1)]:
    Where an offence under this Act has been committed by a Hindu Undivided Family (HUF), the Karta thereof shall be deemed to be guilty of the offence and shall be liable to be proceeded against and punished accordingly.
  • Statutory Defenses Available to the Karta [Proviso to Section 488(1)]:
    The Karta shall not be liable to any punishment if he proves that:
    1. The offence was committed without his knowledge; OR
    2. He had exercised all due diligence to prevent the commission of such offence.
  • Liability of Other Coparceners / Family Members [Section 488(2)]:
    Where an offence has been committed by an HUF and it is proved that the offence has been committed with the consent or connivance of, or is attributable to any neglect on the part of, any member of the HUF, such member shall also be deemed guilty of that offence and shall be liable to be proceeded against and punished accordingly!

Valuation under Rule 3(1)(f) of Black Money Rules, 2015:

Under Rule 3(1)(f) of the Black Money Rules, 2015, the fair market value (FMV) of an immovable property is the higher of:

  1. Its cost of acquisition = ₹ 45 Lakh
  2. The price which the property would ordinarily fetch if sold in the open market on the valuation date as per the valuation report = ₹ 55 Lakh
Conclusion: The Fair Market Value (FMV) of the new apartment = ₹ 55 Lakh.
