28.1 Brief Background of the BEPS Project

Tax Planning Gaps, The 2013 OECD Diagnostic Report & The 2015 15 BEPS Action Package
Historical Origins

Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) refers to tax planning strategies employed by multinational enterprises (MNEs) that exploit gaps and mismatches in national tax rules to artificially shift profits to low- or no-tax jurisdictions where there is little or no substantive economic activity.

Disproportionate Burden on Developing Nations

Developing economies have a substantially higher fiscal reliance on corporate income tax revenues relative to gross domestic product compared to developed nations. Consequently, aggressive cross-border profit shifting and base erosion undermine developing countries' revenue mobilization disproportionately, starving essential public services and infrastructure development.

The 2013 OECD Diagnostic Report

The foundational OECD report, Addressing Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (2013), concluded that no single tax rule on its own enables BEPS. Rather, BEPS is generated by the complex systemic interplay of multiple deficiencies:

  • Uncoordinated domestic laws and mismatched bilateral tax treaties;
  • International tax standards failing to keep pace with digital and globalized supply chains;
  • Pervasive information asymmetry and lack of transparency at the level of tax administrations; and
  • Widespread availability of harmful tax regimes and preferential intellectual property boxes.
The 2015 15 BEPS Action Package

In 2015, the OECD and G20 nations delivered a comprehensive package of 15 BEPS Actions designed to restore international tax coherence, realign taxation with economic substance, and enforce transparency (e.g., Action 1 on Digital Economy, Actions 8-10 on Transfer Pricing Intangibles and Risks, Action 13 CbCR, Action 15 MLI).

Ongoing Monitoring: Member countries committed to monitoring compliance with minimum standards via peer reviews to ensure uniform implementation across jurisdictions.

28.2 Establishment of the OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework

Equal-Footing Multilateralism • 148 Global Member Jurisdictions
148 Members

Because globalization transcends regional blocs, an international tax system capable of combating base erosion requires an inclusive forum extending far beyond the traditional OECD and G20 member countries.

The Equal-Footing Mandate & Membership Growth

Drawing upon the successful experience of the Global Forum on Transparency and Exchange of Information for Tax Purposes, the OECD and G20 established the Inclusive Framework on BEPS in early 2016.

  • Equal Footing: All interested countries and jurisdictions—particularly developing economies—participate on an equal footing in developing international tax standards and monitoring the implementation of the BEPS package.
  • Universal Global Coverage: The Inclusive Framework has expanded to 148 member jurisdictions, with Guatemala having formally joined as the 148th member in January 2026!

28.3 Introduction of the Two-Pillar Solution (BEPS 2.0)

Consensus Timeline • Architectural Blueprint: Pillar One & Pillar Two
BEPS 2.0 Architecture

The digitalization of the global economy exacerbated tax challenges by enabling multinational enterprises to generate colossal profits from customer markets without establishing any physical presence or Permanent Establishment. In response, the Inclusive Framework formulated the historic consensus-based Two-Pillar Solution (BEPS 2.0).

1
1 July 2021: Initial Historic Breakthrough

130 member jurisdictions of the Inclusive Framework (representing over 90% of global GDP) reached a landmark preliminary agreement on the Two-Pillar blueprint.

2
8 October 2021: Statement on the Two-Pillar Solution

136 jurisdictions agreed to the definitive political Statement outlining the detailed technical design, parameters, and timelines for Pillar One and Pillar Two.

3
9 June 2023 & Beyond: Expanded Global Adoption

Consensus expanded to 139+ member jurisdictions, culminating in the release of detailed Model Rules, Commentary, Administrative Guidance, and Multilateral Conventions.

PILLAR ONE: Digital & Global Economy Taxation
Pillar 1 Scope Formulaic Profit Reallocation

Aims to ensure a fairer distribution of taxing rights among market jurisdictions with respect to the largest and most profitable multinational enterprises. Under Pillar One, an MNE is evaluated on a consolidated entity basis:

  • Amount A: New international framework reallocating 25% of residual profits (profits exceeding 10% profit margin) of giant MNEs (revenues > €20B) to market jurisdictions where goods/services are consumed.
  • Amount B: Simplified and streamlined transfer pricing approach standardizing arm's length returns for baseline in-country marketing and distribution activities.
PILLAR TWO: Global Minimum Tax Regime
Pillar 2 Scope 15% Minimum Floor • Anti-Race to Bottom

Establishes a multilateral floor on corporate tax competition, ensuring multinational enterprises pay a minimum effective tax rate across all operating jurisdictions:

  • Global Anti-Base Erosion (GloBE) Rules: Minimum effective tax rate of 15% for MNE Groups with annual revenues of €750 million or more, enforced via QDMTT, IIR, and UTPR.
  • Subject to Tax Rule (STTR): A bilateral treaty-based rule operating at a 9% minimum nominal tax rate, permitting source jurisdictions to "tax back" undertaxed intragroup payments.

Pillar One: Amount A — Residual Profit Reallocation

Scope Thresholds (€20B / 10%), 25% Allocation, Nexus Rules & Sourcing Principles
Amount A

Amount A introduces an innovative international framework that allocates a portion of the residual profits of the largest and most profitable multinational enterprises to market jurisdictions where goods and services are consumed, regardless of physical presence.

1. Scope Quantitative Thresholds (Group-Wide Test)

An MNE is brought within the operative scope of Amount A strictly on the basis of objective quantitative parameters, irrespective of its industry or commercial sector:

Global Turnover Threshold
> €20 Billion

Annual global consolidated revenue exceeding €20 billion. (Contingent on successful implementation, this threshold is slated to be reduced to €10 billion after a 7-year review period.)

Global Profitability Threshold
> 10% Profit Margin

Pre-tax profit margin (Profit Before Tax ÷ Revenue) exceeding 10%, calculated using the financial data from the MNE's consolidated financial statements.

Disclosed Segments Exception: If the MNE group as a whole fails the 10% profitability threshold, but a segment disclosed in the consolidated financial statements has standalone revenues > €20 billion and profitability > 10%, that Disclosed Segment is independently brought into the scope of Amount A!
2. The 25% Residual Profit Reallocation Quantum

Amount A reallocates exactly 25% of the MNE's residual profit to market jurisdictions. Residual profit is defined mathematically as profit in excess of 10% of revenue:

$$\text{Amount A Pool} = 25\% \times \left[\text{Profit Before Tax} - (10\% \times \text{Revenue})\right]$$

The allocable pool is distributed across qualifying market jurisdictions using revenue-based allocation keys.

3. Marketing & Distribution Profits Safe Harbour

To eliminate double taxation, Amount A incorporates a Marketing and Distribution Profits Safe Harbour (MDSH):

  • Where a market country already taxes an MNE's residual profits through a local distributor under routine transfer pricing rules, the Amount A allocation to that country is capped or completely eliminated.
  • Ensures an enterprise is not taxed twice on the same residual profitability in the market jurisdiction.
4. The Special-Purpose Nexus Test & Revenue Sourcing Rules

Amount A applies a standalone quantitative nexus threshold solely to determine whether a market country qualifies for profit reallocation (without creating a taxable presence for any other domestic tax purpose):

Market Jurisdiction Category Annual Market Revenue Threshold Statutory Objective
Standard Economies
(Annual GDP ≥ €40 Billion)		 ≥ €1 Million in local market revenue Establishes significant economic engagement in larger economies.
Smaller / Developing Economies
(Annual GDP < €40 Billion)		 ≥ €250,000 in local market revenue Ensures smaller developing island and low-income economies share in profit reallocations.

Revenue Sourcing Principles:

  • End-Market Destination Rule: Revenue is sourced to the jurisdiction where the goods or services are ultimate used or consumed by the end-user.
  • Predominant Character: Bundled or hybrid revenues are sourced according to their predominant character.
  • Online Advertising: Sourced based on the “eyeballs” of the viewer (using IP address or geolocation), NOT the location of the corporate advertiser paying for the campaign.
  • Cloud Computing & Complex Services: Where locating the end-user directly is challenging, MNEs utilize targeted proxies or allocation keys (e.g., headcount data or macroeconomic indicators) to balance compliance costs with accuracy.
Current Legal Status of Amount A: The text of the Multilateral Convention (MLC) to implement Amount A was released in October 2023. However, the MLC is not yet open for signature, and Amount A is therefore not presently in force internationally.

Pillar One: Amount B — Baseline Marketing & Distribution

Simplified & Streamlined Transfer Pricing • 3 Industry Groups • 2025/2026 OECD Guidelines
Amount B

While Amount A updates international nexus rules, Amount B is a critical component of Pillar One focused on simplifying and standardizing the application of the arm's length principle to in-country baseline marketing and distribution activities.

1. Core Objectives & Rationale
  • Baseline marketing and distribution represents the single most frequent fact pattern MNEs encounter globally.
  • Significantly reduces protracted transfer pricing disputes, eliminates benchmarking litigation, and provides high tax certainty.
  • Specifically assists low-capacity tax administrations that frequently suffer from a severe shortage of local market comparable companies.
2. Key Milestone Chronology
  • 19 February 2024: Inclusive Framework released initial Report on Amount B.
  • 17 June 2024: Publication of pending design aspects (operating expense cross-checks).
  • 24 February 2025: OECD/G20 Consolidated Report on Amount B formally published.
  • 17 February 2026: OECD released the latest Amount B Pricing FAQs and revised Pricing Automation Tool!
Optional Nature & Impact on Indian Domestic Law

Amount B is not automatically applicable in every jurisdiction. Its application is contingent upon a jurisdiction formally choosing to adopt the simplified and streamlined approach in its domestic legislation.
Crucial Rule for India: In the absence of specific domestic statutory adoption by the Indian Government, Amount B does NOT displace or override existing Indian domestic transfer pricing provisions (under Sections 92 to 92F)!

3. The Three-Step Implementation Framework for Amount B
1
STEP 1: Scoping Conditions

Applies to qualifying buy-sell distribution arrangements, commissionaires, and sales agents where the distributor purchases goods from foreign associated enterprises for wholesale distribution in its local market, satisfying defined operational intensity ratios.

2
STEP 2: Pricing of In-Scope Transactions (3 Sub-Steps)

Priced using the Amount B pricing framework (unless a reliable internal CUP is available):

  • Sub-step 2-1: Application of Pricing Matrix:
    Derived from a global dataset of independent baseline distributors. Approximates arm's length return expressed as Return on Sales (ROS) across 3 Pre-Defined Industry Groups:
    Group 1: Perishable foods, grocery, household consumables, construction materials & plumbing supplies, metal.
    Group 2: IT hardware/components, electrical components, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, health/wellbeing products, clothing/apparel, plastics, chemicals, lubricants, auto vehicles & parts, consumer electronics, paper, jewellery.
    Group 3: Medical machinery, industrial machinery, industrial/agricultural vehicles, industrial tools & components.
  • Sub-step 2-2: Operating Expense Cross-Check (OECC):
    Serves as a guardrail, applying caps and collars based on the ratio of operating expenses to sales to ensure the distributor's net return is economically reasonable.
  • Sub-step 2-3: Data Availability Mechanism:
    Applies an upward risk-adjusted return percentage for distributors in qualifying jurisdictions lacking sufficient local benchmarking data.
3
STEP 3: Documentation & Tax Certainty

Distributors must provide standardized local file disclosures, transfer pricing study documentation, and written consent to apply the simplified approach.

Pillar Two: Subject to Tax Rule (STTR — 9% Nominal Floor)

Treaty-Based Tax-Back Mechanism, Covered Incomes & The STTR Multilateral Instrument
STTR 9% Floor

Pillar Two establishes a comprehensive global minimum tax regime designed to eliminate tax competition and harmful "race to the bottom" practices. It is structured around two distinct, complementary components: the Global Anti-Base Erosion (GloBE) Rules and the Subject to Tax Rule (STTR).

1. What is the Subject to Tax Rule (STTR)?

The STTR is a bilateral, treaty-based rule that permits a source jurisdiction to "tax back" (impose limited additional withholding tax) on specified cross-border payments made between connected persons where those payments are subjected to an adjusted nominal tax rate below 9% in the payee's residence jurisdiction.

Specified Categories of Covered Income
  • Interest and royalties;
  • Payments for distribution rights for a product or service;
  • Insurance or reinsurance premiums;
  • Fees to provide a guarantee or other financing fee;
  • Rent for the use of industrial, commercial, or scientific equipment; and
  • Fees for the provision of services.
Connected Persons & Specified Rate
  • Connected Persons Test: Applies to cross-border payments between parties where one has direct or indirect control over the other, or both are under common control (> 50% ownership threshold).
  • The 9% Specified Floor: If the payee State taxes the income at an adjusted nominal rate below 9%, the source State can levy a top-up tax equal to the difference between 9% and the payee's rate (minus any treaty tax already paid).
ICAI Module Practical Illustration: STTR Top-Up Tax Computation

Country A (Source State) and Country B (Residence State) have a DTAA under which Country A has the right to levy a 5% withholding tax on royalties paid to Country B residents. A connected corporate entity in Country B receives royalties from Country A:

  • Country B subjects the royalty income to an adjusted nominal corporate tax rate of only 1% under a special patent regime.
  • Because 1% is strictly below the STTR specified minimum rate of 9%, the STTR is triggered.
  • The gross STTR spread is: $$\text{STTR Spread} = 9\% - 1\% = 8\%$$
  • Because Country A already levies 5% withholding tax under the base DTAA, Country A is entitled to impose an additional top-up tax of: $$\text{Additional STTR Top-up Tax} = 8\% - 5\% = \mathbf{3\%}$$
Result: Total tax imposed in Country A on the royalty increases from 5% to 8% ($5\% \text{ Base Treaty} + 3\% \text{ STTR Top-up}$). The recipient's overall effective tax between both states equals the 9% minimum floor ($8\% \text{ Source} + 1\% \text{ Residence} = 9\%$).
The STTR Multilateral Instrument (MLI): The multilateral convention to facilitate rapid bilateral implementation of the STTR into existing treaties opened for signature in October 2023. By September 2025, 10 sovereign jurisdictions had formally signed the STTR MLI, with San Marino becoming the 10th signatory in September 2025.

Pillar Two: The GloBE Rules (15% Minimum ETR)

Scope Threshold (€750M), Excluded Entities & The 3-Tier Charging Hierarchy
GloBE Rules

The Global Anti-Base Erosion (GloBE) Rules are domestic legislative model rules designed to ensure that large multinational enterprise groups pay an Effective Tax Rate (ETR) of at least 15% in every single jurisdiction in which they operate.

1. Scope Threshold: ≥ €750 Million

The GloBE Rules apply to Constituent Entities that are members of an MNE Group with annual consolidated revenues of €750 million or more in the consolidated financial statements of the Ultimate Parent Entity (UPE) in at least two of the four fiscal years immediately preceding the tested fiscal year.

2. Excluded Entities

Entities that are explicitly excluded fall completely outside the operative GloBE charging rules (their revenues are counted toward group scope, but no top-up tax is levied on them):

  • Governmental entities;
  • International organizations;
  • Non-profit organizations;
  • Pension funds; and
  • Investment funds or Real Estate Investment Vehicles that are UPEs of the group.
3. The Three GloBE Charging Rules in Strict Priority Order

Where an MNE group has low-taxed constituent entities (where the jurisdictional ETR is below 15%), top-up tax is collected following an inflexible three-tier priority hierarchy:

1
PRIORITY 1: Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax (QDMTT)

A QDMTT is a domestic tax implemented by a Low-Taxed Jurisdiction (LTJ) itself. It grants the host/source jurisdiction the first right to collect the top-up tax up to the 15% minimum rate on its own local constituent entities. If an LTJ implements a QDMTT, the top-up tax stays in the source country, and foreign parent jurisdictions are preempted from collecting it!

2
PRIORITY 2: Income Inclusion Rule (IIR)

If the LTJ has not enacted a QDMTT, the top-up tax is collected by parent jurisdictions under the IIR “top-down approach”. The Ultimate Parent Entity (UPE) pays an amount equal to its allocable share of the top-up tax. If the UPE is located in a non-implementing country, the obligation cascades down to Intermediate Parent Entities or Partially-Owned Parent Entities.

3
PRIORITY 3: Undertaxed Profits Rule (UTPR)

The UTPR serves as the ultimate backstop. Where low-taxed profits are not captured under a QDMTT and cannot be taxed under an IIR (e.g., UPE is in a non-GloBE jurisdiction), the residual top-up tax is allocated among other group constituent entities in implementing jurisdictions through the denial of domestic tax deductions or equivalent cash adjustments.

ICAI Module Illustration: GloBE Charging Priority Chain

An MNE group has its Ultimate Parent Entity (UPE) located in Country X, with three wholly owned subsidiaries: WOS 1 (Country A), WOS 2 (Country B), and WOS 3 (Country C). Country C is a Low-Taxed Jurisdiction (LTJ) where WOS 3 pays an effective tax rate of only 8%:

  • Priority 1 (QDMTT in Country C): If Country C has enacted a Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax, Country C imposes and collects the 7% top-up tax directly on WOS 3. No tax is collected by Country X, A, or B.
  • Priority 2 (IIR in Country X): If Country C has no QDMTT, but Country X has implemented a Qualified IIR, the UPE in Country X must pay the 7% top-up tax on WOS 3's low-taxed profits under the top-down approach.
  • Priority 3 (UTPR in Countries A & B): If neither Country C (no QDMTT) nor Country X (no IIR) collects the tax, the residual 7% top-up tax is allocated to WOS 1 in Country A and WOS 2 in Country B under the UTPR formula (via deduction denials).

GloBE Computation: ETR, Blending & SBIE Carve-Out

Adjusted Covered Taxes, Jurisdictional Blending & Transitional Substance Carve-Outs
Core Mechanics
1. Effective Tax Rate (ETR) Formula

The Effective Tax Rate is computed annually for each sovereign jurisdiction in which the MNE group operates:

$$\text{ETR} = \frac{\text{Total Adjusted Covered Taxes in the Jurisdiction}}{\text{Net GloBE Income of the Jurisdiction}}$$
  • Adjusted Covered Taxes: Current tax expense under financial accounts, adjusted for timing differences, deferred tax assets/liabilities (capped at 15%), and tax credits.
  • Net GloBE Income: $\sum \text{GloBE Income} - \sum \text{GloBE Losses}$ of all local constituent entities. If Net GloBE Income is a loss, ETR is not calculated and no top-up tax is due.
2. Jurisdictional Blending

The GloBE rules mandate Jurisdictional Blending. All constituent entities of the MNE group located in the same sovereign jurisdiction blend their profits, losses, and covered taxes together:

  • High taxes paid by one local entity automatically shield the low-taxed profits of another entity located in the same country.
  • However, cross-border blending (e.g., blending profits of Country A with taxes of Country B) is strictly prohibited!
3. Substance-Based Income Exclusion (SBIE Carve-Out)

The Substance-Based Income Exclusion (SBIE) provides a carve-out that reduces Net GloBE Income to determine the Excess Profit upon which top-up tax is actually levied. It permits countries to offer genuine tax incentives on routine returns from substantive investments in tangible assets and local workforce employment without triggering GloBE top-up taxes.

Top-Up Tax Computation Formula & De Minimis Exclusion

Excess Profit Determination • De Minimis Turnover & Income Safe Harbours
Statutory Formula
1. The Jurisdictional Top-Up Tax Formula
$$\text{Top-up Tax Percentage} = 15\% - \text{ETR}$$ $$\text{Excess Profit} = \text{Net GloBE Income} - \text{SBIE}$$ $$\text{Top-up Tax} = (\text{Top-up Tax \%} \times \text{Excess Profit}) + \text{Addl Current Tax} - \text{Domestic Tax}$$

Key Statutory Note: The Top-up Tax Percentage is calculated by reference to the jurisdictional ETR, but it is applied only to the Excess Profit (Net GloBE Income less SBIE), ensuring that the substance-based carve-out is directly reflected in the final top-up tax payable.

2. The De Minimis Exclusion

At the election of the filing entity, a jurisdiction is excluded from the full ETR calculation and top-up tax computation where:

  • The Average GloBE Revenue of the jurisdiction is less than €10 million; AND
  • The Average GloBE Income or Loss of the jurisdiction is a loss or less than €1 million.

Policy Intent: Avoids onerous compliance and audit costs where the amount of any resulting top-up tax would be trivial.

ICAI Master Numerical Case Study: GloBE Top-Up Tax Calculation

Exhaustive Step-by-Step Solution • Blending, ETR, SBIE & €209,667 Top-Up Tax
Master Problem
Comprehensive ICAI Case Study: UPE Co. (Country A) & Subsidiaries in Country B

Problem Statement: UPE Co., incorporated and resident in Country A, is the Ultimate Parent Entity of a large multinational enterprise group with global consolidated revenues exceeding €750 million. UPE Co. owns two wholly owned subsidiaries in Country B: Company B and Company C. For the Fiscal Year beginning in 2026, the operational and tax data of the subsidiaries in Country B is as follows:

Particulars Company B Company C
GloBE Income €5,000,000 €10,000,000
Adjusted Covered Taxes €1,000,000 €1,000,000
Eligible Payroll Costs €4,000,000 €6,000,000
Eligible Tangible Assets €6,000,000 €14,000,000

Required: Compute the Jurisdictional Effective Tax Rate (ETR), Substance-Based Income Exclusion (SBIE), Excess Profit, and the Jurisdictional Top-Up Tax for Country B under the GloBE Rules for the Fiscal Year 2026.

Comprehensive Step-by-Step Statutory Solution:

1
Step 1: Compute Jurisdictional Totals (Jurisdictional Blending)
  • Net GloBE Income of Country B: $$\text{Net GloBE Income} = \text{Income of Company B } (€5,000,000) + \text{Income of Company C } (€10,000,000) = \mathbf{€15,000,000}$$
  • Total Adjusted Covered Taxes in Country B: $$\text{Total Covered Taxes} = \text{Taxes of Company B } (€1,000,000) + \text{Taxes of Company C } (€1,000,000) = \mathbf{€2,000,000}$$
  • Total Eligible Payroll Costs in Country B: $$\text{Total Payroll} = €4,000,000 + €6,000,000 = \mathbf{€10,000,000}$$
  • Total Eligible Tangible Assets in Country B: $$\text{Total Tangible Assets} = €6,000,000 + €14,000,000 = \mathbf{€20,000,000}$$
2
Step 2: Calculate Jurisdictional Effective Tax Rate (ETR)

Under Article 5.1 of the GloBE Rules:

$$\text{ETR} = \frac{\text{Total Adjusted Covered Taxes}}{\text{Net GloBE Income}} = \frac{€2,000,000}{€15,000,000} = \mathbf{13.3333\%}$$

Because the Jurisdictional ETR of 13.3333% is below the 15% Minimum Rate, Country B constitutes a Low-Taxed Jurisdiction (LTJ), triggering the Top-up Tax!

3
Step 3: Calculate the Top-Up Tax Percentage
$$\text{Top-up Tax Percentage} = 15\% - \text{ETR} = 15\% - 13.3333\% = \mathbf{1.6667\%}$$
4
Step 4: Compute the Substance-Based Income Exclusion (SBIE) for 2026

Under Article 9.2 of the GloBE Rules, for a Fiscal Year beginning in 2026, the transitional carve-out rates are:
• Eligible Payroll Costs: 9.4%
• Eligible Tangible Assets: 7.4%

$$\text{Payroll Carve-out} = 9.4\% \times €10,000,000 = €940,000$$ $$\text{Tangible Assets Carve-out} = 7.4\% \times €20,000,000 = €1,480,000$$ $$\text{Total SBIE} = €940,000 + €1,480,000 = \mathbf{€2,420,000}$$
5
Step 5: Determine Excess Profit
$$\text{Excess Profit} = \text{Net GloBE Income } (€15,000,000) - \text{SBIE } (€2,420,000) = \mathbf{€12,580,000}$$
6
Step 6: Compute Final Jurisdictional Top-Up Tax for Country B

Applying the Top-up Tax Percentage to Excess Profit:

$$\text{Jurisdictional Top-up Tax} = 1.6667\% \times €12,580,000 = \mathbf{€209,667} \text{ (approx.)}$$
Computation Parameter Value
Minimum Tax Rate (i) 15%
Jurisdictional ETR (ii) 13.3333%
Net GloBE Income in Country B (iii) €15,000,000
Substance-Based Income Exclusion (SBIE) (iv) €2,420,000
Top-Up Tax Percentage (i) − (ii) 1.6667%
Excess Profit (iii) − (iv) €12,580,000
Final Top-Up Tax Payable for Country B €209,667 (approx.)
Effect of Jurisdictional Blending in Case of Losses: If Company B had incurred an operational tax loss of €5,000,000 instead of a profit, the Net GloBE Income for Country B would have been blended down to €5,000,000 ($€10,000,000 - €5,000,000$). The SBIE of €2,420,000 would absorb nearly half of the net income, substantially curtailing any residual top-up tax!
