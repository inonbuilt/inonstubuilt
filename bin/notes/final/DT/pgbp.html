Sec 26 Chargeability of Profits & Gains of Business or Profession

Charging Section
Statutory Scope under Section 26 (Formerly Section 28)

Under Section 26 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, income is chargeable to income-tax under the head "Profits and gains of business or profession" under the following 10 statutory heads:

  1. Profits & Gains: Profits and gains of any business or profession carried on by the assessee at any time during the tax year.
  2. Management Compensation [Sec 26(b)]: Any compensation or payment due to or received by any person managing the whole or substantially the whole of the affairs of an Indian company or other entity at or in connection with the termination or modification of the terms and conditions of their appointment.
  3. Trade / Professional Associations [Sec 26(c)]: Income derived by a trade, professional or similar association from specific services performed for its members.
  4. Export Incentives [Sec 26(d)]:
    • Profits on sale of a licence granted under the Imports (Control) Order, 1955.
    • Cash assistance (by whatever name called) received or receivable by any person against exports under any scheme of the Government of India.
    • Any duty of customs or excise repaid or repayable as drawback to any person against exports.
    • Profits on the transfer of Duty Entitlement Pass Book (DEPB) Scheme or Duty Free Tariff Preference Scheme.
  5. Perquisites in Business/Profession [Sec 26(e)]: The value of any benefit or perquisite, whether convertible into money or not, arising from business or the exercise of a profession (e.g., group incentive holiday trips given by vendors).
  6. Partner Remuneration & Interest [Sec 26(f)]: Any interest, salary, bonus, commission or remuneration, by whatever name called, due to or received by a partner of a firm from the firm, to the extent allowable under Section 36(5).
  7. Non-Compete & Exclusivity Fees [Sec 26(g)]: Any sum received or receivable under an agreement for not carrying out any activity in relation to any business or profession, or not sharing any know-how, patent, copyright, trademark, licence or franchise.
  8. Keyman Insurance Policy [Sec 26(h)]: Any sum received under a Keyman insurance policy including the sum allocated by way of bonus on such policy.
  9. Conversion of Inventory to Capital Asset [Sec 26(i)]: The Fair Market Value (FMV) of inventory on the date of its conversion or treatment into a capital asset, determined in the prescribed manner.
  10. Recovery against Section 35AD Assets [Sec 26(j)]: Any sum received or receivable on account of demolition, destruction or transfer of any capital asset in respect of which 100% deduction was allowed under Section 35AD.
Business Carried on at Any Time During the Year: It is not necessary that the business or profession must have been carried on throughout the tax year. If it was carried on for even a single day during Tax Year 2026-27, the profits are assessable under Section 26. Furthermore, where an allowance or deduction has been granted in an earlier year, subsequent recovery is deemed to be business profits under Section 38, even if the business is discontinued before the commencement of the tax year.

Sec 27 & ICDS Method of Accounting & ICDS I to X Exhaustive Framework

Statutory Standards

Under Section 276(1) [formerly Section 145(1)], income chargeable under the head PGBP must be computed in accordance with either cash or mercantile system of accounting regularly employed by the assessee. The Central Government has notified 10 Income Computation and Disclosure Standards (ICDS) to be followed by all assessees (other than individuals/HUFs not subject to tax audit) following the mercantile system.

Legal Status of ICDS: ICDS are for computation of income and not for maintenance of books of account. In case of conflict between the provisions of the Income-tax Act, 2025 and ICDS, the provisions of the Act shall prevail.
ICDS Standard Title & Core Scope Mandatory Tax Treatment & ICAI Exam Rules
ICDS I Accounting Policies • Marked-to-Market (MTM) Loss: Marked-to-market loss or expected loss is disallowed unless permitted by other ICDS.
• Prudence: Concept of prudence is modified; anticipated losses cannot be recognized unless explicitly provided.
• Change in Accounting Policy: Permitted only for reasonable cause without intention of tax avoidance.
ICDS II Valuation of Inventories • Inclusive Method [Sec 54]: Costs of purchase must include duties, taxes, freight and other expenditure directly attributable to acquisition (even if credit is claimed).
• Cost or NRV: Valued at lower of cost or net realizable value (NRV). NRV comparison must be done item-by-item, not category-wise.
• Dissolution of Firm: Inventory must be valued at FMV on the date of dissolution, whether business continues or not.
ICDS III Construction Contracts • Percentage of Completion Method (POCM) Mandatory: Completed Contract Method is prohibited.
• Early Stage: Revenue recognized only when stage of completion exceeds 25%. Prior to 25%, revenue equals costs incurred.
• Retention Money: Retention money is included in contract revenue as part of POCM billing.
• Incidental Income: Contract cost cannot be reduced by incidental interest, dividends or capital gains.
ICDS IV Revenue Recognition • Service Contracts: Mandatory POCM if service duration exceeds 90 days. For contracts ≤ 90 days, straight-line method permitted.
• Interest Income: Accrues on time basis determined by the effective rate of interest.
• Dividend Income: Taxable in accordance with Section 8 on date of declaration.
• Royalties: Accrue in accordance with terms of relevant agreement.
ICDS V Tangible Fixed Assets • Actual Cost: Includes purchase price, import duties, and directly attributable costs of bringing asset to working condition.
• Administration/Start-up Costs: Expenses incurred after trial run or commercial production cannot be capitalized.
• Exchange of Assets: Asset acquired in exchange for another is recorded at FMV of asset given up.
ICDS VI Foreign Exchange Rates • Monetary Items: Translated at closing exchange rate; exchange difference recognized as PGBP income/expense.
• Non-Monetary Items: Carried at historical cost using exchange rate on transaction date.
• Capital Assets [Sec 43A]: Adjustments to actual cost of imported capital assets take precedence over ICDS VI.
ICDS VII Government Grants • Depreciable Asset Grant: Deducted from actual cost of the asset or written down value (WDV) of the block.
• Non-Depreciable Asset Grant: Credited as PGBP income over the period of fulfillment of conditions.
• Compensation/Revenue Grant: Recognized in P&L in the period it becomes receivable.
ICDS VIII Securities • Bucket-Wise Valuation: Securities held as stock-in-trade are valued at lower of cost or NRV category-wise (Shares, Debt securities, Convertible securities, Any other securities).
• Individual security comparison is not allowed for securities.
ICDS IX Borrowing Costs • Qualifying Asset: Tangible/intangible asset taking 12 months or more to get ready for intended use.
• Capitalization Period: Commences from date funds are borrowed and ends when asset is first put to use.
• Inventory: Borrowing costs capitalized only if inventory takes 12+ months to bring to saleable condition.
ICDS X Provisions & Contingent Assets • Provision Recognition: Allowed when there is a present obligation resulting from past event, and it is reasonably certain that outflow of resources will be required.
• Contingent Assets: Recognized as income when inflow of economic benefits is reasonably certain (different from AS 29 'virtually certain').

Sec 26 & 113 Speculation Business: Definition, Exceptions & Loss Rules

Loss Ring-Fencing
Statutory Definition [Section 26, Explanation 2]

A speculative transaction means a transaction in which a contract for the purchase or sale of any commodity, including stocks and shares, is periodically or ultimately settled otherwise than by the actual delivery or transfer of the commodity or scrips.

Statutory Exceptions (Transactions NOT Treated as Speculative):

  • Hedging of Raw Materials: A contract in respect of raw materials or merchandise entered into by a person in the course of his manufacturing or merchanting business to guard against loss through future price fluctuations in respect of his contracts for actual delivery of goods manufactured by him or merchandise sold by him.
  • Hedging of Stocks & Shares: A contract in respect of stocks and shares entered into by a dealer or investor to guard against loss in his holdings of stocks and shares through price fluctuations.
  • Forward Arbitrage Contracts: A contract entered into by a member of a forward market or stock exchange in the course of any transaction in the nature of jobbing or arbitrage to guard against loss which may arise in the ordinary course of his business.
  • Exchange-Traded Derivatives: An eligible transaction in respect of trading in derivatives carried out in a recognized stock exchange where Securities Transaction Tax (STT) is paid and supported by time-stamped contract notes.
  • Commodity Derivatives: An eligible transaction in respect of trading in commodity derivatives carried out in a recognized association chargeable to Commodities Transaction Tax (CTT).
Loss Set-Off Restriction [Section 113 / formerly Section 73]: Losses incurred in a speculation business cannot be set off against any profits other than profits of another speculation business under Section 113. Speculation loss can be carried forward for a maximum of 4 tax years immediately succeeding the tax year in which the loss was first computed.

Sec 30 - 33 Statutory Scheme of Depreciation & Block of Assets

Schedule IX
Core Principles of Depreciation [Section 30 & 33]

Depreciation under the Income-tax Act, 2025 is calculated on the Written Down Value (WDV) of a Block of Assets under the Reducing Balance Method (except for power-generating undertakings opting for SLM under Section 31).

  • Mandatory Allowance: Depreciation is mandatory; it cannot be waived by the assessee to artificially manipulate profits.
  • Ownership & Business Use: The asset must be owned, wholly or partly, by the assessee and used for the purpose of the business or profession during the tax year.
  • Goodwill Excluded: Goodwill of a business or profession is NOT an eligible depreciable asset.

Prescribed Depreciation Rates under Schedule IX

Computation of WDV of a Block [Section 41(1)]

$$ ext{Closing WDV} = ext{Opening WDV as on 1st April} + ext{Actual Cost of Assets Acquired during Year} - ext{Moneys Payable on Assets Sold/Discarded}$$

• 180-Day Rule: Where an asset acquired during the tax year is put to use for less than 180 days (i.e. put to use on or after 4th October in a 365-day year), depreciation on such asset is restricted to 50% of normal rate.
• When Depreciation is Zero: Depreciation is NIL if (a) the closing WDV reduces to zero (or negative), or (b) the block ceases to exist because all assets in the block are sold/transferred during the year (triggering Short-Term Capital Gain/Loss under Section 50).

Additional Depreciation under Section 33(8) & Carry-Forward under Section 33(9)(b)

An assessee engaged in the manufacture or production of any article or thing or in the business of generation, transmission or distribution of power is entitled to an Additional Depreciation of 20% on the actual cost of new plant and machinery acquired and installed.

Parameter Statutory Rule & Practical Application
Standard Rate 20% of actual cost of eligible new plant and machinery.
Put to use < 180 days Restricted to 10% (50% of 20%) in the year of acquisition.
Carry-Forward of Balance The balance 10% additional depreciation is allowable in the immediately succeeding tax year [Section 33(9)(b)].
Ineligible Assets for Addl. Depr. • Ships and aircraft.
• Any machinery or plant previously used either in India or abroad (second-hand).
• Any machinery or plant installed in any office premises or residential accommodation/guest house.
• Office appliances or road transport vehicles.
• Any plant/machinery where 100% deduction is allowed under any other provision (e.g. Sec 35, 35AD).

SLM Method for Power Generating Undertakings [Section 31]

Undertakings engaged in generation or generation and distribution of power have the option to claim depreciation on Straight Line Method (SLM) on the actual cost of each individual asset, instead of the WDV block system.

Event on Disposal Tax Treatment & Section Mathematical Formula
Sale Price < WDV Terminal Depreciation [Sec 31(1)]: Fully deductible from business profits. $$ ext{Terminal Depreciation} = ext{WDV} - ext{Sale Price}$$
Sale Price > WDV but ≤ Cost Balancing Charge [Sec 38(2)]: Taxable as PGBP business profits. $$ ext{Balancing Charge} = ext{Sale Price} - ext{WDV}$$
Sale Price > Actual Cost Balancing Charge + Short-Term Capital Gains: Balancing charge up to original cost (PGBP); excess over cost is STCG. $$\text{PGBP} = \text{Cost} - \text{WDV}; \quad \text{STCG} = \text{Sale Price} - \text{Cost}$$
Priority Order of Set-Off [Section 32(2) & Section 72]:
When profits are insufficient to absorb allowances and losses, set-off must strictly follow this statutory hierarchy:
  1. Current Year Depreciation: Set off against any head of income (except Salaries).
  2. Brought Forward Business Loss [Sec 72]: Set off only against business profits (subject to 8-year carry-forward limit).
  3. Unabsorbed Depreciation [Sec 32(2)]: Set off against any taxable income (except Salaries); can be carried forward indefinitely.

Sec 34 & Sched X Scientific Research Expenditure (In-House & External Contributions)

100% Weighted Deduction
Statutory Framework under Section 34 (Formerly Section 35)

Deduction is allowable for expenditure incurred on scientific research related to the business or profession:

  • In-House Revenue Expenditure [Sec 34(1)(i)]: 100% deduction of revenue expenditure incurred on scientific research related to the business. Pre-commencement salary (excluding perquisites) and material expenses incurred within 3 years prior to commencement are allowed in the year of commencement.
  • In-House Capital Expenditure [Sec 34(1)(iv)]: 100% deduction of capital expenditure incurred on scientific research (including buildings). However, expenditure on acquisition of LAND is strictly NOT allowable. Pre-commencement capital expenditure incurred within 3 years is also allowed 100% in the year of commencement.
  • Contributions to External Bodies [Sec 34(1)(ii)/(iii)]: 100% deduction for sums paid to approved research associations, universities, colleges, or National Laboratories/IITs.
  • No Depreciation: Where 100% capital deduction is claimed under Section 34, no depreciation is allowable under Section 33 for the same or any subsequent year.
  • Sale of Research Asset [Sec 38(3)]: When a scientific research asset is sold without having been used for other purposes, the sale proceeds or deduction allowed (whichever is less) is taxable as PGBP business income in the year of sale. Excess of sale price over cost is chargeable as Capital Gains.

Sec 35AD / Sec 46 Deduction for Specified Businesses (14 Statutory Sectors)

100% Capex Write-Off

Under Section 35AD (referred to as Section 46 in the unified code), an assessee is entitled to a 100% deduction of capital expenditure incurred wholly and exclusively for the purpose of any "specified business" during the tax year.

Excluded Capital Expenditures: No deduction is permissible in respect of expenditure incurred on the acquisition of:
(1) Land, (2) Goodwill, or (3) Financial Instruments.
All payments for eligible capital expenditure exceeding ₹10,000 must be made by account payee cheque, bank draft, or electronic clearing system (ECS).
Category 14 Eligible Specified Businesses under Section 35AD
Logistics & Agri 1. Setting up and operating a cold chain facility.
2. Setting up and operating a warehousing facility for storage of agricultural produce.
3. Setting up and operating a warehousing facility for storage of sugar.
4. Laying and operating a cross-country natural gas or petroleum crude or petroleum oil pipeline network.
Infrastructure & Hospitality 5. Building and operating a new hotel of two-star or above category anywhere in India.
