Chapter 5: Income from Other Sources

Comprehensive study notes covering the residual charging mandate under Section 92 [formerly Section 56], deemed dividend codification under Section 2(40) [formerly Section 2(22)], the expansive anti-abuse regime for gifts and inadequate consideration under Section 92(2)(m) [formerly Section 56(2)(x)], High-Premium Life Insurance Policies under Circular No. 15/2023 and Rule 59, flat 30% casual income and online gaming taxation under Section 194 [formerly Sections 115BB & 115BBJ], allowable deductions under Section 93, strict disallowances under Section 94, deemed recovery under Section 95, all 6 ICAI Illustrations, and all 4 Test Your Knowledge examination questions with complete mathematical solutions.

Statutory Charge & Scope

Residual Head of Income [Section 92(1)] Section 92(1) | Formerly Sec 56(1)

Under the scheme of the Income-tax Act, 2025, the total income of an assessee is classified into five heads of income. The head "Income from Other Sources" (IFOS) is the residual head of income. It acts as an omnibus head that sweeps in every taxable item of income which cannot be assigned to any of the first four specific heads.

Statutory Mandate: Section 92(1) of the Income-tax Act, 2025

"Income of every kind which is not to be excluded from the total income under this Act shall be chargeable to income-tax under the head 'Income from other sources', if it is not chargeable to income-tax under any of the heads specified in Section 15, items A to D (viz., Salaries, Income from House Property, Profits and Gains of Business or Profession, and Capital Gains)."

Condition 1: Positive Character as "Income"

The receipt must satisfy the fundamental definition of "income" under Section 2(33) [formerly Section 2(24)] or must be specifically deemed to be income under any statutory provision of the Act. A pure capital receipt (not specifically brought into the tax net by a statutory fiction) cannot be taxed under Section 92.

Condition 2: Non-Chargeability under Specific Heads

The income must not be legally chargeable under the heads Salaries, House Property, PGBP, or Capital Gains. If a receipt falls naturally under a specific head, it cannot be relegated or shifted to IFOS at the option of the assessee or the Revenue.

Condition 3: Not Otherwise Exempt

The receipt must not be an item excluded or exempted from the total income under Section 11 read with Schedules II, III, IV, V, VI, or VII (e.g., agricultural income, certain dividend distributions, specified tax-free bonds).

Accounting Standards & Timing

Method of Accounting [Section 276 & Section 278(1)] Section 276 | Formerly Sec 145

Income chargeable under the head "Income from other sources" is computed in accordance with either the cash or mercantile system of accounting regularly employed by the assessee, subject to the Income Computation and Disclosure Standards (ICDS) notified by the Central Government.

CRITICAL STATUTORY OVERRIDE — Section 278(1) [Interest on Compensation]:
Notwithstanding Section 276(1), Section 278(1) provides that interest received by an assessee on any compensation or enhanced compensation shall be deemed to be the income of the tax year in which it is received, irrespective of the method of accounting followed by the assessee! It is taxed strictly on a cash/receipt basis in that year, subject to a statutory flat deduction of 50% under Section 93(1)(f).

Core Corporate Income

Dividend Taxation Framework [Section 92(2)(a)] Section 92(2)(a)

Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, dividend income received from domestic companies, foreign companies, or mutual fund units is always chargeable to tax in the hands of the recipient shareholder / unit holder under the head "Income from Other Sources" at applicable normal slab rates.

Cap on Interest Deduction: Proviso to Section 93(1)(e) [formerly Proviso to Section 57]

No deduction shall be allowed from dividend income or income in respect of units of a mutual fund, except interest expenditure incurred wholly and exclusively for the purpose of making or earning such dividend income. Furthermore, such interest deduction is strictly capped at a maximum of 20% of such dividend income included in the total income, without any carry-forward of unabsorbed interest expense!

Anti-Avoidance Mandate

Loans & Advances to Substantial Shareholders [Section 2(40)(e)] Section 2(40)(e) | Formerly Sec 2(22)(e)

Section 2(40)(e) is a potent anti-tax avoidance provision designed to prevent closely held companies from distributing profits to controlling shareholders disguised as loans or advances to evade dividend taxation.

The Three Statutory Triggers of Section 2(40)(e)

Any payment of any sum by a company in which the public are NOT substantially interested (closely held company), by way of advance or loan:

  • Trigger 1 — To a Substantial Shareholder: To a shareholder who is the beneficial owner of shares holding not less than 10% of the voting power; OR
  • Trigger 2 — To a Concern in which Shareholder has Substantial Interest: To any concern (HUF, Firm, AOP, BOI, or Company) in which such 10% beneficial shareholder is a member/partner and holds a substantial interest (i.e., entitled to ≥ 20% share of income or ≥ 20% voting power); OR
  • Trigger 3 — For Individual Benefit: Any payment made on behalf of, or for the individual benefit of, any such substantial shareholder,

Extent of Chargeability: Deemed to be dividend in the hands of such shareholder/concern to the extent to which the company possesses accumulated profits (whether capitalized or not) on the date of advancing such loan!

IRRELEVANCE OF REPAYMENT OR MARKET INTEREST RATE:
Under Section 2(40)(e), liability crystallizes on the exact date the loan or advance is made. It is completely immaterial whether:
  1. The loan is repaid in full before the close of the financial/tax year (as affirmed in Tarulata Shyam v. CIT); OR
  2. The company charges interest at commercial or market rates on such loan!
Exclusions & Safe Harbors

Statutory Exceptions: What Does NOT Constitute Dividend Exclusions u/s 2(40)

The Act provides six clear statutory exclusions where a distribution or advance will not be characterized as a deemed dividend:

1. Non-Participating Preference Shares on Liquidation / Capital Reduction:

Any distribution made under Section 2(40)(c) or (d) in respect of any share issued for full cash consideration where the holder is not entitled in the event of liquidation to participate in surplus assets (fixed-rate preference shares).

2. Loans / Advances in Ordinary Course of Money-Lending Business:

Any advance or loan made to a shareholder or concern in the ordinary course of its business, where the lending of money is a substantial part of the business of the company (e.g., banking companies, registered NBFCs).

3. Dividend Declared and Set-Off Against Prior Deemed Dividend:

Where a loan or advance had already been taxed as deemed dividend in an earlier year, and subsequently the company declares and distributes regular dividend and sets it off against the outstanding loan, the set-off amount will not be taxed again as dividend.

4. Distribution of Shares on Demerger:

Any distribution of shares by a resulting company to the shareholders of the demerged company pursuant to a sanctioned scheme of demerger.

5. Inter-Group Entity Finance Advances (New Statutory Exclusion):

Any advance or loan between two group entities where: (i) one entity is a Finance company or Finance Unit; (ii) the other group entity is located in a notified offshore jurisdiction; and (iii) the parent or principal entity is listed on a specified recognized stock exchange outside India.

6. Buy-Back of Shares:

Any payment made by a company on purchase of its own shares under the provisions of corporate law (governed by the specific share buy-back taxation regime).

Judicial Doctrines & Circulars

Accumulated Profits & Trade Advances Jurisprudence Circular 19/2017 & Cases

The computation of "accumulated profits" and the distinction between genuine commercial trade advances and gratuitous loans have generated extensive jurisprudence:

Definition and Scope of "Accumulated Profits"

  • For Clauses (a), (b), (d), and (e): Includes all commercial profits of the company up to the date of distribution or payment of loan.
  • For Clause (c) [Liquidation]: Includes all profits up to the date of liquidation. Exception: Where liquidation follows compulsory acquisition by Government, profits prior to 3 successive tax years preceding acquisition are excluded.
  • Amalgamation Rule: In case of amalgamation, accumulated profits/loss of the amalgamating company on the amalgamation date merge into the accumulated profits of the amalgamated company.
  • Exclusion of Depreciation Fund: Building & Machinery Depreciation Fund is a provision against capital wear-and-tear, not accumulated profit available for distribution [CIT v. Jaldu Rama Rao (1983) 140 ITR 168 (AP)].
CBDT CIRCULAR NO. 19/2017 — Trade Advances Outside Section 2(40)(e):
The CBDT clarified that trade advances which are in the nature of commercial transactions do not fall within the ambit of the word "advance" under Section 2(40)(e). Settled examples include:
  • CIT v. Creative Dyeing & Printing Pvt. Ltd. (Del): Advances to sister concern adjusted against dues for job work done do not constitute deemed dividend.
  • CIT v. Amrik Singh (P&H): Advance given to shareholder to install machinery at his premises to fulfill the company's export order was for business expediency, hence not deemed dividend.
  • CIT v. Atul Engineering Udyog (All): Floating security deposit against use of electricity generators for mutual business operations is a normal business transaction.

ICAI Illustration 1 Trade Advance vs Deemed Dividend u/s 2(40)(e)

Problem Statement:

Dhaval is in the business of manufacturing customized kitchen equipments. He is also the Managing Director and held nearly 65% of the paid-up share capital of Aarav (P) Ltd. A substantial part of the business of Dhaval is obtained through Aarav (P) Ltd. For this purpose, Aarav (P) Ltd. passed on the advance received from its customers to Dhaval to execute the job work entrusted to him.

The Assessing Officer held that the advance money received by Dhaval is in the nature of loan given by Aarav (P) Ltd. to him and accordingly is deemed dividend within the meaning of the provisions of Section 2(40)(e). The Assessing Officer, therefore, made the addition by treating the advance money as deemed dividend.

Examine whether the action of the Assessing Officer is tenable in law.

Statutory Solution & Judicial Analysis

1. Statutory Legal Framework [Section 2(40)(e)]:
As per Section 2(40)(e) of the Income-tax Act, 2025, in case a company, not being a company in which the public are substantially interested, makes payment of any sum by way of advance or loan to a shareholder holding not less than 10% of the voting power / share capital of the company, then the payment so made shall be deemed to be dividend in the hands of such shareholder to the extent to which the company possesses accumulated profits.

2. Analysis of the Transaction:
In the present case, Dhaval holds 65% of the paid-up capital of Aarav (P) Ltd. Aarav (P) Ltd. passed on customer advances to Dhaval exclusively for the execution of job work entrusted to him. This is an advance granted in the ordinary course of commercial business transactions.

3. Jurisprudence & CBDT Circular:
In CIT v. Rajkumar (2009) 318 ITR 462 (Del) and CIT v. Ambassador Travels (P) Ltd. (2009) 318 ITR 376 (Del), it was held that trade advances given to a shareholder which are in the nature of money transacted to give effect to a commercial transaction do not amount to deemed dividend under Section 2(40)(e).

Furthermore, the CBDT Circular No. 19/2017 dated 12.06.2017 clarified that it is a settled position that trade advances, which are in the nature of commercial transactions, do not fall within the ambit of the word "advance" in Section 2(40)(e) and therefore cannot be treated as deemed dividend.

CONCLUSION:
Since the payment was made to Dhaval by Aarav (P) Ltd. for execution of work in the course of a commercial business transaction, it cannot be treated as deemed dividend under Section 2(40)(e). Hence, the action of the Assessing Officer is NOT tenable in law.
ICAI Illustration 2 Loan to 15% Shareholder Repaid Before Year-End

Problem Statement:

MNO (P) Ltd. is a company in which the public are not substantially interested. K is a shareholder of the company holding 15% of the equity shares. The accumulated profits of the company as on 01.10.2026 amounted to ₹ 10,00,000. The company lent ₹ 1,00,000 to K by an account payee bank draft on 01.10.2026. The loan was not connected with the business of the company.

K repaid the loan to the company by an account payee bank draft on 30.03.2027.

Examine the effect of the borrowal and repayment of the loan by K on the computation of his total income for the tax year 2026-27.

Statutory Solution & Workings

1. Statutory Applicability [Section 2(40)(e)]:
As per Section 2(40)(e), any payment by a company in which the public are not substantially interested, by way of advance or loan to a shareholder who is the beneficial owner of shares holding not less than 10% of the voting power, shall be treated as dividend to the extent to which the company possesses accumulated profits on that date.

2. Evaluation of Facts:

  • MNO (P) Ltd. is a closely held company.
  • Accumulated profits on 01.10.2026 = ₹ 10,00,000.
  • K holds 15% equity shares (≥ 10% threshold satisfied).
  • Loan given = ₹ 1,00,000 (not in the course of company's business).
  • Since accumulated profits (₹ 10,00,000) ≥ Loan amount (₹ 1,00,000), the entire loan of ₹ 1,00,000 is deemed to be dividend.

3. Effect of Repayment before Year-End:
Under Section 2(40)(e), tax liability crystallizes the very moment the loan is borrowed by the shareholder. It is completely immaterial whether the loan is repaid before the end of the accounting year or not (settled by the Supreme Court in Tarulata Shyam v. CIT).

CONCLUSION:
The repayment of the loan by K to the company on 30.03.2027 will NOT affect the taxability of the sum of ₹ 1,00,000 as deemed dividend.
Therefore, ₹ 1,00,000 is chargeable to tax as deemed dividend under Section 2(40)(e) under the head "Income from Other Sources" in the hands of Mr. K at normal slab rates for the Tax Year 2026-27.
Comprehensive Anti-Abuse Code

Taxation of Gifts & Under-Valued Receipts [Section 92(2)(m)] Section 92(2)(m) | Formerly Sec 56(2)(x)

To prevent the widespread practice of disguising unaccounted money, bogus transactions, and transfers of valuable assets under the veneer of gifts or sham prices, Section 92(2)(m) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 operates as a comprehensive anti-avoidance provision. It levies tax on any person (individuals, HUFs, firms, LLPs, companies, AOPs, BOIs) who receives money or specified property without consideration or for inadequate consideration.

The Three Statutory Pillars of Section 92(2)(m)

Section 92(2)(m) brings to charge under "Income from Other Sources" any receipt during the tax year of:

  1. Sum of Money: Any cash, cheque, draft, online transfer without consideration exceeding ₹ 50,000 in aggregate.
  2. Immovable Property (Land, Building, or Both): Held as capital asset, received either without consideration (SDV > ₹ 50,000) or for inadequate consideration (SDV exceeding consideration by more than higher of ₹ 50,000 or 10% of consideration).
  3. Movable Property (Specified 9 Capital Assets): Shares/securities, jewellery, archaeological collections, drawings, paintings, sculptures, art works, bullion, or VDAs received without consideration (aggregate FMV > ₹ 50,000) or for inadequate consideration (aggregate difference > ₹ 50,000).
Category 1: Pure Cash Receipts

Sum of Money Received Without Consideration Aggregate > ₹ 50,000

Where any sum of money is received by any person without consideration during a tax year:

Aggregate Limit Exceeds ₹ 50,000

If the aggregate sum received from one or more persons during the entire tax year exceeds ₹ 50,000, the WHOLE OF SUCH SUM is chargeable to tax under IFOS (not merely the excess over ₹ 50,000).

Aggregate Limit Up to ₹ 50,000

If the aggregate sum received across all non-relatives during the tax year is ₹ 50,000 or less, NIL amount is chargeable to tax.

AGGREGATION PRINCIPLE:
The ₹ 50,000 threshold applies to the aggregate sum received from all donors during the tax year. For example, if Mr. Rahul receives ₹ 25,000 from Friend A, ₹ 18,000 from Friend B, and ₹ 12,000 from Friend C, the total is ₹ 55,000. Since ₹ 55,000 > ₹ 50,000, the entire ₹ 55,000 is taxable in Rahul's hands!

Date of Agreement vs Date of Registration Rule

Where the date of an agreement fixing the amount of consideration and the date of registration are not the same:

  • The Stamp Duty Value as on the date of agreement may be adopted for comparing against consideration;
  • MANDATORY PRE-CONDITION: Whole or at least part of the consideration must have been paid by way of an account payee cheque, account payee bank draft, electronic clearing system (ECS), or specified online mode (IMPS, UPI, NEFT, RTGS, debit/credit cards) on or before the date of agreement!
  • If payment is made in cash, bearer cheque, or crossed cheque, the benefit of agreement date SDV is legally forfeitable, and the SDV as on the date of registration MUST be applied!
SYMMETRY BETWEEN SELLER AND BUYER:
When immovable property is transferred for inadequate consideration:
  • In the hands of the SELLER: Full value of consideration is deemed to be SDV under Section 53 (if stock-in-trade) or Section 77 [formerly Section 50C] (if capital asset).
  • In the hands of the BUYER: The difference [SDV - Consideration] is taxable as IFOS under Section 92(2)(m).
  • Cost of Acquisition for Buyer upon Future Sale: As per Section 73(1) (Table Sl. No. 17) [formerly Section 49(4)], the cost of acquisition is stepped up to the SDV charged to tax under Section 92(2)(m)!

Bonus Shares & Rights Issues Jurisprudence PCIT v. Dr. Ranjan Pai

Issue: Can bonus shares received by shareholders be taxable under 'Income from other sources' under Section 92(2)(m) / Section 56(2)(x) on the ground that they are received without consideration?

Judicial Ratio: The issue of bonus shares by capitalization of reserves is merely a reallocation of the company's funds. There is no inflow of fresh funds or increase in capital employed. When a shareholder receives bonus shares, the value of the original shares held goes down proportionally, and the aggregate intrinsic value of the total shares remains the same. Thus, any profit derived on receipt of bonus shares is compensated by depreciation in the value of the original shares.

HELD: The provisions of Section 92(2)(m) are NOT attracted on receipt of bonus shares by existing shareholders.

Subscription to additional right shares in proportion to existing shareholding at a price lower than FMV does not attract Section 92(2)(m), because there is no gratuitous benefit conferred from an external donor; it is merely an exercise of pre-emptive rights where the value of original shares also gets diluted.

The Assessing Officer had treated the amount of advance as deemed dividend.

Is the action of the Assessing Officer correct in law?

Statutory Analysis & Landmark Supreme Court Ratio

1. Statutory Legal Framework [Section 2(40)(e)]:
Under Section 2(40)(e) of the Income-tax Act, 2025, any payment by a company in which the public are not substantially interested, of any sum by way of advance or loan made on behalf of, or for the individual benefit of any shareholder who is the beneficial owner of shares holding not less than 10% of the voting power, shall be treated as deemed dividend to the extent to which the company possesses accumulated profits.

2. Examination of Facts:

  • Heavens Engg. Pvt. Ltd. is a closely held company.
  • Mr. Parimal holds 70% of the paid-up capital (≥ 10% threshold satisfied).
  • Accumulated profits (General Reserve) as on 31.03.2026 = ₹ 6,00,000.
  • The company advanced an interest-free loan of ₹ 5,00,000 to its Supervisor (whose modest salary is ₹ 4,000 p.m.), who within two weeks (on 15.04.2026) passed on the identical loan to Mr. Parimal.

3. Supreme Court Ruling in L. Alagusundaram Chettiar v. CIT (2001) 252 ITR 893:
The Supreme Court held that the law looks at the real substance of the transaction rather than its superficial form. Where an employee of meager financial means is used merely as a conduit or conduit-pipe to route company funds to a substantial shareholder, the loan shall be construed as an amount given for the individual benefit of such substantial shareholder.

Since the company possessed accumulated profits of ₹ 6,00,000, which exceeds the loan of ₹ 5,00,000, the entire amount of ₹ 5,00,000 constitutes deemed dividend.

FINAL CONCLUSION:
The amount of interest-free loan of ₹ 5,00,000 given by the company to the supervisor and immediately passed to Mr. Parimal is taxable as deemed dividend under Section 2(40)(e). Hence, the action of the Assessing Officer is fully CORRECT in law.
TYK Problem 2 Advance for Advantage Conferred (Mortgaged Personal Property)

Examination Problem Statement:

Mr. Santhanam holding 25% voting power in VKS Manufacturing Private Limited permitted his own land to be mortgaged to a bank for enabling the company to obtain a loan.

Mr. Santhanam requested the company to release the property from the mortgage. The company failed to do so, but for retaining the benefit of the bank loan it gave an advance of ₹ 10 lakhs to Mr. Santhanam, which was authorized by a resolution passed by the Board of Directors.

The company's accumulated profit on the date of payment of advance was ₹ 50 lakhs. The Assessing Officer proposes to treat the amount of ₹ 10 lakhs as deemed dividend by invoking the provision of Section 2(40)(e).

Is the proposition of the Assessing Officer correct in law?

Judicial Doctrine of Quid Pro Quo vs Gratuitous Advance

1. Core Statutory Issue:
Whether a loan or advance given to a substantial shareholder by a company in return for an advantage or benefit conferred on the company by the shareholder can be deemed as dividend under Section 2(40)(e).

2. Landmark Precedent — Pradip Kumar Malhotra v. CIT (2011) 338 ITR 538 (Calcutta HC):
The Calcutta High Court held that the phrase "by way of advance or loan" appearing in Section 2(40)(e) must be construed to mean those advances or loans which a shareholder enjoys simply on account of being a substantial shareholder.

In case such loan or advance is given to a shareholder as a consequence of any further consideration received from such shareholder which is beneficial to the company, such advance or loan cannot be a deemed dividend within the meaning of the Act.

Only gratuitous loans or advances given by a company to a substantial shareholder come within the purview of Section 2(40)(e), but NOT cases where the loan/advance is given in return for an advantage conferred upon the company.

3. Application to Mr. Santhanam's Case:
In this case, the advance of ₹ 10 lakhs was given to Mr. Santhanam in lieu of non-release of his personal property from mortgage, thereby enabling the company to retain the benefit of the bank credit facility. It was paid to protect and advance the business interest of the company.

FINAL CONCLUSION:
Applying the ratio of Pradip Kumar Malhotra, the advance of ₹ 10 lakhs was not gratuitous but was given for valuable commercial consideration to protect the company's borrowing facility. Therefore, the proposition of the Assessing Officer to treat the amount as deemed dividend is NOT correct in law.

TYK Problem 4 Marketing Manager Gifted Maruti Car by Distributor

Examination Problem Statement:

In July 2026, Mr. Pervez, employed as Marketing Manager in a Pharma company, received a Maruti car as gift from a distributor of the company. The value of the gifted car is estimated at ₹ 2,60,000.

Is the value of the car taxable as income? If so, under what head is it taxable?

Analysis Across Tax Heads & Scope of "Property"

1. Examination under the head "Salaries" [Section 18]:
Mr. Pervez received the car from a distributor of the company, and NOT from his employer. Since there is no employer-employee relationship between the distributor and Mr. Pervez, the value of the gift cannot be taxed as a salary perquisite under Section 18.

2. Examination under the head "Profits and Gains of Business or Profession" [Section 26(e) / Section 28(iv)]:
Section 26(e) / Section 28(iv) taxes the value of any benefit or perquisite arising from business or the exercise of a profession. Since Mr. Pervez is an employee in employment and does not carry on any independent business or profession, Section 26(e) is NOT attracted.

3. Examination under "Income from Other Sources" [Section 92(2)(m)]:
Section 92(2)(m) brings within the tax net the value of any property received by any person without consideration. For this purpose, "property" is defined exhaustively to mean:

  • Immovable property being land or building or both;
  • Shares and securities;
  • Jewellery;
  • Archaeological collections, drawings, paintings, sculptures, any work of art;
  • Bullion; or
  • Virtual Digital Assets (VDA).

Since a motor car is NOT included in the statutory definition of "property", receipt of a car without consideration falls completely outside the purview of Section 92(2)(m).

FINAL CONCLUSION:
The value of the Maruti car of ₹ 2,60,000 received as a gift by Mr. Pervez from the distributor is NOT TAXABLE AS INCOME under the head "Salaries", nor under "PGBP", nor under "Income from Other Sources", nor under any other provision of the Act!
