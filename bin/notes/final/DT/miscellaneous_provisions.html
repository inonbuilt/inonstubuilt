1. Statutory Architecture & Chapter Scheme
#sec-overview

Chapter 18 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 compiles the vital administrative, enforcement, reporting, and transitory provisions that underpin the direct tax framework in India. It bridges substantive tax charging provisions with procedural integrity, safeguarding public revenue against tax avoidance, unrecorded cash circulation, fraudulent alienations, and procedural invalidations.

2. Cash Restrictions & Prescribed Electronic Modes
Secs 185, 186, 187, 188

To curb the circulation of black money, suppress tax evasion, and promote a digital financial architecture, Sections 185 to 188 impose stringent statutory prohibitions on taking, receiving, and repaying loans, deposits, and specified advance sums in cash, backed by equivalent 100% monetary penalties.

Section 185: Prohibition on Accepting Loans, Deposits and Specified Sums in Cash (Old Sec 269SS)

Section 185 mandates that no person shall take or accept from another person any loan or deposit or specified sum, otherwise than by an:

  • (a) Account payee cheque; or
  • (b) Account payee bank draft; or
  • (c) Electronic clearing system (ECS) through a bank account; or
  • (d) Any other prescribed electronic mode (Rule 133 / Tier-III CBDC).
Statutory Thresholds under Section 185:
The prohibition triggers if, on the date of taking or accepting such loan, deposit, or specified sum:
  1. The amount or aggregate amount of such loan, deposit, or specified sum is ₹20,000 or more; or
  2. The amount or aggregate amount of any previously taken/accepted loan, deposit, or specified sum by such person from such other person, which remains unpaid (whether due for repayment or not) on that date, is ₹20,000 or more; or
  3. The aggregate of the fresh amount (1) and previous unpaid amount (2) is ₹20,000 or more.
Enhanced Limit of ₹2,00,000 for Co-operative Societies:
A higher threshold of ₹2,00,000 is applicable where:
  • Such deposit is accepted by a Primary Agricultural Credit Society (PACS) or a Primary Co-operative Agricultural and Rural Development Bank (PCARDB) from its member; or
  • A loan is taken from a PACS or PCARDB by its member.

Statutory Exceptions from Section 185

The prohibition under Section 185 does NOT apply to:

  1. Government & Regulated Entities: Loan, deposit, or specified sum taken or accepted from or by:
    • The Central Government or any State Government;
    • Any banking company, post office savings bank, or co-operative bank;
    • Any corporation established by a Central, State, or Provincial Act;
    • Any Government company as defined in Section 2(45) of the Companies Act, 2013;
    • Any institution, association, or body notified by the Central Government.
  2. Pure Agriculturists Exemption: Where the person taking or accepting such loan, deposit, or specified sum and the person from whom such loan, deposit, or specified sum is taken or accepted, both have only agricultural income and neither of them has any income chargeable to tax under the Act.
Key Statutory Term Statutory Definition & Scope
Loan or Deposit Any loan or deposit of money.
Specified Sum Any sum of money receivable, whether as advance or otherwise, in relation to transfer of an immovable property, whether or not the transfer takes place.
Prescribed Electronic Modes (a) Credit Card; (b) Debit Card; (c) Net Banking; (d) IMPS; (e) UPI; (f) RTGS; (g) NEFT; (h) BHIM Aadhaar Pay; (i) Tier-III: Full KYC Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC) wallets, P-CBDC, Wholesale/Crossborder CBDC.
Penalty for Contravention of Section 185 [Section 450 / Section 471 (Old Sec 271D)]:
If a person takes or accepts any loan, deposit, or specified sum in contravention of Section 185, the Assessing Officer / Joint Commissioner may impose a penalty of a sum equal to the amount of the loan, deposit, or specified sum so taken or accepted (100% penalty).
Mitigation: Under Section 470 (Old 273B), no penalty shall be imposable if the assessee proves that there was reasonable cause for the failure.

Section 186: Mode of Undertaking Transactions & Prescribed Banking Mode (Old Sec 269ST)

Section 186 enacts an overarching cash receipt ceiling of ₹2,00,000. It provides that no person shall receive an amount of ₹2,00,000 or more:

A
In Aggregate from a Person in a Day
Multiple cash receipts on the same day from the same person summing to ₹2,00,000 or more (even under different bills).
B
In Respect of a Single Transaction
A single transaction or single bill amounting to ₹2,00,000 or more, even if split across multiple days.
C
In Respect of Transactions Relating to One Event or Occasion from a Person
All transactions linked to one event or function (e.g. marriage catering, venue, event decor) from a single payer aggregating to ₹2,00,000 or more.

Receipt of such sums must be made solely through account payee cheque, account payee bank draft, electronic clearing system through a bank account, or other prescribed electronic modes.

Statutory Exceptions under Section 186

The prohibition under Section 186 does not apply to:

  • (1) Any receipt by Government, banking company, post office savings bank, or co-operative bank;
  • (2) Transactions of the nature referred to in Section 185 (loans/deposits/specified immovable property sums);
  • (3) Receipts by business correspondents on behalf of banks per RBI guidelines (Notification No. 28/2017);
  • (4) Receipts by White Label ATM Operators from retail outlets on behalf of banks (Notification No. 28/2017);
  • (5) Cash withdrawals by any person from a bank, co-operative bank, or post office savings bank (Notification No. 28/2017);
  • (6) Receipts from agents by issuers of prepaid payment instruments under RBI authorisation;
  • (7) Receipts by credit card companies against bills raised in respect of credit cards;
  • (8) Receipts which are exempt from total income under Section 10 (Notification No. 57/2017).

Crucial CBDT Clarifications on Section 186

Circular No. 22/2017 (NBFC / HFC Loan Instalments):
In respect of receipts by NBFCs or Housing Finance Companies (HFCs) as loan repayments, the receipt of one instalment of loan repayment constitutes a ‘single transaction’ under clause (b) of Section 186. All instalments paid over the tenure of a loan shall NOT be aggregated together. Thus, cash repayment of an EMI below ₹2 lakh is valid even if total loan repaid over time exceeds ₹2 lakh.
Circular No. 25/2022 (Co-operative Societies Dealership Contracts):
In respect of Co-operative Societies, a dealership or distributorship contract by itself does not constitute an "event or occasion" under clause (c). Receipts related to such contracts on any day within the prescribed limit complying with clauses (a) and (b) may not be aggregated across multiple days for that previous year.
Circular No. 27/2017 (Cash Sale of Agricultural Produce by Cultivators to Traders):
Rule 26 exempts cash purchases of agricultural produce from disallowance u/s 36(4) / 40A(3) in the hands of the trader.
However, Section 186 prohibits receipt of ₹2,00,000 or more in cash.
Harmonious Resolution: Where a cultivator sells agricultural produce to a trader in cash for an amount less than ₹2,00,000:
  • (a) No disallowance u/s 36(4) in the hands of the trader;
  • (b) No violation u/s 186 in the hands of the cultivator; and
  • (c) No requirement for the cultivator to quote PAN or furnish Form 97 (Rule 159).
Penalty for Contravention of Section 186 [Section 473 (Old Sec 271DA)]:
The Joint Commissioner may impose on the recipient a penalty of a sum equal to the amount of such receipt (100% penalty). Section 470 provides relief if reasonable cause is established.

Section 187: Mandatory Acceptance of Payments Through Prescribed Electronic Modes (Old Sec 269SU)

Section 187 mandates that every person carrying on business or profession whose total sales, turnover, or gross receipts in such business/profession exceeds ₹50 Crores during the immediately preceding tax year shall provide a facility for accepting payment through prescribed electronic modes, in addition to any other electronic facilities offered.

Exemption for Pure B2B Entities (Circular No. 12/2020):
The CBDT clarified that the provisions of Section 187 shall NOT apply to a specified person having only B2B transactions (i.e. no transactions with retail customers/consumers) if at least 95% of aggregate of all amounts received during the previous year (including sales turnover and gross receipts) are received by any mode other than cash.
Penalty for Default under Section 187 [Section 452 / Section 474 (Old Sec 271DB)]:
The Joint Commissioner may impose on a person failing to provide such prescribed electronic payment facility a penalty of ₹5,000 for every day during which the default continues. Section 470 reasonable cause defense applies.

Section 188: Mode of Repayment of Loans, Deposits & Specified Advances (Old Sec 269T)

Section 188 provides that no branch of a banking company or a co-operative bank, company, co-operative society, firm, or other person shall repay any loan or deposit made with it or any specified advance received by it, otherwise than through an:

  • (a) Account payee cheque;
  • (b) Account payee bank draft drawn in the name of the payee; or
  • (c) Electronic clearing system (ECS) through a bank account or prescribed electronic modes.
Repayment Aggregation Thresholds under Section 188:
Applicable if, on the date of repayment:
  1. The amount of loan, deposit, or specified advance together with interest is ₹20,000 or more; or
  2. The aggregate amount of loans or deposits held by such person (either in his own name or jointly with any other person) on that date together with interest is ₹20,000 or more; or
  3. The aggregate amount of specified advances received by such person (either in his own name or jointly with any other person) on that date together with interest is ₹20,000 or more.
Enhanced Limit of ₹2,00,000 for PACS / PCARDB:
A higher limit of ₹2,00,000 applies where such deposit is repaid to a member by a PACS / PCARDB, or such loan is repaid by a member to a PACS / PCARDB.

Statutory Exceptions under Section 188

  1. Direct Bank Account Credit: Where repayment is made by a banking company or co-operative bank by directly crediting the loan/deposit amount to the savings or current account of the depositor with such branch.
  2. Government & Institutional Payees: Repayment of loan, deposit, or specified advance taken from Government, banks, post office savings banks, statutory corporations, or Government companies.
Penalty for Contravention of Section 188 [Section 453 / Section 475 (Old Sec 271E)]:
The Joint Commissioner may impose a penalty equal to the amount of the loan, deposit, or specified advance so repaid (100% penalty). Section 470 reasonable cause defense applies.
CIT v. Triumph International Finance (I.) Ltd. (2012) 345 ITR 270 Bombay High Court
Issue: Where an assessee repays a loan merely by passing journal adjustment entries in books of account against cross-liabilities (e.g. sale price of shares), does it contravene Section 188 (Old 269T) and attract penalty?
Decision & Ratio: The High Court held that the obligation to repay loans by account payee instruments is mandatory and admits of no verbal qualification. Repayment via book journal entries technically contravenes Section 188.

However, where the cross-transactions were genuine, transparent, entered into in the ordinary course of business to avoid unnecessary circular movements of funds, and not intended to evade tax, it constitutes ‘reasonable cause’ under Section 470 (Old 273B). Therefore, no penalty can be imposed on the assessee!
3. Safeguarding Revenue: Fraudulent Transfers & Provisional Attachment
Secs 499 & 500

To prevent tax defaulters from defeating tax recovery through collusive alienations or dissipating assets during pending assessments, Sections 499 and 500 provide powerful statutory armor to the Income-tax Department.

Section 499: Certain Transfers to Defraud Revenue to be Deemed Void (Old Sec 281)

Section 499 acts as an statutory safeguard against non-realisation of tax revenue caused by fraudulent transferring of assets by a defaulting assessee.

Statutory Conditions for Invoking Section 499:
  1. Timing of Transfer: During the pendency of any proceeding under the Income-tax Act, 2025, or after completion thereof but before service of notice by the Tax Recovery Officer (TRO) as per Section 413;
  2. Nature of Transfer: Assessee creates a charge on, or parts with the property by way of sale, mortgage, gift, exchange, or any other mode of transfer of any of his assets in favour of any person;
  3. Legal Effect: Such charge or transfer must be deemed to be void as against any claim in respect of any tax or other sum payable by the assessee as a result of completion of proceedings or otherwise.

De Minimis Monetary Thresholds

The provisions of Section 499 apply only to cases where:

  • (a) The amount of tax or other sum of money payable or likely to be payable exceeds ₹5,000; and
  • (b) The assets which are charged or transferred by the assessee exceed ₹10,000 in value.

Statutory Safe Harbours (When Transfer is NOT Void)

A transfer or charge made by the assessee shall NOT be void if it is made:

Bona Fide Transfer for Adequate Consideration
Made for adequate consideration and without any notice of the pendency of such proceeding or without notice of such tax or other monies remaining payable by the assessee.
With Previous Permission of the Assessing Officer
The assessee obtains prior written approval from the jurisdictional Assessing Officer before executing the charge or conveyance.
Scope of ‘Assets’ for Section 499:
The term ‘assets’ covers land, buildings, machinery, plant, shares, securities, fixed deposits in banks, and Virtual Digital Assets (VDAs), to the extent to which any of these do not form part of the stock-in-trade of the business of the assessee.
Dr. Manoj Kabra v. ITO (2014) 364 ITR 541 Allahabad High Court
Issue: Can the Assessing Officer suo motu assume jurisdiction to declare a registered sale deed or property transfer void under Section 499?
Decision & Ratio: The High Court held that the Income-tax Act does not confer any exclusive adjudicatory machinery upon the Assessing Officer to declare a transfer void.

To declare a transfer fraudulent and void against the department, the Assessing Officer must institute a civil suit for a declaration in a competent Civil Court under Section 53 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882. The AO cannot unilaterally pass an administrative order declaring the sale deed void!

Section 500: Provisional Attachment to Protect the Interests of the Revenue (Old Sec 281B)

Section 500 empowers the Assessing Officer to provisionally attach any property belonging to the assessee during the pendency of assessment or penalty proceedings, subject to strict procedural checks.

Parameter Statutory Requirement under Section 500
Jurisdictional Triggers Pendency of:
• Assessment or reassessment of any income which has escaped assessment; or
• Penalty proceedings under Section 444 (false entry / omission in books of account) where penalty likely to be imposed exceeds ₹2 Crores.
Supervisory Approval Mandatory previous approval of the Competent Authority: Principal Chief Commissioner / Chief Commissioner / Principal Commissioner / Commissioner / Principal Director General / Director General / Director.
Initial Validity 6 Months from the date of the provisional attachment order.
Extension of Period Competent Authority may extend for reasons in writing. Total period of extension shall not exceed 2 years or 60 days after the date of assessment/reassessment order, whichever is later.
Revocation on Bank Guarantee [Sec 500(4)-(5)] AO shall revoke attachment if assessee furnishes an irrevocable scheduled bank guarantee for an amount not less than the Fair Market Value (FMV) of the property (or lower if AO is satisfied revenue is protected).
Valuation Officer Reference [Sec 500(6)] AO may refer FMV determination to Valuation Officer u/s 269(3)-(7). Valuation Officer must submit report within 30 days.
Time Limits for Revocation Order [Sec 500(7)] • Where reference made to Valuation Officer: within 45 days from receipt of bank guarantee;
• In any other case: within 15 days from receipt of bank guarantee.
Invocation of Guarantee [Sec 500(8)-(10)] • On failure to satisfy notice of demand within time specified; or
• Failure to renew guarantee before 15 days of its expiry.
Realised funds adjusted against tax demand; surplus kept in Personal Deposit Account of PCIT/CIT with RBI/SBI.
Stock Exchange, Ahmedabad v. ACIT (2001) 248 ITR 209 Supreme Court of India
Issue: Can the membership card of a defaulting stockbroker be attached by the Assessing Officer under Section 500 (Old 281B)?
Decision & Ratio: The Supreme Court held that the right of membership in a Stock Exchange is not a private alienable asset belonging to the member. It is merely a personal privilege granted by the Exchange, subject to its rules, non-transferable at the sole will of the member, and incapable of automatic succession or alienation.

Because the membership card is not the alienable property of the assessee, it CANNOT be attached under Section 500!
4. Service of Notice, Authentication & Technical Regularity
Secs 501, 502, 503, 504, 522, 523

Procedural regularity in issuing, authenticating, and serving statutory notices is the bedrock of valid tax proceedings. Sections 501 to 523 establish modern digital delivery rules while guarding against frivolous invalidations.

Section 501: Permissible Modes of Service of Notice (Old Sec 282)

Section 501 provides that service of any notice, summons, requisition, order, or communication under the Act may be effected by delivering or transmitting a copy thereof to the person named therein:

1
By Post or Approved Courier
Registered post with acknowledgement due, speed post, or courier services approved by the CBDT.
2
Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (CPC)
In such manner as provided in the CPC for service of summons (including personal delivery, affixation, or substituted service).
3
Electronic Record under IT Act, 2000
Transmission of electronic records (emails, portal communications, notifications) under Chapter IV of the Information Technology Act, 2000.
4
Prescribed Digital Addresses (Rule 127)
Delivered to email address available in e-filing portal, PAN database, MCA portal (for companies), or banking KYC records.

Section 502: Authentication of Notices & Mandatory DIN (Old Sec 282A)

Section 502 read with Rule 233 mandates that any notice, summons, or document issued by an income-tax authority must be signed and issued in paper form or communicated in electronic form in accordance with prescribed procedure.

Document Identification Number (DIN) & Digital Signatures:
Every notice, order, or statutory communication issued by any tax authority must bear a computer-generated Document Identification Number (DIN). Under CBDT Circular 19/2019, any communication issued without a DIN is non-est and invalid ab initio.
In electronic communications, authentication is established if the name and office of the designated income-tax authority is printed/stamped thereon and authenticated via Digital Signature (DSC) or Electronic Verification Code (EVC).

Sections 503 & 504: Service of Notice on Disrupted HUF, Dissolved Firms & Discontinued Business

Status of Assessee Section Persons upon Whom Notice Must Be Addressed & Served
Partitioned Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) Sec 503 • On the person who was the last Manager (Karta) of the HUF; or
• If he is dead, then on all adult members who were members of the HUF immediately before the partition.
Dissolved Partnership Firm / AOP Sec 503 On any person who was a partner (not being a minor) or member of the association immediately before its dissolution.
Discontinued Business / Profession Sec 504 • Person whose income is to be assessed; or
• Member of firm/AOP at the time of discontinuance; or
• Principal Officer in the case of a company.

Section 522: Technical Defects Not to Invalidate Returns, Notices & Assessments (Old Sec 292B)

Section 522 provides that no return of income, assessment order, notice, summons, or other proceeding shall be invalid or deemed invalid merely by reason of any mistake, defect, or omission, if such return, order, notice, or proceeding is in substance and effect in conformity with or according to the intent and purpose of this Act.

Scope of Protection under Section 522:
  • Typographical / Clerical Mistakes: An assessment order mentioning Tax Year 2026-27 instead of 2027-28 in one place, while the entire substance pertains to T.Y. 2027-28, remains completely valid.
  • DIN Typographical Errors: No assessment shall be invalid for mistake, defect, or omission in quoting computer-generated DIN, if the order is referenced by such number in any manner.
  • Supervisory Approvals: Under Section 522(3), electronic approvals by superior authorities are administrative/supervisory and cannot be challenged on grounds of insufficiency of reasons or absence of digital signature.

Section 523: Notice Deemed Valid where Assessee Participated in Proceedings (Old Sec 292BB)

Section 523 creates a statutory estoppel. Where an assessee has appeared in any proceeding or co-operated in any inquiry relating to assessment or reassessment, it shall be deemed that any statutory notice required to be served was duly served in time in accordance with the Act.

Estoppel against Raising Service Objections:
The assessee is precluded from raising any objection at an appellate stage that the notice was:
(a) Not served upon him; or
(b) Not served upon him in time; or
(c) Served upon him in an improper manner.

VITAL PROVISO: This deeming fiction does NOT apply if the assessee raised an objection in writing BEFORE the completion of such assessment or reassessment!
CIT v. Laxman Das Khandelwal (2019) 417 ITR 325 Supreme Court of India
Issue: Does Section 523 (Old 292BB) cure complete absence or non-issuance of a statutory jurisdictional notice (e.g. u/s 270(8) / 143(2)) if the assessee participated in the assessment?
Decision & Ratio: The Supreme Court held that Section 523 operates only to cure infirmities in the MANNER of service of a notice that has actually emanated from the Department.

Section 523 DOES NOT cure complete absence or failure to issue the statutory notice. Notice u/s 270(8) is foundational to assumption of jurisdiction; its total non-issuance renders the assessment void ab initio, despite taxpayer participation!
Pr. CIT v. Maruti Suzuki India Ltd. (2019) 416 ITR 613 Supreme Court of India
Issue: Is a notice issued to an amalgamating company (which ceased to exist upon court approval of amalgamation) curable under Section 523 if the amalgamated successor participated?
Decision & Ratio: The Supreme Court held that upon sanctioned amalgamation, the amalgamating entity ceases to exist in law and is no longer a ‘person’ under Section 2(31).

Initiating assessment proceedings against a dead/non-existent entity is a substantive jurisdictional illegality, not a procedural irregularity. Participation by the amalgamated entity cannot cure this fundamental defect under Section 523!
5. Statutory Reporting Obligations & Information Statements
Secs 507, 508, 452, 509, 510

Modern income-tax administration relies extensively on automated, third-party, and institutional reporting. Sections 507 to 510 establish robust frameworks for gathering financial intelligence, eliminating information asymmetry, and powering seamless pre-filling of returns.

Section 507: Statements by Film Producers & Persons in Specified Activities (Old Sec 285B)

Section 507 read with Rule 236 requires every producer of a cinematograph film or person engaged in any specified activity (or both) during the whole or any part of any tax year to furnish a statement in Form No. 164.

Parameter Statutory Rule & Procedure
Prescribed Form Form No. 164 (Rule 236)
Due Date for Filing Within 60 days from the end of the tax year (or within 60 days of completion of production/activity).
Filing Mode & Authority Electronically to the Director General of Income-tax (Systems) or authorised officer, who forwards it to the jurisdictional Assessing Officer.
Reporting Threshold Particulars of all payments exceeding ₹50,000 in the aggregate made by him or due from him to each person engaged in such production or activity.
Scope of ‘Specified Activity’ (i) Event management;
(ii) Documentary production;
(iii) Production of programmes for television, OTT, or similar streaming platforms;
(iv) Sports event management;
(v) Other performing arts or any activity notified by the Central Government.

Section 508: Statement of Financial Transactions (SFT) or Reportable Account (Old Sec 285BA)

Section 508 imposes a mandatory obligation on designated reporting entities to register, maintain records, and furnish a Statement of Financial Transaction (SFT) or Reportable Account in Form No. 165 on or before 31st May following the relevant financial year.

TYK 1 Residuary NBFC — Cash Deposits, Repayments & Joint Holdings Secs 185, 188
Question 1:
Fearless General Finance & Investment Limited, a residuary non-banking company, accepts public deposits, issues deposit certificates and repays the same after some period of time along with interest, under different schemes run by it. Following transactions were noted from their books of account:

(i) Mr. A, an individual, has deposited ₹15,000 on 1st May, 2026 for 48 months by bearer cheque and another ₹15,000 on 30th June, 2026 in cash to purchase a new certificate of 48 months tenure.
(ii) Mr. A has applied for premature withdrawal against both the certificates and the company has paid him ₹16,500, by a bearer cheque, against principal and interest on 23rd March, 2027, due against his first certificate (purchased in May 2026) and ₹15,500 in cash on 25th March, 2027, against the second certificate.

Discuss the violation of income tax provisions, if any, and consequential penalty for each transaction. Will it make any difference if the certificates were held by Mr. A with his wife Mrs. A, jointly, while repaying back in cash or bearer cheque?

Statutory Analysis & Solution:

(i) Acceptance of Deposits [Section 185 (Old Sec 269SS)]:

  • First Deposit (01.05.2026): Deposit of ₹15,000 by bearer cheque does not exceed the threshold limit of ₹20,000. Hence, there is no violation of Section 185 at the time of accepting the first deposit.
  • Second Deposit (30.06.2026): On the date of taking the second deposit in cash of ₹15,000, there is already an earlier unpaid deposit of ₹15,000 remaining outstanding. The fresh deposit of ₹15,000 takes the aggregate outstanding deposit to ₹30,000, which exceeds the threshold limit of ₹20,000.
  • Consequence: Violation of Section 185 is attracted. Penalty under Section 450 / 471 equal to the amount of deposit taken (₹15,000) is leviable on the company.

(ii) Repayment of Deposits [Section 188 (Old Sec 269T)]:

  • First Repayment (23.03.2027): Company paid ₹16,500 by bearer cheque. On that date, the aggregate amount of deposits held by Mr. A with the company (together with interest payable) is ₹16,500 + ₹15,500 = ₹32,000, which exceeds ₹20,000. Repaying by bearer cheque violates Section 188 (which requires an account payee cheque/draft or ECS). Consequential penalty under Section 453 / 475 equal to ₹16,500 is attracted.
  • Second Repayment (25.03.2027): Paid ₹15,500 in cash. On this date, the first certificate has already been repaid and ceased to exist. Neither the amount repaid with interest (₹15,500) nor the aggregate deposits held by Mr. A on that date exceeds ₹20,000. Hence, the second repayment in cash does NOT violate Section 188.

Impact of Joint Holding with Wife:

It will make no difference even if the certificates were held by Mr. A in joint names with his wife Mrs. A. Section 188 expressly aggregates deposits held by such person “either in his own name or jointly with any other person” on the date of repayment. The violation and penalty on the first repayment stand confirmed.

TYK 3 Typographical Error in Tax Year — Section 522 Technical Regularity Sec 522
Question 3:
An order for T.Y. 2026-27 was passed by the Assessing Officer as per section 270, but the typist wrongly typed in the order, the tax year as T.Y. 2025-26. The assessee claimed in appeal that the same is an invalid order which was not accepted by the CIT (Appeals) on the ground of the error being of clerical nature. Discuss the correctness of the order of the CIT(Appeals).

Statutory Analysis & Solution:

Section 522 (Old Sec 292B) provides that no return of income, assessment order, notice, summons, or other proceeding furnished, made, or issued in pursuance of any provision of the Act shall be invalid or deemed invalid merely by reason of any mistake, defect, or omission, if such order, notice, or proceeding is in substance and effect in conformity with or according to the intent and purpose of this Act.

A clerical or typographical error cannot invalidate an otherwise substantive assessment order. In the given case, while the typist inadvertently wrote T.Y. 2025-26 in the heading, the entire computation, facts, and subject-matter of the order pertained to T.Y. 2026-27.

Therefore, the order was in substance and effect in conformity with the Act. The typographical error does not render the order invalid. The action of the CIT(Appeals) in rejecting the assessee's objection is fully valid and correct in law.

TYK 5 Provisional Attachment under Section 500 — Conditions & Revocation Sec 500
Question 5:
Explain the circumstances under which the Assessing Officer can resort to provisional attachment of the property of the assessee. Also, state the period of time for which such attachment can take place.
When can the Assessing Officer revoke provisional attachment of property? Discuss.

Statutory Analysis & Solution:

Circumstances for Provisional Attachment [Section 500 (Old Sec 281B)]:

  1. Pending Proceeding: During the pendency of any proceeding for the assessment of income or for assessment/reassessment of escaped income, OR for imposition of penalty under Section 444 (false entry in books) where penalty likely to exceed ₹2 Crores.
  2. Protection of Revenue: The Assessing Officer must form an opinion that provisional attachment is necessary to protect the interest of Revenue.
  3. Prior Supervisory Approval: The AO must obtain the previous approval of the Principal Chief Commissioner / Chief Commissioner / Principal Commissioner / Commissioner / Director General.
  4. Order in Writing: The AO may, by written order, attach provisionally any property belonging to the assessee in the manner provided in Section 413.

Duration of Provisional Attachment:

Provisional attachment remains valid for 6 months from the date of the order. The Competent Authority may extend the period for reasons recorded in writing, but total extension cannot exceed 2 years or 60 days after the date of assessment/reassessment order, whichever is later.

Revocation of Provisional Attachment:

  • The AO shall revoke attachment where the assessee furnishes a bank guarantee from a scheduled bank for an amount not less than the Fair Market Value (FMV) of the attached property (or for a lower sum if AO is satisfied revenue is protected).
  • Time Limit for Revocation Order: The revocation order must be passed within 15 days of receiving the guarantee (or within 45 days if reference was made to the Valuation Officer to estimate FMV).
  • The AO shall also release the guarantee forthwith if satisfied that it is no longer required to protect revenue.
TYK 6 Attachment of Stock Exchange Membership Card — SC Ruling Sec 500 • SC Landmark
Question 6:
Mr. Biswas, a stock broker, has defaulted with regard to his income-tax payments and the Assessing Officer has attached his membership card of Stock Exchange under section 500 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Mr. Biswas contends that the membership card is not transferable and is not his personal asset. Discuss the validity of attachment of the card by the Assessing Officer in the context of Section 500.

Statutory Analysis & Solution:

The right of membership in a Stock Exchange is not a private alienable asset belonging to the member; it is merely a personal privilege granted to him.

  • The membership card is non-transferable and incapable of alienation by the member or his legal representatives, except to the limited extent provided under the rules and regulations of the Stock Exchange.
  • Nomination, even if permitted, is not automatic and is subject to absolute discretion and rules of the Stock Exchange. Upon death or default of a member, the right of nomination vests absolutely in the Stock Exchange.

Because the membership card is a personal privilege and not the private alienable property of the assessee, it cannot be attached under Section 500.

Authoritative Landmark Precedent:
This proposition was settled by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Stock Exchange, Ahmedabad v. ACIT (2001) 248 ITR 209 (SC).
Therefore, the contention of Mr. Biswas is correct, and the action of the Assessing Officer in attaching the membership card is invalid in law.
TYK 7 Loan Credited in Cash — Penalty Ceiling & Self-Contradiction Secs 185, 450 • Landmark
Question 7:
An assessee had credited a sum of ₹50,000 in cash in the account of Madan, said to represent a loan obtained from him. The Assessing Officer, having gone into the genuineness of the transaction, disbelieved the story of loan and treated the sum of ₹50,000 as the income of the assessee from undisclosed sources. He also started proceedings under section 450 and levied a penalty of ₹60,000 on the assessee for having accepted the loan in contravention of section 185. Examine the correctness of the levy.

Statutory Analysis & Solution:

The action of the Assessing Officer is incorrect in law on two distinct grounds:

1. Penalty Exceeds Statutory Maximum Limit:

Under Section 450 / 471 (Old Sec 271D), the penalty leviable for contravention of Section 185 cannot exceed a sum equal to the amount of loan so taken or accepted. Since the alleged loan taken was ₹50,000, the maximum penalty permissible under law is ₹50,000. Imposing a penalty of ₹60,000 is patently illegal and beyond statutory jurisdiction.

2. Mutual Exclusivity and Self-Contradictory Stand:

The Assessing Officer cannot blow hot and cold simultaneously. On the one hand, the AO disbelieved the genuineness of the loan and assessed ₹50,000 as undisclosed income (cash credit) of the assessee. On the other hand, the AO levied penalty for accepting a loan in contravention of Section 185.

The moment the amount of ₹50,000 is treated and taxed as the assessee's undisclosed income, it ceases to bear the character of a loan. Consequently, the very foundation for the levy of penalty under Section 185 read with Section 450 disappears!

Authoritative Judicial Precedent:
In Diwan Enterprises v. CIT and Others (2000) 246 ITR 571 (Delhi High Court), it was held that treating an amount as undisclosed income and simultaneously levying penalty for accepting it as a loan is self-contradictory. The penalty order cannot stand.
Hence, the levy of penalty of ₹60,000 is completely unsustainable in law.
