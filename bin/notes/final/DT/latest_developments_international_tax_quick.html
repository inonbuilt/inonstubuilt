1. Architecture of the OECD/G20 Two-Pillar Solution

Pillar Primary Problem Addressed Scope of Application Core Economic Mechanism
Pillar One Reallocation of taxing rights to market jurisdictions in digitalized economy. MNEs with global turnover > € 20 Billion and profitability > 10%. Amount A: Reallocates 25% of residual profits to market states.
Amount B: Simplified wholesale distribution margins.
Pillar Two Race to the bottom in corporate tax rates; profit shifting to low-tax havens. MNE groups with consolidated annual revenue ≥ € 750 Million. Global Minimum Corporate Tax of 15% via interlocking rules (QDMTT, IIR, UTPR).

2. Pillar One: Amount A & Amount B Framework

Amount A Calculation Algorithm:
1. Scope Test: Global Turnover > € 20 Billion AND Profit Margin $( ext{PBT} / ext{Revenue}) > 10\%$.
2. Residual Profit: Profits exceeding the 10% routine baseline margin.
3. Reallocation: 25% of Residual Profit is allocated to eligible market jurisdictions based on local in-country sales revenues!
Component Description & Operation
Amount B Provides a streamlined, standardized return for baseline wholesale marketing and distribution activities to reduce transfer pricing disputes in developing countries.
Removal of DSTs All unilateral Digital Services Taxes (including Indian Equalisation Levy) must be permanently repealed upon entry into force of Amount A.

3. Pillar Two: 15% GloBE Minimum Tax Interlocking Rules

Rule Order Name of Rule Statutory Mechanism & Hierarchy
Priority 1 Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax (QDMTT) Allows the Source State where the low-tax entity is located to collect the top-up tax up to 15% before any foreign jurisdiction can tax it.
Priority 2 Income Inclusion Rule (IIR) The Ultimate Parent Entity (UPE) jurisdiction levies a top-up tax on the parent company in respect of low-taxed income of foreign subsidiaries where Effective Tax Rate (ETR) < 15%.
Priority 3 (Backstop) Undertaxed Profits Rule (UTPR) Applies if the low-tax jurisdiction has no QDMTT and the parent jurisdiction has not implemented IIR. Other group countries deny deductions or make adjustments to collect the top-up tax.
Effective Tax Rate (ETR) Formula:
$ ext{ETR in Jurisdiction} = rac{ ext{Adjusted Covered Taxes}}{ ext{GloBE Income}}$.
$ ext{Top-Up Tax Rate} = \maxig(0,\; 15\% - ext{ETR}ig)$.
$ ext{Top-Up Tax Payable} = ext{Top-Up Tax Rate} imes ig( ext{GloBE Income} - ext{Substance-Based Income Exclusion (SBIE)}ig)$.

4. Subject to Tax Rule (STTR @ 9%)

• Nature: Treaty-based rule designed to protect developing countries.
• Operation: Source country is permitted to levy a withholding tax of up to 9% on cross-border intra-group covered payments (interest, royalties, service fees) IF the recipient jurisdiction taxes such income at a nominal tax rate of less than 9%!
• Priority: STTR takes precedence over GloBE rules (taxes paid under STTR count as covered taxes for GloBE ETR).

5. High-Frequency Exam Traps

Trap 1: Substance-Based Income Exclusion (SBIE) protects real economic activity!
Under Pillar Two, top-up tax is NOT charged on the entire GloBE income. A deduction is granted for SBIE based on eligible payroll costs (8% reducing to 5%) and eligible tangible assets (10% reducing to 5%)!
Trap 2: QDMTT keeps the revenue in the host country!
If a developing country enacts a QDMTT, it collects the 15% top-up tax itself. The parent company's home jurisdiction cannot collect any IIR top-up tax on those profits!
