Unit 1 • Indian Accounting Standard 24

Ind AS 24: Related Party Disclosures

Ensures financial statements contain necessary disclosures drawing users' attention to the possibility that financial position and profit or loss may have been affected by the existence of related parties and by transactions, outstanding balances, and commitments with them.

1.1 Introduction, 1.2 Objective & 1.3 Scope

Core Philosophy • Exceptions

Entities frequently carry on business through subsidiaries (control), joint ventures (joint control), or associates (significant influence). Related party relationships can affect profit or loss and financial position in two distinct ways:

  • (a) Exclusive Transactions: Transactions entered into between related parties might not be entered into with unrelated parties (e.g., an entity sells goods to its parent at cost).
  • (b) Influenced Third-Party Dealings: Transactions with unrelated parties get influenced due to related party relationships (e.g., S Ltd, a subsidiary of H Ltd, used to buy steel billets from unrelated UR Ltd; after H Ltd acquires 100% of fellow subsidiary FS Ltd, H Ltd instructs S Ltd to buy billets from FS Ltd instead of UR Ltd).

Therefore, users need knowledge of (i) related party relationships and (ii) transactions, outstanding balances, and commitments to assess operations, risks, and opportunities.

1.2 Objective & 1.3 Scope — Four Pillars & Six Applications:

Objective: Ensure financial statements contain disclosures for (a) related party relationships, (b) related party transactions, (c) outstanding balances with related parties, and (d) commitments with related parties.

Scope (6 Tasks): Identifying (a) related party relationships, (b) related party transactions, (c) outstanding balances, (d) commitments, (e) circumstances requiring disclosure, and (f) the disclosures to be made.

  • Statements Covered: (a) Individual financial statements of the entity, and (b) Consolidated & Separate FS of a parent, venturer, or investor (Ind AS 110 / Ind AS 27).
  • Consolidation Elimination & Investment Entity Exception: Intra-group transactions and balances are disclosed in individual/separate FS, but eliminated in Consolidated FS. Exception: Intra-group transactions & balances between an Investment Entity and its subsidiary measured at FVTPL are NOT eliminated and MUST be disclosed in CFS!
  • Statutory Confidentiality Carve-Out: Disclosures are not required when they conflict with or are prohibited by statutory/regulatory duties of confidentiality. Example 1: Banks are obliged by law to preserve confidentiality of customers' dealings; Ind AS 24 does not override that legal obligation.

1.4 Definitions: Person, Close Family Members & Entity Relationships

Para 9 • Examples 2–13

A related party is (i) a person or (ii) an entity related to the reporting entity (the entity preparing its financial statements). In assessing every relationship, attention is directed to the substance of the relationship and not merely the legal form.

📝 Ind AS 24 • MCQ 1.3 (Associate's Downstream Entities — Ex. 13)
R Ltd. holds 25% voting power (significant influence) in B Ltd. B Ltd. in turn holds 60% in Subsidiary S Ltd., 50% joint control in JV J Ltd., and 30% significant influence in Associate A Ltd. Which of these entities are related parties of R Ltd. under Ind AS 24?
A. B Ltd. only
B. B Ltd. and S Ltd. only (J Ltd. and A Ltd. are NOT related parties of R Ltd.)
C. B Ltd., S Ltd., and J Ltd.
D. All four: B Ltd., S Ltd., J Ltd., and A Ltd.

📝 Ind AS 24 • MCQ 1.4 (Management Entity Group Rule — Ex. 12)
A Ltd. (Parent) has subsidiaries B Ltd. and C Ltd., and an associate E Ltd. Subsidiary F Ltd. of E Ltd. provides Key Management Personnel (KMP) services to A Ltd. Under Para 9(b)(viii), F Ltd. is a related party of which entities?
A. A Ltd. and E Ltd. only
B. A Ltd. only
C. A Ltd., B Ltd., C Ltd., and E Ltd.
D. None, because F Ltd. is a corporate body, not an individual
Correct Answer: C
Per Example 12, when an entity (F Ltd.) or any member of its group provides KMP services to the reporting entity or its parent (A Ltd.), F Ltd. is in a related party relationship with A Ltd., its subsidiaries (B Ltd., C Ltd.), and its own parent/group (E Ltd.).
📝 Ind AS 24 • MCQ 1.5 (Person Cross-Holdings & Close Family)
Mr. X is a Managing Director (KMP) of P Ltd. (which holds 100% of Q Ltd.). Mr. X's step-son (child of his domestic partner) holds 25% significant influence in R Ltd., while Mr. X has zero interest in R Ltd. Are Q Ltd. and R Ltd. related parties?
A. No, because neither Mr. X nor his step-son has control or joint control over P Ltd./Q Ltd. or R Ltd.
B. Yes, because step-son is a close family member and has significant influence over R Ltd.
C. No, because a domestic partner's child is never a close family member
D. Yes, because Mr. X is KMP of Q Ltd.'s parent
Correct Answer: A
While a domestic partner's child IS a close family member (Para 9), for two entities to be linked via persons under Para 9(b)(vi)/(vii), one person must control or jointly control one of the entities. Here Mr. X is only KMP of P Ltd. (parent of Q Ltd.) and his step-son has only significant influence in R Ltd. — neither holds control/joint control. (Note: Mr. X and his step-son are individually related to Q Ltd. and R Ltd. respectively, but Q Ltd. and R Ltd. are NOT related to each other.)

1.4.3 Who Are NOT Related Parties (Paragraph 11)

Exclusions • Examples 14–18

Ind AS 24 explicitly clarifies that the following are NOT related parties merely by virtue of these relationships:

# Non-Related Party Situation ICAI Study Material Examples (14–18)
(a) Two entities simply because they have a director or other KMP in common, or because a KMP of one entity has significant influence over the other entity. Ex 14: Mr. A is director in X Ltd & Y Ltd with no other interest and no transactions → NOT related.
Ex 15 (Substance Check!): Same facts, EXCEPT Y Ltd buys entire production of X Ltd at arm's length → X Ltd & Y Ltd MAY be related if Y Ltd exercises control/significant influence in substance! Cannot conclude purely from transaction alone; investigate further.
(b) Two venturers simply because they share joint control over a joint venture. Ex 16: JV Ltd is an equal JV of J Ltd and V Ltd → J Ltd and V Ltd are NOT related parties.
(c) (i) Providers of finance, (ii) trade unions, (iii) public utilities, and (iv) government departments/agencies that do not control, jointly control, or significantly influence the entity, simply by virtue of normal dealings (even if they affect freedom of action or participate in decision-making). Ex 17: A Bank and B Bank finance XY Ltd and occupy a non-executive observer seat on XY Ltd's Board per loan agreement → A Bank & B Bank are NOT related parties of XY Ltd.
(d) A customer, supplier, franchisor, distributor, or general agent with whom an entity transacts a significant volume of business, simply by virtue of economic dependence. Ex 18: A Ltd (auto ancillary) supplies 100% of its output to an automobile company that holds no other interest in A Ltd → NOT related parties.

1.4.4 Related Party Transactions & 1.4.5 Compensation / Government

11 Transaction Types • 5 Benefit Categories

A related party transaction is a transfer of resources, services, or obligations between a reporting entity and a related party, regardless of whether a price is charged. Even free-of-cost arrangements or unrecognised executory contracts must be disclosed!

11 Examples of Related Party Transactions (Para 21) 1.4.5 Compensation, Government & Govt-Related Entity
  1. Purchases or sales of goods (finished or unfinished)
  2. Purchases or sales of property and other assets
  3. Rendering or receiving of services
  4. Leases
  5. Transfers of research and development
  6. Transfers under licence agreements
  7. Transfers under finance arrangements (loans, equity in cash/kind)
  8. Provision of guarantees or collateral
  9. Commitments if an event occurs/doesn't occur, incl. executory contracts (recognised & unrecognised)
  10. Settlement of liabilities on behalf of entity or by entity on behalf of related party
  11. Management contracts including for deputation of employees

Plus: Participation by a parent or subsidiary in a defined benefit plan sharing risks between group entities is a related party transaction.

Compensation (Para 9): All employee benefits (Ind AS 19) + share-based payments (Ind AS 102) paid, payable, or provided by/on behalf of the entity (incl. consideration paid on behalf of a parent in respect of the entity):

  1. Short-term employee benefits (wages, salaries, social security, paid leave, bonuses within 12 months, non-monetary perks like cars/housing)
  2. Post-employment benefits (pensions, retirement, post-employment life/medical)
  3. Other long-term benefits (sabbatical, jubilee, long-term disability, deferred comp >12 months)
  4. Termination benefits
  5. Share-based payment (ESOPs)

Government: Government, government agencies, and similar bodies — whether local, national, or international.

Government-related entity: An entity controlled, jointly controlled, or significantly influenced by a government.

1.6 Partial Exemption for Government-Related Entities (Para 25–27)

Examples 23–25 • Cochin Shipyard
Scope of Exemption (Para 25): A reporting entity is exempt from detailed Para 18 disclosures for (i) transactions, (ii) outstanding balances, and (iii) commitments with: (a) a government that has control, joint control, or significant influence over the reporting entity; and (b) another entity that is a related party because the same government has control, joint control, or significant influence over both. Crucial: Exemption does NOT apply to transactions with KMP (e.g., Person X in Illustration 4)!

Mandatory Disclosures When Exemption Is Applied (Para 26):

  • (a) Name of the government and (b) nature of its relationship (control, joint control, or significant influence);
  • (c) Sufficient detail to enable users to understand the effect: (i) nature and amount of each individually significant transaction, and (ii) qualitative or quantitative indication of the extent of collectively significant transactions.

7 Judgment Factors (Para 27) to Assess Significance: (a) closeness of relationship, (b) significance in size, (c) whether carried out on non-market terms, (d) whether outside normal day-to-day operations (e.g., buying/selling businesses), (e) whether disclosed to regulatory/supervisory authorities, (f) whether reported to senior management, and (g) whether subject to shareholder approval.

Examples 23–25 & Cochin Shipyard Ltd. (FY 2021-22 Extract)
  • Example 23 (Individually Significant — Non-Market Terms): Entity A (75% indirectly owned by Govt G) sells 10-hectare land to another govt-related utility for ₹5M (similar plot sold 15 days earlier for ₹3M) → disclose nature, ₹5M amount, comparison, and Ind AS 20 note cross-reference.
  • Example 24 (Individually Significant — Size): Govt G gives Entity A a loan equal to 50% of its funding requirement, repayable quarterly over 5 years at 3% interest (comparable to bank loans) → disclose details (plus Ind AS 20 if government assistance).
  • Example 25 (Collectively Significant): Disclose that Entity A's significant transactions with Govt G & fellow govt entities represent ~50% of sales and ~35% of raw material purchases, plus Govt G guarantees on bank borrowings.
  • Cochin Shipyard Ltd. Extract: Under control of Ministry of Shipping (MoS), avails Ind AS 24 partial exemption and discloses individually significant transactions/balances with subsidiaries (HCSL, UCSL) such as secondment, NCD investments (₹7,500L / ₹1,000L), and Rights Issue (₹2,800L).

Unit 2 • Indian Accounting Standard 33

Ind AS 33: Earnings Per Share (EPS)

Prescribes principles for determining and presenting Basic and Diluted Earnings Per Share so performance can be compared across different entities in the same period and across different periods for the same entity. Covers BOTH Separate and Consolidated FS, Preference Share adjustments, WANOS & 9 issue-date rules, Bonus/Splits/Rights (TERP), Convertibles, Options/ESOPs, Contingent Shares, Discontinued Control Number, Cash/Share Contracts, Written Puts, Participating & Partly Paid Shares, and Listed Entity Extracts.

2.1–2.4 Objective, Limitation, Scope & Core Definitions

Para 1–8 • Examples 1–4

2.1–2.2 Objective & Limitation: Equity ("ordinary") shareholders bear business and financial risks as residual owners. EPS gauges profitability and drives P/E valuation. It enables comparison across (i) different entities in the same period (e.g., Hero MotoCorp vs Bajaj Auto) and (ii) different periods for the same entity (e.g., 5-year trend). Limitation (2.2.1): EPS data may have limitations because different companies may use different accounting policies to determine 'earnings'.

2.3 Scope — Mandatory for BOTH Consolidated & Separate FS (Key Ind AS Carve-In!):
  • Applies to all companies (having issued ordinary/equity shares) to which Ind AS notified under the Companies Act apply. Any entity that discloses EPS must compute and disclose it per Ind AS 33.
  • Both CFS & SFS Required: When an entity presents both Consolidated FS (Ind AS 110) and Separate FS (Ind AS 27), Ind AS 33 disclosures MUST be presented in BOTH — based on consolidated information in CFS and based on separate information in SFS! (Unlike IAS 33, which only mandates EPS in CFS if both are presented in the same report.)

2.6.1–2.6.2 Basic EPS: Formula & Measurement of Earnings

Preference Shares • Illus. 1 & 2
Basic Earnings Per Share Formula
$$\text{Basic EPS} = \frac{\text{Profit or Loss attributable to Ordinary Equity Holders of Parent Entity}}{\text{Weighted Average Number of Ordinary (Equity) Shares Outstanding (WANOS)}}$$
Calculated for both (a) Profit/Loss from Continuing Operations attributable to parent equity holders and (b) Total Profit/Loss attributable to parent equity holders.

Critical Numerator Rules (2.6.2):

  • Ind AS 32 Classification First: If preference shares are classified as financial liabilities, their dividends/appropriations are already expensed as finance costs in P&L → NO EPS numerator adjustment. Adjustments apply only to preference shares classified as equity (whose dividends/settlement differences bypass P&L via equity).
  • Items Routed Through Securities Premium / Other Reserves: Where any item of income or expense required by Ind AS to be recognised in P&L is debited/credited to Securities Premium or Other Reserves (e.g., under statutory permission), it shall be deducted from P&L from continuing operations for Basic EPS!
  • Non-Cumulative vs. Cumulative Preference Dividends:
    • Non-Cumulative: Deduct after-tax dividend only when declared for the period.
    • Cumulative: Deduct after-tax dividend for the period whether declared or not. Never deduct arrears of prior-period cumulative dividends when later paid!
  • Increasing Rate Preference Shares (Example 5): Issued at a discount (low initial dividend) or premium (high initial dividend) to compensate investors. Original issue discount/premium is amortised to retained earnings using Effective Interest Method (EIR) and treated as a preference dividend for EPS! Ex 5: ₹100 par shares paying ₹7/yr from 20X4 issued on 1 Jan 20X1 at ₹81.63 (7% market yield) → Imputed dividend deducted: 20X1 = ₹5.71, 20X2 = ₹6.12, 20X3 = ₹6.54, 20X4+ = ₹7.00.
  • Repurchase Under Tender Offer (2.6.2.1 — Example 6): Excess of FV paid over carrying amount is a charge to retained earnings → deducted from Basic EPS earnings (e.g., ₹100 pref shares bought back at ₹120 → deduct ₹20/share).
  • Early Conversion Induced at Premium (2.6.2.2 — Illustration 1): Excess of FV of ordinary shares/consideration paid over FV issuable under original conversion terms is deducted from earnings.
  • Discount on Settlement / Buyback (2.6.2.3 — Illustration 2): Excess of carrying amount over FV paid to settle preference shares is added to earnings!
Companies Act 2013 Note (Illus. 2): Under Sections 53 & 55 of the Companies Act, 2013, Indian companies cannot issue shares at a discount or irredeemable preference shares; however, ICAI illustrations apply pure principle-based Ind AS 33 concepts in FR exams.

2.6.3.1 WANOS: Opening ordinary shares − Shares bought back × time-weighting factor + Shares issued × time-weighting factor (days/total days, or months as reasonable approximation). See Illustration 3 below.

2.6.3.2 Deciding the Date for Inclusion in WANOS (All 9 Situations):

Situation of Issuance of Ordinary SharesDate Included in WANOS
(a) Issued in exchange for cashDate when cash is receivable (generally issue date)
(b) Issued on voluntary reinvestment of dividendsDate when dividends are reinvested
(c) Issued on conversion of a debt instrumentDate when interest ceases to accrue

2.6.3.3 Contingently Issuable & Returnable Shares in Basic EPS: Contingently issuable shares are included in Basic EPS only from the date when all necessary conditions are satisfied (events have occurred). Shares issuable solely after passage of time are NOT contingent. Outstanding shares that are contingently returnable (subject to recall) are excluded from Basic EPS until no longer subject to recall! Example 7: Acquirer agrees to issue extra shares in Feb 20X3 if 20X1 & 20X2 profit budgets are met (Dec year-end) → included in Basic EPS from 1 January 20X3 (not Feb 20X3, because targets are satisfied as at 31 Dec 20X2).

2.6.3.4 Bonus Issues, Share Splits & Reverse Splits: Change in number of shares without change in resources → adjust WANOS for current and all prior periods presented as if the event occurred at the beginning of the earliest period presented! Example 8: 2-for-1 bonus issue → multiply existing shares by 3 (or additional shares = ×2). Special Dividend Nuance: A share consolidation combined with a special dividend has the overall effect of a share repurchase at fair value (reduction in resources) → adjust WANOS from the date the special dividend is recognised (NOT retrospectively!).

2.6.3.5 Rights Issues at Discount — TERP & Bonus Factor (Illustration 6)
$$\text{TERP} = \frac{(\text{Existing Shares} \times \text{Cum-Rights FV}) + (\text{Rights Proceeds})}{\text{Shares Outstanding After Exercise}}, \qquad \text{Bonus Factor} = \frac{\text{Fair Value Immediately Before Exercise}}{\text{TERP}}$$
Apply Bonus Factor to all pre-rights periods (current year pre-rights & prior comparative years; or multiply prior EPS by reciprocal TERP/Cum-Rights). Crucial Exam Trap: If Bonus Factor < 1 (market price crashed below rights price), treat as issue at full market price (no bonus adjustment)!

2.7 Diluted EPS: Earnings, Independent Weighting, Ranking, Convertibles & ESOPs

Illus. 7–11 • Examples 8–10
Diluted EPS Formula & Numerator Adjustments (2.7.1–2.7.2)
$$\text{Diluted EPS} = \frac{\text{Basic Earnings} + \text{After-Tax Dividends/Interest Saved} \pm \text{Other Consequential Income/Expense Changes (After Tax)}}{\text{WANOS} + \text{Weighted Average Dilutive Potential Ordinary Shares}}$$
Interest/expenses include Ind AS 109 transaction costs & EIR discount amortisation. Consequential Changes (Illus. 7): If management receives a bonus of 1% of PBT, saving ₹1,000 interest increases bonus by ₹10 (₹8 net of 20% tax)!
  • 2.7.3.1–2.7.3.2 Base & Independent Weighting: Assume conversion at beginning of period (or issue date if later). Dilutive shares are calculated independently for each period presented (YTD figure is NOT a weighted average of quarterly interims!). Cancelled/lapsed instruments are weighted for the portion outstanding; converted instruments are in Diluted EPS up to conversion date and in both Basic & Diluted EPS thereafter.
  • Most Advantageous Conversion Rate (Illus. 8 & 9): Always assume the most advantageous conversion rate/exercise price from the holder's standpoint — even if conversion appears economically irrational vs current share price (Illus. 8: ₹100 bond converted into 20 shares worth ₹90 still assumed converted!).
  • Incremental EPS Ranking (Control Number = Continuing Operations): Evaluate each potential ordinary share separately (never in aggregate). Rank from lowest incremental EPS (options/warrants = ₹0.00 first!) to highest, adding sequentially while EPS drops. Once an instrument increases EPS, stop — that instrument and all higher-incremental-EPS ones are anti-dilutive.
  • 2.7.3.3 Shares of Subsidiary, JV or Associate: Potential ordinary shares issued by a subsidiary, JV, or associate to third parties (convertible into investee's or parent's ordinary shares) are included in Consolidated Diluted EPS if dilutive on reporting entity's Basic EPS.
  • 2.7.3.4 Options, Warrants & Equivalents (Treasury Stock Method — Ex. 8–10 & Illus. 10): Dilutive ONLY when in the money (Average Market Price > Exercise Price). Free shares added to denominator = $\text{Options} \times \left(1 - \frac{\text{Exercise Price}}{\text{Average Market Price}}\right)$. Numerator = ₹0 change. Previously reported EPS is not retroactively adjusted for share price changes.
  • Ind AS 102 Share-Based Payments & ESOPs (2.7.3.5 — Illus. 11): For Ind AS 102 awards, the assumed exercise/issue price includes the fair value of any goods or services to be supplied in the future! Fixed/time-vested ESOPs are treated as outstanding from the grant date even though unvested (Ex. 10). Performance-based ESOPs are treated as contingently issuable shares!
  • 2.7.3.6 Convertible Instruments Partial Redemption: If only part of convertible preference shares are redeemed/induced to convert, attribute excess consideration only to redeemed/converted shares and test remaining shares separately.

3.1 Core Principle, 3.2 Scope & 3.3 Identifying Operating Segments + CODM

Management Approach • Illus. 1–4

3.1 Core Principle: Disclose information enabling users to evaluate the nature and financial effects of the business activities in which the entity engages and the economic environments in which it operates — seen "through the eyes of management" (better cash flow prediction & low incremental reporting cost).

3.2 Scope: Applies to companies to which Ind AS notified under Companies Act, 2013 apply. If an entity outside scope voluntarily discloses non-compliant segment data, it shall NOT describe it as "segment information". When a financial report contains both Consolidated FS and Parent Separate FS, segment info is required only in Consolidated FS (confirmed in Hindustan Unilever Ltd. Note 46 Extract).

3.3 Operating Segment (3 Cumulative Characteristics) & Special Cases:

Component / IssueInd AS 108 Rule & ICAI Guidance
3 Characteristics of Operating Segment(a) Engages in business activities from which it may earn revenues and incur expenses (incl. inter-segment); (b) Operating results are regularly reviewed by CODM to allocate resources and assess performance; and (c) Discrete financial information is available. Start-up operations may be operating segments even before earning revenues!
Excluded PartsCorporate headquarters or functional departments earning no/incidental revenues, and post-employment benefit plans are NOT operating segments.
3.3.1 Integral Functions (e.g., R&D)An integral function (like R&D in a pharma or software company) can be an operating segment if discrete financial info is available and regularly reviewed by the CODM.
3.3.2 Discontinued OperationsIf a discontinued operation (a) continues business activities, (b) is reviewed by CODM, and (c) has discrete financial info → it meets the definition of an operating segment!
CODM Function & Revenue-Only Review (Illus. 2)'CODM' identifies a function (allocating resources & assessing performance), not a specific job title (could be CEO, COO, or executive directors). If costs are centralized/minimal, CODM reviewing revenue-only data can still suffice (Illus. 2).

3.4 Reportable Segments & 3.5 Aggregation Criteria (Illustrations 5–7)

5 Similarity Factors • Optional

Two or more operating segments may (not mandatory!) be aggregated into a single operating segment ONLY if: (1) aggregation is consistent with the Core Principle, (2) segments have similar economic characteristics (e.g., similar long-term average gross margins), AND (3) segments are similar in ALL 5 respects:

  1. Nature of the products and services;
  2. Nature of the production processes;
  3. Type or class of customer for their products and services;
  4. Methods used to distribute their products or provide their services; and
  5. If applicable, the nature of the regulatory environment (e.g., banking, insurance, public utilities).
ICAI Illustrations 5, 6 & 7 — Aggregation in Action
  • Illustration 5 (Geographic Aggregation): XY Ltd. operates in France, Italy, Germany, UK, and India. Considering exchange controls, currency risks, and economic conditions, France, Italy, and Germany (EU region / Eurozone) may be aggregated, while UK and India must be reported separately.
  • Illustration 6 (Paper Varieties): Adhesive paper, anti-rust paper, antique paper, art paper — if nature of product, production process, customer type, distribution, and regulation are common, no need to split into separate segments.
  • Illustration 7 (T Ltd. Bus Routes — Classic Exam Question!): Local Route (contract awarded by local transport authority on per-km basis, no passenger revenue risk, declining margins) vs. Inter-city Route (company sets ticket prices, exposed to passenger demand/revenue risk, high margins). Cannot aggregate! Class of customers and risk profiles differ, and aggregating would hide the sharp profit decline in Local Route from investors.

3.6 Quantitative Thresholds (10% Tests), Combining Rules & 75% Coverage Rule

Para 13–19 • Illus. 8 & 9

An operating segment (or aggregated group) is reportable if it meets ANY ONE of the three 10% thresholds:

10% TestThreshold RuleExam Trap
1. Revenue TestReported revenue (external + inter-segment) $\ge 10\%$ of combined revenue (external + internal) of all operating segments.Includes inter-segment transfers! Denominator is combined segment revenue (not net entity revenue).
2. Profit/Loss TestAbsolute amount of reported profit or loss $\ge 10\%$ of the GREATER, in absolute amount, of:
(i) combined profit of all profit-making segments; and
(ii) combined loss of all loss-making segments.		Never net profits against losses! Compare 10% against the larger absolute pool.
3. Assets TestSegment assets $\ge 10\%$ of combined assets of all operating segments.Uses segment assets reviewed by CODM.
Additional Reportable Segment Rules (Para 13–19):
  • Combining Below-Threshold Segments (Para 14): Segments that fail all 10% tests may be combined with other below-threshold segments ONLY if they have similar economic characteristics AND share a majority of the 5 aggregation criteria! (Contrast with Para 12: aggregating before 10% test requires ALL 5 criteria!)
  • 75% External Revenue Rule (Para 15): Total external revenue of reportable segments must be $\ge 75\%$ of entity's total external revenue. If <75%, identify additional operating segments (even if <10%!) until $\ge 75\%$ is reached.
  • Management Discretion: Any operating segment below 10% may still be reported separately if management believes it is useful to users.
  • 'All Other Segments': Remaining non-reportable operating segments and other business activities are combined into "All other segments" separately from other reconciling items; disclose sources of revenue included.
  • Continuing Significance & Prior-Period Restatement: If a segment was reportable last year and management judges it of continuing significance → keep reporting it this year even if <10%. Conversely, if a segment becomes newly reportable this year via 10% tests → restate prior-period comparative segment data to show it separately (unless unavailable & excessive cost).
  • Practical Limit: If reportable segments exceed 10, consider whether a practical limit has been reached (yet Illus. 9 notes if no region/product exceeds 5% of revenue and cannot be aggregated, at least $75\% / 5\% = \mathbf{15\text{ segments}}$ must be reported!).

3.7.1 General Information: Disclose (a) factors used to identify reportable segments (basis of organisation & whether aggregated); (b) management judgements in applying aggregation criteria (brief description of aggregated segments & economic indicators assessed); and (c) types of products & services generating revenue per reportable segment.

3.7.2 Profit or Loss, Assets & Liabilities: Always report a measure of segment profit/loss; report total assets and liabilities if regularly provided to CODM. Also disclose the following if included in CODM's measure OR otherwise regularly provided to CODM:

3.8 Measurement Rules & Common Cost Allocation (Illustration 10):
  • Report the exact measure reported to the CODM (even if non-Ind AS, e.g., cash-basis pension or FIFO vs Weighted Average). If CODM uses multiple measures, report the one most consistent with financial statement principles.
  • 6 Mandatory Measurement Explanations (a)–(f): (a) accounting basis for inter-segment transactions; (b)–(d) nature of differences between segment P&L/assets/liabilities and entity P&L/assets/liabilities; (e) changes from prior period in measurement methods & effect; (f) nature & effect of asymmetrical allocations (e.g., allocating depreciation expense to a segment without allocating the related PPE asset!).
  • Illustration 10 (GH Ltd. Common Cost Allocation): Costs need NOT conform to FS policies, just a reasonable basis: Management charges → Net Assets invested or Revenue (watch out: start-ups with nil revenue get zero!); Internal funding interest → actual usage & time (even if loss-making segment uses most funds!); Property management → value of property used; Pension costs → salary expenses.

3.8.1 Mandatory Reconciliations (for every P&L period & every BS date, with prior periods restated): Reconcile total reportable segments' (a) revenues to entity revenue, (b) P&L to entity PBT & discontinued ops (or PAT if tax allocated), (c) assets to entity assets (if reported), (d) liabilities to entity liabilities (if reported), and (e) every other material item disclosed. Separately identify and describe all material reconciling items.

3.9 Restatement for Change in Internal Organisation Structure: If internal restructuring changes composition of reportable segments → restate earlier periods (including interim periods) item-by-item unless unavailable & excessive cost. Disclose whether restated. If NOT restated → disclose current-period segment info on BOTH the old basis and the new basis!
