Unit 1 • Corporate Governance & Related Parties

Ind AS 24: Related Party Disclosures

Ensures that an entity's financial statements contain disclosures drawing attention to the possibility that its financial position and profit or loss may have been affected by the existence of related parties, transactions, outstanding balances, and commitments with such parties.

1. Objective, Scope & Fundamental Philosophy

Related party relationships are a normal feature of commerce and business. Entities frequently carry on activities through subsidiaries, joint ventures, and associates. However, related parties may enter into transactions that unrelated parties would not enter into, or at prices and terms different from those between unrelated parties (e.g., selling goods at cost, interest-free loans, or directing procurement).

Therefore, knowledge of an entity's related party relationships, transactions, and outstanding balances is essential for users to assess the entity's operational risks, opportunities, and financial position.

Scope & Application Level (Paragraphs 3–4):
  • Individual & Separate Financial Statements: Full disclosure of related party relationships, transactions, and balances.
  • Consolidated Financial Statements: Intercompany transactions and balances between group entities are eliminated on consolidation under Ind AS 110, so they are not disclosed in CFS. (Exception: Intercompany transactions with an investment entity subsidiary measured at FVTPL are NOT eliminated and must be disclosed).
  • Confidentiality Carve-Out: Disclosures are not required if they conflict with an entity's duties of confidentiality under a statute, regulator, or similar competent authority.

2. Core Definitions: Control, KMP & Close Family Members

Under Paragraph 9 of Ind AS 24, a person or entity is related to the reporting entity based on defined tests of control, joint control, significant influence, or key management authority.

Standard Term Official Ind AS 24 Definition Critical Exam Nuance
Related Party A person or an entity that is related to the entity that is preparing its financial statements (the "reporting entity"). Evaluates economic substance over legal form. Relates to both natural persons and corporate entities.
Key Management Personnel (KMP) Those persons having authority and responsibility for planning, directing, and controlling the activities of the entity, directly or indirectly, including any director (whether executive or otherwise) of that entity. Includes: Executive Directors, Non-Executive Directors, Independent Directors, Managing Director, CFO, and Company Secretary. Also covers parent's KMP.
Close Members of the Family of a Person Those family members who may be expected to influence, or be influenced by, that person in their dealings with the entity. Includes:
  • That person's children and spouse / domestic partner;
  • Children of that person's spouse / domestic partner; and
  • Dependants of that person or that person's spouse / domestic partner.
Related Party Transaction A transfer of resources, services, or obligations between a reporting entity and a related party, regardless of whether a price is charged. Transactions at free of cost (e.g., zero-cost guarantees, interest-free loans, free management services) MUST be disclosed!
Government-Related Entity An entity that is controlled, jointly controlled, or significantly influenced by a government. Eligible for partial disclosure exemption under Paragraph 25.

3. Related Party Identification Rules (Person vs. Entity)

Paragraph 9 sets out two exhaustive categorizations: when a person is related, and when an entity is related to the reporting entity.

When a PERSON (or Close Family Member) is Related When an ENTITY is Related to the Reporting Entity
A person or a close member of that person's family is related to a reporting entity if that person:
  1. Has control or joint control over the reporting entity;
  2. Has significant influence over the reporting entity; OR
  3. Is a member of the key management personnel (KMP) of the reporting entity or of a parent of the reporting entity.
 An entity is related to a reporting entity if any of the following conditions applies:
  1. The entity and the reporting entity are members of the same group (parent, subsidiary, fellow subsidiary).
  2. One entity is an associate or joint venture of the other entity (or an associate/JV of a member of the group).
  3. Both entities are joint ventures of the same third party.
  4. One entity is a joint venture of a third entity and the other entity is an associate of the third entity.
  5. The entity is a post-employment benefit plan for the benefit of employees of either the reporting entity or an entity related to the reporting entity.
  6. The entity is controlled or jointly controlled by a person identified in the Person category.
  7. A person who controls/jointly controls the reporting entity has significant influence over the entity or is a KMP of the entity (or of a parent of the entity).
  8. The entity, or any member of a group of which it is a part, provides key management personnel services to the reporting entity or to the parent of the reporting entity.
Who are NOT Deemed Related Parties (Paragraph 11):
  • Two entities simply because they have a director or other member of key management personnel in common (unless the director controls or jointly controls both).
  • Two venturers simply because they share joint control over a joint venture.
  • Providers of finance (banks, financial institutions), trade unions, public utilities, and government departments/agencies in the course of their normal dealings.
  • A single customer, supplier, franchisor, distributor, or general agent with whom an entity transacts a significant volume of business, simply by virtue of the resulting economic dependence.
  • Co-associates of the same investor (if Investor A has significant influence over B and C, B and C are NOT related to each other!).
Examiner's Secret • Fellow Associates vs. Fellow JVs
"Watch out in exams! If Entity A has Significant Influence over both Entity B and Entity C (fellow associates), B and C are NOT related parties! But if Entity A has Joint Control over both B and C (fellow joint ventures), B and C ARE related parties under Ind AS 24! Similarly, if A has Joint Control over B and Significant Influence over C, B and C are related parties!"

Comprehensive ICAI Illustrative Case Studies (Ind AS 24)

ICAI Illustration 1 Associates and Subsidiaries Relationship Matrix
Case: Parent P has two subsidiaries, Sub A and Sub B, and an associate, Ass C. Sub A has an associate, Ass D. Identify the related party relationships from the perspective of Sub A's financial statements.
Analysis from Sub A's Perspective:
  • Parent P: Related (Parent of Sub A).
  • Sub B: Related (Fellow subsidiary in the same group).
  • Ass C: Related (Associate of Sub A's parent, P).
  • Ass D: Related (Direct associate of Sub A).
ICAI Illustration 2 Key Management Personnel & Directorships
Case: Mr. K is a director of Company X and also a director of Company Y. Does this common directorship alone make Company X and Company Y related parties?
Analysis & Conclusion:

No. Under Paragraph 11(a) of Ind AS 24, two entities are not related parties simply because they have a director or other member of key management personnel in common. Unless Mr. K has control or joint control over both entities, or significant influence combined with control, the two entities are not related parties.

ICAI Illustration 5 Acquisition & Disposal of Subsidiary During the Year
Case: Power Ltd. regularly sells electricity to Transmission Ltd. Power Ltd. acquired 100% shareholding of Transmission Ltd. on 15 July 20X1 and disposed of the entire shareholding on 21 March 20X2. Both entities had transactions during the period of shareholding as well as outside that period. For which period must Power Ltd. make related party disclosures in its financial statements for the year ended 31 March 20X2?
Analysis & Conclusion:

Power Ltd. must make related party disclosures strictly for the period from 15 July 20X1 to 21 March 20X2, during which the related party relationship (subsidiary) actually existed. Transactions taking place prior to 15 July 20X1 and after 21 March 20X2 were entered into as unrelated third parties and must not be disclosed as related party transactions.

4. Mandatory Disclosure Framework (Paragraphs 13–24)

Ind AS 24 establishes disclosure requirements structured around four distinct pillars: control relationships, KMP compensation, transaction details, and pricing terms.

Disclosure Pillar Specific Disclosure Requirements Exam Rule / Condition
Control Relationships (Paragraph 13) Relationships between a parent and its subsidiaries shall be disclosed irrespective of whether there have been any transactions between them:
  • Name of the entity's parent;
  • Ultimate controlling party (if different from immediate parent); and
  • Next most senior parent that produces consolidated financial statements for public use (if neither parent nor ultimate party produces them).
 Mandatory Always
Required even if there have been ZERO transactions during the entire reporting period!
KMP Compensation (Paragraph 17) Total KMP compensation broken down into the following 5 categories:
  1. Short-term employee benefits (salaries, sitting fees);
  2. Post-employment benefits (pensions, gratuity);
  3. Other long-term benefits (leave encashment);
  4. Termination benefits; and
  5. Share-based payment benefits (ESOPs under Ind AS 102).
 All Directors
Includes independent and non-executive directors' sitting fees and retainers.
Related Party Transactions (Paragraph 18) If an entity has had related party transactions during the period:
  • Nature of the related party relationship;
  • Amount of the transactions;
  • Amount of outstanding balances (including terms, secured/unsecured, settlement mechanism);
  • Commitments (e.g., guarantees, purchase commitments);
  • Provisions for doubtful debts related to outstanding balances; and
  • Expense recognized during the period in respect of bad or doubtful debts from related parties.
 Categorized by Party
Disclosures must be made separately for parent, subsidiaries, associates, JVs, KMP, and other related parties.
Arm's Length Claims (Paragraph 21) Disclosures that related party transactions were made on terms equivalent to those that prevail in arm's length transactions are made only if such terms can be substantiated. Substantiation Required
Management cannot make casual arm's length statements without demonstrable market benchmark evidence.

5. Partial Exemption for Government-Related Entities (Paragraphs 25–27)

When a government controls, jointly controls, or significantly influences multiple commercial entities, extensive disclosures of all mutual transactions would impose an unnecessary administrative burden. Ind AS 24 provides a special relief:

Exemption Provisions (Paragraph 25): A reporting entity is exempt from the general disclosure requirements of Paragraph 18 in relation to related party transactions and outstanding balances, including commitments, with:
  1. A government that has control, joint control, or significant influence over the reporting entity; and
  2. Another entity that is a related party because the same government has control, joint control, or significant influence over both.

Mandatory Simplified Disclosures (Paragraph 26):

  • The name of the government and the nature of its relationship with the reporting entity (control, joint control, or significant influence);
  • The nature and amount of each individually significant transaction; and
  • For other transactions that are collectively, but not individually, significant, a qualitative or quantitative indication of their extent.

6. Significant Differences: Ind AS 24 vis-à-vis AS 18

Area Ind AS 24 (New Framework) Old AS 18 (Previous GAAP)
Key Management Personnel (KMP) Explicitly includes Non-Executive and Independent Directors as KMP. Sitting fees are disclosed as KMP compensation. Excluded non-executive directors from KMP unless they had executive managerial authority.
Close Members of Family Explicitly includes domestic partner, children of domestic partner, and dependants of domestic partner. Limited to spouse, son, daughter, brother, sister, father, and mother who may influence.
Post-Employment Benefit Plans Post-employment benefit plans for the benefit of employees of the entity/group are expressly defined as related parties. Did not consider employee benefit trusts or plans as related parties.
KMP Management Entities An entity providing key management personnel services to the reporting entity is a related party. Did not contain provisions covering corporate management entities providing KMP services.
Government-Related Entities Provides partial exemption from detailed disclosures, requiring only broad qualitative/quantitative disclosures. Exempted state-controlled enterprises completely from related party disclosures in their financial statements.

7. ICAI Test Your Knowledge (TYK) Questions & Detailed Solutions

TYK Question 1 Domestic Partners with Cross Investments in Two Companies
Question: Mr. X is a domestic partner of Ms. Y. Mr. X has an investment in A Ltd. and Ms. Y has an investment in B Ltd.
Required:
  1. Examine when a related party relationship is established from the perspective of A Ltd.'s financial statements.
  2. Examine when a related party relationship is established from the perspective of B Ltd.'s financial statements.
  3. Will A Ltd. and B Ltd. be related parties if Mr. X has only significant influence over A Ltd. and Ms. Y also has only significant influence over B Ltd.?
Solution & Analysis:

Under Paragraph 9(a) of Ind AS 24, close members of the family of a person include that person's domestic partner. Thus, Mr. X and Ms. Y are close family members to each other.

  1. From A Ltd.'s perspective:
    • Mr. X is related to A Ltd. if he has control, joint control, or significant influence over A Ltd., or is a member of its KMP.
    • Ms. Y is related to A Ltd. as a close family member of Mr. X if Mr. X has control, joint control, significant influence, or is a KMP of A Ltd.
    • B Ltd. is related to A Ltd. if Ms. Y controls or jointly controls B Ltd. AND Mr. X controls, jointly controls, or has significant influence over A Ltd. (or is a KMP of A Ltd.).
  2. From B Ltd.'s perspective:
    • Ms. Y is related to B Ltd. if she has control, joint control, significant influence, or is a KMP of B Ltd.
    • Mr. X is related to B Ltd. as a close family member of Ms. Y.
    • A Ltd. is related to B Ltd. if Mr. X controls or jointly controls A Ltd. AND Ms. Y controls, jointly controls, or has significant influence over B Ltd.
  3. Both have ONLY Significant Influence: No, A Ltd. and B Ltd. will NOT be related parties. Under Paragraph 9(b)(vi)/(vii), for two entities to be related through individuals, at least one of the individuals must have control or joint control over one of the entities. If Mr. X has only significant influence over A Ltd. and Ms. Y has only significant influence over B Ltd., no related party relationship exists between A Ltd. and B Ltd.
TYK Question 2 Joint Control and Significant Influence Across Entities
Question: A Ltd. has both (i) joint control over B Ltd. and (ii) joint control or significant influence over C Ltd.
Required:
  1. Examine the related party relationship from the perspective of C Ltd.'s financial statements.
  2. Examine the related party relationship from the perspective of B Ltd.'s financial statements.
Solution & Analysis:

Under Paragraph 9(b) of Ind AS 24:

  1. From C Ltd.'s perspective:
    • A Ltd. is related to C Ltd. (because A Ltd. has joint control or significant influence over C Ltd. under Para 9(b)(ii)).
    • B Ltd. is related to C Ltd. because under Para 9(b)(iv) / 9(b)(v), an entity that is a joint venture of a third entity (A Ltd.) is related to an associate or fellow joint venture (C Ltd.) of that same third entity.
    • Conclusion: C Ltd. is related to both A Ltd. and B Ltd.
  2. From B Ltd.'s perspective:
    • A Ltd. is related to B Ltd. (venturer holding joint control).
    • C Ltd. is related to B Ltd. (fellow joint venture or associate of common venturer A Ltd.).
    • Conclusion: B Ltd. is related to both A Ltd. and C Ltd.
TYK Question 3 Director's Spouse Acquiring Customer Entity with Favourable Terms
Question: ABC Ltd. is a long-standing customer of XYZ Ltd. Mrs. P, whose husband is a director in XYZ Ltd., purchased a controlling interest in entity ABC Ltd. on 1 June 20X1. Sales from XYZ Ltd. to ABC Ltd. in the two months from 1 April 20X1 to 31 May 20X1 totalled ₹8,00,000. Following the share purchase by Mrs. P, XYZ Ltd. began supplying products at a 20% discount to normal selling price and allowed ABC Ltd. 3 months' credit (previously only 1 month). Sales from XYZ Ltd. to ABC Ltd. in the ten-month period from 1 June 20X1 to 31 March 20X2 totalled ₹60,00,000. On 31 March 20X2, trade receivables of XYZ Ltd. included ₹18,00,000 owing by ABC Ltd.

Analyse and show how the above events would be reported in the financial statements of XYZ Ltd. for the year ended 31 March 20X2 as per Ind AS.
Comprehensive Financial Reporting Analysis:
  1. Financial Statement Reporting:
    • Revenue: XYZ Ltd. records total revenue of ₹68,00,000 (₹8,00,000 + ₹60,00,000) in its Statement of Profit and Loss.
    • Trade Receivables: XYZ Ltd. includes ₹18,00,000 within Trade Receivables in the Balance Sheet as of 31 March 20X2.
  2. Related Party Determination:
    • Mr. P (director) is a Key Management Personnel (KMP) of XYZ Ltd.
    • Mrs. P is a close family member of a KMP under Paragraph 9(a)(iii).
    • From 1 June 20X1, ABC Ltd. is an entity controlled by a close family member of a KMP of XYZ Ltd. Therefore, under Paragraph 9(b)(vi), ABC Ltd. became a related party of XYZ Ltd. effective 1 June 20X1.
  3. Mandatory Disclosures in Notes: Under Paragraphs 18 & 19, XYZ Ltd. must disclose:
    • The nature of the related party relationship (customer entity controlled by the spouse of a director of XYZ Ltd.).
    • The transaction amount during the related party period: Sales of ₹60,00,000 for the period 1 June 20X1 to 31 March 20X2. (The ₹8,00,000 sales prior to 1 June 20X1 were unrelated party transactions and are NOT disclosed as related party transactions).
    • The outstanding balance of ₹18,00,000 at 31 March 20X2.
    • The favourable terms and conditions under which the transactions occurred (i.e., 20% discount and 3 months credit vs. standard 1 month credit).
TYK Question 4 Sitting Fees Paid to Independent and Non-Executive Directors
Question: Mr. Atul is an independent director of X Ltd., attending 5 board meetings during the year and receiving a sitting fee of ₹2,00,000 per meeting (Total ₹10,00,000). Mr. Naveen, a non-executive director, attended 5 meetings charging ₹1,50,000 per meeting (Total ₹7,50,000). The company accountant contends that because they are not employees of X Ltd., their fees should not be disclosed as related party transactions under Ind AS 24. Examine the validity of the accountant's view.
Analysis & Legal Conclusion:

The accountant's contention is WRONG.

  1. KMP Definition under Ind AS 24: Paragraph 9 explicitly defines KMP as those persons having authority and responsibility for planning, directing, and controlling the activities of the entity, directly or indirectly, including any director (whether executive or otherwise) of that entity. Both independent director Mr. Atul and non-executive director Mr. Naveen are directors of the entity and therefore qualify as Key Management Personnel.
  2. Employee Benefits Treatment: Under Ind AS 19 and Ind AS 24, directors are considered employees for remuneration disclosure purposes. Sitting fees paid for attending board meetings are short-term employee benefits under Paragraph 17(a).
  3. Mandatory Disclosure: X Ltd. must disclose total KMP compensation of ₹17,50,000 (Mr. Atul ₹10,00,000 + Mr. Naveen ₹7,50,000) under short-term employee benefits in the related party disclosures note.
TYK Question 5 Managing Director Controlling Another Entity (Arm's Length & Materiality)
Question: Mr. Y is the Managing Director of ABC Ltd. and also owns 100% of PQR Ltd. ABC Ltd. purchased goods from PQR Ltd. during the year. Mr. Y queries whether disclosure is necessary because (i) the transaction was conducted strictly on an arm's length commercial basis, and (ii) the amount is numerically insignificant to ABC Ltd. Advise Mr. Y with reference to Ind AS 24.
Legal & Standard Clarifications:
  1. Related Party Relationship: Mr. Y is a KMP of ABC Ltd. and controls 100% of PQR Ltd. Under Paragraph 9(b)(vi), PQR Ltd. is a related party of ABC Ltd.
  2. Arm's Length Argument: Ind AS 24 requires disclosure of related party transactions regardless of whether a price is charged or whether the transaction was executed at arm's length. The mere fact that terms were commercial does not exempt disclosure. (Indeed, arm's length terms can only be stated if substantiated).
  3. Materiality Argument: Materiality under Ind AS is evaluated both by size (quantitative) and by nature (qualitative). Related party transactions are material by nature due to the inherent conflict of interest. Even if numerically insignificant to ABC Ltd., disclosure is required because it may influence users' perception of management conduct and governance.
Unit 2 • Capital Markets & Valuation Metrics

Ind AS 33: Earnings Per Share (EPS)

Prescribes principles for the determination and presentation of earnings per share to improve performance comparisons between different entities in the same reporting period and between different reporting periods for the same entity. Covers Basic EPS, Weighted Average Number of Ordinary Shares (WANOS), Rights Issue Bonus Elements, Diluted EPS, Treasury Stock Method, and Sequential Anti-Dilutive Ranking.

1. Scope, Applicability & Presentation Mandate

Ind AS 33 is mandatory for entities whose ordinary shares or potential ordinary shares are traded in a public market (domestic or foreign stock exchange, or over-the-counter market) or that file, or are in the process of filing, their financial statements with a securities commission or other regulatory organization for the purpose of issuing ordinary shares in a public market.

Standalone vs. Consolidated Presentation Rule (Paragraph 4): When an entity presents both consolidated financial statements (CFS) and separate financial statements (SFS) prepared in accordance with Ind AS 110 and Ind AS 27, the disclosures required by Ind AS 33 need be presented only on the basis of the consolidated information on the face of the Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss. If an entity chooses to disclose EPS in its separate financial statements as well, it must present such information on the face of its Separate Statement of Profit and Loss (never in the notes).
Equal Prominence on Face of P&L (Paragraph 66): An entity shall present in the Statement of Profit and Loss Basic and Diluted EPS for:
  • Profit or loss from continuing operations attributable to the ordinary equity holders of the parent entity; and
  • Profit or loss attributable to the ordinary equity holders of the parent entity for the period for each class of ordinary shares that has a different right for sharing in profit.
Basic and Diluted EPS must be presented with equal prominence for all periods presented, even if the amounts are negative (i.e., loss per share).

2. Basic Earnings Per Share: Numerator & Denominator (WANOS)

Basic EPS measures the earnings generated by each ordinary share outstanding during the reporting period.

Basic Earnings Per Share Formula (Paragraph 9)
$$\text{Basic EPS} = \frac{\text{Net Profit or Loss Attributable to Ordinary Equity Holders (Numerator)}}{\text{Weighted Average Number of Ordinary Shares Outstanding (WANOS - Denominator)}}$$
Calculated separately for continuing operations, discontinued operations, and total net profit.

A. Numerator Adjustments (Earnings Attributable to Ordinary Shareholders):

The profit or loss attributable to the parent entity is adjusted for the after-tax amounts of preference dividends, differences arising on the settlement of preference shares, and other similar effects of preference shares classified as equity:

Preference Share Category Adjustment to Numerator (Profit/Loss) Governing Standard Rule
Non-Cumulative Preference Shares Deduct dividend only if declared in respect of the reporting period. Paragraph 14(a)
Cumulative Preference Shares Deduct dividend whether declared or not for the period. (Any dividend paid in respect of previous years is ignored in current period). Paragraph 14(b)
Redemption Premium / Buyback Discount Any excess of fair value of consideration paid to redeem preference shares over carrying amount is deducted from earnings; any discount is added. Paragraph 15
Increasing Rate Preference Shares Amortization of original issue discount / coupon escalation is treated as a preference dividend and deducted from earnings. Paragraph 16

B. Denominator: Weighted Average Number of Ordinary Shares (WANOS):

The denominator is calculated by adjusting the ordinary shares in issue at the beginning of the period by the number of ordinary shares bought back or issued during the period, multiplied by a time-weighting factor:

Time Weighting Factor
$$\text{Time Weighting Factor} = \frac{\text{Number of days/months shares are outstanding}}{\text{Total number of days/months in the period}}$$
Shares are usually included from the date consideration is receivable (cash, physical asset, services rendered).

3. Capital Changes Without Corresponding Inflow of Resources (Bonus, Splits & Rights)

Where ordinary shares are issued without a corresponding change in economic resources, the number of ordinary shares is adjusted proportionately as if the event had occurred at the beginning of the earliest period reported (retrospective restatement).

Corporate Event Accounting Adjustment Mechanism Retrospective Treatment
Capitalisation or Bonus Issue Ordinary shares issued to existing shareholders for nil consideration. The number of shares outstanding before the event is multiplied by the bonus ratio. Adjusted retrospectively for all comparative periods presented.
Share Split (Sub-division) Reduction in par value increasing number of shares (e.g., 1 share of ₹10 split into 5 shares of ₹2). Multiplied by split ratio retrospectively for all periods.
Reverse Share Split (Consolidation) Increase in par value reducing number of shares (e.g., 5 shares of ₹2 consolidated into 1 share of ₹10). Divided by consolidation ratio retrospectively for all periods.
Rights Issue at a Discount (Bonus Element) The issue contains a combination of shares at fair market value and shares issued for free (bonus element). Requires calculation of the Theoretical Ex-Rights Price (TERP). Pre-rights shares multiplied by Bonus Adjustment Factor retrospectively.
Theoretical Ex-Rights Price (TERP) & Adjustment Factor Formulas
$$\text{TERP} = \frac{(\text{Existing Shares} \times \text{Cum-Rights Fair Value}) + (\text{Rights Shares} \times \text{Exercise Price})}{\text{Total Shares Outstanding Immediately After Rights Issue}}$$
$$\text{Bonus Adjustment Factor} = \frac{\text{Fair Value per Share Immediately Prior to Exercise of Rights (Cum-Rights Price)}}{\text{Theoretical Ex-Rights Price (TERP)}}$$
All shares outstanding prior to the rights exercise date (in current year and prior comparative years) are multiplied by this adjustment factor!

4. Diluted Earnings Per Share: Potential Ordinary Shares & Measurement Methods

An entity shall calculate diluted earnings per share amounts for profit or loss attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent entity and, if presented, profit or loss from continuing operations attributable to those equity holders.

A potential ordinary share is a financial instrument or other contract that may entitle its holder to ordinary shares (e.g., convertible debt, convertible preference shares, share warrants, stock options, employee share purchase plans, contingently issuable shares).

Potential Ordinary Share Numerator (Earnings) Adjustment Denominator (Shares) Adjustment
Convertible Debentures / Bonds ("If-Converted" Method) Add back: After-tax finance costs, interest expense, and amortization of discount recognized during the period: $$\text{Interest Saved} \times (1 - \text{Tax Rate})$$ Add: Number of ordinary shares that would be issued upon conversion from the beginning of the period (or date of issue of bonds, if later).
Convertible Preference Shares ("If-Converted" Method) Add back: Preference dividends deducted in arriving at profit attributable to ordinary shareholders. Add: Number of ordinary shares that would be issued upon conversion from the beginning of the period (or date of issuance).
Share Options & Warrants ("Treasury Stock Method") Nil: No adjustment to numerator (unless options carry dividend/interest features). Add "Free / Incremental Shares" Only: Assumed proceeds from option exercise are used to buy back shares at average market price: $$\text{Free Shares} = \text{Options} \times \left(1 - \frac{\text{Exercise Price}}{\text{Average Fair Value}}\right)$$
The Treasury Stock Method Intuition (Options & Warrants): If an entity has options to issue 100,000 shares at ₹60 when the average market price is ₹100:
  • Cash received on exercise = $100,000 \times ₹60 = ₹60,00,000$.
  • Shares that could be repurchased at market price = $\frac{₹60,00,000}{₹100} = 60,000\text{ shares}$.
  • Net incremental shares issued for zero economic consideration = $100,000 - 60,000 = \mathbf{40,000\text{ shares}}$ added to denominator.

5. Dilutive vs. Anti-Dilutive Instruments: The Sequential Ranking Framework

Potential ordinary shares are treated as dilutive when, and only when, their conversion to ordinary shares would decrease earnings per share or increase loss per share from continuing operations.

Control Number Rule (Paragraph 42): The control number is profit or loss from continuing operations attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent. If an entity has a loss from continuing operations, all potential ordinary shares are automatically anti-dilutive and Diluted EPS equals Basic EPS!

Step-by-Step Multi-Instrument Ranking Procedure:

  1. Step 1: Calculate Incremental EPS for Each Instrument: $$\text{Incremental EPS} = \frac{\text{Incremental Earnings Added to Numerator}}{\text{Incremental Shares Added to Denominator}}$$ (For options/warrants, incremental earnings are zero, so incremental EPS is ₹0.00 → always most dilutive!).
  2. Step 2: Rank Potential Ordinary Shares: Order the instruments from lowest incremental EPS (most dilutive) to highest incremental EPS (least dilutive).
  3. Step 3: Sequential Recalculation: Starting from Basic EPS, incorporate instruments one by one in the order of their ranking:
    • If the resulting EPS decreases → the instrument is dilutive; proceed to the next instrument.
    • If the resulting EPS increases → the instrument is anti-dilutive; stop the process and exclude that instrument and all subsequent instruments!

6. Contingently Issuable Shares & Subsidiary Dilution in CFS

Contingently Issuable Ordinary Shares: Ordinary shares issuable for little or no cash or other consideration upon the satisfaction of specified conditions in a contingent share agreement:

  • In Basic EPS: Treated as outstanding and included only from the date when all necessary conditions are met (i.e., events have occurred).
  • In Diluted EPS: Included from the beginning of the period if the conditions are deemed satisfied based on conditions existing at the end of the reporting period.
Subsidiary Having Potential Ordinary Shares (Group EPS - Paragraph 40): Where a subsidiary issues potential ordinary shares (convertible bonds, warrants, convertible preference shares) exercisable into subsidiary shares:
  • First, calculate the subsidiary's own Basic and Diluted EPS.
  • In Consolidated Basic EPS, include the parent's proportionate share of subsidiary's basic earnings.
  • In Consolidated Diluted EPS, include the parent's proportionate share of subsidiary's diluted earnings (Parent's holding of subsidiary ordinary shares, warrants, and preference shares multiplied by subsidiary's Diluted EPS - TYK 5).

7. Significant Differences: Ind AS 33 vis-à-vis AS 20

Parameter Ind AS 33 (Modern Framework) Old AS 20 (Previous GAAP)
Options & Warrants Dilution Uses the Treasury Stock Method (assumes proceeds used to repurchase shares at average fair value; only free shares dilute). Did not mandate the treasury stock method; gave broader formula.
Mandatorily Convertible Instruments Included in Basic EPS from the date contract is entered into (since conversion is certain, only passage of time remains). Included in Diluted EPS, not in Basic EPS until actual conversion date.

TYK Question 1 Rights Issue Bonus Element & WANOS Calculation
Question: ABC Ltd. has the following details for the calendar year ended 31 December 20X1:
  • 1 January 20X1: Ordinary shares in issue: 1,000,000 shares.
  • 31 March 20X1: Rights issue of 1 share for every 5 shares held at an exercise price of 90 paise (₹0.90).
  • Fair value of shares immediately prior to exercise on 31 March 20X1 (cum-rights price): ₹1.00.
Calculate the number of shares for use in the EPS calculation for the calendar year.
Step-by-Step Calculation:
  1. Rights Shares Issued: $$\text{Rights Shares} = 1,000,000 \times \frac{1}{5} = \mathbf{200,000\text{ shares}}$$ Total shares after rights issue = $1,000,000 + 200,000 = \mathbf{1,200,000\text{ shares}}$.
  2. Theoretical Ex-Rights Price (TERP): $$\text{TERP} = \frac{(1,000,000 \times ₹1.00) + (200,000 \times ₹0.90)}{1,000,000 + 200,000} = \frac{₹10,00,000 + ₹1,80,000}{1,200,000} = \frac{₹11,80,000}{1,200,000} = \mathbf{₹0.9833}$$
  3. Bonus Adjustment Factor: $$\text{Adjustment Factor} = \frac{\text{Cum-Rights Price}}{\text{TERP}} = \frac{₹1.00}{₹0.9833} = \mathbf{1.017}$$
  4. Weighted Average Number of Ordinary Shares (WANOS):
    • Period 1 Jan to 31 March (3 months): $1,000,000 \times 1.017 \times \frac{3}{12} = 254,250\text{ shares}$.
    • Period 1 April to 31 Dec (9 months): $1,200,000 \times \frac{9}{12} = 900,000\text{ shares}$.
    • Total WANOS for 20X1 = $254,250 + 900,000 = \mathbf{1,154,250\text{ shares}}$.

Question: An entity has on 1 January: Ordinary shares in issue: 1,000,000 shares; 5% Convertible bonds: ₹100,000 (terms: 120 ordinary shares for every ₹100 of bonds). On 31 March, holders of ₹25,000 bonds converted into ordinary shares. Profit for the year ended 31 December is ₹200,000. Applicable tax rate is 30%.

Calculate Basic and Diluted EPS for the year. (Ignore the need to split the convertible bonds into liability and equity elements).

    • Basic EPS = $\frac{₹200,000}{1,022,500} = \mathbf{₹0.196\text{ per share}}$.
  2. Diluted EPS Calculation:
    • Adjusted Numerator (Earnings): Interest saved on ₹25,000 bonds for 3 months (prior to conversion) + interest saved on remaining ₹75,000 bonds for full 12 months: $$\text{Interest on ₹25,000 (3 mos)} = ₹25,000 \times 5\% \times \frac{3}{12} = ₹312.50$$ $$\text{Interest on ₹75,000 (12 mos)} = ₹75,000 \times 5\% = ₹3,750$$ $$\text{Total Gross Interest Saved} = ₹312.50 + ₹3,750 = ₹4,062.50$$ $$\text{Net Interest Saved After Tax (70%)} = ₹4,062.50 \times (1 - 0.30) = \mathbf{₹2,844}$$ $$\text{Adjusted Diluted Earnings} = ₹200,000 + ₹2,844 = \mathbf{₹202,844}$$

Shares from remaining ₹75,000 bonds for full year: $\frac{₹75,000}{₹100} \times 120 = 90,000\text{ shares}$. $$\text{Diluted Shares} = 1,022,500 + 7,500 + 90,000 = \mathbf{1,120,000\text{ shares}}$$ (Check: 1,000,000 original + 120,000 full potential = 1,120,000 shares).
    • Diluted EPS = $\frac{₹202,844}{1,120,000} = \mathbf{₹0.181\text{ per share}}$.

Question: Calculate Subsidiary's and Group's Basic and Diluted EPS from the following data:

  • Profit attributable to ordinary equity holders: ₹12,000 (excluding any earnings of or dividends from subsidiary).
  • Ordinary shares outstanding: 10,000 shares.
  • Instruments of subsidiary owned by parent: 800 ordinary shares; 30 warrants to purchase ordinary shares of subsidiary; 300 convertible preference shares.
Subsidiary:
  • Profit: ₹5,400.
  • Ordinary shares outstanding: 1,000 shares.
  • Warrants: 150 exercisable to purchase ordinary shares at exercise price ₹10 (average market price ₹20).
  • Convertible Preference shares: 400 shares (dividend ₹1.00 per share), each convertible into 1 ordinary share. (No intercompany eliminations necessary).
Comprehensive Step-by-Step Group EPS Working:
  1. Part A: Subsidiary's Own EPS:
    • Subsidiary Basic EPS: $$\text{Profit to Ordinary} = ₹5,400 - (400 \times ₹1.00\text{ pref dividend}) = ₹5,000$$ $$\text{Subsidiary Basic EPS} = \frac{₹5,000}{1,000\text{ shares}} = \mathbf{₹5.00\text{ per share}}$$

      • Shares from 400 preference shares = $400 \times 1 = 400\text{ shares}$.
      • Diluted Denominator = $1,000 + 75 + 400 = 1,475\text{ shares}$.
      • $$\text{Subsidiary Diluted EPS} = \frac{₹5,400}{1,475} = \mathbf{₹3.66\text{ per share}}$$

    • Parent's standalone profit: ₹12,000.
    • Parent's share of subsidiary basic earnings: $$\text{From 800 Ordinary Shares} = 800 \times ₹5.00 = ₹4,000$$ $$\text{From 300 Preference Shares} = 300 \times ₹1.00 = ₹300$$ $$\text{Total Subsidiary Contribution} = ₹4,000 + ₹300 = ₹4,300$$
    • Total Consolidated Basic Earnings = $₹12,000 + ₹4,300 = ₹16,300$.
    • Consolidated Basic EPS = $\frac{₹16,300}{10,000\text{ shares}} = \mathbf{₹1.63\text{ per share}}$.
  3. Part C: Consolidated Group Diluted EPS:
    • Parent's standalone profit: ₹12,000.
    • Parent's share of subsidiary diluted earnings: $$\text{From 800 Ordinary Shares} = \frac{800}{1,000} \times (1,000 \times ₹3.66) = 800 \times ₹3.66 = ₹2,928$$ $$\text{From 30 Warrants} = \frac{30}{150} \times (75 \times ₹3.66) = 0.20 \times ₹274.50 = ₹55$$ $$\text{From 300 Preference Shares} = \frac{300}{400} \times (400 \times ₹3.66) = 300 \times ₹3.66 = ₹1,098$$ $$\text{Total Diluted Earnings from Subsidiary} = ₹2,928 + ₹55 + ₹1,098 = ₹4,081$$

Unit 3 • Segment Reporting & Transparency

Ind AS 108: Operating Segments

Adopts the "Management Approach" to segment reporting, requiring entities to disclose financial and descriptive information about their reportable segments based on internal reports reviewed by the Chief Operating Decision Maker (CODM). Covers operating segment criteria, aggregation principles, 10% quantitative thresholds, 75% external revenue rule, entity-wide disclosures, and reconciliations.

1. The Management Approach & Chief Operating Decision Maker (CODM)

Ind AS 108 requires an entity to report financial and descriptive information about its reportable segments through the eyes of management—the Management Approach. This aligns external financial reporting directly with internal organizational structures and managerial decision-making.

Scope Applicability (Paragraph 2): Applies to entities whose debt or equity instruments are traded in a public market or that file financial statements with a securities regulator for the purpose of issuing instruments in a public market. If an entity presents both consolidated and separate financial statements, segment information is required only in the Consolidated Financial Statements.
Core Pillar Official Definition & Criteria (Paragraph 5) Practical Nuance
Chief Operating Decision Maker (CODM) A function, not necessarily a manager with a specific title. That function is responsible for allocating resources to and assessing the performance of the operating segments of an entity (e.g., CEO, COO, or Board/Executive Committee). Identified by function, not executive hierarchy.
Operating Segment A component of an entity that satisfies all three criteria:
  1. Engages in business activities from which it may earn revenues and incur expenses (including inter-segment revenues);
  2. Whose operating results are regularly reviewed by the entity's CODM to allocate resources and assess performance; and
  3. For which discrete financial information is available.
 Corporate headquarters and post-employment benefit plans are NOT operating segments.
Aggregation Criteria (Paragraph 12) Two or more operating segments may be aggregated into a single segment if aggregation is consistent with the standard's core principle, the segments have similar economic characteristics, and are similar in:
  • (a) Nature of products and services;
  • (b) Nature of production processes;
  • (c) Type or class of customer;
  • (d) Methods used to distribute products/services; and
  • (e) Nature of regulatory environment (if applicable).
 All 5 similarity criteria must be satisfied to aggregate!

2. Quantitative Thresholds (10% Tests) & 75% External Revenue Rule

An entity shall report separate information about each operating segment that meets any of the quantitative thresholds set out in Paragraph 13:

Quantitative Test Threshold Formula & Calculation Base Exam Traps & Key Rules
1. Revenue Test (10%) Reported revenue (both external sales and intersegment sales/transfers) is 10% or more of the combined revenue, internal and external, of all operating segments: $$\frac{\text{Segment External Revenue} + \text{Internal Revenue}}{\text{Total Combined Internal and External Revenue}} \ge 10\%$$ Includes Inter-segment
Denominator is combined revenue (not consolidated revenue!).
2. Profit or Loss Test (10%) The absolute amount of its reported profit or loss is 10% or more of the greater, in absolute amount, of:
  • (i) The combined reported profit of all operating segments that reported a profit; and
  • (ii) The combined reported loss of all operating segments that reported a loss.
 Absolute Greater Test
Do NOT net profits against losses when determining the benchmark denominator!
3. Assets Test (10%) Its assets are 10% or more of the combined assets of all operating segments: $$\frac{\text{Segment Assets}}{\text{Combined Assets of All Segments}} \ge 10\%$$ Segment Assets
Denominator is combined segment assets reviewed by CODM.
The 75% External Revenue Rule (Paragraph 15)
$$\frac{\text{Total External Revenue of Identified Reportable Segments}}{\text{Total Entity External Revenue}} \ge 75\%$$
If reportable segments cover <75% of total external revenue, management must designate additional operating segments as reportable until the 75% threshold is achieved!
Practical Limit on Number of Segments (Paragraph 19): While no hard upper ceiling is set, if the number of reportable segments increases above ten (10), the entity should consider whether a practical limit has been reached and whether segments can be aggregated to avoid information overload.

3. Segment Measurement Principles & Mandatory Reconciliations

A unique hallmark of Ind AS 108 is that accounting policies used for segment reporting are those used for internal reporting to the CODM, even if they differ from the accounting policies used in the consolidated financial statements!

  • If the CODM reviews segment inventory using FIFO while financial statements use Weighted Average, segment disclosures MUST be presented on a FIFO basis (TYK 3).
  • If the CODM evaluates performance using EBITDA or cash-basis figures, that exact measure must be presented.

Mandatory Reconciliations to Financial Statement Totals (Paragraph 28):

Segment Total Reconciliation Adjustments Financial Statement Line Item
Total Reportable Segment Revenues Less: Inter-segment revenues eliminated on consolidation; Add: Unallocated corporate revenues. Total Entity Revenue
Total Reportable Segment Profit/Loss Adjust for: Unallocated corporate expenses, finance costs, exceptional items, elimination of intercompany profits. Entity Profit Before Tax
Total Reportable Segment Assets Add: Unallocated corporate assets, general investments, deferred tax assets, corporate headquarters. Total Entity Assets
Total Reportable Segment Liabilities Add: Unallocated corporate debt, borrowings, deferred tax liabilities, unallocated provisions. Total Entity Liabilities

4. Entity-Wide Disclosures: Products, Geography & Major Customers

Entity-wide disclosures apply to all entities subject to Ind AS 108, including entities that have only a single reportable operating segment!

Entity-Wide Category Required Disclosures Specific Rules
Products & Services (Paragraph 32) Revenues from external customers for each product and service, or each group of similar products and services. Based on financial statement figures.
Geographical Information (Paragraph 33) Disclose separately for: (i) Country of Domicile (India) and (ii) All foreign countries in total (and individual foreign countries if material):
  • Revenues from external customers attributed to country; and
  • Non-current assets (excluding financial instruments, deferred tax assets, and post-employment benefit assets).
 Excludes financial assets and DTAs from non-current assets.
Major Customers (Paragraph 34) If revenues from transactions with a single external customer amount to 10% or more of total entity revenues:
  • Disclose that fact;
  • Total revenue derived from each such customer; and
  • The identity of the segment(s) reporting the revenue.
 Identity Protected
The standard does NOT require disclosing the customer's legal name (refer as "Customer A", "Customer B").

5. Significant Differences: Ind AS 108 vis-à-vis AS 17

Parameter Ind AS 108 (Modern Framework) Old AS 17 (Previous GAAP)
Core Approach Management Approach: Segments identified and measured based on internal reports reviewed by CODM. Risk and Return Approach: Identified based on nature of risks and returns.
Classification Single operating segment classification. No distinction between primary and secondary reporting formats. Mandatory classification into Primary and Secondary segments (Business vs Geographical).
Measurement Basis Permits segment measures to follow internal management accounting policies (even if non-Ind AS), provided reconciliation is given. Mandated that segment accounting policies strictly match corporate financial reporting policies.
Major Customer Disclosures Explicit requirement to disclose reliance on major customers generating ≥10% of revenue. No explicit requirement to disclose major customer dependence.

TYK Question 3 CODM Measurement Basis (FIFO vs. Weighted Average)
Question: An entity uses the weighted average cost formula to assign costs to inventories and cost of goods sold for external financial reporting purposes. However, the internal reports provided to the Chief Operating Decision Maker (CODM) use the First-In, First-Out (FIFO) method for evaluating the performance of segment operations. State the cost formula to be used for Ind AS 108 disclosure purposes.
Solution & Core Standards Rule:

Under Paragraphs 25 & 26 of Ind AS 108, the amount of each segment item reported shall be the measure reported to the Chief Operating Decision Maker (CODM) for the purposes of making decisions about allocating resources to the segment and assessing its performance.

  • Therefore, the entity must use the First-In, First-Out (FIFO) method for its segment disclosures under Ind AS 108, because that is the measure used by the CODM.
  • However, under Paragraph 28, the entity is required to provide a reconciliation between the total of the reportable segments' profit or loss (measured under FIFO) and the entity's profit or loss before tax as reported in the financial statements (measured under Weighted Average cost).
