Chapter Architecture & High-Yield Exam Matrix

Understanding how the selection of policies, subsequent events, fair valuation mechanics, and statutory corporate schedules interact in the preparation of general-purpose financial statements.

The Module 1 Interlinking Framework

Foundational Synergy

Financial reporting is not a fragmented exercise of isolated standards. Module 1 constructs an airtight operational architecture:

  • Ind AS 1 prescribes how items are classified (current vs non-current) and mandates a Third Balance Sheet at the beginning of the preceding period whenever an accounting policy is applied retrospectively, restated for errors, or reclassified with a material effect.
  • Ind AS 8 dictates how policies are selected in the absence of a standard, the rigorous limits on changing them, how accounting estimates are treated prospectively, and how prior-period errors are restated retrospectively.
  • Ind AS 10 establishes the temporal boundary of financial statements—the window between the balance sheet date and the board approval date—defining what must be adjusted (existing conditions) vs what must only be disclosed (subsequent conditions), with strict exceptions for Going Concern and Dividends.
  • Ind AS 113 serves as the master measurement engine whenever any Ind AS (such as Ind AS 16, 38, 40, 105, 109, 103) permits or requires fair value measurement, establishing an exit-price model, market participant perspective, and the Level 1, Level 2, Level 3 hierarchy.
  • Division II of Schedule III translates all Ind AS requirements into statutory corporate presentation formats under the Companies Act, 2013, incorporating recent mandatory disclosures including ageing schedules, promoter shareholding, undisclosed income, CSR, and 11 financial analytical ratios.

Section 02 • Unit 1

Ind AS 8: Objective, Scope & Master Definitions

Understanding the criteria for selecting and changing accounting policies, together with the accounting treatment and disclosure of changes in accounting policies, changes in estimates, and corrections of prior period errors.

Statutory Scope & Specific Carve-Outs

Ind AS 8 Para 1–5

Ind AS 8 shall be applied in:

  1. Selecting and applying accounting policies;
  2. Accounting for changes in accounting policies;
  3. Accounting for changes in accounting estimates; and
  4. Accounting for corrections of prior period errors.
Scope Carve-Outs to Note:
  • Tax Effects: The tax effects of retrospective application of accounting policies and retrospective restatement of prior period errors are not dealt with in Ind AS 8; they are governed strictly by Ind AS 12 (Income Taxes).
  • First-time Adoption: The requirements of Ind AS 8 in respect of changes in accounting policies do not apply to an entity's first Ind AS financial statements (governed by Ind AS 101).

Master Definitions (Key Technical Terms)

Ind AS 8 Para 5
1
Accounting Policies Specific principles, bases, conventions, rules and practices applied by an entity in preparing and presenting financial statements (e.g. historical cost basis vs fair value model, FIFO vs weighted average for inventories).
2
Accounting Estimates Monetary amounts in financial statements that are subject to measurement uncertainty (e.g., loss allowance for ECL under Ind AS 109, provision for warranty obligations under Ind AS 37, useful life or residual value of PPE under Ind AS 16).
3
Prior Period Errors Omissions from, and misstatements in, the entity's financial statements for one or more prior periods arising from a failure to use, or misuse of, reliable information that: (a) was available when financial statements for those periods were approved for issue; and (b) could reasonably be expected to have been obtained and taken into account. Errors include mathematical mistakes, mistakes in applying accounting policies, oversights, misinterpretations of facts, and fraud.
4
Retrospective Application Applying a new accounting policy to transactions, other events and conditions as if that policy had always been applied.
5
Retrospective Restatement Correcting the recognition, measurement and disclosure of amounts of elements of financial statements as if a prior period error had never occurred.
6
Prospective Application Applying the new accounting policy to transactions occurring after the date of change, and recognising the effect of a change in an accounting estimate in the current and future periods affected by the change.

The Refined Concept of Materiality & Obscuring Information

Ind AS 1 • Ind AS 8 Alignment

Information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence decisions that the primary users of general purpose financial statements make.

Circumstances where material information is obscured:

  • Information regarding a material item is disclosed using vague or unclear language;
  • Information regarding a material item is scattered throughout the financial statements;
  • Dissimilar items, transactions or other events are inappropriately aggregated;
  • Similar items, transactions or other events are inappropriately disaggregated;
  • The understandability of financial statements is reduced because material information is hidden by immaterial clutter.
Dual Application of Materiality: Ind AS 1 governs materiality in disclosures; Ind AS 8 governs materiality in applying accounting policies and correcting measurement/recognition errors. Immaterial departures from Ind AS are permitted, but it is prohibited to leave immaterial errors uncorrected if done intentionally to achieve a particular presentation of financial position, performance, or cash flows!
Section 03 • Unit 1

Selection, Hierarchy & Consistency of Accounting Policies

How an entity selects policies when a standard exists, the mandatory hierarchy of guidance in the absence of an explicit standard, and strict consistency rules.

Hierarchy for Selecting Policies when No Specific Ind AS Exists

Ind AS 8 Para 10–12

When an Ind AS specifically applies to a transaction, the accounting policy applied must be determined by that Ind AS (supported by ICAI Guidance Notes). In the absence of an Ind AS that specifically applies, management must exercise judgment to develop a policy that results in information that is relevant and reliable (representing economic substance over legal form, neutral, prudent, and complete).

1
Primary Tier (Mandatory Descending Order)
  1. Requirements in other Ind AS dealing with similar and related issues;
  2. Definitions, recognition criteria, and measurement concepts in the Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting under Ind AS.
2
Secondary Tier (Permissible External Literature) Management may consider the most recent pronouncements of the IASB (International Accounting Standards Board), and in absence thereof, pronouncements of other standard-setting bodies with a similar framework, accounting literature, and accepted industry practices—provided they do not conflict with Tier 1 sources.

Consistency Across Categories & Classes

Ind AS 8 Para 13

An entity must select and apply its policies consistently for similar transactions, events, and conditions, unless an Ind AS permits or requires categorisation of items for which different policies may be appropriate:

  • Property, Plant and Equipment (Ind AS 16): An entity categorises PPE into classes (e.g. land, buildings, plant & machinery). It may apply the revaluation model to land while retaining the cost model for plant & machinery, but must apply the model consistently to all assets within that class.
  • Inventories (Ind AS 2): Different cost formulas (FIFO vs Weighted Average) can be applied to different classes of inventory with different nature or use, but items having similar nature/use must use the same formula.
Section 04 • Unit 1

Changes in Accounting Policies (Permissible vs Non-Changes)

Strict criteria governing when an accounting policy can be changed, distinguishing true policy changes from changes in substance and initial applications.

When is a Change in Accounting Policy Permitted?

Ind AS 8 Para 14

An entity shall change an accounting policy only and only if the change:

  1. Is required by an Ind AS; OR
  2. Results in the financial statements providing reliable and more relevant information about the effects of transactions, events, or conditions on financial position, performance, or cash flows.
Exam Alert • Voluntary Changes: Frequent changes are prohibited to preserve trend comparability. A voluntary change requires rigorous justification that the new policy produces more relevant information. For example, changing separate financial statement measurement of subsidiaries from cost to fair value under Ind AS 109 is a voluntary change that must be applied retrospectively. Early application of an Ind AS is not a voluntary change.

What are NOT Changes in Accounting Policies?

Ind AS 8 Para 16–18

The following are explicitly NOT changes in accounting policies:

Scenario Why It is NOT a Policy Change ICAI Example / Illustration
Transactions differing in substance Applying a policy to transactions that differ in substance from previous transactions is an application of an appropriate standard to a different operational reality. Example 5 & Illustration 2: Owner-occupied hotel (PPE under Ind AS 16) vs newly acquired hotel outsourced to passive operator (Investment property under Ind AS 40). Office building relocated and leased out to 3rd party reclassified from PPE to Ind AS 40.
New or previously immaterial transactions Adopting a policy for transactions that did not occur previously or were immaterial is initial adoption, not a change. Entering into foreign currency derivative contracts for the first time.
Change in Functional Currency Functional currency is determined by underlying facts under Ind AS 21. When facts change, translation procedures apply prospectively. Illustration 3: Entity changing functional currency due to shift in sales/expense denomination.
Initial Revaluation of Assets Initial application of revaluation model under Ind AS 16 or Ind AS 38 is treated as a revaluation surplus under those standards, not retrospective under Ind AS 8. Ind AS 16 Para 29 revaluation accounting.
Change in Depreciation / Amortisation Method Reflects expected pattern of consumption of economic benefits $\rightarrow$ treated strictly as a change in accounting estimate! Ind AS 16 Para 60–61 and Ind AS 38 Para 104.
Section 05 • Unit 1

Retrospective Application & Limitations of Impracticability

How retrospective application works in practice, the 3rd Balance Sheet trigger under Ind AS 1, and the three tiers of relief when retrospective calculation is impracticable.

Mechanics of Retrospective Application

Ind AS 8 Para 19–22

When a change in accounting policy is applied retrospectively:

  • The entity must adjust the opening balance of each affected component of equity (usually retained earnings) for the earliest prior period presented;
  • All other comparative amounts disclosed for each prior period presented must be restated as if the new policy had always been applied;
  • Third Balance Sheet Requirement (Ind AS 1 Para 40A): An entity must present a third balance sheet as at the beginning of the preceding period (e.g., as at 1st April 20X1 if reporting for 20X2-20X3 with comparative 20X1-20X2) if it applies an accounting policy retrospectively, makes a retrospective restatement of items, or reclassifies items, and the effect on the opening balance sheet of the preceding period is material.

Impracticability: Meaning & Three-Tier Hierarchy

Ind AS 8 Para 23–27

Applying a requirement is impracticable when the entity cannot apply it after making every reasonable effort to do so. A retrospective change is impracticable for a prior period if:

  1. The effects of retrospective application are not determinable;
  2. Retrospective application requires assumptions about what management's intent would have been in that period; or
  3. Retrospective application requires significant estimates and it is impossible to distinguish objectively information providing evidence of circumstances existing on that date from other subsequent information. Hindsight cannot be used!
Impracticability Level Circumstance Required Accounting Treatment
Level 1: Period-specific effect impracticable Entity cannot determine the specific effect on one or more comparative prior periods presented. Apply the new policy to the carrying amounts of assets/liabilities at the beginning of the earliest period for which retrospective application is practicable (which may be the current period), with a corresponding adjustment to opening equity of that period.
Level 2: Cumulative effect impracticable Entity cannot determine the cumulative effect at the beginning of the current period of applying the policy to all prior periods (e.g. server crash / virus attack wiped past records as in Example 10). Adjust comparative information to apply the new policy prospectively from the earliest date practicable. Disregard cumulative adjustments before that date.
Examiner's Warning on Hindsight!
When applying a policy retrospectively or correcting an error, you CANNOT use hindsight! If you are estimating employee sick leave liability for a prior year, you must disregard subsequent flu epidemics that became known only after those prior financial statements were approved. You only use information that existed and could reasonably be expected to be known at the prior date.
Section 06 • Unit 1

Changes in Accounting Estimates (Nature, Treatment & Disclosures)

Distinguishing estimates from policies, prospective accounting rules, and handling overlaps when a change exhibits characteristics of both.

Nature & Prospective Accounting Treatment

Ind AS 8 Para 32–38

Estimation involves judgments based on the latest available, reliable information. By its nature, a change in an accounting estimate does not relate to prior periods and is not the correction of an error.

The effect of a change in an accounting estimate shall be recognised prospectively by including it in profit or loss in:

  • The period of the change only, if the change affects that period only (e.g. revision of bad debt provision / ECL); OR
  • The period of change and future periods, if the change affects both (e.g. revision of useful life or residual value of PPE affects depreciation for current year and remaining useful life).
Tie-Breaker Rule: Policy vs Estimate!
A change in measurement basis (e.g., historical cost to fair value) is a change in accounting policy. However, when it is difficult to distinguish a change in an accounting policy from a change in an accounting estimate, the change is treated as a change in an accounting estimate (and applied prospectively)!

Real-Life Case Illustration: Warranty Provision Defect

ICAI Example 12

During FY 20X1-20X2, Entity ABC introduced electric motors with a 2-year warranty and made a provision of ₹1,00,000 based on trials. In FY 20X2-20X3, an unexpected defect came to light, requiring repair costs of ₹2,00,000 in 20X2-20X3 and an estimated ₹1,50,000 in 20X3-20X4 for 20X1-20X2 motors:

  • The discovery of new defect information is a change in estimate, not an error.
  • Comparatives for FY 20X1-20X2 are not adjusted.
  • Expense recognised in P&L for FY 20X2-20X3 = ₹2,00,000 (spent) + ₹1,50,000 (closing provision) − ₹1,00,000 (opening provision) = ₹2,50,000.
Section 07 • Unit 1

Prior Period Errors: Classification, Correction & Third Balance Sheet

Handling retrospective restatements for prior period omissions and misstatements, distinguishing them from estimates, and evaluating third balance sheet criteria.

Correction Mechanics for Prior Period Errors

Ind AS 8 Para 41–49

Errors discovered in the current period before financial statements are approved are corrected in that period. Material prior period errors discovered subsequently must be corrected retrospectively:

Discovery Situation Required Restatement Impact on Financial Statements
Situation 1: Error relates to comparative prior period presented Restate comparative amounts for that prior period (e.g. FY 20X1-20X2 restated in 20X2-20X3). P&L of comparative year restated; opening balance of current year automatically reflects correct balances.
Situation 2: Error relates to period BEFORE earliest comparative period presented Restate opening balances of assets, liabilities, and equity for the earliest prior period presented. Triggers presentation of a Third Balance Sheet as at the beginning of the preceding period (e.g. 1st April 20X1) if material!

When is a Third Balance Sheet Required? (Ind AS 1 vs Ind AS 8)

High-Yield Exam Trap

Under Para 40A of Ind AS 1, a third balance sheet as at the beginning of the preceding period is required if:

  1. The entity applies an accounting policy retrospectively, makes a retrospective restatement of items, or reclassifies items; AND
  2. The retrospective application, restatement, or reclassification has a material effect on the information in the balance sheet at the beginning of the preceding period.
Illustration 8 vs TYK 5 Comparison:
  • Illustration 8 (Reclassification of Liabilities from Non-Current to Current): Affected the classification on the Balance Sheet as of 1st April 20X0. Hence, a Third Balance Sheet IS REQUIRED as at 1st April 20X0!
  • TYK 5 (Reclassification of Expenses from Finance Costs to Other Expenses): Only affected the Statement of Profit and Loss for 20X1-20X2. The total expenses and net profit remained unchanged, and there was no effect on the Balance Sheet at 1st April 20X0. Hence, a Third Balance Sheet is NOT REQUIRED!
Section 08 • Unit 1

Significant Differences: Ind AS 8 vs AS 5

Comprehensive comparative matrix between Indian Accounting Standard 8 and previous Indian GAAP AS 5.

Key Feature Ind AS 8 AS 5 (Previous Indian GAAP)
Title & Scope Accounting Policies, Changes in Accounting Estimates and Errors. Deals with criteria for selecting and applying policies. Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies. Selection of policies is in AS 1.
Extraordinary Items Explicitly prohibited by Ind AS 1 and Ind AS 8. No item can be shown as extraordinary. Requires separate presentation of extraordinary items on the face of P&L as distinct from ordinary activities.
Statutory Changes Does not recognise "statutory mandate" as a blanket ground; change must be required by Ind AS or yield reliable and more relevant info. Allows change in accounting policy if required by statute.
Accounting for Policy Changes Requires full retrospective application with restatement of comparatives + Third Balance Sheet (if material). Impact of change is shown in the financial statements of the current period in which the change is made.
Prior Period Terminology Refers to Prior Period Errors (wider scope: includes oversights, misinterpretation of facts, and fraud). Refers to Prior Period Items (limited to errors or omissions in preparation of prior financial statements).
Correction of Errors Corrected by retrospective restatement of comparatives and opening equity (excluded from current P&L). Included in current period net profit or loss (either as part of current profit or as a separate item after current profit).
Third Balance Sheet Mandatory if retrospective application/restatement has a material effect at beginning of preceding period. No concept of a third balance sheet exists under AS.
Section 09 • Unit 1

Ind AS 8: Solved ICAI Illustrations & TYK Problems

Step-by-step solutions with full exam rationale for all Unit 1 illustrations and Test Your Knowledge questions.

ICAI TYK 1 • Unit 1 Carpet Retail Outlet: Point-of-Sale vs Fitting Revenue
A carpet retail outlet sells and fits carpets to the general public. It recognises revenue when the carpet is fitted, which on an average is six weeks after the purchase. It then decides to sub-contract the fitting of carpets to self-employed fitters. It now recognises revenue at the point-of-sale of the carpet. Whether this change in recognising revenue is a change in accounting policy under Ind AS 8?

Analysis & Conclusion:

As per paragraph 16(a) of Ind AS 8, the application of an accounting policy for transactions, other events or conditions that differ in substance from those previously occurring is not a change in accounting policy.

Here, the carpet retailer changed the operational arrangement of how carpets are fitted by sub-contracting fitting to self-employed fitters. The substance of the transaction between the entity and the customer has altered: previously, fitting was an entity obligation; under the new model, the performance obligation for the carpet is satisfied at point of sale.

Therefore, this is not a change in accounting policy. There is no requirement to retrospectively adjust the prior period figures for revenue already recognised.

ICAI TYK 2 • Unit 1 Circumstances Requiring a Third Balance Sheet
Under what circumstances is an entity required to present a third balance sheet at the beginning of the preceding period?

Statutory Requirements (Ind AS 1 Para 40A):

An entity shall present a third balance sheet as at the beginning of the preceding period in addition to the minimum comparative financial statements if:

  1. It applies an accounting policy retrospectively, makes a retrospective restatement of items in its financial statements, or reclassifies items in its financial statements; and
  2. The retrospective application, retrospective restatement, or reclassification has a material effect on the information in the balance sheet at the beginning of the preceding period.

Example: If an entity prepares financial statements for FY 20X2-20X3 (with comparative FY 20X1-20X2) and restates a prior error occurring before 1st April 20X1, it must present balance sheets as at: (1) 31st March 20X3, (2) 31st March 20X2, and (3) 1st April 20X1 (the third balance sheet).

ICAI TYK 3 • Unit 1 Delta Ltd: Component Approach & Revaluation Model Note Disclosure
During 20X2, Delta Ltd. changed its accounting policy for depreciating PPE to apply a component approach completely, whilst adopting the revaluation model. Engineering survey at end of 20X1 provided component info, useful lives, fair values, but cost of components could not be reconstructed. Management determined it was impracticable to apply component approach retrospectively, and revaluation model must be applied prospectively. Hence, applied prospectively from start of 20X2.
Data: Tax rate 30%. End of 20X1: Cost ₹25,000, Accumulated Depreciation ₹14,000, Net Book Value ₹11,000. Prospective depreciation for 20X2 (old basis): ₹1,500. Survey Valuation at start of 20X2: ₹17,000, Residual value ₹3,000, Remaining life 7 years. New depreciation for 20X2: ₹2,000.
Required: Prepare the relevant disclosure note in accordance with Ind AS 8.

Computation of Impact on Current Year (20X2):

  • Carrying amount before revaluation = ₹11,000
  • Revalued amount (Fair Value) = ₹17,000
  • Gross Revaluation Increase = ₹17,000 − ₹11,000 = ₹6,000
  • Deferred Tax Provision on Revaluation = ₹6,000 × 30% = ₹1,800
  • Revaluation Surplus in Equity (OCI) = ₹6,000 − ₹1,800 = ₹4,200
  • Depreciation on new basis = ₹2,000 vs old basis = ₹1,500 $\rightarrow$ Increase in depreciation expense = ₹500
  • Tax relief on additional depreciation = ₹500 × 30% = ₹150 (reduction in tax expense).
Extract from Notes to the Financial Statements:
"From the start of 20X2, Delta Ltd. changed its accounting policy for depreciating property, plant and equipment to apply a components approach fully, whilst at the same time adopting the revaluation model. Management believes this policy provides reliable and more relevant information because it deals more accurately with components based on up-to-date values.

The policy has been applied prospectively from the start of 20X2 because it was not practicable to estimate the effects of applying the policy either retrospectively or prospectively from any earlier date. Accordingly, adoption has no effect on prior years.

The effect on the current year is: (a) increase in carrying amount of PPE at start of year by ₹6,000; (b) increase in opening deferred tax liability by ₹1,800; (c) creation of revaluation surplus in equity of ₹4,200; (d) increase in depreciation expense by ₹500; and (e) reduction in tax expense by ₹150."
ICAI TYK 4 • Unit 1 Depreciation Method Change: Policy vs Estimate
Is a change in the depreciation method for an item of property, plant and equipment a change in accounting policy or a change in accounting estimate?

Statutory Analysis (Ind AS 16 Paras 60 & 61):

The depreciation method applied to an asset must reflect the pattern in which the asset's future economic benefits are expected to be consumed by the entity. The method must be reviewed at least at each financial year-end. If there has been a significant change in the expected pattern of consumption, the method must be changed.

Paragraph 61 of Ind AS 16 explicitly provides that such a change is accounted for as a change in an accounting estimate in accordance with Ind AS 8 (applied prospectively). Determination of depreciation method involves estimation of the pattern of consumption; it is not an accounting policy choice.

ICAI TYK 5 • Unit 1 Finance Costs Misclassified as Other Expenses & Third Balance Sheet
An entity charged off certain expenses as finance costs in FY 20X0-20X1. While preparing FY 20X1-20X2 statements, management discovered they should have been other expenses due to misinterpretation of facts. The entity intends to restate comparative amounts for FY 20X0-20X1. Is this correction of an error under Ind AS 8? Would the entity need to present a third balance sheet?

Analysis & Conclusion:

  1. Correction of Error: As per Para 41 of Ind AS 8, errors include mistakes in applying policies and misinterpretation of facts. Misclassifying other expenses as finance costs is an error in presentation. Correcting it in the comparative period (FY 20X0-20X1) is considered a correction of an error under Ind AS 8.
  2. Third Balance Sheet Evaluation: Under Ind AS 1 Para 40A, a third balance sheet as at the beginning of the preceding period (1st April 20X0) is required only if the restatement has a material effect on the information in the balance sheet at that date.
  3. In this case, the restatement is purely between two expense line items within the Statement of Profit and Loss for FY 20X0-20X1. Total expenses, profit for the period, and retained earnings remain identical. There is no effect on the Balance Sheet as at 1st April 20X0.

Conclusion: The entity is NOT required to present a third balance sheet.

Section 10 • Unit 2

Ind AS 10: Scope, Reporting Period & Date of Approval

Defining events after the reporting period, identifying the precise approving authority, and understanding the temporal cut-off for corporate financial reporting.

What are Events after the Reporting Period?

Ind AS 10 Para 3

Events after the reporting period are those events, favourable and unfavourable, that occur between the end of the reporting period and the date when the financial statements are approved for issue by the Board of Directors (for a company) or by the corresponding approving authority (for any other entity).

Both Favourable & Unfavourable Events Covered: Ind AS 10 requires accounting/disclosure of both favourable and unfavourable events. Entities cannot ignore favourable events under a misconception of conservatism if an existing condition is confirmed (Illustration 4 & TYK 2)!

Determining the Exact Date of Approval for Issue

Ind AS 10 Paras 4–6
1
Companies with Board of Directors The date of approval is the date when the Board of Directors approves the financial statements for issue to shareholders (Para 4). The subsequent approval by shareholders at the AGM does not extend the period (Para 5).
2
Supervisory Board Structure If management issues financial statements to a supervisory board (composed solely of non-executives), approval occurs on the date management approves them for issue to the supervisory board (Para 6).
3
Other Entities (Partnerships / LLPs) The date when the corresponding approving authority (the managing partners / designated partners) approves the financial statements for issue (Illustration 1).
4
Reopened Financial Statements If financial statements are reopened after initial approval due to discovery of fraud/error and re-approved by the Board, the date of approval is the date of final approval (Illustration 3).
5
Public Announcements / SEBI Submissions Events up to board approval date must be considered even if they occur after public announcement of profit or submission of quarterly/annual numbers to regulators (TYK 1).
Section 11 • Unit 2

Adjusting Events: Conditions & Comprehensive ICAI Scenarios

Events that provide evidence of conditions that existed at the end of the reporting period and require mandatory adjustment of recognised amounts.

The Adjusting Event Test

Ind AS 10 Para 8

An entity shall adjust the amounts recognised in its financial statements to reflect adjusting events after the reporting period. The fundamental criterion is: Did the event provide additional evidence of a condition that already existed at the end of the reporting period?

Scenario Why It Is an Adjusting Event Required Accounting Treatment
Court Case Settlement (Ind AS 37) Settlement after reporting date confirms that the entity had a present obligation at the balance sheet date. Adjust existing provision or recognise a new provision. Merely disclosing a contingent liability is prohibited! (Illustration 4).
Customer Bankruptcy (Ind AS 109) Insolvency after reporting date confirms that customer was credit-impaired at the balance sheet date (provided cause existed at year-end). Adjust loss allowance for ECL to write down receivable to net recoverable amount (Illustration 5: pre-year-end earthquake vs post-year-end earthquake).
Subsequent Sale of Inventories (Ind AS 2) Actual sale proceeds after reporting date provide objective evidence of Net Realisable Value (NRV) at balance sheet date. Write down inventory to selling price less selling costs if lower than cost (Illustration 6: car road tax drop, TYK 3: laptop price drop).
Final Cost / Proceeds Determination Subsequent receipt of supplier bills or completion accounts for assets bought/sold before year-end. Adjust capitalisation cost of PPE or sale proceeds in prior year (Illustration 7: machinery installation bills).
Profit Sharing or Bonus Payments (Ind AS 19) Entity had a legal or constructive obligation at year-end; final calculation depends on year-end profit determination. Accrue bonus/profit-sharing expense and liability in prior period financial statements.
Discovery of Fraud or Errors Proves that financial statements were incorrect as at the balance sheet date. Mandatory adjustment before approval for issue.
Construction Contract Rocky Surface Rocky foundation existed at year-end, even though discovered during subsequent excavation. Adjust contract costs and recognize foreseeable losses under Ind AS 115 (TYK 4: XY Ltd ₹50 Cr escalation).
Section 12 • Unit 2

Non-Adjusting Events: Conditions & Mandatory Disclosures

Events indicative of conditions that arose after the reporting period—no balance sheet adjustments, but full disclosure of material events.

Treatment & Disclosure Requirements

Ind AS 10 Paras 10, 21–22

An entity shall not adjust the amounts recognised in its financial statements for non-adjusting events. However, if non-adjusting events are material, non-disclosure could influence the economic decisions of users.

For each material category, the entity must disclose in notes:

  1. The nature of the event; and
  2. An estimate of its financial effect, or a statement that such an estimate cannot be made.
Category of Non-Adjusting Event Standard Interaction Required Disclosure Summary
Major business combination or disposal of a subsidiary Ind AS 103 / Ind AS 110 Details of acquisition consideration, fair values, and net assets acquired.
Announcing plan to discontinue an operation Ind AS 105 Assets/liabilities involved, revenue, and expected timeline.
Destruction of major production plant by fire Ind AS 10 / Ind AS 1 Estimated loss (net of insurance claims) and operational impact (Illustration 9).

The Going Concern Statutory Override

Ind AS 10 Paras 14–16

An entity shall not prepare its financial statements on a going concern basis if management determines after the reporting period either that it intends to liquidate the entity or to cease trading, or that it has no realistic alternative but to do so.

Pervasive Effect: Fundamental Change in Accounting Basis!
Deterioration in operating results or loss of contracts after year-end may indicate that going concern is no longer valid. If going concern is compromised, the effect is so pervasive that the standard requires a fundamental change in the basis of accounting (e.g. liquidation/realisable value basis) rather than an adjustment within the original basis! (Illustration 8: Directory contract expiry without renewal).

ICAI TYK 1 • Unit 2 SEBI Information Filing vs Date of Approval
The AGM of ABC Ltd for year ended 31st March 20X2 was held on 10th July 20X2 and Board Meeting conducted on 15th May 20X2. Meanwhile, the company had to disclose certain financial information to SEBI as per regulations on 20th April 20X2. Management contends that 20th April 20X2 shall be considered the cut-off date for 'after the reporting period'. Is management correct?

Analysis & Statutory Ruling (Ind AS 10 Paras 3 & 7):

Events after the reporting period include all events up to the date when the financial statements are approved for issue by the Board of Directors, even if those events occur after the public announcement of profit or selected financial information (such as filings with stock exchanges or SEBI).

The financial statements were approved by the Board on 15th May 20X2. Therefore, the period 'after the reporting period' runs from 31st March 20X2 to 15th May 20X2. Management's contention that the period ended on 20th April 20X2 is incorrect.

ICAI TYK 2 • Unit 2 Favourable Court Order Reducing Tax Liability
ABC Ltd. is in a legal suit against the GST department. The company gets a court order in its favour on 15th April 20X2 reducing tax liability as on 31st March 20X2. Financial statements were approved on 15th May 20X2. Management did not consider the effect because it is favourable and would hamper the realization concept. Comment on company's views under Ind AS 10.

Analysis & Statutory Ruling (Ind AS 10 Paras 3 & 8):

Paragraph 3 defines events after the reporting period as those events, favourable and unfavourable, that occur between the end of the reporting period and the date of approval.

The legal suit was pending as on 31st March 20X2 (condition existed at balance sheet date). The court order on 15th April 20X2 confirmed that the entity's present obligation was lower. This is an adjusting event.

Management's contention that favourable events should not be adjusted is incorrect. ABC Ltd. must adjust its financial statements for FY 20X1-20X2 to reflect the reduced tax liability.

ICAI TYK 3 • Unit 2 Laptop Inventory Valuation Drop after Balance Sheet Date
ABC Ltd. trades in laptops. On 31st March 20X2, it has 50 laptops purchased at ₹45,000 each. On 15th April 20X2, an advanced series is introduced, causing market price of existing laptops to drop to ₹35,000 each. Financial statements approved on 15th May 20X2. The company does not want to value inventory at ₹35,000 arguing the price reduction occurred after 31st March 20X2. Comment.

Analysis & Statutory Ruling (Ind AS 10 Para 9(b)(ii) & Ind AS 2):

Under Ind AS 2, inventory is valued at lower of cost and Net Realisable Value (NRV). Paragraph 9(b)(ii) of Ind AS 10 states that the sale or valuation evidence of inventories after the reporting period provides evidence about their NRV at the end of the reporting period.

The introduction of the advanced model confirms that the technological obsolescence was impending and the recoverable amount of the existing 50 laptops was ₹35,000. This is an adjusting event.

ABC Ltd. must value the closing inventory at ₹35,000 each (less selling costs) and recognise an inventory write-down of ₹5,00,000 [50 × (₹45,000 − ₹35,000)] in P&L for FY 20X1-20X2.

ICAI TYK 4 • Unit 2 Construction Contract Rocky Surface Escalation (₹50 Crores)
XY Ltd took a large construction contract for a PSU valued at ₹200 crores. Execution started in 20X1-20X2. On 29th May 20X2, while raising foundation work, it found a rocky surface, escalating contract costs by an extra ₹50 crores (unrecoverable from client). Financial year ended 31st March 20X2; statements approved on 15th June 20X2. How will you treat this in financial statements for year ended 31st March 20X2?

Analysis & Statutory Ruling:

The project work commenced during FY 20X1-20X2. The rocky strata physically existed under the ground as on 31st March 20X2; it was merely confirmed on 29th May 20X2 before board approval on 15th June 20X2.

In accordance with Ind AS 10, because the condition existed at the end of the reporting period, the discovery is an adjusting event.

Accounting Treatment: Under Ind AS 115, total estimated contract costs must be revised upward by ₹50 crores as of 31st March 20X2. If total contract costs now exceed total contract revenue (₹200 crores), the contract is onerous and the expected loss must be recognised immediately as an expense in P&L for FY 20X1-20X2!

What is Fair Value? (Exit Price vs Entry Price)

Ind AS 113 Para 9
Statutory Definition of Fair Value
$$\text{Fair Value} = \text{The price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date under current market conditions.}$$
Market-based exit price, not entity-specific settlement price or historical entry cost!
  • Exit Price: The price received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability. It is NOT the entry price paid to acquire the asset or received to assume the liability.
  • Orderly Transaction: Assumes exposure to the market for a period prior to the measurement date to allow for marketing activities that are usual and customary; it is not a forced transaction (e.g. forced liquidation or distress sale).
  • Market-Based: Measured using the assumptions market participants would use, including risk assumptions. Management's intent to hold an asset or settle a liability is irrelevant!

Section 18 • Unit 3

The Four Parameters of Fair Value Measurement

Asset/liability characteristics, unit of account, market participants, and transport vs transaction costs.

Parameter 1: Asset-Specific vs Entity-Specific Restrictions

Crucial Distinction

A fair value measurement takes into account characteristics of the asset/liability that market participants would consider:

  • Asset-Specific Restrictions (INCLUDED): Restrictions that transfer with the asset to any buyer (e.g. statutory zoning restriction that a piece of land cannot be used commercially for 10 years). Included in fair valuation!
  • Entity-Specific Restrictions (EXCLUDED): Restrictions that apply solely to the reporting entity (e.g. an entity has a corporate charter restricting it from developing land commercially, but any buyer can freely develop it). Ignored in fair valuation!

Parameter 2: Unit of Account

Aggregation vs Disaggregation

Ind AS 113 determines how to measure fair value; the unit of account (whether an individual asset/liability or a group of assets/liabilities like a Cash Generating Unit) is determined by the specific Ind AS requiring the measurement (e.g. Ind AS 109 specifies individual security level; Ind AS 36 specifies CGU level).

Parameter 3 & 4: Market Participants & Costs (Transport vs Transaction)

Key Valuation Rules

Market Participants: Buyers and sellers in the principal (or most advantageous) market who are independent (not related parties, unless arm's length demonstrated), knowledgeable, able, and willing to transact in their economic best interest.

Cost Element Nature & Definition Treatment in Fair Value
Transaction Costs Costs to enter into the transaction (brokerage, stamp duty, legal fees). Characteristic of the transaction, not the asset. NOT deducted from fair value! Used only to determine the most advantageous market.
Transport Costs Costs incurred to transport asset from its current location to the principal market. Location is a characteristic of the asset. DEDUCTED from fair value (adjusts the price in the market).
Section 19 • Unit 3

Principal Market vs Most Advantageous Market

Step-by-step decision framework for selecting the reference market when multiple active trading markets exist.

The Market Hierarchy Rule

Ind AS 113 Paras 16–19
1
Principal Market (First Priority) The market with the greatest volume and level of activity for the asset or liability that the entity can access. If a principal market exists, the price in that market MUST be used, even if a higher net price is available in another market!
2
Most Advantageous Market (Default in Absence of Principal Market) The market that maximises the net amount received to sell the asset (or minimises the net amount paid to transfer a liability), after deducting both transaction costs and transport costs.
Exam Computation Rule:
When determining which market is the most advantageous, compare Net Proceeds = Price − Transaction Costs − Transport Costs.
Once that market is selected, Fair Value = Quoted Price − Transport Costs ONLY (do NOT deduct transaction costs from the fair value)!

Highest and Best Use (HBU) for Non-Financial Assets

Valuation of real estate and non-financial assets: physical possibility, legal permissibility, and financial feasibility criteria.

The Three Pillars of Highest and Best Use

Ind AS 113 Paras 27–30

Fair value measurement of a non-financial asset takes into account a market participant's ability to generate economic benefits by using the asset in its Highest and Best Use (HBU) or by selling it to another participant who would use it in its highest and best use.

P
Physically Possible Considers physical characteristics that market participants would take into account (e.g. location, size of property, soil conditions).
L
Legally Permissible Considers legal restrictions on use (e.g. government zoning regulations, environmental clearances).
F
Financially Feasible Takes into account whether the use produces adequate income or cash flows to generate an investment return market participants require (after conversion/demolition costs).
Current Use Presumption: An entity's current use of a non-financial asset is presumed to be its highest and best use unless market or other factors suggest that a different use would maximise value (e.g., factory site in an area rezoned for high-rise residential towers — TYK 2).
Section 21 • Unit 3

Fair Value of Liabilities, Own Equity & Portfolio Netting Exception

Valuing liabilities based on transfer rather than settlement, incorporating non-performance risk, and the portfolio market risk exception.

Transfer Value vs Settlement Value

Ind AS 113 Paras 34–41

Fair value of a liability assumes that the liability is transferred to a market participant at the measurement date; the liability remains outstanding and is not settled or extinguished with the counterparty.

  • Non-Performance Risk: Fair value of a liability reflects the effect of non-performance risk, which includes, but may not be limited to, the entity's own credit standing.
  • Held by Other Parties as Assets: If quoted prices are not available for the transfer of an identical/similar liability, but the identical item is held by another party as an asset, fair value is measured from the perspective of a market participant holding that asset (quoted price of asset in active market $\rightarrow$ observable inputs).

The Portfolio Exception (Offsetting Market & Credit Risk)

Ind AS 113 Paras 48–56

An entity that manages a group of financial assets and financial liabilities on the basis of its net exposure to market risks or credit risk is permitted an exception to measure fair value on the basis of the price that would be received to sell a net long position or transfer a net short position.

Conditions: Must be managed in accordance with documented risk management/investment strategy, reported internally to KMP, and duration of market risks must be substantially identical.

Section 22 • Unit 3

Valuation Approaches: Market, Income & Cost Approaches

The three master valuation approaches, technique selection, and mathematical models (Discounted Cash Flow, Multiples, Matrix Pricing).

The Three Valuation Approaches

Ind AS 113 Paras 61–66
Approach Core Methodology Typical Techniques & Applications
1. Market Approach Uses prices and other relevant information generated by market transactions involving identical or comparable (similar) assets, liabilities, or a group of assets (e.g. a business). • Market Multiples: EV/EBITDA, P/E multiples derived from comparable listed peers (TYK 3).
• Matrix Pricing: Relates debt securities to benchmark quoted securities (coupons, credit ratings) without relying exclusively on direct quotes.
2. Income Approach Converts future amounts (cash flows or income/expenses) to a single current discounted amount reflecting current market expectations. • Discounted Cash Flow (DCF): Free cash flows discounted by Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) + Terminal Value (TYK 4).
• Option Pricing Models: Black-Scholes-Merton, Binomial models.
• Multi-Period Excess Earnings Method (MPEEM): Valuing intangible assets.
3. Cost Approach Reflects the amount that would be required currently to replace the service capacity of an asset (often referred to as Current Replacement Cost). Considers cost to acquire or construct a modern equivalent asset, adjusted for physical obsolescence, functional obsolescence, and economic obsolescence. Widely used for specialised industrial plant & machinery.
Section 23 • Unit 3

The Fair Value Hierarchy: Level 1, Level 2 & Level 3 Inputs

Prioritising valuation inputs: unadjusted active market quotes, observable benchmarks, and internal unobservable estimates.

Overview of Hierarchy Priority

Ind AS 113 Paras 72–75

The hierarchy prioritises the inputs to valuation techniques, NOT the valuation technique itself. A fair value measurement is categorised in its entirety based on the lowest level input that is significant to the entire measurement!

L1
Level 1 Inputs (Highest Priority • Most Reliable) Quoted prices (unadjusted) in active markets for identical assets or liabilities that the entity can access at the measurement date (e.g. shares quoted on NSE/BSE). Used without adjustment whenever available. Exception: If significant event occurs after market close (e.g. regulatory drug rejection announced after close), the quoted price may be adjusted, but the measurement drops to Level 2!
L2
Level 2 Inputs (Observable Inputs other than Level 1 Quotes) Inputs other than quoted prices included in Level 1 that are observable for the asset/liability directly or indirectly for substantially the full term:
  • Quoted prices for similar assets/liabilities in active markets;
  • Quoted prices for identical/similar assets in markets that are not active;
  • Observable interest rates, yield curves, implied volatilities, and credit spreads;
  • Market-corroborated inputs (e.g. deriving an 11-year swap rate by extrapolating a 10-year observable curve).
L3
Level 3 Inputs (Lowest Priority • Unobservable Inputs) Unobservable inputs used to the extent relevant observable inputs are not available. Reflects assumptions market participants would use, including risk adjustments:
  • Entity's internal long-term cash flow projections / business plans for CGU impairment;
  • Non-binding broker quotes;
  • Adjustments to Level 2 observable quotes using significant unobservable entity estimates.

The highest and best use of the land is determined by comparing:

  1. Value under current use: Land as an industrial factory site in combination with other assets (factory buildings, plant, working capital); against
  2. Value under alternative potential use: Land as a vacant site for high-rise residential use, taking into account costs of demolishing factory, zoning conversion costs, and risk premiums.

Decision Tree 1: Policy Change vs Estimate Change vs Error

Ind AS 8 Decision Matrix

Question 1: Does it arise from mathematical mistakes, fraud, or misuse of reliable info that existed? → YES: It is a Prior Period Error → Apply Retrospective Restatement of comparatives and opening equity. Evaluate Third Balance Sheet criteria!

Question 2: Does it involve changing the fundamental measurement base (e.g. FIFO to Weighted Average)? → YES: It is a Change in Accounting Policy → Must be required by Ind AS or result in more relevant info → Apply Retrospective Application!

Decision Tree 2: Adjusting vs Non-Adjusting Event

Ind AS 10 Decision Matrix
Q
Did the condition exist at the balance sheet date?
  • YES: Adjusting Event → Adjust numbers in financial statements (Settlement of prior lawsuit, pre-year-end debtor bankruptcy, subsequent sale showing inventory NRV, rocky foundation, installation bill).
  • NO: Non-Adjusting Event → Do NOT adjust balance sheet. Disclose nature & estimated financial effect if material in notes (Fire, market decline, post-year-end business acquisition).
!
Exceptions to Remember:
  • Going Concern Compromised: Even if caused by post-year-end event → Fundamental change in accounting basis (Adjust everything)!
  • Equity Dividends Declared after year-end: Never a liability at balance sheet date → Disclose in notes only!

Decision Tree 3: Fair Value Hierarchy Determination

Ind AS 113 Decision Matrix
  • Quoted price in active market for identical asset/liability without adjustment? → Level 1 (e.g. listed shares on BSE/NSE).
  • Observable inputs other than Level 1 (similar assets in active markets, identical assets in inactive markets, yield curves, observable spreads)? → Level 2.
  • Unobservable internal projections, management budgets, or non-corroborated broker estimates? → Level 3.
  • Key Exam Rule: If an observable quote is adjusted using a significant unobservable input, the entire measurement falls into Level 3!
