Unit 1 • Indian Accounting Standard 12

Ind AS 12: Income Taxes

Prescribes accounting for income taxes under the Balance Sheet Liability Approach: Current Tax (Part B), 10-Step Deferred Tax Recognition, Measurement & Offsetting (Part C), Practical Applications (Part D), All 15 Disclosures (Part E), Appendix C Uncertain Tax Treatments, and the OECD Pillar Two Amendment.

1.1 Objective & 1.2 Scope (Including Pillar Two Exception)

Balance Sheet Approach • Scope

Historically, "zero income tax" entities claimed accelerated tax benefits (e.g., higher WDV depreciation for tax vs. lower SLM depreciation in books) and recorded zero tax provision in initial years, distorting Profit After Tax when tax depreciation fell below book depreciation in later years. Following periodicity, matching, and accrual conventions, Ind AS 12 accounts for deferred tax using the Balance Sheet Approach (comparing carrying amount vs. tax base) rather than the old P&L timing-difference approach (AS 22).

What "Income Taxes" Includes & Excludes (1.2 Scope):

  • Included: All domestic and foreign taxes based on taxable profits; AND withholding taxes (TDS) payable by a subsidiary, associate, or joint venture on distributions to the reporting entity.
  • Excluded: Methods of accounting for Government Grants (Ind AS 20) or Investment Tax Credits (though Ind AS 12 does govern temporary differences arising from them).
  • Current Loss Carry-back: If an entity incurs a tax loss and carries it back against prior-period profits (where permitted), the recoverable tax benefit is recognized as an asset.
  • P&L vs. Outside P&L: Current and deferred tax are recognized in Profit or Loss except when the underlying transaction is recognized in OCI or directly in Equity (or in a Business Combination).
  • OECD Pillar Two Amendment (Mandatory Exception): Ind AS 12 applies to income taxes arising from tax law enacted/substantively enacted to implement OECD Pillar Two model rules (including Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Taxes / QDMTT). However, as a mandatory exception, an entity shall neither recognise nor disclose information about deferred tax assets and liabilities related to Pillar Two income taxes.
Fundamental Principle of Deferred Tax Recognition: "It is inherent in the recognition of an asset or liability that the reporting entity expects to recover or settle the carrying amount of that asset or liability. If it is probable that recovery or settlement of that carrying amount will make future tax payments larger (smaller) than they would be if such recovery or settlement were to have no tax consequences, this Standard requires an entity to recognise a deferred tax liability (deferred tax asset)."
Example 2 • Indexation Benefit on Land
Land recognized at cost ₹1,00,000. Income-tax gives 10% p.a. indexation if held >1 year: indexed cost = ₹1,10,000 (after 1 yr), ₹1,21,000 (after 2 yrs). Tax rate = 30%. If sold after 2.5 years for ₹1,50,000, tax payable = 30% × (₹1,50,000 − ₹1,21,000) = ₹8,700 vs. ₹15,000 without indexation — a tax saving of ₹6,300 (30% of ₹21,000). Entity recognizes a DTA of ₹6,300 in Year 1.
Note: There are controversial views on indexation of land if the land is held for business use and not intended to be sold in the near future; in such cases it may not be advisable to compute temporary difference.

1.3 Key Definitions & 1.4 Part A: Tax Expense (Tax Income)

Core Terminology
Term Official Definition under Ind AS 12
Accounting ProfitProfit or loss for a period before deducting tax expense.
Taxable Profit (Tax Loss)Profit (loss) for a period, computed as per the Income-tax Act, upon which income taxes are payable (recoverable).
Tax Expense (Tax Income)The aggregate amount included in the determination of P&L for the period in respect of Current Tax + Deferred Tax (can be positive = expense, or negative = income).
Current TaxAmount of income taxes payable (recoverable) in respect of the taxable profit (tax loss) for a period.
Deferred Tax Liabilities (DTL)Amounts of income taxes payable in future periods in respect of taxable temporary differences.
Deferred Tax Assets (DTA)Amounts of income taxes recoverable in future periods in respect of: (i) deductible temporary differences, (ii) carry forward of unused tax losses, and (iii) carry forward of unused tax credits.
Temporary DifferencesDifferences between the carrying amount of an asset or liability in the balance sheet and its tax base. Classified as Taxable (future taxable amounts) or Deductible (future deductible amounts).
Tax BaseThe amount attributed to an asset or liability for tax purposes.
Ind AS 12 vs. Old AS 22 (Approach): AS 22 adopted an Income Statement (P&L) approach based on "timing differences" and "permanent differences" between accounting and taxable income. Ind AS 12 adopts a Balance Sheet approach based on temporary differences between carrying amounts and tax bases of assets/liabilities. Every timing difference is a temporary difference, but temporary differences also include non-timing balance sheet adjustments (e.g., asset revaluations, business combination fair values).

1.5 Part B: Current Tax — Recognition, Measurement & Presentation

Paragraphs 12–14 & 71–73
  • Recognition (1.5.2): Current tax for current and prior periods, to the extent unpaid (excess over advance tax & TDS), is recognized as a Current Tax Liability. If amounts already paid exceed the amount due, the excess is recognized as a Current Tax Asset.
  • Measurement (1.5.3): Measured at the amount expected to be paid to (recovered from) taxation authorities using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the end of the reporting period.
    • Uncertain Tax Positions: Compute based on expected amount to pay (statistical tools / Appendix C); subsequent revisions are changes in accounting estimate under Ind AS 8.
    • Enacted vs. Substantively Enacted in India: Enactment occurs upon Gazette notification after the 6-step Parliamentary process: (1) Finance Bill presented in Lok Sabha → (2) Passed by Lok Sabha → (3) Moves to Rajya Sabha → (4) Discussed in Rajya Sabha → (5) Presidential Assent → (6) Gazette Notification. Because the Finance Bill is typically presented in Feb and enacted in May while financial statements close on 31st March, Ind AS 12 does not prescribe a specific cutoff stage — entities must disclose their accounting policy on "substantive enactment" consistently (and material variations if any).
  • Accounting of Current Tax Effects (1.5.4): Consistent with the underlying transaction (P&L, OCI, or Direct Equity).
  • Offsetting Current Tax Assets & Liabilities (1.5.5): Offset if and only if the entity: (a) has a legally enforceable right to set off the recognized amounts (normally when levied by the same tax authority permitting a single net payment), AND (b) intends either to settle on a net basis, or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously. In CFS, CTA of one group entity offsets CTL of another only if both entities satisfy these two criteria.

1.6 Part C: Deferred Tax — Steps 1 to 4 (Carrying Amount, Tax Base & Classification)

10-Step Methodology

Ind AS 12 prescribes a structured 10-Step Process for deferred tax: (1) Carrying amount → (2) Tax base → (3) Temporary difference → (4) Classify TTD/DTD → (5) Exceptions → (6) Assess DTDs/losses/credits → (7) Tax rate → (8) Calculate & recognise → (9) Accounting (P&L/OCI/Equity) → (10) Offsetting.

Step 1 — Carrying Amount: Balance as per ledger recognized in the balance sheet (net of depreciation, impairment, etc.). (Example 3: P&M ₹1,00,000 − 10% SLM ₹10,000 = ₹90,000).

Step 2 — Tax Base (Four Scenarios):

1
Tax Base of an Asset (Para 7): Amount deductible for tax purposes against any taxable economic benefits flowing when the entity recovers the asset's carrying amount. If those economic benefits are not taxable, Tax Base = Carrying Amount. $$\text{Tax Base (Asset)} = \text{Cost} - \text{Cumulative Tax Deductions / Tax Depreciation}$$
  • Example 4: P&M cost ₹1,00,000; 30% WDV tax depr = ₹30,000 → TB = ₹70,000. Even if revalued in books to ₹1,50,000, TB remains ₹70,000.
  • Example 5: Inventory CA ₹1,00,000 → sale proceeds taxable, cost deductible → TB = ₹1,00,000.
  • Example 6: Asset ₹1,00,000 (50% book depr Y1 = ₹50k; 75% tax ded Y1 = ₹75k) → remaining tax ded Y2 = ₹25,000 → TB = ₹25,000.
  • Example 7: Interest receivable CA ₹100 taxed on cash basis → TB = Nil.
  • Examples 8 vs. 9 (Receivables ₹1,000, Bad Debt Prov ₹50, CA ₹950): General provision (not tax deductible now) → full ₹1,000 deductible on recovery → TB = ₹1,000. Specific provision (deductible immediately in IT) → only ₹950 deductible later → TB = ₹950.
  • Examples 10 & 11: Listed equity shares with tax-exempt capital gains → TB = Carrying Amount. Loan given ₹10,000 (repayment has no tax effect) → TB = ₹10,000.
2
Tax Base of a Liability (Para 8): Carrying amount less any amount deductible for tax purposes in future periods. If non-deductible (e.g., fines/penalties, loan principal), Tax Base = Carrying Amount. $$\text{Tax Base (Liability)} = \text{Carrying Amount} - \text{Future Tax Deductions}$$
  • Examples 12 & 13: Accrued expenses ₹100 deductible on cash basis → TB = Nil. If already deducted for tax → TB = ₹100.
  • Examples 14 & 15: Accrued fines/penalties ₹100 (disallowed) → TB = ₹100 (permanent / non-temporary difference). Loan payable ₹100 → TB = ₹100.
  • Example 16 (Revenue Received in Advance): Tax Base = Carrying Amount − Amount of revenue not taxable in future. Interest received in advance ₹100 already taxed on receipt (cash basis) → TB = 100 − 100 = Nil.
3
Items with a Tax Base but NO Carrying Amount (Examples 17 & 18): Preliminary / incorporation expenses (₹1,00,000 expensed in Year 1 in books, allowed 1/5th over 5 years in tax → Year-1 CA = Nil, Tax Base = ₹80,000) and Public issue expenses written off in Year 1.
4
Items Where Tax Base is Not Apparent (Example 19): E.g., an industrial undertaking (land, building, P&M) where management is unsure between slump sale vs. piecemeal disposal (different tax heads, indexation, LTCG vs. STCG vs. business income). Apply the fundamental principle using judgment and disclose uncertainties if material. Note: In CFS, tax base follows consolidated tax returns if permitted by law, otherwise individual entity tax returns.

Steps 3 & 4 — Computing & Classifying Temporary Differences (Examples 20–34):

Step 5: Recognition Exceptions (Goodwill, IRE & Group Investments)

Paragraphs 15, 21, 22A, 39 & 44
Exception 1 — Initial Recognition of Goodwill in a Business Combination:
  • Situation A1 (Goodwill NOT tax deductible): Tax base of goodwill is Nil → creates a TTD. However, Ind AS 12 prohibits recognizing DTL because goodwill is measured as a residual, so recognizing DTL would increase goodwill, creating a circular computation loop (Example 35: Consideration ₹1,00,000 − Net Assets ₹65,000 = Goodwill ₹35,000; creating ₹10,500 DTL @ 30% pushes GW to ₹45,500, needing ₹13,650 DTL, ad infinitum). Any subsequent impairment reduction in this unrecognised DTL (Example 36: ₹10,000 GW impairment) is also NOT recognized.
  • Situation A2 (Goodwill IS tax deductible): DTL must be recognized.
  • Situation B (Deductible TD on Goodwill): DTA is recognized subject to probability criteria.
Exception 2 — Initial Recognition Exemption (IRE) (Paras 15(b), 22(c), 24):

No DTL or DTA is recognized on initial recognition of an asset or liability in a transaction that satisfies ALL THREE conditions:

  1. It is NOT a business combination; AND
  2. At the time of the transaction, it affects neither accounting profit nor taxable profit (tax loss); AND
  3. At the time of the transaction, it does NOT give rise to equal taxable and deductible temporary differences.

Examples 37 & 38: Buying a foreign vehicle (₹1,00,000) or an intangible license (₹10 Cr) where neither tax depreciation nor capital loss/gain deduction is permitted → Tax Base = Nil, but NO DTL is recognized at initial recognition or as the asset is subsequently depreciated/amortised.
CRITICAL LEASE / DECOMMISSIONING RULE: At lease commencement under Ind AS 116, a lessee recognizes a Right-of-Use (ROU) Asset and an equal Lease Liability, giving rise to equal taxable and deductible temporary differences. Therefore, the IRE does NOT apply — the entity recognizes both the resulting DTL and DTA (see Illustration 3).

Exception 3 — Investments in Subsidiaries, Branches, Associates & Joint Ventures (Paras 39 & 44):

Temporary differences arise from undistributed profits, foreign exchange translation differences, or impairment of associates. Note that CFS temporary differences may differ from SFS temporary differences.

  • For DTL (Taxable TDs — Para 39): Recognize DTL for ALL taxable TDs, EXCEPT if BOTH conditions hold: (i) parent/investor/venturer controls the timing of reversal (e.g., dividend policy), AND (ii) it is probable that it will not reverse in the foreseeable future.
    • Subsidiaries & Branches: Parent controls dividend policy → NO DTL if profits won't be distributed in foreseeable future.
    • Associates: Investor has only significant influence (NO control over dividends) → DTL MUST be recognized (absent an agreement preventing distribution).
    • Joint Ventures: Depends on whether venturer can control profit sharing under the joint arrangement.
  • For DTA (Deductible TDs — Para 44): Recognize DTA only to the extent that it is probable that: (i) the TD will reverse in the foreseeable future, AND (ii) taxable profit will be available.
  • Foreign Currency Non-Monetary Mismatch: If non-monetary assets/liabilities are measured in functional currency while taxable profit/tax base is in another currency, exchange rate changes create temporary differences → deferred tax charged/credited to P&L.

Steps 6 & 7: Assessing DTDs / Unused Losses, Appendix C & Tax Rate Determination

Paragraphs 27–52 & Appendix C

Step 6 — Three-Step Serial Criteria for Recognizing DTA (Same Entity & Same Tax Authority):

  1. Criteria 1 — Existence of Reversing TTDs (Example 40): Match the reversal pattern of Taxable TDs against Deductible TDs. If DTDs cannot be carried forward, DTA is capped at TTDs reversing in the matching years (e.g., ₹4,000 in Y1 + ₹3,000 in Y2 = ₹7,000 out of ₹8,000).
  2. Criteria 2 — Probable Future Taxable Profits (Examples 41–43): Probability (>50%) of sufficient taxable profits (excluding taxable profits caused by future originating DTDs — unpaid statutory liability disallowed in Y2 and reversing in Y3 requires Y3 profit!).
  3. Criteria 3 — Availability of Tax Planning Opportunities: Actions to create/advance taxable profit before a loss/credit expires (e.g., switching interest tax basis from receipt to receivable, deferring deductions, sale-and-leaseback of appreciated assets, switching tax-exempt government bonds to taxable investments).

Unused Tax Losses & Tax Credits (History of Recent Losses): Existence of unused tax losses is strong evidence that future taxable profit may not be available. An entity with a history of recent losses recognizes DTA only to the extent of sufficient TTDs OR convincing other evidence (and must disclose the DTA amount & nature of evidence under Para 82). Consider: (i) reversing TTDs before expiry, (ii) probable taxable profits before expiry, (iii) whether losses arose from identifiable non-recurring causes, and (iv) tax planning opportunities. Reassess unrecognised DTAs at every reporting date!

Illustration 2 H Ltd. • Unused Trading Losses & Depressed Order Book
Question: Directors of H Ltd. want to recognize DTA on ₹250 Cr of unused trading losses at 31 March 20X1, projecting ₹80 Cr profit in FY 20X2 growing 20% annually. However, Q1 20X2 sales orders are at a 5-year low, market is depressed, breakeven is 2+ years away, and tax losses expire after 2 years. Can DTA be recognized?
Solution: No. Forecasts must be based on reasonable and supportable assumptions. The directors' profit forecast contradicts actual sales order data. Since tax losses carry forward for only 2 years and breakeven is not expected within 2 years, it is not probable that sufficient taxable profits will be available before expiry. Hence, no DTA can be recognized.
Appendix C to Ind AS 12 — Uncertainty over Income Tax Treatments (IFRIC 23):
  • Zero Detection Risk Assumption: Assume the taxation authority will examine all amounts it has a right to examine and will have full knowledge of all related information.
  • If Probable (> 50%) that Tax Authority Will Accept: Measure current and deferred tax consistently with the tax filing treatment.
  • If NOT Probable: Reflect uncertainty using whichever method best predicts resolution: (a) Most Likely Amount (best for binary/concentrated outcomes) or (b) Expected Value (probability-weighted sum, best for wide range of outcomes).
  • Consistency: Make consistent judgments for both current tax and deferred tax if an uncertain treatment affects both.

Step 7 — Determining the Applicable Tax Rate (Law):

  • Use enacted or substantively enacted rates expected to apply when the asset is realised or liability is settled.
  • Manner of Recovery (Para 51 & Examples 44–45): Reflect expected recovery through use vs. sale (e.g., Asset CA ₹100, TB ₹60: DTL = ₹8 if sold @ 20% vs. ₹12 if used @ 30%; Revalued asset Cost ₹100, CA ₹80 → ₹150, TB ₹70, gain above cost tax-exempt: DTL = ₹24 @ 30% on ₹80 if used, vs. ₹9 @ 30% on ₹30 recaptured depr if sold immediately).
  • Non-Depreciable Revalued Assets (Ind AS 16 Para 31 — e.g., Revalued Land): DTL/DTA MUST be measured based on tax consequences of recovery through sale, regardless of carrying amount basis.
  • Levels of Taxable Income: When slab/graduated rates apply, use average rates expected to apply when temporary differences reverse.
  • Distribution of Dividends (Example 46): Where tax rates depend on whether profits are distributed (e.g., 50% undistributed vs. 35% distributed with 15% refund), measure current & deferred tax at the undistributed profit rate (50%). Recognize the 15% tax refund (₹1,500 on ₹10,000 dividend) only when the dividend is recognized as a liability.

Steps 8–10: Calculation, Accounting Destination, Leases & Offsetting

Paragraphs 53–76
  • Step 8 — Calculate & Review: Deferred Tax = Temporary Difference × Applicable Rate. Discounting is STRICTLY PROHIBITED (Para 53). Review DTA carrying amount at each reporting date; write down if no longer probable and reverse write-down if it becomes probable again.
  • Step 9 — Accounting of Deferred Tax (P&L vs. OCI vs. Equity):
    • P&L: Normal operating items, depreciation, warranties, provisions. Also changes in DTA/DTL due to tax rate changes, DTA reassessment, or change in manner of recovery (unless the underlying item was previously recognized outside P&L).
    • OCI: Ind AS 16 PPE Revaluation, Ind AS 19 Defined Benefit Remeasurements, Ind AS 109 FVTOCI assets, Ind AS 21 Foreign Operation FCTR. Also tax-basis revaluations linked to accounting revaluations.
    • Directly in Equity: Ind AS 8 retrospective policy changes/error corrections, Ind AS 32 equity issue transaction costs / compound instrument equity component, Share-based payment excess tax deductions, Dividend withholding tax paid on behalf of shareholders.
    • Pro-rata Allocation: If tracing tax changes to P&L vs. OCI/Equity is impracticable, use a reasonable pro-rata allocation.
    • Revaluation Transfer: Any annual/disposal transfer from Revaluation Surplus to Retained Earnings under Ind AS 16 is made net of related deferred tax.
  • Step 10 — Offsetting DTA & DTL (Para 74): Offset if and only if: (a) entity has a legally enforceable right to set off current tax assets against current tax liabilities, AND (b) DTA and DTL relate to income taxes levied by the same taxation authority on either (i) the same taxable entity, or (ii) different taxable entities intending net/simultaneous settlement in each significant future reversal period.

Question: On 1 April 20X1, S Ltd. leased a machine for 5 years. PV of lease liability = ₹120 Cr (@ 8% discount rate) with corresponding ROU Asset of ₹120 Cr (SLM depreciation over 5 years). Annual lease rental = ₹30 Cr paid starting 31 March 20X2. Tax allows deduction on actual rent payment. Tax rate = 30%. Compute deferred tax at 31 March 20X2.
Solution:
  • ROU Asset at 31 March 20X2 = ₹120 Cr − (₹120 Cr / 5) = ₹96.00 Cr (Dr.); Tax base = Nil → TTD = ₹96.00 Cr.

1.7 Part D: Practical Application (Business Combinations, Share-Based Payments & Change in Tax Status)

Paragraphs 66–68C & Appendix A

1.7.1 Deferred Tax in a Business Combination:

  • Identifiable assets/liabilities are recognized at Ind AS 103 fair values; resulting DTAs/DTLs affect Goodwill or Bargain Purchase Gain (except no DTL on initial goodwill).
  • Acquirer's Own Pre-acquisition DTA: If the combination makes it probable (or improbable) that the acquirer will recover its own previously unrecognised DTA, recognize the change in P&L in the combination period — do NOT include in Goodwill/BPG.
  • Acquiree's Unrecognised DTA Realised Post-Acquisition:
    • Recognised within measurement period due to new info on acquisition-date facts → reduce Goodwill (if Goodwill hits zero, excess to OCI/Capital Reserve per Ind AS 103).
    • All other subsequently realised acquired tax benefits → recognized in Profit or Loss (or outside P&L if required).

Question: On 1 April 20X1, A Ltd. acquired 12 Cr shares (80% stake) in B Ltd. for ₹25 Cr cash. NCI is measured at fair value (share price ₹2.00). 1.7.2 Current & Deferred Tax from Share-Based Payment Transactions (Ind AS 102):

  • Accounting expense is recognized over the vesting period (grant-date fair value), whereas tax deduction often occurs only at exercise based on intrinsic value at exercise date.
  • At each period-end, estimate the future tax deduction based on the entity's share price at the end of the period (carrying amount = Nil → Tax Base = estimated future deduction for service received to date → DTA).
  • Equity Split Rule (Para 68C): If the tax deduction (or estimated future tax deduction) exceeds the related cumulative remuneration expense, the excess current/deferred tax is recognized directly in Equity (not P&L).

1.7.3 Change in Tax Status of an Entity or its Shareholders (Appendix A):

E.g., public listing (IPO), equity restructuring, or controlling shareholder moving abroad → may alter tax rates or incentives. Current and deferred tax consequences are recognized in Profit or Loss, unless they relate to transactions/events recognized in Equity (credited/charged directly to Equity) or OCI (recognized in OCI).

📝 Ind AS 12 • Question 1.1 (Tax Base of Receivables)
An entity following mercantile accounting has gross trade receivables of ₹2,000. It creates a general bad debt allowance of ₹150 (carrying amount = ₹1,850). 2.4.4 Net Investment in a Foreign Operation & 2.4.5 Change in Functional Currency

A monetary item receivable from or payable to a foreign operation (subsidiary, associate, JV) forms part of the net investment if settlement is neither planned nor likely to occur in the foreseeable future (excludes trade receivables/payables!).

  • On-Demand Loans vs. Fixed-Maturity Loans: A loan repayable on demand qualifies if there is demonstrably no intent to demand repayment (continuously rolled over). Conversely, a 10–15 year fixed-maturity loan does not automatically qualify unless management documents its intention to renew at maturity via auditable evidence (e.g., board minutes).
  • SFS vs. CFS Recognition:
    • Separate FS (SFS) / Individual FS: Recognized in Profit or Loss (in foreign op's books if denominated in parent's FC; in parent's books if in foreign op's FC; in both if in a third currency).
    • Consolidated FS (CFS): Initially recognized in OCI (FCTR) and reclassified to P&L upon disposal of the net investment.
  • Fellow Subsidiary Routing (Para 15A — Illus. 6): Even if Parent P routes the permanent loan to Sub S through another Sub T, exchange differences in P's CFS go to OCI / FCTR regardless of loan currency.

2.4.5 Change in Functional Currency vs. Change in Presentation Currency:
  • Change in Functional Currency (Prospective): Permitted ONLY if underlying transactions/conditions change. Accounted prospectively from the date of change: translate all items into the new functional currency at the date-of-change spot rate (translated non-monetary amounts become new historical cost). Existing FCTR in OCI stays until disposal; Ind AS 101 equity balances continue per prior policy.
  • Change in Presentation Currency (Retrospective!): Choice of presentation currency is an accounting policy → any change is applied retrospectively under Ind AS 8 (comparatives restated as if the new presentation currency had always been used), unless impracticable.

2.4.6 Lack of Exchangeability (MCA Amendment Rules, 2025 — G.S.R. 291(E) dated 7 May 2025)

Effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 April 2025 (aligning Ind AS 21 with IAS 21), this amendment prescribes a mandatory Two-Step Approach when one currency cannot be exchanged into another at the measurement date.

Step I: Assessing Whether a Currency is Exchangeable (5 Mandatory Factors):

A currency is exchangeable when an entity can obtain the other currency within a time frame allowing for normal administrative delay through a market/exchange mechanism creating enforceable rights and obligations. Note: Currency PC may lack exchangeability into LC even if LC is exchangeable into PC!

  1. Time Frame: Normal administrative delays (public holidays, legal/regulatory processing) do NOT indicate lack of exchangeability.
  2. Ability vs. Intention: Consider the entity's ability (directly or indirectly via a third currency FC) to obtain the currency, NOT its intent/decision to do so.
  3. Markets or Exchange Mechanisms: Consider ONLY mechanisms creating enforceable rights and obligations (exclude black/unofficial unenforceable markets for Step I).
  4. Purpose: Assess for the specific purpose (different rates/restrictions may apply for dividends vs. imports vs. debt settlement).
  5. Only Insignificant Amounts Obtainable: A currency is NOT exchangeable if the entity can obtain no more than an insignificant amount compared to the total aggregated amount required for that purpose.

Step II: Estimating the Spot Exchange Rate (When Not Exchangeable): Objective is to reflect the rate at which an orderly exchange transaction would take place at the measurement date between market participants under prevailing economic conditions, using either:

  • (a) An Observable Exchange Rate Without Adjustment: Assessed across 4 factors (whether several observable rates exist, purpose, nature, update frequency). Examples: spot rate for another purpose, or the first subsequent exchange rate after exchangeability is restored (considering time lag & inflation rates).
  • (b) Another Estimation Technique: Using any observable rate (even from mechanisms without enforceable rights) and adjusting it as necessary (e.g., inflation/PPP differentials).

Mandatory Disclosures When a Currency is Not Exchangeable:

  • General (6 items): (a) Currency & description of restrictions; (b) Affected transactions; (c) Carrying amount of affected assets/liabilities; (d) Spot rates used and whether unadjusted observable vs. estimated; (e) Description of estimation technique + qualitative/quantitative inputs & assumptions; (f) Qualitative info on each risk exposed & nature/carrying amount of exposed assets/liabilities.
  • Foreign Operation Additional (3 items): (a) Name, type (sub/JO/JV/assoc/branch), and principal place of business; (b) Summarised financial information; (c) Nature & terms of contractual financial support arrangements & loss exposures.

📝 Ind AS 21 • Question 2.2 (Monetary vs Non-Monetary & Impairment)
An Indian company (functional currency INR) purchases specialized equipment for €1,00,000 when 1€ = ₹80 (cost = ₹80,00,000). At year-end, the equipment's recoverable amount is €90,000 and the closing rate is 1€ = ₹92. Ignore depreciation. What impairment loss should be recognized in INR?
A. ₹9,20,000 (€10,000 × ₹92)
B. ₹8,00,000 (€10,000 × ₹80)
C. Nil, because Recoverable Amount in INR (₹82,80,000) exceeds Carrying Cost in INR (₹80,00,000)
D. Revalue equipment upward by ₹2,80,000 in P&L
Correct Answer: C
Under the two-amount comparison rule (Para 25 & Example 1), Carrying Cost at historical rate = ₹80,00,000; Recoverable Amount at valuation-date rate (€90,000 × ₹92) = ₹82,80,000. Lower of the two is ₹80,00,000 → zero impairment loss in INR!
📝 Ind AS 21 • Question 2.3 (Which Item is Monetary?)
Under Ind AS 21, which of the following foreign-currency items is a MONETARY item that must be retranslated at the closing rate at each reporting date?
A. Capital advance paid to a German machinery supplier
B. Right-of-Use (ROU) Asset recognized under Ind AS 116
C. Lease Liability denominated in USD under Ind AS 116
D. Advance revenue received from an overseas customer
Correct Answer: C
Under Section 2.4.2 of Study Material, Lease Liabilities require paying a fixed/determinable number of currency units → Monetary item (retranslated at closing rate to P&L). ROU assets, capital advances, and deferred revenue are all non-monetary items.
📝 Ind AS 21 • Question 2.4 (Net Investment in Foreign Operation)
Parent P (INR) extends a 12-year USD loan to its US Subsidiary S (USD). Management has NOT documented any intention to renew the loan upon maturity. Where are the exchange differences on this loan recognized in P's Separate FS (SFS) and Consolidated FS (CFS)?
A. P&L in SFS AND P&L in CFS (does not qualify as net investment)
B. P&L in SFS and OCI (FCTR) in CFS
C. OCI (FCTR) in both SFS and CFS
D. Eliminated completely in CFS
Correct Answer: A
Per Section 2.4.4.3, a long-term loan with a fixed maturity (e.g., 10–15 years) does NOT automatically qualify as part of net investment unless management documents its intention to renew (e.g., via board minutes). Without intent to renew, settlement is planned at maturity → P&L in both SFS and CFS.
📝 Ind AS 21 • Question 2.5 (Change in Functional vs Presentation Currency)
On 1 October 20X2, an entity's primary economic environment shifts permanently, requiring a change in functional currency from INR to USD, and management simultaneously changes its presentation currency from INR to USD. How are both changes accounted for?
A. Both changes are accounted for prospectively from 1 October 20X2

Under Para 35–37 & Section 2.4.5, a change in functional currency is always applied prospectively from the date of change. However, choosing a presentation currency is an accounting policy, so changing it is applied retrospectively under Ind AS 8 (unless impracticable).
📝 Ind AS 21 • Question 2.6 (2025 Lack of Exchangeability Amendment)
Under the MCA 2025 Amendment to Ind AS 21 on "Lack of Exchangeability", when assessing Step I (whether currency LC is exchangeable into currency PC), which statement is INCORRECT?
A. Normal administrative delays (e.g., bank holidays) do not mean exchangeability is lacking
B. Only markets/mechanisms creating enforceable rights and obligations are considered in Step I

Statement C is FALSE: Under Step I Factor 2, an entity considers its ability (direct or indirect) to obtain the other currency, NOT its intention or decision to do so.
📝 Ind AS 21 • Question 2.7 (Disposal vs Partial Disposal FCTR Recycling)
Parent P holds 100% of Foreign Subsidiary S (FCTR balance = ₹100 Cr) and 40% of Foreign Associate A (FCTR balance = ₹40 Cr). During the year, P sells 15% of S (retaining 85% control) and sells 10% of A (retaining 30% significant influence). What total FCTR is reclassified to Consolidated Profit or Loss?
A. ₹25 Cr (₹15 Cr from S + ₹10 Cr from A)

Per Para 48C & Examples 4–5: Partial disposal of a subsidiary without loss of control reattributes 15% of S's FCTR (₹15 Cr) to NCI inside equity (NO P&L recycling). Partial disposal of an associate recycles the proportionate 10/40 = 25% of ₹40 Cr = ₹10 Cr to Profit or Loss.
📝 Ind AS 21 • Question 2.8 (Intra-Group Dividends & Balances)
Foreign Subsidiary S (USD) declares a dividend of USD 1,00,000 to Parent P (INR) on 15 March 20X2 (spot rate ₹80/$). Payment is received on 10 April 20X2 (spot rate ₹82/$). How is the ₹2,00,000 exchange gain in FY 20X2-X3 treated in P's Consolidated Financial Statements?
A. Eliminated completely on consolidation as an intra-group item
B. Transferred to OCI (FCTR) in CFS
C. Recognized and retained in Consolidated Profit or Loss
D. Adjusted against Retained Earnings directly
Correct Answer: C
Per Section 2.8.1, once a dividend is declared, the parent has a functional currency exposure on a monetary receivable. The exchange difference between declaration date and receipt date is recognized in P&L and remains in Consolidated Profit or Loss (never eliminated, never OCI).
📝 Ind AS 21 • Question 2.9 (Goodwill & Reporting Date Gap)
Parent P (INR) acquires a US Subsidiary S (USD) generating USD 10 million of Goodwill. At subsequent reporting dates, at what exchange rate is the USD 10 million Goodwill translated into INR in the Consolidated Balance Sheet?

Under Para 47 (Section 2.9), goodwill and fair value adjustments arising on acquiring a foreign operation are treated as assets/liabilities of the foreign operation and are translated at the closing rate at each balance sheet date, with translation differences flowing to OCI (FCTR).

TYK Question 2 Perpetual Debt as Net Investment in Foreign Operation
Question: Entity A (INR) holds Euro perpetual debt (6% annual interest, no maturity, no call/put option) issued by its foreign operation Entity B (EUR). Can it be treated as part of A's net investment in B under Para 15?
