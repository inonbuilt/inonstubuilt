Unit 1 • Pervasive Tax Accounting

Ind AS 12: Income Taxes

Prescribes the accounting treatment for income taxes under the balance sheet liability approach, defining the tax base of assets and liabilities, temporary differences, deferred tax assets (DTA) and liabilities (DTL), recognition exemptions (IRE & Goodwill), business combinations, and Appendix C on uncertain tax treatments.

1. The Balance Sheet Liability Approach

Ind AS 12 vs Old AS 22

Under Indian Accounting Standards, Ind AS 12 replaces the old income statement approach (which focused on "timing differences" between accounting profit and taxable income) with the Balance Sheet Liability Approach, which focuses on Temporary Differences between the carrying amount of an asset or liability in the balance sheet and its tax base.

Feature Old AS 22 (Income Statement Approach) Ind AS 12 (Balance Sheet Approach)
Focus Statement of Profit and Loss (timing differences). Balance Sheet (temporary differences).
Core Concept Differences originating in one period and reversing in one or more subsequent periods in P&L. Differences between the Carrying Amount of an asset/liability and its Tax Base.
Coverage Failed to capture balance sheet adjustments not passing through P&L (e.g. revaluations, business combinations). Captures all balance sheet items: P&L, Other Comprehensive Income (OCI), and directly in Equity!
Discounting Prohibited. STRICTLY PROHIBITED (Paragraph 53: DTA and DTL shall not be discounted).

2. Tax Base of Assets and Liabilities

Paragraphs 7–11

Definition of Tax Base (Paragraph 5): The tax base of an asset or liability is the amount attributed to that asset or liability for tax purposes.

A
Tax Base of an Asset (Paragraph 7):
The amount that will be deductible for tax purposes against any taxable economic benefits that will flow to an entity when it recovers the carrying amount of the asset. $$\text{Tax Base of Asset} = \text{Cost} - \text{Cumulative Tax Deductions / Tax Depreciation}$$ Rule: If those economic benefits will not be taxable, the tax base of the asset is equal to its carrying amount (resulting in zero temporary difference).
B
Tax Base of a Liability (Paragraph 8):
The carrying amount of the liability, less any amount that will be deductible for tax purposes in respect of that liability in future periods. $$\text{Tax Base of Liability} = \text{Carrying Amount} - \text{Future Tax Deductions}$$ Revenue Received in Advance: The tax base of the resulting liability is its carrying amount, less any amount of the revenue that will not be taxable in future periods (e.g. if tax is paid on receipt basis, Tax Base = Carrying Amount - Carrying Amount = Nil).

3. Taxable Temporary Differences (DTL) vs Deductible Differences (DTA)

The Core Tax Equations
Item Balance Sheet Condition Tax Impact in Future Periods Deferred Tax Classification
Asset $$\text{Carrying Amount} > \text{Tax Base}$$ Taxable economic benefits exceed tax deductions → Future Taxable Amount Deferred Tax Liability (DTL)
Asset $$\text{Carrying Amount} < \text{Tax Base}$$ Tax deductions exceed taxable economic benefits → Future Deductible Amount Deferred Tax Asset (DTA)
Liability $$\text{Carrying Amount} > \text{Tax Base}$$ Liability will be deductible when settled (e.g. provision) → Future Deductible Amount Deferred Tax Asset (DTA)
Liability $$\text{Carrying Amount} < \text{Tax Base}$$ Settlement will generate future taxable income → Future Taxable Amount Deferred Tax Liability (DTL)
Deferred Tax Mathematical Formulation
\text{Temporary Difference} = \text{Carrying Amount} - \text{Tax Base} \\[6pt] \text{Deferred Tax} = \text{Temporary Difference} \times \text{Applicable Enacted / Substantively Enacted Tax Rate}
Measured at the tax rates expected to apply when the asset is realised or liability is settled. No discounting allowed!

4. Recognition Exceptions: Goodwill & Initial Recognition Exemption (IRE)

Paragraphs 15 • 21 • 39
Strict Exceptions to Recognizing Deferred Tax Liabilities (Paragraph 15):

A deferred tax liability shall be recognized for all taxable temporary differences, EXCEPT to the extent that the deferred tax liability arises from:

  1. The Initial Recognition of Goodwill: Goodwill is measured as a residual. If a DTL were recognized on goodwill, it would increase the carrying amount of goodwill, creating an infinite circular accounting loop! Hence, no DTL is recognized on initial recognition of goodwill.
  2. The Initial Recognition Exemption (IRE): The initial recognition of an asset or liability in a transaction which:
    • Is NOT a business combination; AND
    • At the time of the transaction, affects neither accounting profit nor taxable profit (tax loss).
    Example: Acquisition of an asset where cost is not fully deductible immediately and does not qualify for tax allowances (e.g., land with zero tax depreciation).
  3. Investments in Subsidiaries, Branches, Associates and Joint Ventures (Paragraph 39):
    A parent/investor shall NOT recognize a DTL on taxable temporary differences (e.g. undistributed profits) if BOTH conditions are met:
    • The parent/investor is able to control the timing of the reversal of the temporary difference (e.g. controls dividend policy in a subsidiary); and
    • It is probable that the temporary difference will not reverse in the foreseeable future.
    Contrast with Associates: In an associate, the investor does NOT control dividend policy; therefore, a DTL MUST be recognized on undistributed profits of associates!

5. Recognition of DTA for Unused Tax Losses & MAT Credit

Paragraphs 34–36

A deferred tax asset shall be recognized for the carryforward of unused tax losses and unused tax credits (such as Minimum Alternate Tax - MAT credit under Section 115JB) to the extent that it is probable that future taxable profit will be available against which the unused tax losses and unused tax credits can be utilised.

Strict Standard of Prudence for Loss-Making Entities (Paragraph 35):

When an entity has a history of recent losses, the entity recognizes a deferred tax asset arising from unused tax losses or tax credits ONLY to the extent that:

  1. The entity has sufficient taxable temporary differences relating to the same taxation authority and the same taxable entity, which are expected to reverse in the same period as the tax loss or in periods into which the tax loss can be carried back/forward; OR
  2. There is other convincing evidence that sufficient taxable profit will be available against which the unused tax losses or unused tax credits can be utilised by the entity.

Manner of Recovery (Paragraph 51):

The measurement of deferred tax liabilities and deferred tax assets shall reflect the tax consequences that would follow from the manner in which the entity expects, at the end of the reporting period, to recover or settle the carrying amount of its assets and liabilities (e.g. recovery through use vs recovery through sale, affecting indexation benefits and capital gains tax rates vs normal business income tax rates).

6. Tax Allocation: P&L, OCI and Directly in Equity

Paragraphs 58–68

Current and deferred tax shall be recognized as income or an expense and included in profit or loss for the period, except to the extent that the tax arises from:

Profit or Loss Normal operating transactions, accelerated tax depreciation, provisions, warranty accruals. Recognized in Profit or Loss as Tax Expense.

7. Appendix C to Ind AS 12: Uncertainty over Income Tax Treatments

IFRIC 23 Equivalent

Appendix C clarifies how to apply the recognition and measurement requirements in Ind AS 12 when there is uncertainty over income tax treatments.

The Detection Risk Axiom:

In assessing whether and how an uncertain tax treatment affects the determination of taxable profit, tax bases, unused tax losses, and tax rates, an entity shall assume that a taxation authority will examine amounts it has a right to examine and will have FULL KNOWLEDGE OF ALL RELATED INFORMATION when making those examinations! Detection risk is strictly assumed to be ZERO.

1
Is it Probable the Tax Authority Will Accept the Treatment?
  • If Probable (> 50%): The entity shall determine its taxable profit (tax loss), tax bases, unused tax losses, or tax rates consistently with the tax treatment used or planned to be used in its income tax filings.
  • If NOT Probable: The entity shall reflect the effect of uncertainty for each uncertain tax treatment by using either of the following methods, whichever best predicts the resolution of the uncertainty:
    1. The Most Likely Amount: The single most likely amount in a range of possible outcomes (best for binary outcomes).
    2. The Expected Value: The sum of the probability-weighted amounts in a range of possible outcomes (best for large range of outcomes).

TYK Question 1 Tax Base & DTL Calculation
Question: An asset which cost ₹150 has a carrying amount of ₹100. Cumulative depreciation for tax purposes is ₹90 and tax rate is 25%. Calculate the tax base and corresponding deferred tax asset or liability.
Solution:
  1. Tax Base of the Asset: $$\text{Tax Base} = \text{Cost (₹150)} - \text{Cumulative Tax Depreciation (₹90)} = \mathbf{₹60}$$
  2. Carrying Amount = ₹100. Tax Base = ₹60.
  3. Since Carrying Amount > Tax Base, there is a Taxable Temporary Difference (TTD): $$\text{TTD} = ₹100 - ₹60 = \mathbf{₹40}$$
  4. Deferred Tax Liability (DTL): $$\text{DTL} = ₹40 \times 25\% = \mathbf{₹10}$$ The entity recognizes a DTL of ₹10 representing the future taxes it will pay when it recovers the carrying amount of ₹100.
TYK Question 2 100% Subsidiary Undistributed Profits DTL Exemption
Question: On 1 April 20X1, ABC Ltd. acquired 100% of XYZ Ltd. for ₹4,373 crore. By 31 March 20X5, XYZ Ltd. made undistributed profits of ₹5 crore. Tax base of investment in XYZ Ltd. is original cost of ₹4,373 crore. Show deferred tax treatment.
Solution:

A taxable temporary difference of ₹5 crore exists between the carrying amount of the investment (₹4,378 crore) and its tax base (₹4,373 crore).

Under Paragraph 39 of Ind AS 12, an entity shall NOT recognize a deferred tax liability for taxable temporary differences associated with investments in subsidiaries if:

  1. The parent is able to control the timing of the reversal of the temporary difference; and
  2. It is probable that the temporary difference will not reverse in the foreseeable future.

Since ABC Ltd. owns 100% of XYZ Ltd., it completely controls the dividend policy of the subsidiary. Therefore, ABC Ltd. controls the reversal of this difference, and NO deferred tax liability is provided unless ABC Ltd. plans to distribute dividends or sell the subsidiary in the foreseeable future.

TYK Question 3 30% Associate Undistributed Profits DTL Provision
Question: ABC Ltd. acquired 30% of PQR Ltd. on 1 January 20X1 for ₹1,000 crore. By 31 March 20X5, PQR Ltd. made undistributed profits of ₹50 crore (ABC Ltd.'s share). Tax base is original cost of ₹1,000 crore. Show deferred tax treatment.
Solution:

A taxable temporary difference of ₹50 crore exists between the carrying amount under equity method (₹1,050 crore) and its tax base (₹1,000 crore).

Unlike a subsidiary, an investor in an associate (having significant influence but not control) is NOT in a position to control the dividend policy of the associate. Therefore, the investor cannot control the timing of the reversal of the temporary difference.

Consequently, the exception in Paragraph 39 does NOT apply, and ABC Ltd. MUST recognize a Deferred Tax Liability on the temporary difference of ₹50 crore.

TYK Question 7 Consolidated Group Deferred Tax: Losses, Provisions, R&D, Loan
Question: Consolidated group X Ltd. (tax rate 20%) has events on 31 March 20X2:
  1. Subsidiary Y Ltd. tax loss ₹30,00,000 (cannot transfer to group, no future profits expected in Y Ltd.).
  2. Provision for closure costs ₹20,00,000 (deductible on payment). X Ltd. has future profits > ₹20,00,000 and reversing TTDs > ₹20,00,000.
  3. Capitalised development costs under Ind AS 38: ₹16,00,000 on 1 April 20X1 (amortisation begins 1 Jan 20X2 over 5 years). Tax deduction 100% taken in 20X1-20X2.
  4. Zero-coupon 3-year loan of ₹1,00,00,000 on 1 April 20X1, issue costs ₹2,00,000 (deducted immediately for tax). Effective rate 10%, repayment ₹1,30,43,800 on 31 March 20X4 (tax deduction of ₹30,43,800 upon repayment).
Explain and compute deferred tax impacts on consolidated balance sheet.
Solution:

(i) Subsidiary Y Ltd. Tax Loss (₹30,00,000):

Since tax law does not permit loss transfer to group companies and Y Ltd. is not expected to generate future taxable profits, no DTA can be recognized on Y Ltd.'s tax loss of ₹30,00,000 (Paragraph 34).

(ii) Closure Provision (₹20,00,000):

Carrying amount = ₹20,00,000; Tax base = Nil (deductible when paid). Deductible Temporary Difference = ₹20,00,000. Because X Ltd. expects sufficient future taxable profits and has reversing TTDs, a Deferred Tax Asset of ₹4,00,000 (₹20,00,000 × 20%) is recognized.

(iii) Capitalised Development Costs (Ind AS 38):

  • Cost = ₹16,00,000. Amortisation for 3 months (Jan–March 20X2) = $₹16,00,000 \times \frac{3}{60} = ₹80,000$.
  • Carrying amount at 31 March 20X2 = ₹16,00,000 - ₹80,000 = ₹15,20,000.
  • Tax Base = Nil (fully deducted in tax return).
  • Taxable Temporary Difference = ₹15,20,000.
  • Deferred Tax Liability = ₹15,20,000 × 20% = ₹3,04,000 recognized.

(iv) Zero-Coupon Loan (Amortised Cost under Ind AS 109):

  • Initial carrying amount = ₹1,00,00,000 - ₹2,00,000 = ₹98,00,000.
  • Finance cost for year 1 at 10% EIR = $₹98,00,000 \times 10\% = ₹9,80,000$.
  • Carrying amount at 31 March 20X2 = ₹98,00,000 + ₹9,80,000 = ₹1,07,80,000.
  • Tax Base = Nominal liability ₹1,00,00,000 (tax deduction of ₹30,43,800 is claimable only on repayment).
  • Because Carrying Amount (₹1,07,80,000) > Tax Base (₹1,00,00,000), there is a deductible difference on the liability of ₹7,80,000 → Deferred Tax Asset of ₹1,56,000 (₹7,80,000 × 20%).
Unit 2 • Multicurrency & Consolidation Accounting

Ind AS 21: The Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates

Prescribes how to incorporate foreign currency transactions and foreign operations into financial statements and how to translate financial statements into a presentation currency. Covers the functional currency framework, monetary vs. non-monetary valuation, net investment accounting, FCTR recycling, Appendix B advance consideration, and the MCA 2025 amendment on lack of exchangeability.

1. Architectural Framework, Scope & Core Definitions

An entity may carry on foreign activities in two distinct ways: it may have transactions in foreign currencies, or it may have foreign operations (subsidiaries, branches, associates, or joint ventures). Furthermore, an entity may present its financial statements in a currency other than its functional currency. Ind AS 21 provides the universal accounting framework governing both aspects.

Scope Exclusions under Ind AS 21:
  • Derivative Transactions & Hedging: Foreign currency derivatives and hedge accounting are governed strictly by Ind AS 109: Financial Instruments.
  • Cash Flow Statement Translation: Presentation in a presentation currency of cash flows arising from transactions in a foreign currency is covered by Ind AS 7: Statement of Cash Flows.
  • Long-Term Foreign Currency Monetary Items (Previous GAAP Exemption): Ind AS 21 does not apply to long-term foreign currency monetary items recognized in the financial statements before the beginning of the first Ind AS reporting period if the entity elected to continue the accounting policy under Paragraph 46/46A of AS 11 as permitted by Ind AS 101.
Standard Term Official Ind AS 21 Definition Exam & Practical Significance
Functional Currency The currency of the primary economic environment in which the entity operates (Paragraph 8). Governs how transactions are initially recorded and subsequently remeasured in individual accounts. Every entity has exactly one functional currency.
Foreign Currency A currency other than the functional currency of the entity. Any transaction denominated in a currency other than the entity's functional currency is a foreign currency transaction subject to Ind AS 21.
Presentation Currency The currency in which the financial statements are presented. An entity may choose any currency as its presentation currency. If presentation currency differs from functional currency, full translation procedures apply.
Foreign Operation A subsidiary, associate, joint venture, or branch of a reporting entity whose activities are based or conducted in a country or currency other than those of the reporting entity. Triggers consolidation translation procedures, FCTR creation in OCI, and eventual recycling upon disposal.
Closing Rate The spot exchange rate at the end of the reporting period. Used to translate monetary assets/liabilities and foreign operation balance sheets at the reporting date.
Monetary Items Units of currency held and assets and liabilities to be received or paid in a fixed or determinable number of units of currency. Must be retranslated at each closing date; resulting exchange differences must be recognized in Profit or Loss.
Non-Monetary Items Items that lack a right to receive or an obligation to deliver a fixed or determinable number of units of currency. Carried at historical cost remain at transaction spot rate; carried at fair value are translated at the rate when fair value was determined.
Net Investment in a Foreign Operation The amount of the reporting entity's interest in the net assets of that operation, including long-term loans for which settlement is neither planned nor likely to occur in the foreseeable future. Exchange differences in CFS are recognized in Other Comprehensive Income (OCI) and accumulated in FCTR until disposal.

2. Determination of Functional Currency (Paragraphs 9–12)

The primary economic environment in which an entity operates is normally the one in which it primarily generates and expends cash. Management must evaluate the hierarchical criteria laid down in Paragraphs 9 to 11 to identify its functional currency.

Category Specific Indicators Hierarchy & Decision Rule
Primary Indicators (Paragraph 9)
  • Sales Price Influence: The currency that mainly influences sales prices for goods and services (often the currency in which sales prices are denominated and settled); AND
  • Competitive & Regulatory Country: The currency of the country whose competitive forces and regulations mainly determine the sales prices of its goods and services.
  • Cost Influence: The currency that mainly influences labour, material, and other costs of providing goods or services (often the currency in which such costs are denominated and settled).
 Absolute Priority
Primary indicators must be considered first. If primary indicators point clearly to a currency, that currency must be adopted as the functional currency.
Secondary Indicators (Paragraph 10)
  • Financing Funds: The currency in which funds from financing activities (issuing debt and equity instruments) are generated.
  • Operating Receipts Retention: The currency in which receipts from operating activities are usually retained.
 Supporting Evidence
Considered only when primary indicators are mixed, contradictory, or inconclusive.
Foreign Operation Autonomy Indicators (Paragraph 11)
  • Autonomy vs. Extension: Whether activities are carried out with significant degree of autonomy or as a mere extension / conduit of the parent.
  • Volume of Intercompany Transactions: Whether transactions with the reporting entity are a high or low proportion of activities.
  • Direct Cash Flow Remittance: Whether cash flows directly affect the parent's cash flows and are readily available for immediate remittance.
  • Financial Self-Sufficiency: Whether cash flows are sufficient to service existing and normally expected debt obligations without parent support.
 Subsidiary / Branch Test
Determines whether the foreign operation has its own independent functional currency or shares the parent's functional currency.
Management Judgement in Mixed Indicators: When indicators are mixed and the functional currency is not obvious, management must apply judgement to select the currency that most faithfully represents the economic results of underlying transactions. Priority must always be given to Primary Indicators before considering secondary indicators.
Examiner's Rule of Thumb • Functional Currency
"Location never dictates functional currency! Even if a company is incorporated in India, if its revenues are set by global USD prices and its goods are imported in USD, Indian Rupee is NOT automatic. Check sales price influence and cost influence first. If costs are in INR but sales are globally driven in USD, evaluate whether it is a pass-through conduit or an autonomous unit!"

Comprehensive ICAI Illustrative Case Studies (Functional Currency)

ICAI Illustration 1 Convenience Pricing in USD vs. Local Cost Currency
Case: Future Ltd. sells an energy drink worldwide comprising >90% of revenue. For convenience and consistency in international pricing, sales are invoiced in USD. However, all financing activities and almost all costs (labour, materials, overheads) are incurred in local currency (L$). The company holds some USD reserves. Determine the functional currency.
Analysis & Conclusion:

The functional currency of Future Ltd. is L$ (Local Currency).

  • Primary Cost Indicator: Almost all operating costs (labour, raw material, manufacturing overheads) are denominated and settled in L$.
  • Primary Revenue Indicator: While invoices are stated in USD, this is done solely for international convenience and pricing consistency. There is no evidence that the competitive forces and regulatory environment of the US determine sales prices.
  • Under Paragraph 9 of Ind AS 21, the primary economic environment generating and expending cash is the local environment (L$). Therefore, L$ is the functional currency.
ICAI Illustration 2 Pass-Through Conduit Subsidiary vs. Autonomous Entity
Case: Small India Pvt. Ltd. (Small) is an Indian subsidiary of Big Inc. (US parent, functional currency USD). Small takes orders from Indian customers for Big Inc.'s merchandise, maintains a local warehouse, bills and collects in INR, and remits all cash flows generated to Big Inc. Small's operations and inventory are primarily financed by Big Inc. Determine Small's functional currency.
Analysis & Conclusion:

The functional currency of Small India Pvt. Ltd. is USD.

  • Application of Paragraph 11 (Foreign Operation Autonomy): Small is essentially an extension and "pass-through company" of Big Inc. It lacks significant operational autonomy.
  • Small relies entirely on Big Inc. for inventory and financing. Its cash flows directly affect Big Inc. and are immediately remitted to the parent.
  • Therefore, Small's primary economic environment is that of its parent (US), making USD its functional currency despite being located in India and billing in INR.
ICAI Illustration 3 Indian Project Entity with Global USD Revenues and INR Costs
Case: Company A (Oman, functional currency OMR) sets up an Indian project entity B for a construction contract. 78% of B's financing was raised in USD by contribution from A. Cash flows generated are remitted monthly to A in USD. Revenues of B are in USD; global tendering took place in USD; competitors are global. B incurs 70% of costs in INR and 30% in USD. Can B presume INR to be its functional currency because of Indian location?
Analysis & Conclusion:

No, B cannot presume INR is its functional currency based on location.

  • Primary Revenue Indicator: Revenues are in USD, tendering occurred in USD, and pricing is dictated by international competitive forces. USD dominates the sales price indicator.
  • Primary Cost Indicator: Costs are mixed (70% INR, 30% USD).
  • Secondary Indicators (Financing & Cash Flow): 78% financing is in USD, and cash flows are remitted monthly in USD.
  • Conclusion: Weighing all indicators under Paragraphs 9–11, USD most faithfully portrays the economic effects of B's operations. Hence, USD is the functional currency.
ICAI Illustration 4 Imported Luxury Goods Sold in India with Mixed Invoicing
Case: S Ltd. is an Indian listed company that imports motorcycles exclusively from the US (purchases denominated in USD). It sells them across India. Operating costs are incurred in INR. Customers are corporate (70%, invoiced in USD for hedging) and private (30%, invoiced in INR). The USD-denominated prices in India are different from global prices. Determine the functional currency.
Analysis & Conclusion:

The functional currency of S Ltd. is Indian Rupees (₹).

  • Local Competitive Environment: Even though corporate customers are billed in USD, this is done solely as a contractual hedge. The motorcycle prices in India are specific to the Indian market and detached from global US price levels. Indian regulatory and demand conditions determine sales prices.
  • Operating Costs: All local operating expenses, distribution expenses, and overheads are incurred in INR.
  • Listing & Capital Market: The company is listed on the BSE and generates equity funds in India.
  • Consequently, the primary economic environment is India, and the functional currency is INR.
Change in Functional Currency (Paragraphs 35–37):
  • An entity changes its functional currency only if there is a change in underlying transactions, events, and economic conditions (e.g., business model shift).
  • Prospective Accounting: The effect of a change in functional currency is accounted for prospectively from the date of change. Comparative amounts are never restated.
  • All assets, liabilities, and equity items are translated into the new functional currency using the exchange rate on the date of change. The resulting translated amounts for non-monetary items are treated as their new historical cost.
  • Cumulative FCTR previously recognized in OCI is not reclassified to Profit or Loss until the eventual disposal of the foreign operation.

MCA 2025 Notification Lack of Exchangeability for Foreign Currencies (Amendment Rules, 2025)

Effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2025 (notified via G.S.R. 291(E) dated 7 May 2025 aligning with IAS 21 amendments), Ind AS 21 addresses rare situations where a currency cannot be exchanged into another currency at the measurement date.

Mandatory Two-Step Framework:

  1. Step 1: Assessing Exchangeability: A currency is exchangeable into another currency if an entity is able to obtain the other currency within a time frame allowing for normal administrative delay, through a market or exchange mechanism that creates enforceable rights and obligations.
    • Assess the ability to obtain currency, not the entity's intent or decision.
    • Normal administrative delays (e.g., bank holidays, routine documentation) do not constitute lack of exchangeability.
    • If the entity can obtain only an insignificant amount, exchangeability is deemed absent.
  2. Step 2: Estimating Spot Exchange Rate: When a currency is not exchangeable, the entity must estimate the spot rate that reflects the rate at which an orderly exchange transaction would take place between market participants under prevailing economic conditions.
    • Use an observable exchange rate without adjustment (e.g., an official rate, alternative market rate, or first subsequent observable rate); OR
    • Use another estimation technique (e.g., economic indicators, purchasing power parity, or inflation differentials).

3. Foreign Currency Transactions: Initial Recognition & Subsequent Reporting

In an entity's individual financial statements, foreign currency transactions must be recorded initially at the spot exchange rate and subsequently remeasured depending on whether items are monetary or non-monetary.

Initial Recognition Valuation Rule
$$\text{Initial Carrying Value} = \text{Foreign Currency Amount} \times \text{Spot Exchange Rate at Transaction Date}$$
A weekly or monthly weighted average rate may be used if exchange rates do not fluctuate significantly.
Item Category Examples Subsequent Reporting Rate Exchange Gain / Loss Accounting
Monetary Items Trade receivables, trade payables, cash & bank, loans, debentures, provisions payable in cash. Closing Exchange Rate at the reporting date. Recognized in Profit or Loss in the period in which they arise.
Non-Monetary Items at Historical Cost PPE, intangible assets, goodwill, prepaid expenses, advance consideration, inventory at cost. Historical Spot Rate at the date of the original transaction. No exchange difference recognized. Carrying amount never updated for exchange rate movements.
Non-Monetary Items at Fair Value / Revalued Amount Revalued PPE (Ind AS 16), investment property at fair value (Ind AS 40), FVTOCI / FVTPL equity investments (Ind AS 109). Exchange Rate on Date of Fair Value Determination (valuation date). Follows the underlying gain or loss:
  • If gain/loss goes to OCI → exchange component recognized in OCI (e.g., Revaluation Surplus).
  • If gain/loss goes to P&L → exchange component recognized in Profit or Loss.

4. Net Investment in a Foreign Operation & Intercompany Balances (Paragraphs 15, 32–33)

An entity may have a monetary item that is receivable from or payable to a foreign operation. If settlement of this item is neither planned nor likely to occur in the foreseeable future, the item is, in substance, an extension of or deduction from the entity's net investment in that foreign operation.

Net Investment in a Foreign Operation (Paragraph 15)
$$\text{Net Investment} = \text{Carrying Value of Net Equity Assets} + \text{Permanent / Long-Term Monetary Loans}$$
Trade receivables and payables resulting from normal sales/purchases of goods or services NEVER qualify as part of net investment.
Financial Statement Level Accounting Treatment of Exchange Difference Governing Standard Rule
Separate Financial Statements (SFS) of Parent / Subsidiary Recognized immediately in Profit or Loss in the period in which the exchange rate moves. Paragraph 32 (Individual legal entities face real currency risk on their contracts).
Consolidated Financial Statements (CFS) of Group Recognized initially in Other Comprehensive Income (OCI) and accumulated in Foreign Currency Translation Reserve (FCTR) under Equity. Paragraph 32 & 33 (From the group's economic perspective, the loan is an equity-like investment).
Upon Disposal of Foreign Operation The cumulative FCTR balance in equity is reclassified (recycled) to Profit or Loss as part of the net gain/loss on disposal. Paragraph 48 (Triggered on loss of control).
Funding Routed Through Fellow Subsidiaries (Paragraph 15A): The permanent loan does not need to be provided directly by the parent. If Parent P directs Subsidiary T to advance a permanent loan to Subsidiary S, the exchange differences arising in the consolidated financial statements are still recognized in OCI / FCTR, regardless of the currency in which the loan is denominated (Illustration 6).
Intra-Group Transactions & Consolidation Mechanics:
  • Intra-Group Trading Balances: While intra-group debtor and creditor balances eliminate against each other upon consolidation under Ind AS 110, the related foreign exchange gains or losses do NOT eliminate! They remain in consolidated Profit or Loss because the group retains an actual currency exposure to convert funds.
  • Intra-Group Profit Elimination on Inventory / PPE: When goods are sold between group entities with different functional currencies, unrealized intercompany profit must be eliminated using the spot exchange rate at the transaction date (date of sale), NOT the closing rate (Illustrations 7 & 8).

ICAI Illustration 5 Trade Balance vs. Permanent Intercompany Loan
Case: Parent P (functional currency EUR) has a subsidiary S (functional currency USD). P sells inventory to S and has an outstanding trade receivable of $300 that recurs continuously year after year. Additionally, P has lent $500 to S that is not expected to be repaid in the foreseeable future. How should exchange differences be recognized in SFS and CFS?
Analysis & Solution:
  1. Trading Balance ($300): Even though a revolving trade balance remains outstanding at year-end, it represents an operating trade receivable expected to be settled in the normal operating cycle. It does not form part of the net investment in a foreign operation. Consequently, exchange gains/losses on the $300 balance are recognized in Profit or Loss in both P's SFS and the Group's CFS.
  2. Permanent Loan ($500): Settlement of this loan is neither planned nor likely to occur in the foreseeable future. Therefore, under Paragraph 15, it qualifies as part of P's net investment in foreign operation S.
    • In P's Separate Financial Statements (SFS): Exchange gain or loss is recognized in Profit or Loss.
    • In the Group's Consolidated Financial Statements (CFS): The exchange difference is recognized in Other Comprehensive Income (OCI) and accumulated in FCTR until disposal.
ICAI Illustration 6 Permanent Loan Extended via Fellow Subsidiary
Case: In Illustration 5, suppose for tax reasons, the permanent funding of $500 to S is routed through Subsidiary T (wholly owned by P) rather than directly from P. Demonstrate where the exchange difference should be recognized.
Analysis & Solution:

Under Paragraph 15A of Ind AS 21, the entity that provides or receives the loan does not have to be the parent company itself; it can be any fellow subsidiary within the consolidated group.

  • In Subsidiary T's separate financial statements, the exchange difference is recognized in Profit or Loss.
  • In the consolidated financial statements of Parent P, because the loan represents permanent funding to a foreign operation from the group's perspective, the exchange difference is recognized in Other Comprehensive Income (OCI) and accumulated in FCTR.
ICAI Illustration 7 Non-Elimination of Forex Gain/Loss on Intra-Group Balances in CFS
Case: Parent P (functional currency USD) sold goods of USD 100 to subsidiary S (functional currency EUR) when USD 1 = EUR 2. At year-end, the amount is unpaid and exchange rate is USD 1 = EUR 2.2. Advise how the exchange difference is accounted for in CFS.
Analysis & Solution:
  1. In the Books of Subsidiary S:
    • Initial trade payable = $100 \times \text{EUR } 2 = \text{EUR } 200$.
    • Closing trade payable = $100 \times \text{EUR } 2.2 = \text{EUR } 220$.
    • Foreign exchange loss = $\text{EUR } 220 - \text{EUR } 200 = \text{EUR } 20$, recognized in S's Profit or Loss.
  2. In the Books of Parent P: Trade receivable remains at USD 100 (no forex difference in P's functional currency).
  3. In Consolidated Financial Statements (CFS):
    • Upon consolidation, S's payable of EUR 220 translates at the closing rate of EUR 2.2 / USD to USD 100, which eliminates perfectly against P's receivable of USD 100.
    • However, the forex loss of EUR 20 recognized in S's P&L has no offsetting gain in P's financial statements. Therefore, the exchange loss remains in the Consolidated Profit or Loss!
    • Economic Rationale: The group has an actual exposure to foreign currency risk because Subsidiary S must obtain USD to settle the obligation.

5. Translation to Presentation Currency & FCTR Mechanics (Paragraphs 38–49)

When translating the financial statements of a foreign operation into the reporting entity's presentation currency for consolidation, the standard prescribes distinct conversion rates for different components of the financial statements.

Financial Statement Item Applicable Translation Rate Reasoning & Accounting Treatment
All Assets & Liabilities (Monetary & Non-monetary) Closing Exchange Rate at the reporting date. Reflects current purchasing power equivalent at the balance sheet date. Includes goodwill and fair value adjustments from acquisition.
Income & Expense Items Transaction Date Exchange Rates (or weighted average rate for the period). Reflects flows throughout the period. Average rate is permitted only if exchange rates do not fluctuate significantly.
Equity Share Capital & Pre-Acquisition Reserves Historical Exchange Rates at date of issuance / acquisition. Preserves historical capital contributed by equity holders.
Exchange Difference (Balancing Figure) Recognized in Other Comprehensive Income (OCI). Accumulated in a separate equity component: Foreign Currency Translation Reserve (FCTR). Never hits P&L during holding period.
Mathematical Composition of FCTR
$$\text{FCTR Movement} = [\text{Opening Net Assets} \times (\text{Closing Rate} - \text{Opening Rate})] + [\text{Profit for Period} \times (\text{Closing Rate} - \text{Average Rate})]$$
This equation explains why translating assets/liabilities at closing rate while translating P&L at average rate creates an unavoidable translation imbalance.
Event / Circumstance Rule under Ind AS 21 & Ind AS 110 P&L vs. Equity Recycling
Allocation of FCTR to NCI If the foreign operation is not wholly owned, the cumulative exchange differences relating to NCI are allocated to, and recognized as part of, Non-Controlling Interests in the consolidated balance sheet. Allocated between Parent FCTR and NCI in equity.
Full Disposal / Loss of Control On disposal of a foreign operation (sale, liquidation, abandonment), the cumulative amount of exchange differences in FCTR relating to that foreign operation is reclassified (recycled) from equity to Profit or Loss as a reclassification adjustment. Recycled to Profit or Loss (Forms part of gain/loss on disposal).
Partial Disposal of Subsidiary WITHOUT Loss of Control The entity reattributes the proportionate share of cumulative exchange differences recognized in OCI to the Non-Controlling Interests. NO Recycling to P&L (Treated as an equity transaction under Ind AS 110).
Partial Disposal of Associate or Joint Venture The entity reclassifies to Profit or Loss only the proportionate share of cumulative exchange differences previously recognized in OCI. Proportionate Recycling to P&L.

6. Appendix B to Ind AS 21: Foreign Currency Transactions & Advance Consideration

Appendix B clarifies how to determine the "date of the transaction" for calculating the exchange rate on initial recognition of the related asset, expense, or income (or part thereof) when an entity receives or pays consideration in advance in a foreign currency.

Transaction Step Core Accounting Principle Practical Illustration
Advance Payment to Vendor (Non-monetary Asset) The transaction date is the date on which the advance payment is made and the non-monetary asset (Prepaid Expense / Capital Advance) is initially recognized. When inventory/PPE arrives later, it is recorded using the exchange rate on the date of advance payment, NOT the delivery date.
Advance Receipt from Customer (Non-monetary Liability) The transaction date is the date on which the cash is received and the non-monetary liability (Deferred Revenue / Contract Liability) is initially recognized. When revenue is recognized upon performance under Ind AS 115, it is translated at the exchange rate on the date of advance receipt.
Multiple / Tranche Advance Payments If there are multiple payments or receipts in advance, the entity must determine a separate transaction date for each payment or receipt. Final revenue/asset is the sum of tranches translated at their respective historical advance spot rates.
No Subsequent Retranslation Because advance payments/receipts are non-monetary items, they are never retranslated at subsequent closing exchange rates. Protects revenue and asset cost from exchange volatility occurring after cash transfer.

TYK Question 1 Disposal of Foreign Subsidiary & FCTR Recycling to Profit or Loss
Question: Parent P acquired 90% of subsidiary S some years ago. P now sells its entire investment in S for ₹1,500 lakhs. The net assets of S are ₹1,000 lakhs and the NCI in S is ₹100 lakhs. The cumulative exchange differences that have arisen during P's ownership are gains of ₹200 lakhs, resulting in P's foreign currency translation reserve (FCTR) in respect of S having a credit balance of ₹180 lakhs, while the cumulative exchange differences attributed to NCI is ₹20 lakhs.

Calculate P's gain on disposal in its consolidated financial statements.
Solution & Analysis:

Under Paragraph 48 of Ind AS 21 and Paragraph B98 of Ind AS 110, on the disposal of a foreign operation, the cumulative amount of exchange differences relating to that foreign operation recognized in OCI and accumulated in equity (FCTR) must be reclassified from equity to Profit or Loss as a reclassification adjustment (recycling).

Consolidated Gain on Disposal of Foreign Operation
$$\text{Gain on Disposal} = \text{Sale Proceeds} + \text{NCI Derecognized} + \text{FCTR Recycled} - \text{Net Assets Derecognized}$$
Particulars Amount (₹ in lakhs)
Disposal Proceeds Realized 1,500
Add: Non-Controlling Interest (NCI) Derecognized 100
Add: Cumulative FCTR Recycled from Equity to Profit or Loss 180
Total Inflows & Credit Adjustments 1,780

TYK Question 2 Perpetual Debt as Part of Net Investment in Foreign Operation
Question: Entity A (functional currency INR) has a foreign operation, Entity B, with a Euro functional currency. Entity B issues to A perpetual debt (no maturity date) denominated in Euros with an annual interest rate of 6%. The perpetual debt has no issuer call option or holder put option; contractually it is an infinite stream of interest payments in Euros.

Analyse in A's consolidated financial statements whether the perpetual debt can be considered in accordance with Paragraph 15 of Ind AS 21 as a monetary item "for which settlement is neither planned nor likely to occur in the foreseeable future" (i.e., part of A's net investment in B), with exchange gains/losses being recorded in equity (OCI)?
Comprehensive Legal & Standards Analysis:
  1. Monetary Item Characterization: Paragraph 16 of Ind AS 21 explicitly confirms that the essential feature of a monetary item is a right to receive (or an obligation to deliver) a fixed or determinable number of units of currency. Contractual interest payments in Euros represent fixed monetary obligations. Therefore, the perpetual debt is a monetary item.
  2. Application of Paragraph 15 Test: Paragraph 15 states: "An item for which settlement is neither planned nor likely to occur in the foreseeable future is, in substance, an extension of the entity's net investment in that foreign operation."
    • Because the debt has no contractual maturity and neither entity possesses an option to demand redemption, settlement of the principal amount is neither planned nor likely to occur in the foreseeable future.
    • The ongoing periodic payment of 6% interest does not preclude the principal balance from being considered as permanent funding.
  3. Consolidated Financial Statements Conclusion: In Entity A's Consolidated Financial Statements, the perpetual debt qualifies under Paragraph 15 as part of the net investment in foreign operation B.
    • Consequently, under Paragraph 32, foreign exchange gains and losses arising on the principal balance are recognized in Other Comprehensive Income (OCI) and accumulated in the Foreign Currency Translation Reserve (FCTR) within equity.
    • (In Entity A's Separate Financial Statements, the exchange gain/loss is recognized in Profit or Loss).

Unrealized Profit on Intra-Group Inventory Sale Unrealized profit eliminated in CFS at spot rate of transaction date. Standalone forex gain on intercompany receivable remains in CFS P&L. Deferred tax asset (DTA) is recognized in CFS on the eliminated intra-group profit at the buyer's tax rate (ICAI Ind AS 12 guidance).
Foreign Branch / Integral Unit Assets & Liabilities Monetary items retranslated to P&L; non-monetary items at historical rates. Temporary differences arise between Indian Rupee carrying amounts and foreign currency local tax bases converted to INR. Deferred tax recognized in Profit or Loss.
Exam Golden Rule for Dual Standard Problems: Whenever a question involves both foreign currency translation and taxation (such as TYK 4 or TYK 7), always trace the accounting destination of the underlying item first! If the foreign exchange movement goes to Profit or Loss (monetary items), the related current and deferred tax goes to Profit or Loss. If the foreign exchange movement goes to OCI (revalued PPE or net investment in foreign operation), the associated deferred tax MUST be recognized in OCI!
