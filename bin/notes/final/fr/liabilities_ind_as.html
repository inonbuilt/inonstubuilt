Unit 1 • Standard Architecture

Ind AS 19: Employee Benefits

Prescribes the accounting and disclosure for all employee benefits: short-term benefits, post-employment benefits (Defined Contribution vs Defined Benefit Plans), other long-term benefits, and termination benefits. Details the Projected Unit Credit Method (PUCM), actuarial valuations, plan asset accounting, and the strict segregation between Profit & Loss components and OCI Remeasurements.

1. The Four Categories of Employee Benefits

Scope & Framework

Ind AS 19 applies to all employee benefits (including benefits provided under formal schemes, legislative requirements, or constructive obligations), except share-based payment transactions which are governed by Ind AS 102.

Category Definition & Timing Practical Examples Core Accounting Model
1. Short-Term Employee Benefits Benefits expected to be settled wholly before 12 months after the end of the annual reporting period in which employees render the related service. Wages, salaries, paid annual leave, paid sick leave, profit-sharing/bonuses, non-monetary benefits (housing, cars). Undiscounted basis. Recognized as an expense in P&L (unless capitalized under Ind AS 16/Ind AS 2) and accrued liability.
2. Post-Employment Benefits Benefits payable after the completion of employment (excluding termination benefits). Pensions, gratuity, post-employment life insurance, post-employment medical care. Classified into:
• Defined Contribution Plans (DCP): Contribution expensed.
• Defined Benefit Plans (DBP): Actuarial valuation (PUCM), P&L vs OCI remeasurements.
3. Other Long-Term Employee Benefits All employee benefits other than short-term, post-employment, and termination benefits (settled after 12 months). Long-service leave, jubilee or other long-service benefits, long-term disability benefits, deferred compensation. Simplified Model: Current service cost, net interest, and ALL remeasurements recognized in PROFIT OR LOSS (Nothing in OCI!).
4. Termination Benefits Benefits provided in exchange for the termination of an employee's employment (not for service rendered). Voluntary retirement schemes (VRS), severance pay, redundancy compensation. Recognized at the earlier of when entity cannot withdraw offer or when restructuring provisions under Ind AS 37 are recognized.

2. Short-Term Benefits, Compensated Absences & Bonus Plans

Paragraphs 11–25

Accounting Principle: When an employee has rendered service to an entity during an accounting period, the entity shall recognize the undiscounted amount of short-term employee benefits expected to be paid in exchange for that service as a liability (accrued expense) and as an expense.

A
Short-Term Compensated Absences (Paragraphs 13–18):
  • Accumulating Absences: Those that are carried forward and can be used in future periods if the current period's entitlement is not used in full.
    • Vesting: Employees are entitled to a cash payment for unused entitlement on leaving the entity. Recognized as an obligation for all unused leaves.
    • Non-Vesting: Employees are not entitled to cash settlement on leaving. An obligation arises as employees render service, but the liability is measured at the expected cost of additional payments that the entity expects to incur as a result of unused entitlement (taking into account expected employee departures and probability of leave utilization).
  • Non-Accumulating Absences: Those that do not carry forward (e.g. maternity or paternity leave, casual leave). Recognized only when the absence occurs.
B
Profit-Sharing and Bonus Plans (Paragraphs 19–24):
An entity shall recognize the expected cost of profit-sharing and bonus payments when, and only when:
  1. The entity has a present legal or constructive obligation to make such payments as a result of past events; and
  2. A reliable estimate of the obligation can be made.
Constructive Obligation: Exists when the entity has no realistic alternative but to make the payments (e.g., past practice of paying bonuses).

3. Post-Employment Benefits: Defined Contribution vs Defined Benefit

Fundamental Classification
Feature Defined Contribution Plan (DCP) Defined Benefit Plan (DBP)
Core Obligation Entity pays fixed contributions into a separate fund; no legal or constructive obligation to pay further contributions. Entity's obligation is to provide the agreed benefits to current and former employees (e.g. gratuity, final salary pension).
Actuarial Risk Falls on the Employee (benefits may be lower than expected if investments perform poorly). Falls on the Entity (if actuarial experience is worse than expected, entity must fund the deficit).
Investment Risk Falls on the Employee. Falls on the Entity.
Accounting Complexity Simple: Contribution payable recognized as expense in P&L. (Discounted only if not due wholly within 12 months). Complex: Requires actuarial valuation (PUCM), discount rates, plan asset fair valuation, and OCI remeasurement separation.

4. Actuarial Valuations & The Projected Unit Credit Method (PUCM)

Paragraphs 67–74 • Mandatory Method

Ind AS 19 mandates the use of the Projected Unit Credit Method (PUCM) (sometimes known as the accrued benefit method pro-rated on service) to determine the present value of its defined benefit obligations and the related current service cost.

PUCM Attribution Concept
\text{Unit of Benefit} = \frac{\text{Total Estimated Benefit at Retirement}}{\text{Total Working Life Service (Years)}}
Each period of service gives rise to an additional unit of benefit entitlement, and each unit is measured separately to build up the final obligation.
Attributing Benefit to Periods of Service (Paragraphs 70–74):
  • An entity shall attribute benefit to the current period (in determining current service cost) and the current and prior periods (in determining the present value of defined benefit obligations).
  • The Straight-Line Rule: If an employee's service in later years will lead to a materially higher level of benefit than in earlier years, an entity shall attribute benefit on a straight-line basis from the date when service first leads to benefits until the date when further service will lead to no material amount of further benefits.
  • Vesting vs Attribution: Benefit is attributed up to the date when further service leads to no material increase in benefits, regardless of whether the benefits are conditional on further employment (vested or unvested).

Actuarial Assumptions (Paragraphs 75–98):

  • Demographic Assumptions: Mortality (during and after employment), rates of employee turnover, disability, and early retirement.
  • Financial Assumptions:
    • Discount Rate (Paragraph 83): The rate used to discount post-employment benefit obligations shall be determined by reference to market yields at the end of the reporting period on government bonds. (In countries where there is a deep market in high quality corporate bonds, corporate bond yields are used; however, in India, government bond yields are used).
    • Future salary levels and benefit increases (reflecting inflation, seniority, promotion).
    • Expected medical costs and healthcare trends (for medical plans).

5. Net Defined Benefit Liability / Asset & The Asset Ceiling Test

Paragraphs 63–65
Balance Sheet Net Position
\text{Net Defined Benefit Liability / (Asset)} = \text{PV of Defined Benefit Obligation (PVDBO)} - \text{Fair Value of Plan Assets (FVPA)}
The Asset Ceiling Test (Paragraph 64):

When an entity has a surplus in a defined benefit plan (i.e. FVPA exceeds PVDBO), it shall measure the Net Defined Benefit Asset at the lower of:

  1. The surplus in the defined benefit plan (FVPA - PVDBO); and
  2. The Asset Ceiling: The present value of any economic benefits available in the form of refunds from the plan or reductions in future contributions to the plan.

Any write-down to the asset ceiling is recognized in Other Comprehensive Income (OCI) as part of Remeasurements.

Qualifying Plan Assets (Paragraph 8):

  • Assets held by a long-term employee benefit fund (legally separate from reporting entity, used solely to pay employee benefits, not available to entity's creditors).
  • Qualifying insurance policies issued by non-related insurers.
  • Plan assets exclude unpaid contributions due from the reporting entity and non-transferable financial instruments issued by the entity.

6. Profit & Loss Components vs OCI Remeasurements

Paragraphs 120–130 • Critical Exam Distinction

An entity shall recognize the components of defined benefit cost as follows:

Reporting Location Component Item Mathematical Formula & Computation
Recognized in Profit or Loss (Operating / Finance) 1. Current Service Cost The increase in the present value of the defined benefit obligation resulting from employee service in the current period.
2. Past Service Cost and (Gain)/Loss on Settlement Change in PVDBO arising from plan amendment (introduction or change of benefits) or curtailment (significant reduction in employees).
Mandatory Rule: Recognized immediately in P&L at earlier of when amendment occurs or when related restructuring/termination costs are recognized! (No deferred amortization).
3. Net Interest on Net Defined Benefit Liability / (Asset) $$\text{Net Interest} = \Big(\text{Opening PVDBO} - \text{Opening FVPA}\Big) \times \text{Discount Rate}$$ (Net Interest Expense if net liability; Net Interest Income if net asset).
Recognized in Other Comprehensive Income (OCI) • REMEASUREMENTS 1. Actuarial Gains and Losses on DBO Arising from: (a) Experience adjustments (differences between previous actuarial assumptions and what actually occurred); (b) Changes in demographic assumptions; (c) Changes in financial assumptions (e.g. discount rate).
2. Return on Plan Assets (Excluding Net Interest) $$\text{Actual Return on Plan Assets} - \Big(\text{Opening FVPA} \times \text{Discount Rate}\Big)$$
3. Change in Effect of Asset Ceiling (Excluding Net Interest) $$\text{Actual Change in Asset Ceiling} - \Big(\text{Opening Asset Ceiling} \times \text{Discount Rate}\Big)$$
Strict OCI Prohibition on Reclassification!
Paragraph 122 mandates that remeasurements recognized in Other Comprehensive Income shall NEVER be reclassified to Profit or Loss in a subsequent period! However, the entity may transfer those amounts recognized in other comprehensive income within equity (e.g., to Retained Earnings).

7. Other Long-Term Employee Benefits & Termination Benefits

Paragraphs 153–171
Other Long-Term Employee Benefits (Paragraphs 153–158):

For other long-term employee benefits (e.g. jubilee awards, long-service sabbatical leave, long-term disability):

The standard establishes a simplified accounting model: An entity recognizes the net total of:

  • Current service cost;
  • Past service cost and gain/loss on settlement;
  • Net interest on the net defined benefit liability/(asset); and
  • Remeasurements of the net defined benefit liability/(asset)

ALL IN PROFIT OR LOSS! None of the remeasurements are recognized in Other Comprehensive Income for other long-term benefits.

Termination Benefits (Paragraphs 159–171):

An entity shall recognize a liability and expense for termination benefits at the earlier of:

  1. When the entity can no longer withdraw the offer of those benefits (e.g., when employees accept voluntary redundancy, or when a formal plan without withdrawal options is communicated); and
  2. When the entity recognizes costs for a restructuring that is within the scope of Ind AS 37 and involves the payment of termination benefits.

Measurement: If termination benefits are expected to be settled wholly within 12 months, apply short-term rules. If not expected to be settled wholly within 12 months, discount using the government bond discount rate.

8. Key Architectural Differences: Ind AS 19 vs AS 15

Exam Theory Hotspot

For CA Final examinations, students must master the sharp contrast between Ind AS 19 (converged with IAS 19) and previous AS 15 (Employee Benefits):

Feature Ind AS 19 (New Scheme / Converged) AS 15 (Previous Indian GAAP)
1. Actuarial Gains & Losses (Remeasurements) Mandatorily recognized in OCI in the period incurred. Never reclassified to P&L in subsequent periods. Recognized immediately in the Statement of Profit and Loss as part of employee expenses.
2. Past Service Cost Recognized immediately in Profit or Loss on the date of plan amendment, whether vested or unvested. Vested cost recognized immediately; unvested cost was amortized straight-line over the remaining vesting period.
3. Return on Plan Assets Net Interest Approach: Plan assets yield interest income at the bond discount rate (in P&L). Any excess or shortfall is recognized in OCI as a remeasurement. Based on management's expected rate of return on plan assets, credited entirely in the Statement of Profit and Loss.
4. Short-Term Employee Benefits Definition Benefits expected to be settled wholly before 12 months after the end of the annual reporting period in which service is rendered. Defined as benefits due to be settled within 12 months after the end of the period.
5. Termination Benefits Recognition Recognized at the earlier of when the entity can no longer withdraw the offer or when it recognizes restructuring costs under Ind AS 37. Recognized when the entity has a present obligation as a result of a past event.

Illustration 1 • Short-Term Benefits Vested Accumulating Leaves Cash Settlement
Question: Mr. Rajan is working for Infotech Ltd. Consider the following particulars:
• Annual salary of Mr. Rajan = ₹30,00,000
• Total working days in 20X0-20X1 = 300 days
• Leaves allowed in 20X0-20X1 as per company policy = 10 days
• Leaves utilized by Mr. Rajan in 20X0-20X1 = 8 days
The unutilized leaves are settled by way of payment and accordingly, carry forward of such leaves to the subsequent period is not allowed. Compute the total employee benefit expense for Infotech Ltd. in respect of 20X0-20X1.
Solution:

Mr. Rajan is entitled to an annual salary of ₹30,00,000 for 300 working days. Therefore, salary per working day is:

$$\text{Per Day Salary} = \frac{₹30,00,000}{300 \text{ days}} = ₹10,000 \text{ per day}$$

In 20X0-20X1, Mr. Rajan availed 8 out of 10 allowed leaves. Unutilized leaves = $10 - 8 = 2 \text{ days}$.

Under company policy, Infotech Ltd. must pay for unutilized leaves as a vested short-term benefit:

$$\text{Total Employee Benefit Expense} = ₹30,00,000 + (2 \text{ days} \times ₹10,000) = \mathbf{₹30,20,000}$$

Illustration 4 • Short-Term Benefits Segmented Employee Population Leave Estimation
Question: An entity has 80 employees in Category A (entitled to 10 casual leaves and 5 sick leaves) and 30 employees in Category B (entitled to 15 casual leaves and 10 sick leaves). Based on past experience, the entity expects that 30 employees of Category B will take 5 days of casual leave and 10 employees of Category B will take 1 day of sick leave from their carried forward accumulated entitlement. Calculate the liability to be recognized.
Solution:

Under Ind AS 19, an entity measures the obligation for accumulating non-vesting compensated absences based on the expected additional payments resulting from unused accumulated leave:

Illustration 5 • Short-Term Benefits Vested vs Non-Vested Leave Valuation Comparison
Question: An entity has 100 employees, who are each entitled to 10 working days of paid sick leave each year. Unused sick leave may be carried forward for 1 financial year on a LIFO basis. At 31 March 20X1, the average unused entitlement is 2 days per employee. Based on past experience, management expects that only 20% of employees will use 1 day from carried forward leave. Salary per day is ₹2,500. Compute the expense if leaves are: (a) Vested, and (b) Non-vested.
Solution:

(a) Vested Short-Term Compensated Absences:
Because employees are entitled to a cash payment for all unused leaves upon departure, the entity must provide for the full accumulated entitlement:

$$\text{Liability \& Expense} = 100 \text{ employees} \times 2 \text{ days} \times ₹2,500 = \mathbf{₹5,00,000}$$

(b) Non-Vested Short-Term Compensated Absences:
Because unused leave cannot be encashed, the liability is measured at the expected cost of additional payments:

$$\text{Liability \& Expense} = 100 \text{ employees} \times 20\% \times 1 \text{ day} \times ₹2,500 = \mathbf{₹50,000}$$
Illustration 6 • Short-Term Benefits Acer Ltd. Attrition, FIFO Leave & Salary Increment
Question: Acer Ltd. has 350 employees (same as a year ago). Staff attrition rate is 6% p.a. Paid vacation: 10 days per year. Unused vacation can be carried forward for 1 year. As at 31 March 20X1: unused vacation carried forward was 3 days per employee, average salary was ₹15,000 per day, and accrued expense was ₹65,00,000. During 20X1-20X2: employees took 9 days of vacation on average. Salary increase was 10%. Analyse how Acer Ltd. recognizes liabilities and expenses as of 31 March 20X2 and pass journal entries.
Solution:

Step 1: Revised Daily Salary for 20X1-20X2:

$$\text{Daily Salary} = ₹15,000 \times (1 + 10\%) = \mathbf{₹16,500 \text{ per day}}$$

Step 2: Unused Vacation in Man-Days as at 31 March 20X2 (FIFO Basis):

  • Continuing Employees (94% of 350 = 329 employees):
    Opening carried forward = 3 days; fresh entitlement = 10 days. Total available = 13 days.
    Average taken = 9 days (3 days opening used + 6 days current used).
    Unused at year-end = $10 - 6 = 4 \text{ days per employee}$.
    Total man-days $= 329 \times 4 = \mathbf{1,316 \text{ man-days}}$.
  • New Employees (6% of 350 = 21 employees):
    Fresh entitlement = 10 days; average taken = 9 days.
    Unused at year-end = $10 - 9 = 1 \text{ day per employee}$.
    Total man-days $= 21 \times 1 = \mathbf{21 \text{ man-days}}$.

Total Unused Vacation: $1,316 + 21 = \mathbf{1,337 \text{ man-days}}$.

Step 3: Required Closing Provision:

$$\text{Closing Provision} = 1,337 \text{ man-days} \times ₹16,500 = \mathbf{₹2,20,60,500}$$

Step 4: Net P&L Expense for 20X1-20X2:

$$\text{Increase in Provision} = ₹2,20,60,500 - ₹65,00,000 = \mathbf{₹1,55,60,500}$$

Illustration 7 • Profit-Sharing Plans Laxmi Mills Turnover-Adjusted Profit-Sharing Payout
Question: Laxmi Mills reported profit of ₹200 crore in 20X1-20X2. Under its profit-sharing plan, it pays 5% of profit to employees who complete 1 year of service. Due to expected staff turnover, the management estimates the actual payout will be around 4.5%. Compute the liability and expense.

Illustration 9 • Plan Classification Lump-Sum Retirement Benefit: DCP vs DBP Test
Question: A company pays each employee a lump-sum one-time benefit upon retirement computed based on years of service and final salary prior to retirement. To cover its liabilities, the company contributes 3% of annual gross salaries to an independent fund. Comment whether this obligation represents a Defined Contribution Plan (DCP) or Defined Benefit Plan (DBP).
Solution:

Classification: Defined Benefit Plan (DBP).

Reasoning:

  • The definitive test under Ind AS 19 is who bears the actuarial and investment risk.
  • Even though the company makes regular monthly/annual contributions of 3% into a separate fund, the promised benefit payable to the employee is pre-determined by a formula linked to final salary and tenure.
  • If the fund's accumulated assets fall short of the formula-determined payout, the company remains legally obligated to make good the shortfall. Consequently, the actuarial risk and investment risk fall on the entity, making it a Defined Benefit Plan.
Illustration 10 • Plan Classification Statutory State Pension Scheme Classification
Question: In accordance with applicable legislation, a company contributes 12% and employees contribute 12% of annual gross salaries to a statutory provident and pension fund. Upon retirement, employees receive an accumulated balance calculated based on years of service and average salary for past 15 years. The pension is paid out of state fund assets and the company has no further obligation except to make contributions. Analyse whether this represents a DCP or DBP.
Solution:

Classification: Defined Contribution Plan (DCP).

Reasoning:

  • The company's obligation is strictly limited to paying the statutory 12% contribution to the state-managed fund.
  • Because the company has no legal or constructive obligation to pay further contributions if the state fund does not hold sufficient assets to pay all employee benefits, the entity bears neither actuarial risk nor investment risk.
  • The risk is carried by the state fund, so the plan is accounted for as a Defined Contribution Plan.

Illustration 12 • Defined Benefit Plans Dinkar Ltd. Prohibition of Cash Basis Accounting for Gratuity
Question: Dinkar Ltd., an IT company, accounts for gratuity on a payment (cash) basis, stating in note disclosures: "Due to high labour turnover, a large degree of uncertainty is involved in estimating the liability of gratuity. Recording on cash basis avoids confusing readers." Management contends that full disclosure satisfies all Ind AS requirements. Is management's contention correct?
Solution:

  3. The Present Value of Defined Benefit Obligation (PVDBO) is the present value of monthly pension payments of 0.3% of final salary multiplied by the number of completed years of service up to the reporting date.

Question: A plan pays a benefit of ₹140 for each year of service, excluding service before the age of 25. The benefits vest immediately. Compute the benefit to be attributed before age 25 and after age 25.
Solution:

Under Paragraph 71 of Ind AS 19:

  • Before age 25: No benefit is attributed to service before age 25 because service before that date does not lead to any benefits (conditional or unconditional) under the plan.
  • After age 25: A benefit of ₹140 is attributed to each subsequent year of service rendered after age 25.
TYK Question 4 Post-Employment Medical Plan Brackets Attribution
Question: B Pvt. Ltd. post-employment medical plan reimburses 20% of medical costs if an employee leaves after more than 10 and less than 20 years of service, and 50% if employee leaves after 20 or more years of service. State how benefit is attributed for service: within 10 years, between 10 and 20 years, and for employees expected to serve 20+ years.
Solution:

Under Paragraph 71, if service in later years leads to a materially higher level of benefit, benefit is attributed on a straight-line basis until further service leads to no material increase:

  1. Employees expected to leave after 20 or more years:
    Service beyond 20 years leads to no further benefits (capped at 50%).
    The benefit attributed to each of the first 20 years is 2.5% ($\frac{50\%}{20 \text{ years}}$) of the present value of expected medical costs. Service beyond 20 years receives zero attribution.
  2. Employees expected to leave between 10 and 20 years:
    The benefit attributed to each of the first 10 years is 2% ($\frac{20\%}{10 \text{ years}}$) of the present value of expected medical costs. For these employees, no benefit is attributed to service between the end of year 10 and the estimated date of leaving.
  3. Employees expected to leave within 10 years:
    No benefit is attributed because they will receive zero benefits upon leaving.
TYK Question 5 Cisca Pvt Ltd: Compensated Absences, Profit-Sharing & DCP
Question: Cisca Pvt. Ltd. has 1,000 employees. Leave policy: 35 days privilege leave (PL), 15 days sick leave (SL), 10 days casual leave. Up to 10 PL and 5 SL can be carried forward. Expected leave usage: 200 employees take 5 PL and 2 SL; 800 employees take 10 PL and 5 SL. Profit-sharing: 4% declared, but expected payout is 3.5% due to turnover. Current year profit = ₹2,000 crore. Defined contribution plan: contribution due = ₹100 crore (payable within 12 months), ₹20 crore paid during year. State accounting treatment for each item.
Solution:
  1. Short-Term Compensated Absences:
    Cisca Pvt. Ltd. recognizes a liability for the expected additional cost of unused accumulating leaves:
    • PL: (200 employees × 5 days) + (800 employees × 10 days) = 1,000 + 8,000 = 9,000 days of PL.
    • SL: (200 employees × 2 days) + (800 employees × 5 days) = 400 + 4,000 = 4,400 days of SL.
    Liability is measured at the salary cost of these 13,400 days as short-term compensated absences.
  2. Profit-Sharing Plan:
    Recognized based on expected payout reflecting turnover: $$\text{Liability \& Expense} = ₹2,000 \text{ crore} \times 3.5\% = \mathbf{₹70 \text{ crore}}$$
  3. Defined Contribution Plan:
    Total contribution due = ₹100 crore. Amount already paid = ₹20 crore.
    Liability (accrued contribution) recognized = ₹100 - ₹20 = ₹80 crore, and ₹100 crore recognized as expense in Profit or Loss.
    Payment Timing: Since payable within 12 months, no discounting is required. If payment did not fall due wholly within 12 months, contributions would be discounted using the government bond rate.
TYK Question 6 Plan Amendment & Immediate Past Service Cost Recognition
Question: OPQ Ltd. amended its defined benefit pension plan, increasing the pension to 3% of final salary for each year of service for past service rendered from 1 April 20X1 to 1 April 20X8. The actuarial valuation shows that the present value of the additional obligation for past service is ₹4,96,000 (of which ₹2,75,000 relates to vested employees and ₹2,21,000 relates to unvested employees who must complete 3 further years of service). How should OPQ Ltd. recognize this past service cost?
Solution:

Under Paragraphs 102–104 of Ind AS 19:

  1. Past service cost is the change in the present value of the defined benefit obligation resulting from a plan amendment or curtailment.
  2. An entity shall recognize past service cost as an expense at the earlier of:
    • When the plan amendment or curtailment occurs; and
    • When the entity recognizes related restructuring costs under Ind AS 37 or termination benefits.
  3. Vested vs Unvested Distinction Abolished: Unlike older accounting standards (like AS 15) which permitted unvested past service cost to be amortized over the vesting period, Ind AS 19 mandates that ALL past service costs (both vested and unvested) MUST BE RECOGNIZED IMMEDIATELY IN PROFIT OR LOSS on the date of amendment!
  4. Therefore, OPQ Ltd. must recognize the entire ₹4,96,000 immediately as an expense in Profit or Loss in the period of amendment.
Unit 2 • Liabilities of Uncertain Timing & Amount

Ind AS 37: Provisions, Contingent Liabilities & Contingent Assets

Prescribes the criteria for recognizing provisions, distinguishing them from contingent liabilities and contingent assets, measuring provisions at the present value of expected cash flows, accounting for onerous contracts and restructuring costs, and applying Appendix C on government levies.

1. Provisions vs Contingent Liabilities vs Contingent Assets

Core Terminology

Definition of Provision (Paragraph 10): A provision is a liability of uncertain timing or amount.

Term Definition Balance Sheet & Disclosure Treatment
Provision A present obligation arising from past events, where an outflow of resources embodying economic benefits is probable (> 50%), and a reliable estimate can be made of the amount. RECOGNIZED on Balance Sheet as a liability. Expense recognized in P&L (or capitalized into PPE/inventory).
Contingent Liability 1. A possible obligation that arises from past events and whose existence will be confirmed only by the occurrence or non-occurrence of one or more uncertain future events not wholly within the control of the entity; OR
2. A present obligation that arises from past events but is NOT recognized because:
  • It is not probable that an outflow of resources will be required; or
  • The amount cannot be measured with sufficient reliability.
 NEVER RECOGNIZED on Balance Sheet!
Disclosed in the notes to financial statements, unless the possibility of any outflow in settlement is remote (< 5%).
Contingent Asset A possible asset that arises from past events and whose existence will be confirmed only by the occurrence or non-occurrence of one or more uncertain future events not wholly within the control of the entity (e.g. pending insurance claim or court lawsuit filed by the entity). NEVER RECOGNIZED on Balance Sheet!
• Disclosed in notes when an inflow of economic benefits is probable (> 50%).
• When the realization of income is virtually certain (> 95%), the asset is no longer contingent and is recognized on Balance Sheet.
The Master Probability Matrix (Paragraphs 23–35)
\begin{array}{|c|c|c|c|} \hline \textbf{Probability Band} & \textbf{Term} & \textbf{Obligation / Outflow} & \textbf{Asset / Inflow} \\ \hline > 95\% & \text{Virtually Certain} & \text{Recognize Provision} & \textbf{Recognize Asset on Balance Sheet} \\ 50\% - 95\% & \text{Probable} & \textbf{Recognize Provision} & \text{Disclose Contingent Asset in Notes} \\ 5\% - 50\% & \text{Possible} & \text{Disclose Contingent Liability in Notes} & \text{No Disclosure / Ignored} \\ < 5\% & \text{Remote} & \text{No Disclosure / Ignored} & \text{No Disclosure / Ignored} \\ \hline \end{array}

2. Recognition Criteria for Provisions (Three Cumulative Conditions)

Paragraph 14

A provision shall be recognized when, and only when, all three of the following criteria are met:

1
Present Obligation as a Result of a Past Event (Obligating Event)
An obligating event is a past event that leads to a present obligation where the entity has no realistic alternative to settling that obligation.
  • Legal Obligation: Arises from a contract, legislation, or other operation of law.
  • Constructive Obligation: Arises from an entity's actions where by an established pattern of past practice, published policies, or a sufficiently specific current statement, the entity has indicated to other parties that it will accept certain responsibilities, and as a result, has created a valid expectation in those parties that it will discharge those responsibilities.
2
Probable Outflow of Resources Embodying Economic Benefits
An outflow of resources is regarded as probable if it is more likely than not to occur (i.e. the probability that the event will occur is greater than the probability that it will not occur, > 50%).
3
Reliable Estimate of the Obligation
Except in extremely rare cases, an entity will be able to determine a range of possible outcomes and make an estimate of the obligation that is sufficiently reliable to use in recognizing a provision.
Independent of Future Actions (Paragraph 19): Provisions are recognized only for obligations that exist independently of an entity's future actions (i.e. the future conduct of its business).
Exam Example: Penalties for environmental contamination already caused must be provided for. However, future costs of fitting pollution filters or future overhaul costs (e.g. ship dry-docking every 3 years) are NOT provisions because the entity can avoid the future expenditure by its future actions (e.g. by selling the ship or closing the factory)!

3. Measurement, Discounting and Reimbursements

Paragraphs 36–58

Best Estimate (Paragraphs 36–41): The amount recognized as a provision shall be the best estimate of the expenditure required to settle the present obligation at the end of the reporting period.

  • Large Population of Items (e.g. product warranties): The obligation is estimated by weighting all possible outcomes by their associated probabilities (Expected Value Method). $$\text{Provision} = \sum \Big(\text{Possible Cost Outcome} \times \text{Assigned Probability}\Big)$$
  • Single Obligation (e.g. individual lawsuit, environmental cleanup): The individual most likely outcome may be the best estimate of the liability, adjusted for other possible outcomes if they are mostly higher or lower.
Present Value Discounting & Unwinding of Discount
\text{Provision Recognized} = \text{Present Value of Expected Future Cash Outflows} \\[6pt] \text{Finance Cost (P\&L)} = \text{Carrying Amount of Provision} \times \text{Pre-Tax Discount Rate}
The discount rate shall be a pre-tax rate that reflects current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific to the liability. The unwinding of the discount is recognized as a Finance Cost in Profit or Loss!
Reimbursements (Paragraph 53):

Where some or all of the expenditure required to settle a provision is expected to be reimbursed by another party (e.g., through an insurance contract or supplier indemnity clause):

  1. The reimbursement shall be recognized when, and only when, it is VIRTUALLY CERTAIN (> 95%) that reimbursement will be received if the entity settles the obligation.
  2. The reimbursement shall be treated as a SEPARATE ASSET. The amount recognized for the reimbursement shall not exceed the amount of the provision.
  3. Statement of Profit and Loss Presentation: In the Statement of Profit and Loss, the expense relating to a provision may be presented net of the amount recognized for a reimbursement.

4. Future Operating Losses and Onerous Contracts

Paragraphs 63–69
Future Operating Losses Prohibited (Paragraph 63): Provisions shall NOT be recognized for future operating losses! Future operating losses do not meet the definition of a liability (there is no present obligation arising from a past event) and the general recognition criteria. An expectation of future operating losses is an indication that certain assets of the operation may be impaired under Ind AS 36.

Onerous Contracts (Paragraphs 66–69):

An onerous contract is a contract in which the unavoidable costs of meeting the obligations under the contract exceed the economic benefits expected to be received under it.

Onerous Contract Provision Measurement
\text{Unavoidable Costs} = \min\Big(\text{Cost of Fulfilling the Contract}, \; \text{Penalties / Compensation from Exiting/Failure}\Big)
Cost of fulfilling the contract includes costs that relate directly to the contract: incremental costs of fulfilling (direct labour/materials) + an allocation of other costs that relate directly to fulfilling contracts (depreciation of plant used).
Mandatory Prior Asset Impairment (Paragraph 69): Before a separate provision for an onerous contract is established, an entity recognizes any impairment loss that has occurred on assets dedicated to that contract in accordance with Ind AS 36! Only the remaining unhedged unavoidable net loss is recognized as a provision under Ind AS 37.

5. Restructuring Provisions Framework

Paragraphs 70–83

Definition of Restructuring (Paragraph 10): A programme that is planned and controlled by management, and materially changes either:

  • The scope of a business undertaken by an entity; or
  • The manner in which that business is conducted (e.g. sale/termination of a line of business, closure of business locations, changes in management structure).
1
Condition 1: Detailed Formal Plan (Paragraph 72(a)) Must identify at least: the business or part of business concerned; principal locations affected; location, function, and approximate number of employees who will be compensated for terminating their services; expenditures that will be undertaken; and when the plan will be implemented.
2
Condition 2: Valid Expectation Raised in Affected Parties (Paragraph 72(b)) Has raised a valid expectation in those affected that it will carry out the restructuring by starting to implement that plan or announcing its main features to those affected by it on or before the end of the reporting period.
Exam Rule: A management or board decision alone taken before the end of the reporting period does NOT create a constructive obligation unless the plan has been communicated to affected employees/customers before the reporting date!
Costs INCLUDED in Restructuring Provision (Para 80) Costs EXCLUDED from Restructuring Provision (Para 81)
Direct expenditures arising from the restructuring that are necessarily entailed by the restructuring and not associated with ongoing activities:
  • Employee redundancy / voluntary retirement termination payments.
  • Lease termination penalties for closed premises (onerous lease contracts).
  • Contract cancellation penalties directly resulting from closure.
 Costs associated with the future conduct of the business:
  • Retraining or relocating continuing staff.
  • Marketing and advertising for restructured operations.
  • Investment in new systems and distribution networks.
  • Future operating losses up to the date of restructuring.

6. Accounting for Levies (Appendix C of Ind AS 37)

Appendix C Guidance

Definition: A levy is an outflow of resources embodying economic benefits that is imposed by governments on entities in accordance with legislation (excluding income taxes under Ind AS 12, fines, and payments for goods/services).

Core Principles of Appendix C:
  • The Obligating Event: The obligating event that gives rise to a liability to pay a levy is the activity that triggers the payment of the levy, as identified by the legislation.
    Example: If the trigger is the generation of revenue in the current period, generation of revenue in the previous period is necessary but NOT sufficient to create a present obligation.
  • No Constructive Obligation from Going Concern: The preparation of financial statements under the going concern assumption does not imply that an entity has a present obligation to pay a levy that will be triggered by operating in a future period.
  • Threshold Triggers: If an obligation to pay a levy is triggered when a minimum activity threshold is reached (e.g. minimum turnover or volume), the liability is recognized only when that minimum activity threshold is reached (no progressive accrual before reaching threshold).
  • Progressive Recognition: The liability to pay a levy is recognized progressively if the obligating event occurs over a period of time (e.g. generation of revenue over time).

7. Key Architectural Differences: Ind AS 37 vs AS 29

Exam Theory Hotspot

For CA Final examinations, students must understand the crucial differences between Ind AS 37 and AS 29 (Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets):

Feature Ind AS 37 (Converged Indian Accounting Standard) AS 29 (Previous Indian GAAP)
1. Discounting of Provisions Mandatory Discounting: Where the effect of the time value of money is material, provisions are discounted to present value. Unwinding of discount is recognized as a finance cost. Strictly Prohibited: Provisions are measured on an undiscounted basis regardless of settlement horizon.
2. Constructive Obligations Recognizes provisions for both legal obligations and constructive obligations (arising from published policies or valid expectations created by past practices). Recognizes provisions only for legal or contractual obligations. No provision for constructive obligations.
3. Appendix C on Levies Includes comprehensive mandatory guidance in Appendix C defining the obligating event that gives rise to a liability to pay a government levy. No corresponding guidance or appendix exists in AS 29.
4. Contingent Assets Disclosure Where an inflow of economic benefits is probable (> 50%), disclosure of the contingent asset is required in the notes to financial statements. Disclosure of contingent assets in financial statements is strictly prohibited (only permitted in the Board's Report).
5. Onerous Contracts Measurement Unavoidable costs equal the least net cost of exiting (the lower of the cost of fulfilling the contract and penalties/compensation for cancellation). Similar onerous contract principle, but measured on an undiscounted basis without explicit least-cost-of-exit rules.

Illustration 1 • Present Obligation ABC Ltd. Future Delivery Schedule Penalty Evaluation
Question: ABC Limited is an automobile component manufacturer. The automobile manufacturer has specified a delivery schedule, non-adherence to which will entail a penalty. As on 31st March, 20X1 (reporting date), the manufacturer has a delivery scheduled for June 20X2. However, the manufacturer is aware that he will not be able to meet the delivery schedule in June 20X2. Determine whether the entity has a present obligation as at 31st March, 20X1 requiring recognition of a provision.
Solution:

Accounting Principle (Paragraphs 14–19): A provision is recognized only when a present obligation exists as a result of a past obligating event that exists independently of the entity's future actions.

Evaluation:

  • As on 31st March, 20X1, the goods are scheduled for delivery in June 20X2 (15 months later). There has been no delay or contractual breach as at 31st March, 20X1.
  • The event that would give rise to the penalty is the failure to deliver on time in June 20X2, which is a future event dependent on future operations. The entity could alter its production schedule, subcontract manufacturing, or renegotiate terms.
  • Consequently, there is no present obligation arising out of a past event as at 31st March, 20X1.
  • Conclusion: No provision shall be recognized in the financial statements for the year ended 31st March, 20X1.
Illustration 2 • Constructive Obligation Contaminated Land Clean-Up Under Published Environmental Policy
Question: An entity operates a chemical plant in a country where there is no environmental legislation requiring the clean-up of contaminated land. However, the entity has a widely published environmental policy in which it undertakes to clean up all contamination that it causes, and has an established record of honouring this published policy. During the year, land was contaminated. Can a provision for clean-up costs be recognized?
Solution:

Under Paragraph 10 & 17 of Ind AS 37, a constructive obligation arises when:

  1. By an established pattern of past practice, published policies, or a sufficiently specific current statement, the entity has indicated to other parties that it will accept certain responsibilities; and
  2. As a result, the entity has created a valid expectation on the part of those other parties that it will discharge those responsibilities.

Application:

  • Past Obligating Event: The contamination of the land occurred during the current period.
  • Constructive Obligation: Although no legal statute exists, the published policy and consistent past practice have created a valid expectation among local communities and environmental authorities that the entity will clean up.
  • Outflow & Estimate: An outflow of economic resources is probable, and a reliable estimate of clean-up costs can be made.
  • Conclusion: A provision MUST be recognized for the best estimate of the costs of cleaning up the contaminated land.
Illustration 3 • Contingent Liability X Chemical Ltd. Environmental Pollution Notice Under Investigation
Question: X Chemical Ltd. has operated near a river for 20 years. A local community claims that the entity's operations contaminated drinking water. X Chemical Ltd. received a notice from the environmental agency that official investigations will be conducted. If found responsible, fines and penalties will be levied. Management believes it implemented all required safety measures and is unlikely to have caused pollution, but historical records over 20 years are incomplete. Neither management nor external experts can assess liability until the investigation concludes. How should X Chemical Ltd. account for this?
Solution:

Evaluation under Paragraphs 15–16 & 27–30:

  1. Present Obligation Check: Because the investigation has just commenced and expert assessments cannot determine whether the entity caused the contamination, it is not probable that a present obligation exists at the balance sheet date.
  2. Prohibition on Provision: Since it cannot be determined whether an outflow of resources is probable, and no reliable estimate can be made, no provision can be recognized in the Balance Sheet.
  3. Contingent Liability Disclosure: The situation represents a possible obligation arising from past operations whose existence will be confirmed only by the outcome of the government investigation. Unless the possibility of an outflow is remote, X Chemical Ltd. must disclose the matter as a Contingent Liability in the notes to financial statements, describing the nature of the claim and the uncertainties involved.
Illustration 4 • Product Warranties Warranty Commitment Recognition Criteria
Question: An entity sells consumer electronic goods with a 1-year manufacturer warranty under which it promises to repair or replace defective goods free of charge. Explain whether a provision is required at the balance sheet date.
Solution:

All three cumulative recognition criteria under Paragraph 14 of Ind AS 37 are met:

  • 1. Present Obligation from Past Event: The obligating event is the sale of the product with the warranty clause. The sale creates a contractual/legal obligation.
  • 2. Probable Outflow of Resources: For the warranty portfolio as a whole, it is probable (more likely than not) that some products will prove defective, requiring repair or replacement outflows.
  • 3. Reliable Estimate: Based on historical warranty claim rates and engineering defect tests, the entity can arrive at a reliable statistical estimate of the required expenditure.

Illustration 6 • Measurement Single Legal Claim: Most Likely Outcome vs Expected Value
Question: An entity faces a single legal claim where legal counsel assesses:
• 40% probability of success (court dismisses claim with zero payout).
• 60% probability of failure with damages of ₹1,00,000. How should the entity measure the provision?

Under Paragraph 40 of Ind AS 37, where a single obligation is being measured, the individual most likely outcome may be the best estimate of the liability:

Illustration 7 • Measurement Continuous Range of Equally Likely Outcomes (Mid-Point Rule)
Question: An entity has an obligation to clean up environmental contamination. Technical experts advise that the cost will fall within a continuous range between ₹5,00,000 and ₹9,00,000, and each point within that range is as likely as any other. How should the provision be measured?
Solution:

Under Paragraph 39 of Ind AS 37:

Under Paragraph 66–68 of Ind AS 37, an onerous contract is one in which the unavoidable costs of meeting the obligations under the contract exceed the economic benefits expected to be received under it.

Step 1: Compute Least Net Cost of Exiting the Contract:

The unavoidable costs reflect the least net cost of exiting, which is the lower of:

  • Cost of Fulfilling the Contract:
    Contract Purchase Price $= 10,000 \times ₹50 = ₹5,00,000$
    Less: Proceeds from resale $= 10,00,000 \times ₹45 = ₹4,50,000$
    Net loss on fulfillment $= ₹5,00,000 - ₹4,50,000 = \mathbf{₹50,000}$
  • Penal Cost of Cancellation: $= \mathbf{₹60,000}$
$$\text{Provision for Onerous Contract} = \min(₹50,000, ₹60,000) = \mathbf{₹50,000}$$

Conclusion: X Metals Ltd. must recognize a provision for onerous contract of ₹50,000 in Profit or Loss.

Illustration 13 • Restructuring Provisions X Cements Ltd. Constructive Obligation for Unit Closure
Question: X Cements Ltd. has 3 manufacturing units. On 10th January 20X1, the Board of Directors decided to close down operations in one unit for environmental reasons. A detailed formal plan specifying affected employees, compensations, expenditures, and implementation date was approved in that meeting. Before 31st March 20X1, meetings were held with customers, suppliers, and workers where the main features of the closure plan were communicated. Evaluate whether a constructive obligation exists requiring a restructuring provision as at 31st March 20X1.
Solution:

Under Paragraph 72 of Ind AS 37, a constructive obligation to restructure arises only when two cumulative conditions are met:

  1. Detailed Formal Plan: The entity has a detailed formal plan identifying: (i) the business concerned, (ii) principal locations affected, (iii) approximate number and compensation of employees to be terminated, (iv) expenditures to be undertaken, and (v) implementation timeline. (Satisfied on 10th January 20X1).
  2. Valid Expectation Raised: The entity has raised a valid expectation in those affected that it will carry out the restructuring by starting implementation or announcing its main features to those affected before the balance sheet date. (Satisfied through formal stakeholder meetings held prior to 31st March 20X1).

Conclusion: A constructive obligation exists as at 31st March 20X1. X Cements Ltd. must recognize a restructuring provision for direct expenditures necessarily entailed by the closure (e.g. employee termination payments, contract exit penalties).

📝 Test Your Knowledge • Question 2.1
Which of the following conditions must ALL be satisfied simultaneously for an entity to recognize a provision under Ind AS 37?
A. Present obligation arising from a past event + Probable (> 50%) outflow of resources + Reliable estimate of amount
B. Possible obligation + Probable outflow + Reliable estimate approved by the Board of Directors
C. Present obligation + Future planned commitment + Certain (100%) cash expenditure
D. Future obligation from planned restructuring + Outflow is possible (5% to 50%)
Correct Answer: A
Statutory Triad (Para 14):
Under Paragraph 14 of Ind AS 37, a provision shall be recognized when, and only when:
1. An entity has a present obligation (legal or constructive) as a result of a past obligating event;
2. It is probable (> 50% probability) that an outflow of resources embodying economic benefits will be required to settle the obligation; and
3. A reliable estimate can be made of the amount of the obligation. If any one criterion fails, no provision is recognized.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Question 2.2
An entity has operated an oil exploration plant in an emerging country without environmental clean-up laws for 15 years. However, the entity has a widely publicized environmental policy stating it will restore all drilling sites, and in all prior projects it has completely restored sites. Is the entity required to recognize a site restoration provision?
A. Yes, because the entity has created a constructive obligation through its published policy and established past practice

D. Yes, but only in the final year when dismantling operations physically commence
Correct Answer: A
Constructive Obligation Principle (Para 10 & 17):
Under Paragraph 10 of Ind AS 37, an obligating event is an event that creates a legal or constructive obligation that results in an entity having no realistic alternative to settling that obligation. Through its published environmental charter and uniform past conduct, the entity has raised a valid expectation among communities and governments that it will clean up the site. This creates a present constructive obligation requiring a provision.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Question 2.3
A manufacturer of air conditioners sells 50,000 units with a 1-year free replacement warranty. Based on actuarial defect rates: 75% of units will require zero repair; 20% will require minor repairs costing ₹500 each; and 5% will suffer major breakdowns costing ₹3,000 each. What is the provision amount measured under Ind AS 37?
A. ₹1,25,00,000 (Expected Value method)
B. ₹1,50,00,000 (Maximum possible cost of major breakdown)
C. ₹50,00,000 (Minor repair expected outcome)
D. ₹Nil (Warranty is recognized only as actual claims are lodged by buyers)
Correct Answer: A
Expected Value Method (Para 39):
Where the provision being measured involves a large population of items, the obligation is estimated by weighting all possible outcomes by their associated probabilities:
$$\text{Minor Repairs} = 50,000 \times 20\% \times ₹500 = 10,000 \times ₹500 = ₹50,00,000$$
$$\text{Major Breakdowns} = 50,000 \times 5\% \times ₹3,000 = 2,500 \times ₹3,000 = ₹75,00,000$$
$$\text{Total Provision} = ₹50,00,000 + ₹75,00,000 = \mathbf{₹1,25,00,000}.$$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Question 2.4
On 1 April 20X1, an entity recognized a site restoration provision of ₹100 lakhs discounted at 10% p.a. During FY 20X1-20X2, due purely to the passage of time, the carrying amount increases by ₹10 lakhs (10% of ₹100 lakhs). How is this ₹10 lakhs increase presented in the Statement of Profit and Loss?
A. Finance Cost (Borrowing / Interest Expense)
B. Operating Expense under Manufacturing Overheads
C. Capitalized into the cost of Property, Plant and Equipment (PPE)
D. Other Comprehensive Income (OCI) as an actuarial remeasurement
Correct Answer: A
Unwinding of Discount (Para 60):
Under Paragraph 60 of Ind AS 37, where a provision is discounted to present value, the carrying amount of a provision increases in each period to reflect the passage of time. This increase is recognized as a Finance Cost in Profit or Loss. It represents the time value of money unwinding and cannot be capitalized or treated as an operating cost.

When an entity incurs a present obligation to decommission an industrial asset at the end of its 20-year useful life at the time of constructing the facility, what is the initial accounting recognition of the present value of the decommissioning cost?

📝 Test Your Knowledge • Question 2.6
Entity Alpha recognizes a lawsuit damages provision of ₹80 lakhs. Its insurer agrees in writing to reimburse ₹70 lakhs under an indemnification policy, subject to formal claim processing. How must this reimbursement be presented on the Balance Sheet under Ind AS 37?
A. As a separate asset of ₹70 lakhs (not netted off against the ₹80 lakhs liability on the Balance Sheet)
B. Netted off directly against the provision, presenting only a net liability of ₹10 lakhs on the Balance Sheet
C. As a Contingent Asset disclosed solely in the notes until the cash is physically banked
D. Ignored completely until the court lawsuit concludes
Correct Answer: A
Reimbursement Presentation (Para 53):
Where some or all of the expenditure required to settle a provision is expected to be reimbursed by another party, the reimbursement is recognized only when it is virtually certain that reimbursement will be received if the entity settles the obligation. 📝 Test Your Knowledge • Question 2.7
A manufacturing conglomerate forecasts that its division in Region Y will incur operating losses of ₹200 lakhs over the next two financial years due to severe price competition. The CFO proposes creating a provision for future operating losses of ₹200 lakhs. What is the mandatory accounting treatment under Ind AS 37?
A. Provisions shall NEVER be recognized for future operating losses; instead, test the division's assets for impairment under Ind AS 36
B. Recognize a provision of ₹200 lakhs if approved by a special board resolution
C. Recognize the discounted present value of the ₹200 lakhs losses over 2 years
D. Classify the division as a Discontinued Operation under Ind AS 105
Correct Answer: A
Explicit Statutory Prohibition (Para 63):
Paragraph 63 of Ind AS 37 states unequivocally: "Provisions shall not be recognised for future operating losses." Future operating losses do not meet the definition of a liability (there is no past obligating event; the entity can avoid future costs through future operations or closure). An expectation of future operating losses is an indicator that certain assets of the operation may be impaired under Ind AS 36.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Question 2.8
An entity has an onerous contract to supply widgets. Total fulfillment cost = ₹1,200 lakhs, while total revenue from the buyer = ₹900 lakhs (net fulfillment loss = ₹300 lakhs). The contract allows early termination by paying a penalty of ₹180 lakhs. Dedicated production machinery has a carrying amount of ₹50 lakhs and recoverable amount of ₹30 lakhs. What is the correct sequence and amount of accounting entries?
A. First recognize ₹20 lakhs impairment on dedicated machinery under Ind AS 36; then recognize ₹180 lakhs provision for onerous contract under Ind AS 37
B. Recognize only ₹300 lakhs provision for onerous contract; ignore machinery impairment
C. Recognize ₹180 lakhs provision directly without testing machinery for impairment
D. Recognize ₹200 lakhs provision (₹180 lakhs penalty + ₹20 lakhs machinery write-off together as one provision)
Correct Answer: A
Onerous Contract Architecture (Para 68 & 69):
1. Under Paragraph 69, before a separate provision for an onerous contract is established, an entity recognizes any impairment loss that has occurred on assets dedicated to that contract under Ind AS 36:
$$\text{Machinery Impairment} = ₹50 \text{ lakhs} - ₹30 \text{ lakhs} = \mathbf{₹20 \text{ lakhs}}.$$
2. Under Paragraph 68, the unavoidable cost of the onerous contract is the least net cost of exiting, which is the lower of fulfillment cost and termination penalty:
$$\text{Least Cost} = \min(₹1,200 - ₹900, \; ₹180) = \min(₹300, ₹180) = \mathbf{₹180 \text{ lakhs}}.$$
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Question 2.9
In a formal restructuring plan announced to staff prior to the reporting date, which of the following costs is PERMITTED to be included in the restructuring provision under Ind AS 37?
A. Severance compensation for retrenched workers and lease break penalties for terminated facility leases
B. Cost of retraining continuing employees to work on new enterprise software
C. Relocation expenses to transfer retained staff to another operating branch
D. Marketing campaigns to reposition the brand following the restructuring
Correct Answer: A
Allowable Restructuring Costs (Para 80–81):
A restructuring provision includes only direct expenditures arising from the restructuring that are necessarily entailed and not associated with ongoing activities. Paragraph 81 explicitly EXCLUDES:
• Retraining or relocating continuing staff;
• Marketing or brand-building expenditures;
• Investment in new software systems or distribution networks.
Only direct, incremental costs like employee severance and contract break penalties qualify.
📝 Test Your Knowledge • Question 2.10
Under Appendix C to Ind AS 37 (Levies), a government statute specifies that a municipal banking levy is charged to any financial institution that holds a valid operating licence on 1 January 20X2, computed as 1% of the total deposits held on 31 December 20X1. When does the obligating event arise and when must the liability be recognized?
A. On 1 January 20X2, because holding the licence on that specific date is the statutory trigger activity
B. On 31 December 20X1, when the deposit base used to calculate the levy is finalized
C. Accrued monthly throughout the calendar year 20X1 as deposits are received
D. Only when the municipal council issues the assessment bill
Correct Answer: A
Appendix C Obligating Event Rule (Para 8):
The obligating event that gives rise to a liability to pay a levy is the activity that triggers the payment of the levy, as identified by the legislation. TYK Question 1 Income Tax Scrutiny Notice Section 143(2)
Question: During FY 20X1-20X2, Income Tax authorities sent a scrutiny assessment notice under Section 143(2) for return filed for PY 20X0-20X1 on account of an inadmissible deduction claimed by X Ltd. Proceedings are ongoing and no demand notice has been issued yet. Should X Ltd. create a provision or disclose a contingent liability?
Solution:
  1. A scrutiny assessment notice under Section 143(2) is merely an intimation that the tax authorities have selected the return for scrutiny and initiated proceedings. At this stage, no tax demand has been raised.
  2. The company must evaluate based on legal advice whether an adverse assessment is probable, possible, or remote:
    • If an outflow of tax is probable (> 50%) and can be reliably estimated → A Provision is recognized under Ind AS 37.
    • If the company believes its tax position is tenable but an adverse order is possible (5% to 50%) → It discloses a Contingent Liability in the notes, stating the nature of the dispute and estimated financial effect.
    • If the likelihood of an adverse order is remote (< 5%) → No provision or disclosure is required.
TYK Question 2 Chemical Pollution Lawsuit in Supreme Court
Question: An environmental NGO filed a lawsuit against X Chemicals Ltd. for alleged river pollution. The High Court ordered X Chemicals Ltd. to pay compensation of ₹5 crore. X Chemicals Ltd. appealed to the Supreme Court. As on 31 March 20X1, legal experts advise that it is probable (70% chance) that the Supreme Court will uphold the compensation of ₹5 crore, with a 30% chance of complete acquittal. How should X Chemicals Ltd. account for this?
Solution:

Applying the three criteria of Paragraph 14:

  1. Present obligation as a result of past event: River contamination has already taken place (past obligating event), and the High Court judgment creates a legal obligation under dispute.
  2. Probable outflow: Based on senior legal counsel's advice, there is a 70% probability (> 50%) that the liability will be upheld by the Supreme Court. Thus, an outflow of economic benefits is probable.
  3. Reliable estimate: The amount ordered by the High Court is ₹5 crore (most likely outcome for a single obligation).

Therefore, X Chemicals Ltd. MUST recognize a provision of ₹5 crore in its Balance Sheet as on 31 March 20X1, with a corresponding expense in Profit or Loss.

TYK Question 4 Duty Drawback Claim: Contingent Asset to Realised Asset

TYK Question 5 Onerous Contract: Fulfillment Cost vs Cancellation Penalty
Question: An entity entered into a contract to supply goods. Revenue to be received is ₹2.0 million. Due to increase in input costs, total cost to fulfill the contract will be ₹2.5 million. Solution:
  1. Cost of fulfilling the contract = Total cost (₹2.5 million) - Revenue (₹2.0 million) = ₹0.50 million net loss.
  2. Cost of exiting / terminating the contract by paying penalty = ₹0.25 million.
  3.
