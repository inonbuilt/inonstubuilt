Section 02 • Unit 1

Ind AS 1: Complete Set of Financial Statements & General Features

Foundational principles, components of a complete set, and the 8 non-negotiable statutory presentation rules.

1. Components of a Complete Set of Financial Statements (Para 10)

Statutory Set
A complete set of financial statements prepared under Ind AS comprises:
  1. A Balance Sheet as at the end of the period;
  2. A Statement of Profit and Loss for the period (comprising Profit or Loss and Other Comprehensive Income);
  3. A Statement of Changes in Equity (SOCIE) for the period;
  4. A Statement of Cash Flows for the period (prepared in accordance with Ind AS 7);
  5. Notes, comprising material accounting policy information and other explanatory information;
  6. Comparative Information in respect of the preceding period as specified in paragraphs 38 and 38A;
  7. A Third Balance Sheet as at the beginning of the preceding period when an entity applies an accounting policy retrospectively, makes a retrospective restatement of items, or reclassifies items, and that restatement/reclassification has a material effect on the opening balance sheet information.
The Third Balance Sheet Rule (Para 40A–40D):
An entity must present a third balance sheet as at the beginning of the preceding period (e.g. as at 1st April 20X0 for financial statements ending 31st March 20X2) if and only if:
• It applies an accounting policy retrospectively, makes a retrospective restatement, or reclassifies items in its financial statements; and
• The retrospective application, restatement, or reclassification has a material effect on the information in the balance sheet at the beginning of the preceding period.
Note: Related opening notes for the third balance sheet are NOT required (only opening balance sheet columns are required).

2. Eight Core General Features of Financial Statements

Exam Essential
1
True & Fair View and Explicit Compliance Statement (Paras 15–24) Financial statements shall present fairly the financial position, financial performance, and cash flows. An entity whose financial statements comply with Ind AS shall make an explicit and unreserved statement of such compliance in the notes.
Crucial Rule: Inappropriate accounting policies are NOT rectified either by disclosure of the accounting policies used or by notes or explanatory material! Departure from an Ind AS requirement is permissible only in extremely rare circumstances where management concludes compliance would conflict with the objective set out in the Conceptual Framework.
2
Going Concern Assessment (Paras 25–26) Management must assess the entity's ability to continue as a going concern, assessing all available information for at least, but not limited to, twelve months from the reporting period. If management has significant doubt regarding going concern, material uncertainties must be disclosed. When financial statements are not prepared on a going concern basis, that fact must be disclosed together with the basis used and reason why the entity is not a going concern.
3
Accrual Basis of Accounting (Paras 27–28) An entity prepares its financial statements, except for cash flow information, using the accrual basis of accounting.
4
Materiality and Aggregation (Paras 29–31) An entity shall present separately each material class of similar items and items of a dissimilar nature or function unless they are immaterial. Information is material if omitting, misstating, or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence decisions that the primary users make.
5
Prohibition of Offsetting (Paras 32–35) An entity shall NOT offset assets and liabilities or income and expenses, unless required or permitted by an Ind AS.
Permissible Offsetting: Measuring assets net of valuation allowances (e.g., allowance for obsolescence on inventories or allowance for doubtful debts on receivables) is not offsetting. Reporting gains/losses on disposal of non-current assets net of selling costs is permissible because it reflects the substance of the transaction.
6
Frequency of Reporting (Paras 36–37) Financial statements must be presented at least annually. If an entity changes its reporting period (longer or shorter than one year), it must disclose the reason for using a different period and the fact that amounts presented are not entirely comparable.
7
Comparative Information (Paras 38–44) An entity must present comparative information in respect of the preceding period for all amounts reported in current period statements. Narrative and descriptive information must also be included if relevant to understanding the current period's financial statements.
8
Consistency of Presentation (Para 45) The presentation and classification of items shall be retained from one period to the next unless: (a) a significant change in the nature of operations or a review of financial statements demonstrates that another presentation is more appropriate under Ind AS 8; or (b) an Ind AS requires a change in presentation.
Section 03 • Unit 1

Ind AS 1: Balance Sheet & The Landmark 2025 Covenants Amendment

The operating cycle doctrine, classification criteria for assets and liabilities, and the newly amended statutory rules for loan covenants.

1. The Operating Cycle Concept (Para 68)

Foundation
The Operating Cycle of an entity is the time between the acquisition of assets for processing and their realization in cash or cash equivalents.
Operating Cycle Formulation
$$\text{Operating Cycle} = \text{Raw Material Holding Period} + \text{WIP Period} + \text{Finished Goods Holding Period} + \text{Debtors Collection Period}$$
When the entity's normal operating cycle is not clearly identifiable, its duration is assumed to be 12 months.
Multi-Cycle Entity: Where an entity has different identifiable operating cycles for different business lines (e.g. real estate development taking 36 months vs retail grocery taking 2 months), assets and liabilities are classified according to the specific operating cycle applicable to that distinct segment.

2. Current vs. Non-Current Assets Classification (Para 66)

Asset Criteria
An entity shall classify an asset as Current when it satisfies any one of the following four conditions:
  • Condition (a): It expects to realize the asset, or intends to sell or consume it, in its normal operating cycle (e.g. inventory of aging scotch whiskey or raw materials for real estate projects taking 3 years remain current because they are part of the normal operating cycle);
  • Condition (b): It holds the asset primarily for the purpose of trading (e.g., financial assets classified as held for trading under Ind AS 109);
  • Condition (c): It expects to realize the asset within twelve months after the reporting period; or
  • Condition (d): The asset is cash or a cash equivalent (as defined in Ind AS 7), unless the asset is restricted from being exchanged or used to settle a liability for at least twelve months after the reporting period.
All other assets shall be classified as Non-Current.

3. Current vs. Non-Current Liabilities Classification (Para 69)

Liability Criteria
An entity shall classify a liability as Current when it satisfies any one of the following four conditions:
  • Condition (a): It expects to settle the liability in its normal operating cycle (e.g., trade payables and accruals for employee/operating costs are current liabilities even if due to be settled more than 12 months after reporting date, because they form part of operating working capital);
  • Condition (b): It holds the liability primarily for the purpose of trading (e.g., derivative liabilities not designated as hedges);
  • Condition (c): The liability is due to be settled within twelve months after the reporting period; or
  • Condition (d): The entity does not have the right at the end of the reporting period to defer settlement of the liability for at least twelve months after the reporting period.
All other liabilities shall be classified as Non-Current.

🚨 Landmark 2025/2026 Amendment: Liabilities with Covenants (Para 72A–76)

Notified vide Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Second Amendment Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 549(E)) — Mandatory for May 2027 Exams!

This amendment clarifies how an entity assesses its right to defer settlement for at least 12 months when that right is subject to compliance with loan covenants:
Scenario Timing of Covenant Compliance Impact on Balance Sheet Classification
Case 1: Covenants tested ON or BEFORE reporting date Entity must comply with covenants on or before the end of the reporting period (e.g. debt-equity ratio test as of 31st March). Affects classification at the reporting date, even if compliance is tested/evaluated only after the reporting date (e.g. when audited numbers are finalized). If breached on or before 31st March, liability is CURRENT.
Case 2: Covenants tested AFTER reporting date Entity must comply with covenants only after the end of the reporting period (e.g., current ratio tested on 30th June or 31st December). Does NOT affect classification at the reporting date! The loan remains NON-CURRENT at 31st March. However, mandatory note disclosure is required regarding the carrying amount, nature of covenant, and compliance date.
Case 3: Breach and Post-Balance Sheet Waiver Breach occurred on or before 31st March making debt payable on demand. Lender grants a waiver/agrees not to demand repayment in May (before financials are approved). Remains CURRENT! Post-balance sheet waivers are non-adjusting events under Ind AS 10. The right to defer did NOT exist on 31st March.
Case 4: Grace Period granted by Reporting Date Lender agreed by or before 31st March to provide a grace period ending at least 12 months after the reporting date, during which lender cannot demand repayment. Classified as NON-CURRENT, because the entity secured an unconditional right to defer for at least 12 months on or before the reporting date.
Case 5: Equity Conversion Feature Loan includes an option for counterparty to convert loan into entity's equity instruments. Does NOT affect current/non-current classification ONLY IF the conversion option is recognized as an equity component under Ind AS 32. If the option is a derivative liability, it affects classification.

4. Refinancing & Roll-Over Under Existing Loan Facilities (Para 73)

Discretion Rule
If an entity expects, and has the discretion (unilateral right), to refinance or roll over an obligation for at least twelve months after the reporting period under an existing loan facility, it classifies the obligation as Non-Current, even if it would otherwise be due within a shorter period.
However, when refinancing or rolling over the obligation is not at the discretion of the entity (e.g. requires lender's fresh approval or agreement is concluded after the reporting date), the potential to refinance is ignored and the obligation is classified as Current.

Ind AS 1: Profit & Loss, Other Comprehensive Income (OCI), SOCIE & Notes

The total comprehensive income architecture, the critical recycling vs non-recycling OCI divide, and statutory note disclosures.

1. Statement of Profit and Loss Structure (Para 81A–87)

P&L Structure
Under Ind AS 1 and Division II of Schedule III to the Companies Act, 2013, all items of income and expense recognized in a period are presented in a single Statement of Profit and Loss comprising two distinct sections:
  1. Profit or Loss Section: The standard operating and financial performance resulting in Net Profit or Loss for the period;
  2. Other Comprehensive Income (OCI) Section: Items of income and expense (including reclassification adjustments) that are not recognized in profit or loss as required or permitted by other Ind AS.
Total Comprehensive Income Equation
$$\text{Total Comprehensive Income (TCI)} = \text{Profit or Loss for the Period} + \text{Other Comprehensive Income (OCI)}$$
Both Profit/Loss and TCI must be allocated on the face of P&L to (a) Owners of the Parent and (b) Non-Controlling Interests (NCI).
Strict Prohibition of Extraordinary Items (Para 87):
An entity shall NOT present any items of income or expense as extraordinary items in the statement of profit and loss or in the notes. Exceptional items (such as disposal of PPE or litigation settlements) are presented as a separate line item within Profit before Tax, but NEVER termed extraordinary.

2. The OCI Classification: Reclassifiable vs. Non-Reclassifiable

Core Exam Test
Ind AS 1 mandates that items of OCI must be grouped into two strictly segregated categories based on whether they will be reclassified (recycled) to Profit or Loss in subsequent periods:
Category Qualifying Line Items Accounting Treatment on Derecognition
Category A:
Items that will NEVER be reclassified to Profit or Loss
(Non-recyclable)		 1. Remeasurements of defined benefit plans (actuarial gains/losses under Ind AS 19).
2. Changes in Revaluation Surplus of Property, Plant and Equipment (Ind AS 16) or Intangible Assets (Ind AS 38).
3. Fair value changes of Equity Instruments designated at FVTOCI (irrevocable election under Ind AS 109).
4. Fair value changes of financial liabilities designated at FVTPL attributable to changes in the entity's own credit risk. 		Gains or losses accumulated in OCI are NEVER recycled to P&L upon disposal or settlement. They may only be transferred within Equity directly to Retained Earnings (or General Reserve).
Category B:
Items that WILL be reclassified subsequently to Profit or Loss
(Recyclable)		 1. Exchange differences in translating financial statements of a foreign operation (Foreign Currency Translation Reserve - FCTR under Ind AS 21).
2. Effective portion of gains/losses on hedging instruments in a Cash Flow Hedge (Ind AS 109).
3. Fair value changes of Debt Instruments measured at FVTOCI (under Ind AS 109). 		When the underlying transaction affects profit or loss (e.g. sale of foreign operation, realization of hedged item, or derecognition of debt security), accumulated gains/losses are reclassified (recycled) from OCI into Profit or Loss as a reclassification adjustment.

3. Statement of Changes in Equity (SOCIE) (Paras 106–110)

Equity Reconciliations
The Statement of Changes in Equity is a primary financial statement showing a complete reconciliation between opening and closing carrying amounts for each component of equity:
  • Total Comprehensive Income for the period, showing separately amounts attributable to owners of the parent and to non-controlling interests;
  • Retrospective application / restatements under Ind AS 8, showing the cumulative effect on opening retained earnings and reserves;
  • Transactions with owners in their capacity as owners, showing separately contributions by owners (share issuances), distributions to owners (dividends), and changes in ownership interests in subsidiaries that do not result in loss of control.

4. Notes to Financial Statements & Capital Disclosures (Paras 112–138)

Disclosures
The notes must present information systematically and cross-reference each item in the Balance Sheet, P&L, SOCIE, and Cash Flow Statement:
A
Material Accounting Policy Information (Para 117): Disclosing measurement bases and policies that are entity-specific and essential for understanding the statements.
B
Sources of Estimation Uncertainty (Para 125): Disclosing assumptions about the future and other major sources of estimation uncertainty at the reporting date that have a significant risk of causing material adjustments to carrying amounts of assets and liabilities within the next financial year.
C
Capital Management Disclosures (Paras 134–136): Entity must disclose qualitative information about its objectives, policies, and processes for managing capital; summary quantitative data about what it manages as capital; compliance with externally imposed capital requirements (such as bank debt covenants); and the consequences of any non-compliance.

Ind AS 34: Interim Financial Reporting — Components & Periods

Statutory architecture of interim reports, condensed statement requirements, and the mandatory comparative periods matrix.

1. Scope, Objective & Minimum Content (Paras 1–8)

Unit 2 Foundation
An Interim Period is a financial reporting period shorter than a full financial year. An Interim Financial Report means a financial report containing either a complete set of financial statements (as per Ind AS 1) or a condensed set of financial statements.
An interim financial report shall include, at a minimum, the following components:
  • A Condensed Balance Sheet;
  • A Condensed Statement of Profit and Loss;
  • A Condensed Statement of Changes in Equity;
  • A Condensed Statement of Cash Flows; and
  • Selected Explanatory Notes (as mandated by paragraph 16A).
Headings & Subtotals Rule (Para 10):
If an entity publishes condensed statements, those statements must include, at a minimum, each of the headings and subtotals that were included in its most recent annual financial statements, plus selected explanatory notes. Basic and Diluted Earnings Per Share (EPS) must be presented on the face of the condensed Statement of Profit and Loss.

2. Mandatory Comparative Periods Matrix (Para 20)

Frequent Exam Topic
The following matrix specifies the exact periods for which interim statements must be presented:
Financial Statement Current Interim Period Comparative Preceding Period
Balance Sheet As of the end of the current interim period (e.g., 30th Sept 20X1). As of the end of the immediately preceding financial year (e.g., 31st March 20X1).
(NOT the comparable date of the previous year!)
Statement of Profit and Loss (a) Current interim period (e.g., 3 months ended 30th Sept 20X1); AND
(b) Cumulatively for the financial year-to-date (e.g., 6 months ended 30th Sept 20X1). 		(a) Comparable interim period of the preceding year (3 months ended 30th Sept 20X0); AND
(b) Comparable year-to-date period of the preceding year (6 months ended 30th Sept 20X0).
Statement of Changes in Equity Cumulatively for the financial year-to-date (e.g., 6 months ended 30th Sept 20X1). Comparable year-to-date period of the immediately preceding financial year (6 months ended 30th Sept 20X0).
Statement of Cash Flows Cumulatively for the financial year-to-date (e.g., 6 months ended 30th Sept 20X1). Comparable year-to-date period of the immediately preceding financial year (6 months ended 30th Sept 20X0).

Ind AS 34: Recognition, Measurement, Interim Taxes & Impairment

The discrete doctrine, seasonal revenue rules, weighted-average annual tax rate computation, and non-reversal of interim impairment.

1. Discrete Approach vs. Integral Approach

Measurement Doctrine
Under Ind AS 34, an entity shall apply the same accounting policies in its interim financial statements as are applied in its annual financial statements (Para 28). Ind AS 34 adopts the discrete period approach for recognition purposes (each interim period is treated as a distinct discrete accounting period), coupled with year-to-date measurements:
1
Revenues Received Seasonally, Cyclically, or Occasionally (Para 37): Revenues received seasonally, cyclically, or occasionally within a financial year shall NOT be anticipated or deferred for interim reporting purposes if anticipation or deferral would not be appropriate at the end of the financial year. E.g., dividend revenue, seasonal agricultural sales, or retail festive surges must be recognized in the quarter they occur.
2
Costs Incurred Unevenly During the Financial Year (Para 39): Costs incurred unevenly during the financial year (e.g. annual advertising campaign, routine overhaul costs, charitable donations) shall be anticipated or deferred ONLY IF it is appropriate to anticipate or defer that type of cost at the end of the financial year. If an expense does not qualify as an asset at the interim date, it must be expensed immediately!
3
Year-end Bonuses and Variable Incentives: A bonus is anticipated for interim reporting if, and only if, the entity has a present legal or constructive obligation as a result of past events and a reliable estimate of the obligation can be made.

2. Interim Income Tax Expense: Weighted-Average Annual Tax Rate

Exam Favorite
As per Para 30(c) of Ind AS 34, income tax expense is recognized in each interim period based on the best estimate of the weighted-average annual income tax rate expected for the full financial year applied to the pre-tax income of the interim period.
Weighted-Average Annual Effective Tax Rate Formulation
$$\text{Effective Tax Rate (ETR)} = \frac{\text{Estimated Total Annual Tax Expense on Ordinary Income}}{\text{Estimated Total Gross Annual Ordinary Income}}$$
Quarterly Tax Expense = Interim Pre-Tax Ordinary Income × Estimated Annual ETR
Special Items & Capital Gains Rule:
If different income tax rates apply to different categories of income (such as capital gains taxed at a flat 12% or income from special economic zones), to the extent practicable, a separate tax rate is applied to each individual category of interim period pre-tax income.

3. Non-Reversal of Interim Impairment Losses (Appendix A / IFRIC 10)

Strict Rule
An entity shall NOT reverse an impairment loss recognized in a previous interim period in respect of Goodwill or an Investment in Equity Instruments classified as financial assets measured at cost/FVTOCI.
Reason: Because Ind AS 36 prohibits reversing goodwill impairment at any subsequent annual date, permitting reversal merely because of interim timing would create an impermissible inconsistency.

Ind AS 7: Statement of Cash Flows — Cash Equivalents & 3 Activities

The definition of cash and cash equivalents, bank overdraft nuances, and the fundamental three-way classification of cash flows.

1. Cash and Cash Equivalents (CCE) (Paras 6–9)

Unit 3 Foundation
Cash comprises cash on hand and demand deposits.
Cash Equivalents are short-term, highly liquid investments that are readily convertible to known amounts of cash and which are subject to an insignificant risk of changes in value.
The 3-Month Acquisition Rule (Para 7):
An investment normally qualifies as a cash equivalent only when it has a short maturity of, say, three months or less from the date of acquisition. Equity investments are excluded from cash equivalents unless they are, in substance, cash equivalents (e.g., preference shares acquired within 3 months of their specified redemption date).
Bank Overdrafts Nuance (Para 8):
• In the Statement of Cash Flows, where bank overdrafts which are repayable on demand form an integral part of an entity's cash management, they are included as a negative component of Cash and Cash Equivalents.
• In the Balance Sheet, bank overdrafts must be presented within Financial Liabilities (Current Borrowings) and CANNOT be netted against positive bank balances unless the strict legal offset conditions of Ind AS 32 (Para 42) are satisfied. A reconciliation between Balance Sheet cash balances and Cash Flow Statement CCE must be provided in the notes!

2. The Three Activity Streams (Paras 10–17)

3 Pillars
An entity shall report cash flows during the period classified into three mutually exclusive categories:
Activity Definition & Nature Typical Inflows & Outflows
Operating Activities Principal revenue-producing activities of the entity and other activities that are not investing or financing. Primary indicator of operating self-sufficiency. • Cash receipts from sales of goods & services.
• Cash payments to suppliers for goods & services.
• Cash payments to and on behalf of employees.
• Cash payments or refunds of income taxes.
Investing Activities Acquisition and disposal of long-term assets and other investments not included in cash equivalents. Represents expenditures made for resources intended to generate future cash flows. • Cash payments to acquire PPE, intangibles, and other long-term assets.
• Cash receipts from sale of PPE and intangibles.
• Cash payments/receipts for equity or debt instruments of other entities.
• Cash advances and loans made to other parties and their repayments.
Financing Activities Activities that result in changes in the size and composition of the contributed equity and borrowings of the entity. • Cash proceeds from issuing shares or other equity instruments.
• Cash payments to owners to acquire or redeem entity's shares (buy-backs).
• Cash proceeds from issuing debentures, loans, notes, bonds, mortgages, and borrowings.
• Cash repayments of amounts borrowed and principal lease liability payments.

Ind AS 7: Reporting Operating Cash Flows — Direct vs. Indirect Method

Step-by-step mathematical mechanics of constructing operating cash flows under both statutory methods.

1. The Direct Method (Para 18(a))

Gross Cash Flows
Under the Direct Method, major classes of gross cash receipts and gross cash payments are disclosed. Ind AS 7 encourages this method because it provides useful information in estimating future cash flows.
Direct Method Operating Working Computations
$$\begin{aligned} \text{Cash Received from Customers} &= \text{Sales} - (\text{Closing Debtors} - \text{Opening Debtors}) + (\text{Closing Advances} - \text{Opening Advances}) \\ \text{Purchases} &= \text{COGS} + (\text{Closing Inventory} - \text{Opening Inventory}) \\ \text{Cash Paid to Suppliers} &= \text{Purchases} - (\text{Closing Creditors} - \text{Opening Creditors}) \\ \text{Cash Paid for Expenses} &= \text{Operating Expenses} - (\text{Closing O/S} - \text{Opening O/S}) + (\text{Closing Prepaid} - \text{Opening Prepaid}) \end{aligned}$$

2. The Indirect Method (Para 18(b))

Reconciliation Method
Under the Indirect Method, net profit or loss is adjusted for the effects of transactions of a non-cash nature, any deferrals or accruals of past or future operating cash receipts or payments, and items of income or expense associated with investing or financing cash flows:
1
Start with Profit Before Tax (PBT): Net Profit before Tax and Exceptional Items as per Statement of Profit and Loss.
2
Adjust for Non-Cash & Non-Operating Items:
• Add: Depreciation & Amortization, Impairment losses, Unrealized Foreign Exchange losses, Finance costs, Loss on sale of PPE/investments, Share-based payment expense.
• Less: Interest income, Dividend income, Profit on sale of PPE/investments, Unrealized FX gains.
= Operating Profit before Working Capital Changes
3
Adjust for Working Capital Changes:
• Add: Decrease in Current Assets (excluding CCE) & Increase in Current Liabilities (excluding bank overdrafts).
• Less: Increase in Current Assets & Decrease in Current Liabilities.
= Cash Generated from Operations
4
Less: Direct Income Taxes Paid (Net of Refunds):
= Net Cash Flow from Operating Activities
Section 09 • Unit 3

Ind AS 7: Specific Issues — Foreign Currency, Interest, Dividends & Taxes

Treatment of financial institution vs corporate cash flows, unrealized currency adjustments, and financing liability reconciliations.

1. Interest & Dividends Classification Policy (Paras 31–34)

Classification Rules
Ind AS 7 requires separate disclosure of cash flows from interest and dividends received and paid, classified consistently from period to period:
Item Non-Financial Entities (Corporate) Financial Entities (Banks / NBFCs)
Interest Paid Classified as Financing Activity (cost of obtaining capital). Alternatively, may be classified as Operating. Classified as Operating Activity.
Dividends Paid Classified as Financing Activity (cost of obtaining equity resources). Classified as Financing Activity.
Interest Received Classified as Investing Activity (return on investments). Alternatively, may be classified as Operating. Classified as Operating Activity.
Dividends Received Classified as Investing Activity (return on investments). Alternatively, may be classified as Operating. Classified as Operating Activity.

2. Foreign Currency Cash Flows & Unrealized FX Gains/Losses (Paras 25–28)

FX Treatment
• Cash flows arising from transactions in a foreign currency shall be recorded in an entity's functional currency by applying the exchange rate between the functional currency and the foreign currency at the date of the cash flow.
• Unrealized gains and losses arising from changes in foreign currency exchange rates are NOT cash flows.
• However, the effect of exchange rate changes on cash and cash equivalents held or due in a foreign currency is reported in the statement of cash flows as a separate line item at the bottom of the statement in order to reconcile cash and cash equivalents at the beginning and the end of the period.

3. Reconciliation of Liabilities Arising from Financing Activities (Para 44A)

Disclosure Mandate
An entity shall provide disclosures that enable users to evaluate changes in liabilities arising from financing activities, including both:
  • Changes from financing cash flows: Borrowing drawdowns, loan repayments, lease principal payments; and
  • Non-cash changes: Changes from obtaining/losing control of subsidiaries, the effect of changes in foreign exchange rates, changes in fair values, and other non-cash movements (e.g. new lease liabilities recognized under Ind AS 116).
This is customarily satisfied by presenting a tabular reconciliation of opening to closing financing balances in the notes.

Ind AS 7: Supplier Finance Arrangements (Reverse Factoring) — New 2025 Amendment

The newly mandated disclosure framework for supply chain financing, reverse factoring, and payable financing arrangements.

⚡ New Amendment: Supplier Finance Arrangements (Paras 44F–44H)

Notified vide Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Second Amendment Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 549(E)) — Mandatory for May 2027 Examinations!

Definition & Characteristics (Para 44G):
Supplier finance arrangements are characterized by one or more finance providers offering to pay amounts an entity owes its suppliers, and the entity agreeing to pay according to the terms and conditions of the arrangements at the same date as, or a date later than, suppliers are paid. These arrangements provide the entity with extended payment terms, or the entity's suppliers with early payment terms, compared to the related invoice payment due date.
Commonly referred to as: Supply chain finance, payables finance, or reverse factoring arrangements.

What is EXCLUDED from Supplier Finance Arrangements?

Statutory Scope
Arrangements that are solely credit enhancements for the entity (for example, financial guarantees including letters of credit used as guarantees) or instruments used by the entity to settle directly with a supplier the amounts owed (for example, corporate credit cards) are NOT supplier finance arrangements.

Mandatory Disclosures Required under Ind AS 7 (Para 44H)

Core Disclosure Rules
An entity shall disclose in aggregate the following information to enable users to assess the effects of supplier finance arrangements on its liabilities, cash flows, and liquidity risk:
1
Terms & Conditions of Arrangements: Disclose key terms and conditions (e.g. extended payment terms, security, covenants, or guarantees provided). Arrangements with dissimilar terms must be disclosed separately.
2
Carrying Amounts & Associated Line Items:
As at the beginning and end of the reporting period:
(i) The carrying amounts and associated line items presented in the balance sheet (e.g. within Trade Payables or Other Financial Liabilities); and
(ii) The carrying amounts and associated line items of liabilities for which suppliers have already received payment from the finance providers.
3

Ind AS 7: Group Cash Flows — Business Combinations & NCI Transactions

Accounting for acquisitions of subsidiaries, treatment of non-cash equity consideration, transactions with non-controlling interests, and consolidated cash flow eliminations.

1. Acquisition & Disposal of Subsidiaries (Paras 39–42)

Investing Activity
The aggregate cash flows arising from obtaining or losing control of subsidiaries or other businesses shall be presented separately and classified as Investing Activities.
Net Cash Flow from Acquisition of Subsidiary
$$\text{Cash Outflow on Acquisition} = \text{Total Cash Consideration Paid} - \text{Cash and Cash Equivalents Acquired in Subsidiary}$$
Shown under Investing Activities as: "Acquisition of subsidiary, net of cash acquired"
Treatment of Non-Cash Consideration:
If an entity acquires a subsidiary partly for cash and partly by issuing its own equity shares, the equity share consideration is a NON-CASH transaction. Under Para 43 of Ind AS 7, it is completely excluded from the Statement of Cash Flows and disclosed in the notes! Only the cash consideration net of cash acquired enters the cash flow statement.

2. Changes in Ownership Without Loss of Control (Paras 42A–42B)

Financing Activity
Cash flows arising from changes in ownership interests in a subsidiary that do NOT result in a loss of control (such as subsequently buying an additional 10% or selling a 5% stake while retaining control) are classified as Financing Activities!
Reason: Under Ind AS 110, such transactions are treated as equity transactions with owners in their capacity as owners. Therefore, the resulting cash flows are classified in the same way as other transactions with owners.

3. Consolidated Cash Flow Working Capital Adjustment Elimination

Master Technique
When preparing a Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows for an entity that acquired a subsidiary during the year, a common exam trap is calculating working capital changes directly from consolidated balance sheets.
1
Deduct Acquired Balances: From the closing consolidated balance of inventories, trade receivables, and trade payables, deduct the assets/liabilities taken over from the subsidiary at acquisition.
2
Compute True Operating Movement: Compare the adjusted closing balance against the opening balance of the group to isolate true operational working capital cash flows.

Top 5 High-Yield Traps in CA Final FR Chapter 3:

TYK 1.1 • ICAI Study Material
Classification of Inventory and Trade Receivables based on Operating Cycle
Problem Statement:
An entity manufactures passenger vehicles. The time between purchasing of underlying raw materials to manufacture the passenger vehicles and the date the entity completes the production and delivers to its customers is 11 months. Customers settle the dues after a period of 8 months from the date of sale.
(a) Will the inventory and the trade receivables be current in nature?
(b) Assuming that the production time was say 15 months and the time lag between the date of sale and collection from customers is 13 months, will the answer be different?
Detailed Statutory Solution & Analysis:

As per paragraph 66(a) of Ind AS 1, an asset shall be classified as Current if an entity expects to realize the asset, or intends to sell or consume it, in its normal operating cycle.
Paragraph 68 defines the operating cycle of an entity as the time between the acquisition of assets for processing and their realization in cash or cash equivalents.

(a) In Case (a):
• Production & delivery time = 11 months
• Customer collection period = 8 months
• Total length of the Operating Cycle = \(11 + 8 = 19\text{ months}\).
Since the inventory is expected to be consumed/sold and the trade receivables are expected to be realized within this normal operating cycle of 19 months, both the inventory and the trade receivables shall be classified as CURRENT ASSETS, even though the realization period extends beyond 12 months from the reporting date.

(b) In Case (b):
• Production & delivery time = 15 months
• Customer collection period = 13 months
• Total length of the Operating Cycle = \(15 + 13 = 28\text{ months}\).
The answer will NOT be different! Even with an operating cycle of 28 months, because the inventory is consumed/sold and debtors are realized within the entity's normal operating cycle, both inventory and trade receivables continue to be classified as CURRENT ASSETS under paragraph 66(a) of Ind AS 1.
TYK 1.2 • 2025/2026 Amendment Case
Breach of Loan Covenants & Post-Balance Sheet Waiver
Problem Statement:
Company A has taken a long-term loan from Company B. In the month of December 20X1, there was a breach of a material provision of the arrangement. As a consequence of which the loan becomes payable on demand on 31st March, 20X2. In the month of May 20X2, the company started negotiation with Company B not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach. The financial statements were approved for issue in the month of June 20X2. In the month of July 20X2, both companies agreed that payment will not be demanded immediately as a consequence of breach.
Advise on the classification of the liability as current / non-current in line with the amendment in Ind AS 1 effective from annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1st April 2026.
Detailed Statutory Solution & Analysis:

Statutory Provisions under Ind AS 1 (Amended 2025):
1. Paragraph 69(d) of Ind AS 1 specifies that an entity classifies a liability as Current if it does not have the right at the end of the reporting period to defer settlement of the liability for at least twelve months after the reporting period.
2. Paragraph 74 explicitly provides that when an entity breaches a provision of a long-term loan arrangement on or before the end of the reporting period with the effect that the liability becomes payable on demand, the liability is classified as CURRENT, even if the lender agreed, after the reporting period and before the approval of the financial statements for issue, not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach.
3. Under paragraph 75, an entity classifies the liability as non-current only if the lender agreed by the end of the reporting period (i.e. on or before 31st March 20X2) to provide a period of grace ending at least twelve months after the reporting period.

Application to Case Facts:
• As on the reporting date (31st March 20X2), the breach subsisted and the loan was payable on demand.
• No agreement or grace period had been agreed to by Company B on or before 31st March 20X2.
• The negotiations initiated in May 20X2 and the waiver agreed in July 20X2 occurred after the reporting period and represent non-adjusting events.

Conclusion:
Company A must classify the loan as a CURRENT LIABILITY in its Balance Sheet as at 31st March 20X2.

Compliance Statement, Identification, Currency & Related Party Comparatives
Problem Statement:
XYZ Limited is into manufacturing of tractor parts, supplying to OEMs. The Company has no subsidiaries, joint ventures, or associates. During preparation of financial statements for the year ended 31st March 20X1, the accounts department approached you to advise on:
(i) There are qualifications in the audit report of the Company with reference to two Ind AS.
(ii) Is it mandatory to add the word “standalone” before each of the components of financial statements?
(iii) The Company is an Indian Company and presents its financial statements in ₹. Is it necessary to write in the financial statements that they are presented in ₹?
(iv) The Company had sales transactions with 10 related parties during previous year. However, during current year, there are no transactions with 4 related parties out of aforesaid 10. Hence, Company is of the view that it need not disclose sales transactions with these 4 parties in related party disclosures because there are no transactions during current year.
Detailed Statutory Solution & Analysis:

(i) Qualification in Audit Report & Compliance Statement:
As per paragraph 16 of Ind AS 1, an entity whose financial statements comply with Ind AS shall make an explicit and unreserved statement of such compliance in the notes. Financial statements shall not be described as complying with Ind AS unless they comply with all the requirements of Ind AS. Since the audit report contains qualifications regarding non-compliance with two Ind AS, the Company cannot make an explicit and unreserved statement of compliance with Ind AS.

(ii) Mandatory use of the word “Standalone”:
As per paragraph 49 and 51 of Ind AS 1, an entity shall clearly identify each financial statement and distinguish it from other information in the same published document. While an entity must disclose whether the financial statements are of an individual entity or a group, it is NOT mandatory to use the word “standalone” before each component, provided the financial statements clearly state that they pertain to the single entity.

(iii) Disclosure of Presentation Currency:
As per paragraph 51(d) and 51(e) of Ind AS 1, an entity shall prominently display and repeat when necessary: (a) the presentation currency as defined in Ind AS 21, and (b) the level of rounding used (e.g. in Lakhs or Crores). Therefore, it is mandatory to state that the financial statements are presented in ₹ (INR) and disclose the rounding unit.

(iv) Related Party Comparative Disclosures:
As per paragraph 38 of Ind AS 1, an entity shall present comparative information in respect of the preceding period for all amounts reported in the current period's financial statements. Furthermore, paragraph 38 states that comparative information shall be included for narrative and descriptive information if it is relevant to understanding the current period's financial statements. Since sales transactions took place with those 4 parties in the previous year and were included in previous year's financials, the Company MUST disclose the comparative previous year sales transactions with all 10 parties, even though no transactions occurred with 4 of them in the current year.

Company A acquires 70% equity stake in Company B on July 20, 20X1. The consideration paid is:
(a) Cash consideration of ₹ 15,00,000;
(b) 2,00,000 equity shares having face value of ₹ 10 and fair value of ₹ 15 per share (₹ 30,00,000).
On acquisition date, Company B has cash and cash equivalents of ₹ 2,50,000 in its books.
On October 10, 20X2, Company A further acquires a 10% stake in Company B for cash consideration of ₹ 8,00,000.
Advise how the above transactions will be disclosed/presented in the statement of cash flows as per Ind AS 7.
Detailed Statutory Solution & Analysis:

1. Acquisition of Initial 70% Controlling Stake (FY ended 31st March 20X2):
• As per Para 39 & 42 of Ind AS 7, the cash flows arising from obtaining control of a subsidiary are presented separately and classified as Investing Activities, reported net of cash and cash equivalents acquired.
$$\text{Net Investing Cash Outflow} = \text{Cash Consideration Paid } (\text{\rupee } 15,00,000) - \text{Cash Acquired } (\text{\rupee } 2,50,000) = \mathbf{\text{\rupee } 12,50,000}$$
• Non-Cash Equity Consideration: Under Para 43 of Ind AS 7, the 2,00,000 equity shares valued at ₹ 30,00,000 represent a non-cash transaction. It is completely excluded from the Statement of Cash Flows and disclosed in the notes to provide all relevant information about the financing and investing transaction.

2. Acquisition of Additional 10% Stake (FY ended 31st March 20X3):
• As per Para 42A of Ind AS 7, cash flows arising from changes in ownership interests in a subsidiary that do not result in a loss of control are classified as FINANCING ACTIVITIES.
• Accordingly, the ₹ 8,00,000 cash consideration paid to acquire the additional 10% non-controlling interest is presented as a cash outflow from Financing Activities in the FY 20X2–20X3 statement of cash flows.
