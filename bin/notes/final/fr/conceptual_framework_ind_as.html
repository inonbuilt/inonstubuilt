Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting under Ind AS

Explore the foundational bedrock of Indian Accounting Standards: defining the objectives of general purpose financial reporting, fundamental & enhancing qualitative characteristics, rigorous definitions of assets and liabilities, recognition and derecognition criteria, historical cost vs current value measurement bases, and capital maintenance doctrines.
Status: Not a Standard (Standard Overrides)
Key Focus: Economic Resources & Claims
Target Attempts: May 2027 & Nov 2026/2027
1 Purpose and Legal Status of the Conceptual Framework

The Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting under Indian Accounting Standards sets out the fundamental concepts for financial reporting that guide the standard-setter (ICAI/NFRA), preparers of financial statements, and auditors when no specific Ind AS applies to a transaction.

🛠️ For Standard-Setters
Assists the ASB and NFRA in developing Ind AS based on consistent, coherent economic concepts, ensuring standards are harmonized globally.
👩‍💼 For Financial Preparers
Assists management in developing consistent accounting policies under Ind AS 8 when no specific Ind AS applies to a particular transaction or event.
🔍 For Auditors & Users
Assists all parties to understand and interpret existing Ind AS requirements and evaluate management estimates and fair value assumptions.
⚠️
Legal Status of the Framework:
• The Conceptual Framework is NOT an Indian Accounting Standard (Ind AS).
• Nothing in the Framework overrides any specific Ind AS or any requirement in a specific Ind AS.
• In the rare event of a direct conflict between the Framework and an explicit Ind AS requirement (e.g. Ind AS 115, Ind AS 109), the specific Ind AS requirement always PREVAILS.
2 Objective of General Purpose Financial Reporting & Primary Users
Core Objective
"The objective of general purpose financial reporting is to provide financial information about the reporting entity that is useful to existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors in making decisions relating to providing resources to the entity."

Decisions by primary users relate to: (1) buying, selling, or holding equity and debt instruments; (2) providing or settling loans and other credit; and (3) exercising rights to vote on or influence management’s actions that affect the use of the entity’s economic resources (stewardship).

📊 Economic Resources, Claims & Changes:
  • Economic Resources & Claims (Financial Position): Information about the nature and amounts of an entity's assets, liabilities, and equity helps users assess liquidity, solvency, capital structure, and future financing needs.
  • Financial Performance Reflected by Accrual Accounting: Depicts the effects of transactions and events in the periods in which those effects occur, even if the resulting cash receipts and payments happen in a different period.
  • Financial Performance Reflected by Past Cash Flows: Helps users assess how the entity obtains and spends cash, its borrowing and repayment cycles, dividend distributions, and cash liquidity.
3 Qualitative Characteristics of Useful Financial Information

The Framework distinguishes between Fundamental Qualitative Characteristics (which make information useful) and Enhancing Qualitative Characteristics (which increase its usefulness).

Classification Characteristic Components / Definition Exam Keynote
Fundamental Characteristics
(Must be present)		 1. Relevance Financial information is capable of making a difference in the decisions made by users. It has:
• Predictive Value: Can be used as an input to predict future outcomes.
• Confirmatory Value: Provides feedback on previous evaluations.
• Materiality: Entity-specific aspect based on nature and magnitude. 		Omitting or misstating material info could influence decisions.
2. Faithful Representation To be a faithful representation, the depiction must be:
• Complete: Includes all necessary numerical and explanatory info.
• Neutral: Unbiased; supported by the exercise of Prudence.
• Free from Error: No errors or omissions in process or description. 		Faithful representation represents the economic substance rather than mere legal form.
Enhancing Characteristics
(Increase usefulness)		 1. Comparability Enables users to identify similarities and differences across periods and across entities. Consistency refers to using the same methods. Comparison is not uniformity; like things look alike and unlike things look different.
2. Verifiability Different knowledgeable and independent observers could reach consensus that a particular depiction is a faithful representation. Can be direct (counting cash) or indirect (checking inputs and formulas).
3. Timeliness Having information available to decision-makers in time to be capable of influencing their economic decisions. Older info is generally less useful; some trend info remains timely.
4. Understandability Classifying, characterising, and presenting financial information clearly and concisely. Complex transactions cannot be excluded merely because they are difficult!
⚖️
The Cost Constraint on Financial Reporting:
Reporting financial information imposes costs (collecting, verifying, analyzing, disseminating). The benefits derived from providing financial information should justify the costs incurred to provide and use that information.
4 Financial Statements, Reporting Entity & Underlying Assumptions
⏳ Going Concern Assumption
Financial statements are prepared under the fundamental assumption that the entity is a going concern and will continue in operation for the foreseeable future (at least 12 months). If intention or necessity to liquidate exists, statements are prepared on an alternative basis (e.g. liquidation/realisable value).
🏢 The Reporting Entity
A reporting entity is an entity that is required, or chooses, to prepare financial statements. It can be a single entity or a portion of an entity (e.g., a branch), or can comprise more than one entity (e.g., parent and subsidiaries). It is not necessarily a legal entity!
📑 Consolidated vs Combined
• Consolidated Financial Statements: Reporting entity comprises both parent and subsidiaries.
• Unconsolidated Financial Statements: Standalone parent only.
• Combined Financial Statements: Two or more entities not linked by a parent-subsidiary relationship (e.g., entities under common control).
5 The Elements of Financial Statements (Assets, Liabilities, Equity)

The revised Conceptual Framework provides refined, highly objective definitions for the five fundamental building blocks of financial reporting:

Definition of Asset
"An asset is a present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events. An economic resource is a right that has the potential to produce economic benefits."
A
1. Right (Corresponds to an obligation of another party or not)
Rights established by contract, legislation or equivalent: rights to receive cash, goods, services, or physical objects (PPE, inventory), rights to use intellectual property (patents, software), and know-how.
B
2. Potential to Produce Economic Benefits
It does not need to be certain, or even likely, that the right will produce economic benefits. It only requires that the right already exists and that, in at least one circumstance, it would produce for the entity economic benefits beyond those available to all other parties.
C
3. Control
Control links an economic resource to the entity. An entity controls an economic resource if it has the present ability to direct the use of the resource and obtain the economic benefits that may flow from it, and prevent others from directing its use.
Definition of Liability
"A liability is a present obligation of the entity to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events."
  • Obligation: A duty or responsibility that the entity has no practical ability to avoid (legal contracts, legislation, or constructive obligations created by customary practices/published policies).
  • Transfer of an Economic Resource: Obligation must have the potential to require the entity to transfer cash, deliver goods, provide services, or exchange resources on unfavourable terms.
  • Present Obligation as a Result of Past Events: The entity has already obtained the economic benefits or taken an action, and will have to transfer an economic resource it would not otherwise have had to transfer.
⚖️ Equity
The residual interest in the assets of the entity after deducting all its liabilities. Equity claims represent ownership rights and do not carry an obligation to transfer cash.
📈 Income
Increases in assets, or decreases in liabilities, that result in increases in equity, other than those relating to contributions from holders of equity claims.
📉 Expenses
Decreases in assets, or increases in liabilities, that result in decreases in equity, other than those relating to distributions to holders of equity claims.
6 Recognition and Derecognition Criteria

Recognition is the process of capturing for inclusion in the statement of financial position (Balance Sheet) or the statement(s) of financial performance (P&L / OCI) an item that meets the definition of an element.

✅
Universal Recognition Criteria:
An asset or liability is recognized only if recognition of that asset or liability and of any resulting income, expenses or changes in equity provides users of financial statements with:
1. Relevant information about the asset or liability and about any resulting income, expenses or changes in equity; and
2. A Faithful representation of the asset or liability and of any resulting income, expenses or changes in equity.
🚪 Derecognition Criteria:
  • For an Asset: Derecognition normally occurs when the entity loses control of all or part of the recognized asset (e.g., selling goods, expiration of contractual rights).
  • For a Liability: Derecognition occurs when the entity no longer has a present obligation for all or part of the recognized liability (i.e. when the obligation is discharged, cancelled, or expires).
7 Measurement Bases (Historical Cost vs Current Value)

The Conceptual Framework groups all measurement bases into two fundamental categories:

Measurement Base Nature & Key Concept Perspective Standard Examples
1. Historical Cost Reflects the transaction price at which the asset was acquired or liability was incurred, plus transaction costs, reduced by subsequent depreciation, amortisation, and impairment losses. Entry price • Entity-specific historical data • Does not reflect price changes. Ind AS 16 (Cost model for PPE), Ind AS 2 (Inventories at cost), Ind AS 38 (Intangibles at cost).
2. Fair Value The price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at measurement date. Exit price • Market-based perspective (not entity-specific) • Reflects market conditions. Ind AS 113, Ind AS 109 (FVTPL, FVTOCI), Ind AS 103 (Business Combinations), Ind AS 41 (Agriculture).
3. Value in Use / Fulfilment Value • Value in Use (Assets): Present value of cash flows entity expects to derive from use and ultimate disposal.
• Fulfilment Value (Liabilities): Present value of cash entity expects to transfer as it fulfils the liability. 		Exit / Fulfilment • Entity-specific perspective based on entity's own assumptions. Ind AS 36 (Impairment testing), Ind AS 37 (Provisions discounting).
4. Current Cost The cost of an equivalent asset at measurement date, comprising consideration that would be paid at measurement date plus transaction costs incurred. Entry price • Current market conditions reflecting current replacement cost. Inventory valuation under certain replacement cost scenarios.
8 Presentation and Disclosure as Communication Tools

Financial reporting is not merely a data-dump of statutory schedules; it is a structured communication tool. The Framework establishes that effective communication of financial information requires:

🎯 Focusing on Objectives
Focusing on presentation and disclosure objectives rather than rigid boilerplate checklists, ensuring information is entity-specific.
🧩 Aggregating Efficiently
Classifying items together based on shared characteristics (e.g. nature, function, measurement base) and aggregating neither too much (masking details) nor too little (obscuring relevance).
⚖️ Classification in OCI
Income and expenses are included in profit or loss unless specific Ind AS requires inclusion in Other Comprehensive Income (OCI) (e.g. remeasurements of defined benefit plans, revaluation surplus).
9 Concepts of Capital and Capital Maintenance

A firm can state that it has earned a profit only after its capital has been fully maintained. The Framework identifies two primary concepts of capital:

🪙 1. Financial Capital Maintenance
Capital is defined in terms of monetary units or purchasing power (Net Assets / Equity).
• Nominal Monetary Units: Profit is earned only if financial (money) amount of net assets at the end of the period exceeds beginning net assets, excluding equity distributions/contributions.
• Constant Purchasing Power: Profit is earned only if financial capital at end exceeds beginning capital adjusted for general inflation (CPI/WPI).
🏭 2. Physical Capital Maintenance
Capital is defined in terms of physical productive capacity (e.g. units of output per day).
Profit is earned only if the physical productive capacity (operating capacity) of the enterprise at the end of the period exceeds beginning productive capacity, after excluding distributions to and contributions from owners. Price changes affecting assets are treated as capital maintenance adjustments in equity.
10 ICAI Test Your Knowledge (TYK) Problems & Practical Illustrations
ICAI TYK 1 • Asset Recognition
Recognition of Research & Development Know-How as an Asset
Problem Statement:
Pharma Innovations Ltd. spent ₹40 Crores during the year on researching a new molecule for diabetes treatment. The scientists believe there is a 30% probability that the molecule will clear regulatory clinical trials in 3 years and generate substantial future profits. The CFO argues that since there is a potential to produce economic benefits, the ₹40 Crores should be capitalized as an asset under the Conceptual Framework. Evaluate the CFO's argument.
Detailed Solution & Analysis:

1. Conceptual Framework Asset Definition vs Specific Ind AS:
Under the Conceptual Framework, an asset is a present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events. An economic resource is a right that has the potential to produce economic benefits.
However, the Conceptual Framework itself explicitly provides that nothing in the Framework overrides any specific Ind AS.

2. Application of Ind AS 38 (Intangible Assets):
Under Ind AS 38, expenditure incurred during the Research Phase of an internal project can NEVER be capitalized as an intangible asset because an entity cannot demonstrate that an intangible asset exists that will generate probable future economic benefits.
All research expenditure must be recognized as an expense in the Statement of Profit and Loss when incurred.

3. Conclusion:
The CFO's argument is INVALID. Since Ind AS 38 specifically prohibits capitalizing research expenditure, the ₹40 Crores must be charged entirely to Profit and Loss for the year.
ICAI TYK 2 • Liability & Past Event
Present Obligation vs Future Operating Intent
Problem Statement:
Clean Energy Ltd. operates a power plant. The government recently enacted environmental legislation requiring all power plants to install carbon filtration equipment by 31st March 2028, or face severe penalties of ₹25 Crores annually. As on 31st March 2027, Clean Energy Ltd. has not installed the equipment. The accountant wants to recognize a liability of ₹25 Crores as on 31st March 2027. Discuss whether a liability exists as on 31st March 2027.
Detailed Solution & Analysis:

1. Criteria for a Liability under the Framework:
A liability is a present obligation of the entity to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events. For a present obligation to exist as a result of a past event:
• The entity must already have obtained economic benefits or taken an action; and
• As a consequence, the entity has no practical ability to avoid transferring an economic resource.

2. Analysis of Clean Energy Ltd.:
As on 31st March 2027, the deadline of 31st March 2028 has not yet arrived. Clean Energy Ltd. can avoid the penalty by either installing the filtration equipment during FY 2027-28 or choosing to cease operations of the plant prior to the deadline.
The past event that would trigger the penalty is operating the plant after 31st March 2028 without the required filtration equipment. That past event has not yet occurred as on 31st March 2027.

3. Conclusion:
No present obligation exists as on 31st March 2027. Therefore, no liability of ₹25 Crores should be recognized in the balance sheet as of 31st March 2027.
ICAI TYK 3 • Capital Maintenance
Computation of Profit under Financial vs Physical Capital Maintenance
Problem Statement:
Zenith Traders commenced business on 1st April 2026 with a capital of ₹1,00,000 represented by 1,000 units of a commodity at ₹100 per unit. During the year, all 1,000 units were sold for ₹1,60,000. At the end of the year, the replacement cost of the commodity is ₹120 per unit. The general price level index rose by 10% during the year. Calculate the profit under:
(a) Financial Capital Maintenance at historical cost.
(b) Financial Capital Maintenance at current purchasing power.
(c) Physical Capital Maintenance.
Detailed Solution & Analysis:

1. Financial Capital Maintenance at Historical Cost:
• Closing Capital (Cash realized): ₹1,60,000
• Less: Opening Capital at historical cost: (₹1,00,000)
Profit = ₹60,000.

2. Financial Capital Maintenance at Current Purchasing Power:
• Opening Capital adjusted for 10% inflation: ₹1,00,000 × 1.10 = ₹1,10,000
• Closing Capital (Cash realized): ₹1,60,000
• Less: Inflation-adjusted Capital to be maintained: (₹1,10,000)
Profit = ₹50,000.

3. Physical Capital Maintenance:
• Cost to replace physical productive capacity (1,000 units × ₹120): ₹1,20,000
• Closing Capital (Cash realized): ₹1,60,000
• Less: Physical Capital to be maintained: (₹1,20,000)
Profit = ₹40,000 (The ₹20,000 increase in replacement cost is treated as a Capital Maintenance Adjustment recognized directly in Equity).
