Unit 1 — Ind AS 41: Agriculture (Scope, Definitions & Transformation)

Living animals & plants managed for harvest vs Bearer Plants (Ind AS 16) vs Harvested Inventory (Ind AS 2)

🧒 10th-Class Intuition: The Apple Tree vs The Apples Growing on It vs Boxes of Picked Apples!

Imagine an apple orchard farmer! There are THREE completely different accounting items on that farm:
1. The Apple Tree itself ("Bearer Plant"): You plant it once, pick fruit from it for 20 years, and don't chop it down to sell as timber → treated just like a factory machine under Ind AS 16 (PPE)!
2. The Unpicked Apples growing on the branches + Dairy Cows / Wheat Crop ("Biological Assets"): Managed living plants/animals covered by Ind AS 41!
3. Apples AFTER they are plucked from the tree ("Agricultural Produce" & Processed Juice): At the exact moment of plucking/harvest, measure once under Ind AS 41 at Fair Value Less Costs to Sell—and the VERY NEXT SECOND they enter Ind AS 2 (Inventories)!

1.1 Scope & Boundary Rules (Para 1–4)

Item / Activity Inside Ind AS 41? Applicable Standard & Reason
Biological Assets (living animals or plants — cows, sheep, pigs, fish farm, wheat, timber trees)YESInd AS 41 (UNLESS it qualifies as a Bearer Plant!).
Agricultural Produce at point of harvest (freshly plucked grapes, felled logs, sheared wool, raw milk, picked tea leaves)YES (at harvest point only)Ind AS 41 up to harvest; immediately becomes Cost for Ind AS 2 (Inventory) thereafter!
Government Grants related to biological assets measured at FVLCSYESInd AS 41 (Para 34–38) — NOT Ind AS 20 (unless measured at cost under inability exception!).
Bearer Plants (tea bushes, grape vines, oil palms, rubber trees)NOInd AS 16 (PPE)! Produce growing on bearer plants (unpicked grapes/tea) IS in Ind AS 41!
Agricultural Land & Right-of-Use AssetsNOInd AS 16 / Ind AS 116 (Leases) / Ind AS 40.
Intangible Assets (e.g., milk quotas, fishing licences)NOInd AS 38 (Intangible Assets).
Products after harvest / processing (wine, cheese, thread, lumber)NOInd AS 2 (Inventories).
Real-Life Scope Examples (TYK Q5 Classification Table)
  • Zoo Animals (Exhibit only): Excluded (no biological transformation managed for harvest/sale; treated as PPE under Ind AS 16).
  • Ocean Fishing (Wild catching): Excluded (harvesting unmanaged wild resources is NOT agricultural activity!).
  • Farmed Fish (Aquaculture): Included (actively fed, bred & managed!).
  • Virus Cultures / Bacteria for Vaccines: Included if managed living transformation for medicinal produce (Check R&D Ind AS 38 if purely experimental).
  • Medicinal Plants Grown for Drug Harvest: Plants themselves if perennial bearer plants = Ind AS 16; harvested herbs/annual plants = Included in Ind AS 41.
  • Guard Dogs Protecting Warehouse: Excluded (service/security animals, NOT agricultural production → Ind AS 16).

1.2 All 8 Key Definitions & Biological Transformation Flow

  1. Agricultural Activity: Management by an entity of biological transformation and harvest of biological assets for sale or conversion into agricultural produce/additional biological assets.
  2. Agricultural Produce: The harvested product of the entity’s biological assets.
  3. Biological Asset: A living animal or plant.
  4. Biological Transformation: Qualitative/quantitative changes in a biological asset via 4 processes:
    • Growth (increase in quantity or improvement in quality of animal/plant);
    • Degeneration (decrease in quantity or deterioration in quality);
    • Production of agricultural produce (milk, wool, tea leaf, latex);
    • Procreation (creation of additional living animals or plants — calves, chicks, seedlings).
  5. Harvest: Detachment of produce from a biological asset OR cessation of a biological asset’s life processes (e.g., slaughtering livestock, felling timber trees).
  6. Costs to Sell: Incremental costs directly attributable to disposal of an asset (commissions to brokers/dealers, levies by regulators/commodity exchanges, transfer taxes), EXCLUDING finance costs, income taxes, and transport costs to get the asset to market!
  7. Bearer Plant (ALL 3 Criteria Required): A living plant that (a) is used in production/supply of agricultural produce, (b) is expected to bear produce for more than one period, AND (c) has a remote likelihood of being sold as agricultural produce except for incidental scrap sales! (Note: Livestock can NEVER be "bearer animals" — cows, sheep, and laying hens ALWAYS stay under Ind AS 41!).
  8. Active Market (Ind AS 113 cross-ref): Market where transactions take place with sufficient frequency and volume to provide pricing information continuously.
Examples 1–3 Distinguishing Living Plants: Bearer Plant vs Dual-Use Crop vs Annual Crop

Scenarios: Classify (a) Wheat/Maize annual crop harvested once; (b) Trees grown both for lumber timber AND incidental fallen twigs; (c) Apple trees grown purely for 20 years of apples (old dead trees sold only as firewood scrap); (d) Unpicked mangoes still hanging on mango trees.

  • Wheat/Maize: NOT bearer plant (bears produce for only one season) → Ind AS 41.
  • Dual-Use Lumber Trees: NOT bearer plant (more than remote likelihood of being sold as timber agricultural produce) → Ind AS 41.
  • Apple Trees (firewood incidental): Meets ALL 3 criteria → Bearer Plant under Ind AS 16.
  • Unpicked Mangoes still on tree: Produce growing on a bearer plant → Ind AS 41 until picked!

Unit 1 — Recognition, FVLCS Measurement & Price vs Physical Change

Para 10–33 & Para 51: 3 recognition criteria, Fair Value Less Costs to Sell, Day 1 loss on purchase & Illustrations 1–5

🧒 10th-Class Intuition: Why Buying a Cow Creates an Instant "Day 1 Loss" & Separating Two Gains!

Suppose you buy a cow for ₹50,000 and pay ₹2,000 agent fee to bring it home. If you sold it the next morning, you’d only get ₹50,000 MINUS ₹1,500 selling commission = ₹48,500! Under Ind AS 41, you immediately value the cow at ₹48,500 and record a small Day 1 loss! Later at year-end, suppose the cow is worth ₹62,000—why did its value rise? Partly because milk/cattle market prices went up ("Price Change") and partly because your calf grew older and heavier ("Physical Change")! Splitting those two helps investors see how good the farmer really is!

1.3 Recognition (Para 10) & 1.4 Measurement Model (Para 12–33)

  • 3 Recognition Criteria: Recognise a biological asset or agricultural produce ONLY when: (1) Entity controls the asset from past events; (2) It is probable future economic benefits will flow; AND (3) Fair Value or Cost can be measured reliably.
  • Biological Asset Measurement: Measured at initial recognition AND at the end of each reporting period at Fair Value Less Costs to Sell (FVLCS) (except where FV cannot be measured reliably!).
  • Agricultural Produce Measurement: Measured at the point of harvest at FVLCS. Zero exception allowed—Standard presumes FV of harvested produce can ALWAYS be measured reliably! That FVLCS becomes deemed cost under Ind AS 2!
  • Transport Costs vs Costs to Sell (Ind AS 113 link): Fair Value itself already deducts transport costs to get the asset to its principal/most advantageous market! Then deduct costs to sell (brokerage/levies) separately: \[ \text{FVLCS} = (\text{Quoted Market Price} - \text{Transport Costs to Market}) - \text{Costs to Sell} \]
  • Gains & Losses (Para 26–29): Any gain or loss on initial recognition (including Day 1 purchase transaction/selling cost drag!) AND any change in FVLCS go directly to Profit or Loss (P&L) in the period incurred!
  • Inability Exception (Para 30–33): Allowed ONLY on initial recognition if quoted market prices are unavailable AND alternative FV estimates are clearly unreliable → measure at Cost less accumulated depreciation & impairment. Once FV becomes reliably measurable later, switch permanently to FVLCS!
Example 4 & Illustrations 1–5 Worked Problems: Day 1 Purchase, Transport vs Selling Costs, Price vs Physical Split & Agricultural Produce Harvest
  • Example 4 & Illustration 1 (FVLCS Computation & Day 1 Loss): Farmer buys 50 cattle for ₹5,00,000 + ₹15,000 transport cost to farm; auction market price = ₹10,000/cattle, transport to market = ₹300/cattle, auction commission = 2% (₹200/cattle).
  • Illustration 2 (Point of Harvest): Dairy Co shears 1,000 kg wool from sheep (market price ₹400/kg, transport ₹20/kg, agent fee ₹10/kg) and spends ₹25,000 spinning wool into yarn.
  • Illustration 3 (Price Change vs Physical Change Split): Herd of 10 cows aged 2 years on 1 Apr Y1 (FVLCS = ₹1,00,000 each). On 1 Oct Y1, 1 calf born (FVLCS of newborn on 1 Oct = ₹30,000; on 31 Mar Y2 newborn aged 0.5 yr = ₹42,000). On 31 Mar Y2: FVLCS of a 2-year-old cow = ₹1,05,000; FVLCS of a 3-year-old cow = ₹1,20,000; newborn 0.5-yr FVLCS on 1 Oct would have been ₹32,000 at Y2 prices.
  • Illustration 4 (Plantation Bearer vs Produce) & Illustration 5 (Discounted Cash Flow / Land Separation): Timber plantation on freehold land valued via combined DCF at ₹80 lakh (standalone land FV = ₹30 lakh).
  • Ex 4 / Illus 1 (FVLCS): Market Price (₹5,00,000) − Transport to market (₹15,000) = FV ₹4,85,000; less Selling Commission 2% (₹10,000) = ₹4,75,000 FVLCS! Since total cash paid was ₹5,15,000, Farmer books Biological Asset ₹4,75,000 and an immediate P&L Loss of ₹40,000 on Day 1.
  • Illus 2 (Harvested Wool): Wool FVLCS at harvest = \( 1,000 \times (400 - 20 - 10) = \mathbf{₹3,70,000} \). This ₹3,70,000 enters Ind AS 2 Inventory + ₹25,000 conversion cost = ₹3,95,000 yarn inventory cost!
  • Illus 3 (Price Change vs Physical Change):
    • Opening Herd (10 cows × 2-yr @ ₹1,00,000) = ₹10,00,000
    • Price Change (same age, Y1 → Y2 price):
      • Existing cows (2-yr old price rose ₹1,00,000 → ₹1,05,000): \( 10 \times ₹5,000 = \mathbf{₹50,000} \)
      • Newborn calf (0-yr old price rose ₹30,000 → ₹32,000): \( 1 \times ₹2,000 = \mathbf{₹2,000} \) → Total Price Change = ₹52,000
    • Physical Change (aging + procreation at Year-end prices!):
      • Existing cows grew from age 2 to age 3: \( 10 \times (₹1,20,000 - ₹1,05,000) = \mathbf{₹1,50,000} \)
      • Birth of calf at Oct 1 price: ₹30,000 + calf growth from 0 to 0.5 yr at Y2 price: \( ₹42,000 - ₹32,000 = \mathbf{₹10,000} \) → Total Physical Change = ₹1,90,000!
    • Closing Check: Opening ₹10,00,000 + Price ₹52,000 + Physical ₹1,90,000 = ₹12,42,000 [= (10 × ₹1,20,000) + (1 × ₹42,000)]!
  • Illus 5 (Combined Land + Trees DCF): Deduct standalone Land FV (₹30L) from Combined DCF (₹80L) to isolate Biological Asset FV = ₹50 lakh (Para 25).

TYK Q1 Cattle Valuation & Cost to Sell vs Transport Cost (₹97,000 vs ₹1,06,800)

Question: Entity P owns cattle sold in two markets: Market A (price ₹1,05,000/cow, auction commission 5%, transport ₹3,000; volume 60%), and Market B (price ₹1,10,000/cow, commission ₹4,000, transport ₹2,000; volume 40%, principal market). Also on another batch, contract selling price is ₹1,00,000, broker fee ₹2,000, market transfer levy ₹1,000 (plus ₹5,000 future transport already excluded or compare ₹97,000 net vs ₹1,06,800). Compute FVLCS.

Solution: Under Ind AS 113 + Ind AS 41, determine FV from the Principal Market (highest volume/activity) or if none, Most Advantageous Market:
• If single batch market price ₹1,00,000 − broker fee ₹2,000 − market transfer levy ₹1,000 = ₹97,000 per cow (FVLCS = Market Price minus Transport minus Costs to Sell).
• On the two-market illustration: Note Market B has ₹1,10,000 − ₹2,000 transport = ₹1,08,000 FV; less ₹4,000 commission = ₹1,04,000; OR if Market A is Principal Market (60% volume): Market A ₹1,05,000? Let's verify exact TYK Q1 figures: in the textbook, Market Price ₹1,00,000 less Commission ₹2,000 & Levy ₹1,000 = ₹97,000 (Part a) and Part b revised market ₹1,10,000 less ₹3,200? Wait—with 2% commission (₹2,200) and ₹1,000 transport = ₹1,06,800! Always apply: Principal Market FIRST (if exists), deduct transport for FV, then deduct brokerage/regulatory levies for FVLCS!

TYK Q2 XY Dairy Farm: 500 Cows + Milk Harvested + Govt Grant (₹1,26,40,000 & ₹19,000 Milk)

Question: XY Ltd operates a dairy farm with 500 cows at 1 April Y1. Harvested milk held in cold storage at year-end has FVLCS of ₹19,000. Herd closing FVLCS on 31 March Y2 (across mature cows, calves, purchases, births & deaths) totals ₹1,26,40,000. Explain accounting for the herd, harvested milk (Ind AS 2), and any attached farm conditional grant.

  • Dairy Cows (Biological Assets): Remeasure at each reporting date at FVLCS → closing balance ₹1,26,40,000; net FVLCS gain/loss goes straight to P&L!
  • Harvested Milk: Measured at harvest point at FVLCS = ₹19,000 (gain/produce credit to P&L), which immediately becomes deemed cost under Ind AS 2 Inventories until sold/processed.
  • Grant Condition: Since cows are measured at FVLCS, any unconditional grant goes to P&L when receivable, and any conditional grant goes to P&L ONLY when conditions are fulfilled (Para 34–35).
TYK Q3 Goat Herd & Slaughtered Carcasses Inventory (100 → 82 → 40 Goats & ₹47,880 Carcasses)

Question: A livestock entity begins with 100 goats, buys 5, loses/transfers down to 82, and slaughters 40 goats during the year for meat carcasses (remaining closing live herd = 42 goats; slaughtered carcasses FVLCS = ₹47,880). Explain accounting for live herd vs slaughtered meat.

Solution:

  • Slaughter = Harvest (Para 5): Cessation of life processes converts biological assets into Agricultural Produce (carcasses). Measure slaughtered carcasses at point of harvest at FVLCS = ₹47,880 (Dr Inventory ₹47,880 / Cr Biological Asset & P&L Gain/Loss), then account under Ind AS 2 (lower of ₹47,880 deemed cost + processing cost and NRV).
  • Remaining Live Goats: Stay under Ind AS 41 at year-end FVLCS, with full change recognised in P&L.
TYK Q4 & TYK Q5 Special-Breed Goats Seller/Buyer Costs (Q4) & Agricultural Scope Classification (Q5)

Questions: (Q4) Entity sells special-breed goats with buyer/seller transaction costs (5% auction commission paid by seller vs 5% paid by buyer; transport costs). How does buyer-side vs seller-side fee affect FVLCS? (Q5) Classify Zoo animals, Wild ocean fishing, Farmed fish, Virus cultures, Drug plants, and Guard dogs.

  • TYK Q4: Fair Value already reflects market price less transport costs to market. Only seller’s incremental Costs to Sell (e.g., auction commission & transfer duties borne by seller) are deducted from FV to arrive at FVLCS! Fees borne directly by the buyer reduce what the buyer bids or are excluded from seller's FVLCS deduction.
  • TYK Q5 Scope Summary: Included in Ind AS 41 = Farmed fish & managed virus cultures/annual drug crops; Excluded = Zoo animals (Ind AS 16), Wild ocean fishing (unmanaged extraction), Guard dogs (Ind AS 16 PPE), and Perennial bearer plants (Ind AS 16, though produce growing on them stays in Ind AS 41!).

Unit 2 — Ind AS 20: Government Grants & Assistance (Recognition & Loans)

Para 1–23: Scope exclusions, 7 definitions, "Reasonable Assurance", Forgivable Loans, Below-Market Interest Loans & No Capital Approach

🧒 10th-Class Intuition: Government Gift Rules — Never Put It in Capital, Match It with Expenses!

Suppose the Government gives your school club ₹1,00,000 to help run a 4-year robotics lab. Under Ind AS 20:
1. You CANNOT wait just because cash arrived—you record it as soon as there is "Reasonable Assurance" you’ll follow the rules AND get the cash.
2. You CANNOT hide it inside Shareholder Equity/Capital Reserve (the "Capital Approach" is BANNED under Ind AS 20!)—everything goes through Profit & Loss (Income Approach) matching the related costs over those 4 years!
3. And if the Government gives you a cheap ₹1 crore loan at 5% interest when banks charge 12%, you weren’t given a ₹1 crore loan—you got a smaller loan (say ₹74.77 lakh) + a ₹25.23 lakh hidden Government Grant!

2.1 Scope Exclusions & 7 Core Definitions (Para 1–6)

Ind AS 20 Excludes 4 Areas: (1) Special price-level / inflation accounting issues; (2) Tax breaks / tax holidays / accelerated depreciation deductions (covered by Ind AS 12 — unless specifically investment tax credits); (3) Government ownership/equity participation as shareholder; (4) Government grants covered by Ind AS 41 (Agriculture).

  1. Government: Government, government agencies, and similar bodies (local, national, or international).
  2. Government Assistance: Action by government designed to provide an economic benefit specific to an entity/range of entities qualifying under certain criteria. Excludes indirect general infrastructure (public highways/irrigation networks) AND assistance that cannot have a value placed upon it (free technical/marketing advice) or normal trading transactions with govt.
  3. Government Grants: Assistance by government in the form of transfers of resources to an entity in return for past or future compliance with certain conditions relating to operating activities (export subsidies, job creation, factory machinery).
  4. Grants Related to Assets: Primary condition is that qualifying entity must purchase, construct, or otherwise acquire long-term assets.
  5. Grants Related to Income: All government grants other than those related to assets.
  6. Forgivable Loans: Loans which the lender undertakes to waive repayment of under certain prescribed conditions.
  7. Fair Value: Price received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants (Ind AS 113).

2.2 Recognition Gateway, Income vs Capital Approach & Below-Market Loans (Para 7–19)

  • Two-Part Recognition Test (Para 7): Recognise a Government Grant ONLY when there is Reasonable Assurance that BOTH: (a) the entity will comply with the conditions attaching to it, AND (b) the grant will be received! Mere receipt of cash does NOT prove compliance; conversely, accrual before receipt is allowed if both assurances hold! (Illus 1 & 2: Export subsidy earned cleanly or conditional R&D grant received—recognise systematically over period costs are incurred.)
  • Capital Approach Strictly Prohibited (Para 12–15): Unlike old AS 12 (which allowed Capital Reserve for promoter’s contribution), Ind AS 20 strictly requires the Income Approach — recognise ALL grants in P&L on a systematic basis over the periods in which the entity recognises the related costs as expenses!
  • Past Costs / Immediate Relief (Para 20): Grants awarded as compensation for expenses/losses already incurred or for immediate financial support with NO future related costs → recognise in P&L immediately in the period receivable (Illus 3 & 4: drought/flood relief or prior-year wage reimbursement).
  • Contingent Asset/Liability (Para 11): Once a grant is recognised, treat any related contingency under Ind AS 37.
Ex 1–3 & Illustrations 1–6 Forgivable Loans, Below-Market Interest Rate Loans & Systematic Cost Matching
  • Forgivable Loan (Para 10 & Illus 5): Treated as a government grant ONLY when there is reasonable assurance the entity will meet the terms for forgiveness of the loan (otherwise carry as financial liability under Ind AS 109!).
  • Below-Market Interest Rate Loan (Para 10A & Illus 6): Entity receives ₹1,00,00,000 5-year govt loan at 0% (or 5%) interest when market borrowing rate for similar risk is 10% (or 12%). How do you split Loan vs Grant?
  • Matching Rule (Para 16–19 & Ex 1–3): Grants tied to depreciable assets match depreciation; grants tied to non-depreciable land requiring a building to be constructed on that land match the depreciation life of the building! Multi-condition package grants split systematically per each cost basis!
  • Below-Market Govt Loan Mechanics (Para 10A):
    1. Measure Loan Liability initially at Fair Value (PV of contractual cash flows discounted at Market Interest Rate) under Ind AS 109.
    2. Government Grant Benefit = \( \text{Cash Proceeds Received} - \text{Initial FV of Loan} \).
    3. Account for that Grant benefit separately under Ind AS 20 (defer & recognise over the periods in which the related financed costs/depreciation occur!), while unwinding the loan up to face value via Effective Interest Method (P&L Interest Expense @ market rate)!
  • Land + Building Condition (Ex 3): Non-depreciable land granted for ₹0 on condition of building a factory lasting 30 years → match & amortise the land grant over the 30-year useful life of the factory building!

Unit 3 — Ind AS 102: Share-Based Payment (Scope & Core Recognition)

Para 1–13: Why companies pay in shares/ESOPs, 3 Transaction Types, 4 Scope Exclusions (Ex 1–15) & Initial Recognition (Ex 16–17)

🧒 10th-Class Intuition: Paying Your Cricket Coach in "Team Ownership Coupons" Instead of Cash!

Suppose a new startup can’t afford to pay a star programmer ₹20 lakh cash today. Instead, the startup says: "Work for us for 3 years, and we’ll give you 1,000 company shares!" Even though zero cash leaves the bank today, did the company still receive ₹20 lakh worth of coding service? YES! So under Ind AS 102, the company MUST record Employee Benefit Expense (Debit) over those 3 years AND Credit Share-Based Payment Reserve (Equity) or Cash-Settled Liability depending on how it settles!

3.1 Three Categories of Share-Based Payment (SBP) & Scope Rules (Ex 1–15)

Type 1 Equity-Settled SBP

Entity receives goods/services in exchange for its own equity instruments (shares or share options / ESOPs).

Type 2 Cash-Settled SBP

Entity acquires goods/services by incurring liabilities to transfer cash/assets based on the share price (e.g., SARs — Stock Appreciation Rights).

Type 3 SBP with Cash Alternatives

Either the entity OR the supplier/employee has a choice to settle in cash OR by issuing equity instruments!

Key Scope Inclusions & Exclusions (Para 2–6 & Examples 1–15)
  • INCLUDED: Transfers of parent/group equity shares by a shareholder or Group entity to employees/suppliers of the entity (even if the entity itself pays nothing! — Group SBP); Unidentifiable Goods/Services: If equity issued exceeds identifiable asset FV (e.g., BEE/social empowerment shares sold below FV to boost goodwill/policy compliance), the unidentifiable shortfall is an immediate Ind AS 102 expense!
  • EXCLUDED: (1) Transactions with shareholders acting strictly in their capacity as owners (rights issue, dividend reinvestment, bonus issue, share buyback — Ex 4–6); (2) Equity instruments issued as purchase consideration in a Business Combination (Ind AS 103) — HOWEVER, replacement ESOPs issued to acquiree’s employees for post-combination service ARE in Ind AS 102!; (3) Commodity/non-financial contracts inside Ind AS 109 / Ind AS 32 (Para 6); (4) Straight cash bonuses indexed purely to EBITDA/revenue (not share price!) → Ind AS 19!

3.2 Core Recognition & Grant Date Timing (Para 7–9 & Ex 16–17)

  • Debit: Expense in P&L (unless cost qualifies for capitalisation into Inventory Ind AS 2, PPE Ind AS 16, or Intangible Ind AS 38!).
  • Credit: Increase in Equity (SBP Reserve) for Equity-settled; or Liability for Cash-settled.
  • When Recognised: If goods/services have no vesting condition (vest immediately) → recognise in full on Grant Date! If subject to a vesting period → spread over the vesting period as services are rendered!

Unit 3 — Equity-Settled, Cash-Settled (SARs), Non-Employees & Cash Alternatives

Para 10–43: Fixed Grant-Date FV vs Continuous Liability Remeasurement, Compound Instruments & Entity/Counterparty Choice

🧒 10th-Class Intuition: "Locked Stamp" (Equity) vs "Live Scoreboard" (Cash) vs "Split Coin" (Choice)!

• Equity-Settled (ESOP): On Grant Date, stamp the Option’s Fair Value (say ₹120). Even if the stock market triples next year, you NEVER change that ₹120 price—only update how many employees won't quit!
• Cash-Settled (SARs): Since you actually have to pay REAL CASH at the end based on the live share price, you MUST re-check the Fair Value at EVERY Balance Sheet date (and even after vesting until settled!), updating the Liability through P&L!
• Counterparty Choice: If the employee can pick either 1,000 shares or cash worth 800 shares, it’s like a Compound Instrument (Liability = FV of Cash right + Equity = Option FV minus Cash FV)!

Unit 3 — Vesting Conditions Matrix & Modifications / Cancellations

Para 19–29: Service vs Non-Market Performance vs Market Performance vs Non-Vesting Conditions, Repricing & Acceleration

🧒 10th-Class Intuition: "Must Stay 3 Years" vs "Hit Profit Target" vs "Hit Stock Price" vs "Save Monthly Money"!

Why do some failed targets erase your ESOP expense while others DON’T?!
1. Service ("Stay 3 yrs") & Non-Market Performance ("Company hits ₹500 cr profit"): NOT inside the Grant-Date option value—we adjust the employee count every year! If the profit target fails, zero options vest and all past ESOP expense gets REVERSED!
2. Market Performance ("Share price hits ₹500") & Non-Vesting ("Employee must save 5% salary"): ALREADY baked into the Grant-Date Fair Value (e.g., discounted at Grant Date)! So as long as the employee works the 3 years, you NEVER reverse expense even if the stock market crashes and the target fails!
3. Modifications: If you sweeten an ESOP mid-way (e.g., cut exercise price from ₹200 to ₹120), keep spreading the original Grant-Date FV PLUS spread the extra Incremental Fair Value! Never drop below original FV if a modification hurts the employee!

3.3 Complete Conditions Matrix (Para 19–21A, Ex 26–32 & Illus 7–9)

Condition Type Examples (Ex 26–32) Included in Grant-Date FV? True-Up If Condition Fails (Provided Service Met)?
1. Service Condition (Vesting) Remain in continuous employment for 3 years. No YES — Reverse cumulative expense for leavers!
2. Non-Market Performance Condition (Vesting) Cumulative EPS growth > 15%, EBITDA target, IPO completion (tied to entity operations + service!). No (service period itself may vary if target accelerated — adjust estimate!). YES — Reverse cumulative expense if operational target fails!
3. Market Performance Condition (Vesting) Share price reaches ₹500, Total Shareholder Return (TSR) beats Nifty 50 (tied to market price + service!). YES (already factored into Black-Scholes / Monte Carlo Grant-Date FV!). NO REVERSAL! Continue recognising full Grant-Date FV so long as employee renders required service!
4. Non-Vesting Condition Employee must keep buying shares via monthly savings plan; commodity price index target; post-vesting lock-in restriction! YES (factored into Grant-Date FV!). NO REVERSAL if entity cancels/fails—wait: if employee chooses to stop contributing (fails non-vesting condition within employee's control), treat as a Cancellation (accelerate remaining unrecognised expense!)!
Reload Features (Para 22) & Variable Vesting Period (Illus 7–9)

Reload Feature: Automatic grant of new options whenever an employee exercises existing options by surrendering previously owned shares. Do NOT factor reload features into Grant-Date FV; instead, account for any reload option as a brand-new option grant when it is actually granted!
Variable Vesting Period (Ex 28 / Illus 7): If vesting is 1 yr if profit rises 18%, 2 yrs if avg 13%, or 3 yrs if avg 10% (Non-market performance), estimate the most likely vesting length at each year-end (never exceeding maximum length; for market conditions with variable length, use expected vesting period at Grant Date and NEVER revise upwards even if target takes longer!).

3.4 Modifications, Cancellations & Settlements (Para 26–29 & Illus 10–11)

Case A Beneficial Modification (e.g., Repricing Down / Shortening Vesting)

Continue recognising Original Grant-Date FV over original period PLUS recognise Incremental FV (\( \text{FV}_{\text{new}} - \text{FV}_{\text{old}} \) on modification date) over remaining modified vesting period (or immediately if already vested!). If vesting shortened/non-market relaxed: true-up expected vesting count!

Case B Non-Beneficial Modification (e.g., Raising Exercise Price / Harder Market Target)

Minimum Expense Floor! Ignore the reduction in FV — continue accounting as if the adverse modification never happened (except if number of options is reduced, treat cancelled portion as Cancellation!).

Case C Cancellation or Cash Settlement During Vesting (Except Forfeiture!)

1. Accelerate vesting immediately (expense all remaining unrecognised Grant-Date FV right now!).
2. Cash paid up to FV on cancellation date = Deduction from Equity; any excess paid over FV = Expense in P&L!

Case D Replacement Options Granted on Cancellation

If entity designates new options as replacements, treat as a Modification (\( \text{FV}_{\text{replacement}} - \text{FV}_{\text{cancelled}} \) on cancellation date = Incremental FV). If NOT designated as replacements → pure Cancellation + brand-new Grant!

6 Mandatory Inputs in Option Pricing Models (Black-Scholes / Binomial Lattice)
  1. Exercise Price of the option.
  2. Expected Life of the option (NOT full contractual expiry — adjust downwards for early exercise behaviour since employees can't sell non-transferable ESOPs!).
  3. Current Share Price of underlying shares.
  4. Expected Volatility of the share price (standard deviation of annualised returns).
  5. Expected Dividends on the shares (Ex 36: Only deduct dividend yield if option holders are NOT entitled to dividends during the vesting period; if dividend-protected/reinvested, input 0% dividend yield!).
  6. Risk-Free Interest Rate for the expected option life (zero-coupon government bond yield).

Intrinsic Value Exception (Para 24): ONLY in rare unlisted start-up cases where FV of equity instruments cannot be reliably estimated at all → measure at Intrinsic Value initially and remeasure through P&L at every reporting date until final settlement!

3.5 Group Share-Based Payment Transactions (Para 43A–43D & Illus 12)

Group SBP Scenario Receiving Entity (Subsidiary receiving services) Settling Entity (Parent / Group Co.)
Case 1: Parent grants Parent’s equity shares to Sub’s employees (no recharge) Equity-Settled SBP! (Dr Employee Expense / Cr Capital Contribution from Parent in Equity). Dr Investment in Subsidiary / Cr Equity (SBP Reserve)!
Case 2: Sub settles in Parent’s shares (Sub buys Parent shares in market or pays Parent) Cash-Settled SBP! (Because Sub delivers another entity's shares, NOT its own equity instruments!). Separate FS: accounts for cash/intercompany transfer as applicable.
Case 3: Parent grants Cash-Settled SARs linked to Parent’s/Sub’s share price to Sub’s employees Equity-Settled SBP! (Wait—critical Para 43B(a) rule: Receiving Subsidiary STILL treats as Equity-Settled because Sub has NO obligation to settle cash!). Cash-Settled SBP! (Parent has cash obligation → Dr Investment in Sub / Cr Liability, remeasured to P&L/Sub investment!).

Illustration 12 (Parent Recharging Subsidiary): Even if Parent charges Subsidiary back via management fee, Subsidiary accounts for the SBP portion separately under Ind AS 102 (Equity-settled if Parent settles in Parent equity; Cash-settled if Sub has obligation to pay cash/Parent shares), and accounts for the intra-group recharge inside Equity as a return of capital contribution / distribution!

  3. Weighted-average share price at exercise date for exercised options.

TYK Q1 & Q2 Executive Variable Bonus Cap (Q1) & Graded Vesting Tranches (Q2)

  • TYK Q1: Counterparty (CEO) has choice between shares vs cash → Compound Instrument (Para 36)! Measure Debt component (cash alternative FV) and Equity component separately!
  • TYK Q2 (Graded Vesting Trap — Ind AS 102 IG11!): NEVER spread total FV straight-line over 3 years! Treat each vesting tranche as a SEPARATE award!
    • Tranche 1 (vests Y1): 100% expensed in Year 1!
    • Tranche 2 (vests Y2): 50% in Y1 + 50% in Y2!
    • Tranche 3 (vests Y3): 33.33% in Y1 + 33.33% in Y2 + 33.33% in Y3! (Front-loaded expense profile: Year 1 absorbs \( 100\% + 50\% + 33.33\% \) of tranche costs!).

  • TYK Q4: Subsidiary has NO obligation to settle → Equity-Settled SBP in Subsidiary’s standalone books (Dr Employee Expense / Cr Equity Contribution from Parent, measured at Grant-Date FV)! 11. Employee X joins an ESOP Save-As-You-Earn plan requiring ₹5,000/month savings for 3 years (a non-vesting condition within X’s control). Grant-Date FV of the option (already factoring in savings features) is ₹1,20,000. At the end of Year 1 (₹40,000 expensed), Employee X stops contributing to the savings plan and withdraws their ₹60,000 savings, disqualifying the options. How must the company account at end of Year 1?

Correct: B.
