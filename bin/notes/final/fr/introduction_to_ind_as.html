Module 1 • Chapter 1 • Foundational Framework

Introduction to Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS)

Master the statutory framework of Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS), understanding the transition from Indian GAAP (AS), the philosophy of Convergence with Carve-outs, the phased corporate and NBFC roadmap, and the structural requirements of Division II of Schedule III.
Target Syllabus: May 2027 & Nov 2026/2027
Key Threshold: Net Worth ≥ ₹250 Cr / ₹500 Cr
Governing Law: Companies Act 2013 (Sec 133)
1 Chapter Overview & Master Concept Matrix

Financial reporting is the language of business that connects an entity with external stakeholders: equity investors, debt providers, regulators, credit rating agencies, and tax authorities. As Indian businesses rapidly integrated into the global economy through cross-border M&A, foreign direct investments (FDI), and overseas listings (ADR/GDR/ECBs), the historical Accounting Standards (AS) notified under the Companies (Accounting Standards) Rules, 2006 proved inadequate.

💡 What are Ind AS?
Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) are IFRS-converged standards notified by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013, formulated by the ICAI and recommended by the National Financial Reporting Authority (NFRA).
⚖️ Principles-Based Approach
Unlike old Indian GAAP which relied heavily on rigid form-over-substance rules, Ind AS is strictly substance-over-form and fair value driven, demanding substantial management judgment and rigorous disclosures.
🔒 Irrevocable Application
Once an entity becomes liable to prepare its accounts under Ind AS (either mandatorily or voluntarily), it can never switch back to Indian GAAP ("Once an Ind AS, always an Ind AS").
ℹ️
Core Exam Focus: In CA Final Financial Reporting examinations, Chapter 1 questions primarily test the exact net worth calculation for corporate/NBFC applicability, the treatment of overseas subsidiaries in consolidated accounts, the distinction between Carve-outs vs Carve-ins, and Division II Schedule III balance sheet classification.
2 Indian Scenario Prior to Ind AS & Limitations of AS

Prior to 2015, Indian companies prepared financial statements based on the Accounting Standards (AS 1 to AS 32) formulated by the ICAI and notified under the Companies (Accounting Standards) Rules, 2006. While AS brought consistency across Indian industries, several structural deficiencies emerged as business complexity exploded.

Dimension Old Indian GAAP (AS) Modern Ind AS (Converged IFRS)
Measurement Base Predominantly Historical Cost; conservatism was often biased toward understating assets. Mixed measurement model; Fair Value is heavily applied (FVTPL, FVTOCI, Business Combinations).
Financial Instruments Fragmented (AS 30, 31, 32 were never formally notified by MCA; AS 13 was basic cost/market rule). Comprehensive governance under Ind AS 32, 109, and 107 using the Expected Credit Loss (ECL) model.
Revenue Recognition AS 9 focused strictly on the legal transfer of significant risks and rewards of ownership. Ind AS 115 enforces a 5-step model based on transfer of control over performance obligations.
Lease Accounting AS 19 recognized operating leases off-balance-sheet with zero asset/liability recognized by lessee. Ind AS 116 eliminates off-balance-sheet leases; mandates Right-of-Use (ROU) Asset & Lease Liability.
Business Combinations AS 14 focused on amalgamation with amalgamation reserve / goodwill at book values. Ind AS 103 mandates the Acquisition Method at Fair Value; non-controlling interests (NCI) recognized at FV.
3 Emergence of Global Standards & Need in India

With cross-border capital flows exceeding trillions of dollars annually, investors demanded financial statements that could be compared side-by-side whether an entity was incorporated in Mumbai, London, Tokyo, or New York. The International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), issued by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB), emerged as the global benchmark accepted across 140+ countries.

1
Inflow of Foreign Capital & FPI Confidence
Global private equity funds, sovereign wealth funds, and Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) avoid markets with unfamiliar local accounting rules. Ind AS provides transparent, globally verifiable numbers.
2
Reduced Cost of Capital on Global Debt & Equity Markets
Indian conglomerates raising funds through External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs), Masala Bonds, or ADRs/GDRs previously had to perform dual-audits and reconciliation statements (often costing millions). Ind AS eliminates dual accounting.
3
Enhanced Cross-Border M&A Capabilities
Indian companies acquiring foreign targets or forming global joint arrangements can evaluate synergies and conduct financial due diligence seamlessly using identical accounting metrics.
4 Convergence vs Adoption • Carve-Outs and Carve-Ins

When transitioning to IFRS, countries generally choose one of two distinct routes:

  • Adoption: The country adopts the exact text of IFRS as issued by the IASB without any modifications, replacing domestic GAAP entirely.
  • Convergence: The country aligns its domestic standards with IFRS, creating national standards that closely mirror IFRS but retain specific deviations (carve-outs/carve-ins) to reflect local economic conditions, legal frameworks, and regulatory realities.
⚠️
Why India Chose Convergence: India's unique corporate ownership structures (family-owned promoter-dominated conglomerates), legal constraints in the Companies Act, and historical foreign currency borrowing practices necessitated custom adjustments termed Carve-outs and Carve-ins.
✂️ What is a Carve-Out?
A Carve-out occurs when an Ind AS departs from an IFRS requirement by eliminating an accounting treatment or modifying a standard to suit Indian conditions. E.g., Appendix C to Ind AS 103 requires pooling of interest method for common-control business combinations, whereas IFRS 3 does not govern common-control transactions.
➕ What is a Carve-In?
A Carve-in occurs when an Ind AS provides additional guidance, options, or transitional relief not found in the original IFRS standard. E.g., Optional continuation of AS 11 Para 46A capitalizing foreign exchange differences on long-term foreign currency monetary items into fixed assets under Ind AS 101.
5 Standard Setting & Finalisation Process

The formulation and notification of an Indian Accounting Standard follows a strict four-tier institutional hierarchy established by the Companies Act, 2013:

Tier 1
ICAI Accounting Standards Board (ASB)
ASB prepares the initial draft of the Ind AS based on corresponding IAS/IFRS, invites stakeholder feedback, holds public outreach meetings, and reviews potential carve-outs. The Council of ICAI approves the standard.
Tier 2
National Financial Reporting Authority (NFRA)
Under Section 133, NFRA examines the draft submitted by the ICAI, assesses regulatory harmony with RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, and recommends the final standard to the Central Government.
Tier 3
Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) Notification
The Central Government officially notifies the standard in the Official Gazette under the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, granting it statutory force of law across India.
6 Structure and Numbering Scheme

To enable international comparability while retaining identity as an Indian statute, the MCA devised an elegant numbering formula:

  • Standards corresponding to International Accounting Standards (IAS 1 to 41) are numbered by adding 100 to the IAS number. E.g., IAS 1 becomes Ind AS 1; IAS 16 becomes Ind AS 16; IAS 38 becomes Ind AS 38.
  • Standards corresponding to International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS 1 to 17) retain their exact IFRS number preceded by Ind AS 100 series. E.g., IFRS 3 becomes Ind AS 103; IFRS 9 becomes Ind AS 109; IFRS 15 becomes Ind AS 115; IFRS 16 becomes Ind AS 116.
📚
Standard Layout within an Ind AS: Every Ind AS contains: (1) Objective, (2) Scope, (3) Key Definitions, (4) Recognition & Classification, (5) Measurement (Initial & Subsequent), (6) Derecognition, (7) Disclosures, (8) Appendices (Integral parts containing Carve-outs or interpretations), and (9) Comparison with IFRS/IAS.

🧮
Statutory Net Worth Formula (Section 2(57) of Companies Act, 2013):
Net Worth = Paid-up Share Capital
          + All Reserves created out of profits
          + Securities Premium Account
          + Debit or Credit balance of P&L Account
          − Accumulated Losses
          − Deferred Expenditure and Miscellaneous Expenditure not written off
* EXCLUSIONS: Revaluation Reserve, Capital Reserve from amalgamations, Capital Redemption Reserve, Debenture Redemption Reserve, and Share Application Money Pending Allotment.
8 Group Companies & Overseas Entities Governance

One of the most heavily tested examination concepts is how Ind AS applicability cascades across related entities within a corporate conglomerate:

🔗 Group Cascading Principle
If an Indian company crosses the net worth threshold or lists its shares, Ind AS automatically becomes mandatory for its Holding Company, Subsidiary Companies, Associate Companies, and Joint Ventures, regardless of their standalone net worth!
🌏 Overseas Subsidiaries / Associates
An overseas subsidiary/associate incorporated in a foreign country (e.g. US, UK) is NOT required to prepare its standalone financial statements in Ind AS; it follows its local GAAP. However, it must submit Ind AS reporting packages to the Indian parent for consolidated financial reporting.
🚫 Individual Fellow Subsidiaries Exception
If Subsidiary A falls under Ind AS due to its own net worth, the Holding company must adopt Ind AS. Once Holding adopts Ind AS, all fellow subsidiaries (B, C, D) are pulled into Ind AS by virtue of being subsidiaries of an Ind AS holding company.
9 Key Statutory Provisions & SEBI Regulations
Section / Regulation Statutory Mandate Impact on Financial Reporting

10 Division II of Schedule III (Ind AS Balance Sheet & P&L)

Division II of Schedule III to the Companies Act, 2013 sets the mandatory format for financial statements of companies complying with Ind AS (other than NBFCs which follow Division III).

⚖️ Key Structural Rules of Division II Balance Sheet:
  • Current vs Non-Current Classification: Based on the entity's Operating Cycle. If the operating cycle cannot be clearly identified, its duration is assumed to be 12 months.
  • Order of Balance Sheet:
    1. ASSETS:
      • (1) Non-Current Assets: Property, Plant & Equipment (PPE), Capital Work-in-Progress (CWIP), Investment Property, Goodwill, Other Intangible Assets, Intangible assets under development, Biological Assets, Financial Assets (Investments, Loans, Others), Deferred Tax Assets (Net), Other Non-Current Assets.
      • (2) Current Assets: Inventories, Financial Assets (Investments, Trade Receivables, Cash & Cash Equivalents, Bank balances, Loans, Others), Current Tax Assets (Net), Other Current Assets.
    2. EQUITY AND LIABILITIES:
      • (1) Equity: Equity Share Capital, Other Equity (Reserves, Surplus, OCI reserve, Equity component of compound instruments).
      • (2) Non-Current Liabilities: Financial Liabilities (Borrowings, Lease liabilities, Trade payables, Others), Provisions, Deferred Tax Liabilities (Net), Other Non-Current Liabilities.
      • (3) Current Liabilities: Financial Liabilities (Borrowings, Lease liabilities, Trade payables, Others), Other Current Liabilities, Provisions, Current Tax Liabilities (Net).
📈 Statement of Profit and Loss & Other Comprehensive Income (OCI):
1. Profit or Loss Section
Revenue from Operations, Other Income, Expenses (Cost of materials consumed, Purchases of Stock-in-Trade, Changes in inventories, Employee benefits, Finance costs, Depreciation & Amortisation, Other expenses), Tax expense, and Profit from Discontinued Operations.
2. Other Comprehensive Income (OCI)
Divided strictly into two parts:
• Items that will not be reclassified to profit or loss: Revaluation surplus on PPE (Ind AS 16), Actuarial gains/losses on defined benefit plans (Ind AS 19), Fair value changes in equity instruments designated at FVTOCI (Ind AS 109).
• Items that will be reclassified to profit or loss: Debt instruments measured at FVTOCI, Cash flow hedges effective portion, Foreign Currency Translation Reserve (FCTR) on foreign operations.
11 ICAI Test Your Knowledge (TYK) Problems & Practical Case Studies
ICAI TYK 1 • Net Worth Calculation
Assessment of Ind AS Applicability on Unlisted Entity with Revaluation Reserve
Problem Statement:
ABC Private Limited is an unlisted company having the following financial parameters as on 31st March 2026:
• Paid-up equity share capital: ₹120 Crores
• Free reserves (accumulated profits): ₹90 Crores
• Securities premium account: ₹30 Crores
• Revaluation reserve created on fixed assets: ₹50 Crores
• Miscellaneous expenditure not written off: ₹10 Crores
• Turnover for FY 2025-26: ₹800 Crores
The Board of Directors contends that since the total net worth including revaluation reserve is ₹280 Crores (exceeding ₹250 Crores), the company is mandatorily required to follow Ind AS. Examine whether the contention of the management is correct.
Detailed Solution & Statutory Analysis:

1. Statutory Definition of Net Worth:
As per Section 2(57) of the Companies Act, 2013 and Rule 4 of the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015, "Net Worth" means the aggregate value of the paid-up share capital and all reserves created out of the profits and securities premium account, after deducting the aggregate value of the accumulated losses, deferred expenditure and miscellaneous expenditure not written off.
Specifically, Revaluation Reserve is explicitly excluded from the computation of Net Worth.

2. Computation of Qualifying Net Worth of ABC Private Limited:
• Paid-up Equity Share Capital: ₹120 Crores
• Add: Free Reserves (Profit & Loss): ₹90 Crores
• Add: Securities Premium: ₹30 Crores
• Less: Miscellaneous Expenditure not written off: (₹10 Crores)
• Revaluation Reserve: Nil (Excluded)
Adjusted Net Worth = ₹120 + ₹90 + ₹30 − ₹10 = ₹230 Crores.

3. Conclusion:
For an unlisted corporate entity under Phase II, mandatory adoption requires Net Worth ≥ ₹250 Crores. Since the qualifying Net Worth is ₹230 Crores, the contention of the management is INCORRECT. ABC Private Limited is not mandatorily required to adopt Ind AS, though it may adopt voluntarily.
ICAI TYK 2 • Group Cascading
Cascading Effect on Unlisted Subsidiary and Foreign Associate
Problem Statement:
Global Retail Ltd. is an Indian listed company with a Net Worth of ₹600 Crores (qualifying under Phase I). It has:
1. An Indian subsidiary, Sub Retail Pvt Ltd., with Net Worth of ₹15 Crores.
2. A foreign associate company incorporated in the United Kingdom, UK Fashion Ltd., where Global Retail Ltd. holds a 35% equity interest.
Examine: (a) Does Sub Retail Pvt Ltd. have to adopt Ind AS? (b) Does UK Fashion Ltd. have to prepare its standalone financial statements in Ind AS?
Detailed Solution & Statutory Analysis:

1. Indian Subsidiary (Sub Retail Pvt Ltd.):
As per Rule 4(1)(c) of the Companies (Ind AS) Rules, 2015, once Ind AS applies to a parent company, it automatically applies to all its holding, subsidiary, joint venture, and associate companies irrespective of their individual net worth or turnover.
Therefore, Sub Retail Pvt Ltd. MUST mandatorily adopt Ind AS for both standalone and consolidated reporting.

2. Foreign Associate (UK Fashion Ltd.):
As clarified by MCA circulars, overseas subsidiaries, joint ventures, and associates incorporated outside India are governed by the company laws and accounting standards of their host country. Hence, UK Fashion Ltd. is NOT required to prepare its standalone financial statements under Ind AS.
However, for Global Retail Ltd. to prepare its consolidated financial statements in India under Ind AS 28, UK Fashion Ltd. must provide audited reporting schedules adjusted to Ind AS.
ICAI TYK 3 • SME Exchange Listing
Listing on BSE SME Platform and Net Worth Threshold
Problem Statement:
Apex Instruments Ltd. listed its equity shares on the BSE SME platform during FY 2025-26. Its net worth as of 31st March 2026 is ₹110 Crores. The CFO states that because the company's shares are listed on a recognized stock exchange, it must transition to Ind AS under Phase II. Assess the validity of this statement.
Detailed Solution & Statutory Analysis:

1. SME Exchange Exemption:
Under Rule 4(1)(iii) of the Companies (Ind AS) Rules, 2015, Phase II applies to all companies whose equity or debt securities are listed or in the process of being listed on any stock exchange in India or outside India, EXCLUDING companies listed on SME exchanges.

2. Application to Apex Instruments Ltd.:
Listing on an SME platform does NOT trigger mandatory Ind AS adoption. Since Apex Instruments Ltd. is on the BSE SME platform, its listing status is disregarded. It would only fall under Ind AS if its Net Worth reached or exceeded ₹250 Crores.
Since its Net Worth is ₹110 Crores (< ₹250 Crores), the CFO's statement is INVALID. Apex Instruments Ltd. will continue to follow Accounting Standards (AS) under Companies (AS) Rules, 2006.
