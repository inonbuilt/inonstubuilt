Audit of Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs)

Comprehensive master revision notes covering Section 45-I(f) & 45-IA of the RBI Act 1934, 50-50 Principal Business Test, Net Owned Fund (NOF) Computation, Scale Based Regulation (SBR) 4-Layer Architecture, NBFC Auditor's Report (Reserve Bank) Directions 2016, CARO 2020 Clause 3(xvi), and Ind AS 109 ECL vs. IRAC Prudential Floor.

Section 1 • Statutory Definition

Definition of NBFC & The 50-50 Principal Business Test

1. Legal Definition under Section 45-I(f) of the RBI Act, 1934

A Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) is a company registered under the Companies Act, 2013 engaged in the business of loans and advances, acquisition of shares/stocks/bonds/debentures/securities, leasing, hire-purchase, insurance business, or chit business.

Exclusions: It does not include any institution whose principal business is that of agriculture activity, industrial activity, purchase or sale of any goods (other than securities), or the providing of any services, or sale/purchase/construction of immovable property.

The 50-50 Principal Business Criteria (Twin Test)

To determine whether a corporate entity's principal business is financial in nature (mandating registration with RBI as an NBFC), the RBI applies the objective 50-50 Twin Criteria:

Criterion 1: Asset Test

The company's Financial Assets must constitute more than 50% of its total assets (net of intangible assets).

Criterion 2: Income Test

The company's Income from Financial Assets must constitute more than 50% of its gross total income.

⚠️ Mandatory Registration Consequence

Both criteria are CUMULATIVE! A company that satisfies both the asset test and income test is treated as conducting financial business as its principal business, and MUST mandatorily obtain a Certificate of Registration (CoR) from the RBI under Section 45-IA!

2. Net Owned Fund (NOF) Requirement & Calculation Formula

Under Section 45-IA of the RBI Act, 1934, no NBFC can commence or carry on financial business without: (a) Obtaining a Certificate of Registration (CoR); and (b) Having a minimum prescribed Net Owned Fund (NOF).

Minimum NOF Trajectory under Scale Based Regulation (SBR)

Under the RBI Scale Based Regulation (SBR) framework, the minimum Net Owned Fund requirement for NBFC-ICC, NBFC-MFI, and NBFC-Factor has been enhanced from ₹ 2 Crores to ₹ 10 Crores (phased glide path: ₹ 5 Crores by March 31, 2025 and ₹ 10 Crores by March 31, 2027). For NBFC-P2P, NBFC-AA, and NBFCs without public funds, it remains ₹ 2 Crores.

Step-by-Step Statutory Formula for Computing NOF:

Step Component Description Exact Statutory Inclusions & Deductions
Step 1 Owned Fund (OF) Paid-up Equity Capital + Free Reserves + Balance in Share Premium Account + Capital Reserves (representing surplus arising out of sale proceeds of assets, excluding revaluation reserves)
LESS: Accumulated balance of loss, Deferred revenue expenditure, and Other intangible assets.
Step 2 Specified Investments & Exposures (SIE) Sum total of:
  • Investments in shares of subsidiaries, companies in same group, and other NBFCs; AND
  • Book value of debentures, bonds, outstanding loans and advances (including hire purchase and lease finance) made to, and deposits with, subsidiaries and companies in the same group.
Step 3 Excess over 10% of Owned Fund Amount by which the Specified Investments & Exposures (Step 2) exceeds 10% of the Owned Fund (Step 1).
Step 4 Net Owned Fund (NOF) NOF = Owned Fund (Step 1) − Excess over 10% (Step 3)
Section 2 • Modern Regulatory Architecture

Scale Based Regulation (SBR): The 4-Layer Architecture

1. Why Scale Based Regulation? (Regulatory Alignment with Banks)

Historically, NBFC regulations were based on whether an entity was deposit-taking (NBFC-D) or non-deposit-taking (NBFC-ND), and asset size ≥ ₹ 500 Crores (Systemically Important - ND-SI).

To eliminate regulatory arbitrage and address systemic interconnectedness, the RBI introduced the Scale Based Regulation (SBR) Framework (effective October 2022). Regulation is now tailored progressively across four distinct horizontal layers based on asset size, activity, and systemic risk:

SBR Layer Qualifying NBFC Categories & Asset Size Thresholds Regulatory Stringency & Key Governance Mandates
1. Base Layer
(NBFC-BL)		 • Non-deposit taking NBFCs with asset size < ₹ 1,000 Crores
• NBFC-Peer to Peer Lending (P2P)
• NBFC-Account Aggregator (AA)
• Non-Operative Financial Holding Companies (NOFHC)
• Type I NBFCs (no public funds, no customer interface) 		• Principal business 50-50 compliance
• Minimum NOF glide path to ₹ 10 Cr
• Leverage Ratio ≤ 7
• 90-day NPA overdue norm alignment
• Simpler governance standards
2. Middle Layer
(NBFC-ML)		 • ALL Deposit-Taking NBFCs (NBFC-D), irrespective of asset size!
• Non-deposit taking NBFCs with asset size ≥ ₹ 1,000 Crores
• Standalone Primary Dealers (SPDs)
• Infrastructure Debt Fund NBFCs (IDF-NBFC)
• Core Investment Companies (CICs)
• Housing Finance Companies (HFCs) 		• CRAR minimum 15% (Tier 1 min 10%)
• Mandatory Chief Risk Officer (CRO)
• Mandatory Chief Compliance Officer (CCO)
• Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment (ICAAP)
• Stricter Large Exposure Framework (LEF)
3. Upper Layer
(NBFC-UL)		 • NBFCs identified by RBI based on a quantitative parametric scoring model (Asset size, Interconnectedness, Complexity, Nature of activity)
• The Top 10 eligible NBFCs in terms of asset size are automatically included in the Upper Layer! 		• Bank-like regulatory oversight
• Mandatory Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) min 9%
• Mandatory public listing on stock exchanges within 3 years of classification
• Standard asset provisioning aligned to banks
4. Top Layer
(NBFC-TL)		 • Currently Empty Layer: Populated by RBI only if an Upper Layer NBFC demonstrates extreme systemic risk potential capable of causing systemic contagion. • Higher, customized capital surcharges and intrusive supervisory monitoring by RBI.

2. Fundamental Differences Between Banks and NBFCs

Although both banks and NBFCs engage in financial intermediation, they differ across critical legal boundaries:

Comparison Dimension Commercial Banks Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs)
Demand Deposits Can accept demand deposits (Savings and Current accounts repayable on demand). STRICTLY PROHIBITED: NBFCs cannot accept demand deposits. Only eligible NBFC-D can accept term deposits for 12 to 60 months.
Payment & Settlement Form an integral part of the national Payment and Settlement System; can issue cheques drawn on themselves. CANNOT issue cheques drawn on themselves and do not form part of the Payment and Settlement System.
Deposit Insurance (DICGC) Deposit insurance facility of DICGC (up to ₹ 5 Lakhs per depositor) is available. DICGC deposit insurance is NOT available to depositors of NBFCs!
Statutory Liquidity (CRR) Subject to Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) maintenance under Section 42 of RBI Act. Not required to maintain CRR with RBI (though required to maintain liquid assets against public deposits).

3. Comprehensive Types of NBFCs in the Financial System

1. Investment & Credit Company (ICC)

Lending asset finance and acquiring securities as principal business (merger of former AFC, Investment Co, and Loan Co).

2. Infrastructure Finance Company (IFC)

Deploys at least 75% of total assets in infrastructure loans; min NOF ₹ 300 Cr; min credit rating 'A'; CRAR 15%.

3. Core Investment Company (CIC-ND-SI)

Holds at least 90% of net assets in equity/debt of group companies (min 60% in equity shares); asset size ≥ ₹ 100 Cr.

4. NBFC - Micro Finance Institution (MFI)

Deploys at least 75% of total assets in microfinance loans to low-income households (annual income ≤ ₹ 3 Lakhs) without collateral.

5. Peer to Peer Lending (NBFC-P2P)

Online marketplace platform connecting verified borrowers and lenders; no balance sheet lending; min NOF ₹ 2 Crores.

6. Account Aggregator (NBFC-AA)

Data consolidator providing consolidated financial information of customers across financial entities based on explicit customer consent.

Section 4 • Prudential Guidelines

Prudential Norms: Capital Requirements, Income Recognition & Provisioning

1. Capital Adequacy Requirements (CRAR & Leverage Ratio)

Under the Scale Based Regulation (SBR) framework, NBFCs must maintain adequate capital buffers calibrated to their layer and risk profile:

Middle & Upper Layer: CRAR Norms

NBFC-ML and NBFC-UL must maintain a minimum Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio (CRAR) of 15% on an ongoing basis:

  • Tier 1 Capital: Must not be less than 10% (minimum loss-absorbing core capital).
  • Tier 2 Capital: Cannot exceed 100% of Tier 1 Capital. Includes revaluation reserves (at 55% discount) and general provisions up to 1.25% of risk-weighted assets.
  • Upper Layer CET1: NBFC-UL must maintain Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) of at least 9%.

Base Layer: Leverage Ratio Ceiling

NBFCs in the Base Layer (NBFC-BL) are not subject to the 15% CRAR requirement. Instead, they are governed by a statutory Leverage Ratio:

  • Leverage Ratio Formula: Total Outside Liabilities divided by Owned Fund.
  • Ceiling: The Leverage Ratio must NOT exceed 7 at any point in time!
  • Perpetual Debt Restriction: NBFCs-BL are not eligible to include perpetual debt instruments in their Tier 1 capital.

2. Income Recognition & Harmonized 90-Day NPA Criteria

Under SBR, the criteria for classification of assets as Non-Performing Assets (NPAs) across all categories of NBFCs (including Base Layer) has been strictly aligned to the 90-day overdue norm:

Facility / Advance Type RBI SBR Non-Performing Asset (NPA) Trigger Point
Term Loans & Demand Loans An asset in respect of which interest or installment of principal has remained overdue for more than 90 days.
Lease Rentals & Hire Purchase Lease rental or hire purchase instalment has remained overdue for more than 90 days.
Bills Purchased & Discounted The bill remains overdue and unpaid for more than 90 days.
Income Recognition Rule Income from NPAs CANNOT be recognized on an accrual basis! It can be recognized strictly on actual cash realization. Any unrealized interest already accrued in previous quarters must be immediately reversed from the Profit & Loss account!

3. RBI Prudential Provisioning Matrix for NBFCs

NBFCs must maintain provisions against their loan portfolio in accordance with the following regulatory schedule:

Asset Classification Overdue Period / Status Secured Portion Provision Unsecured Portion Provision
Standard Assets Regular servicing / Overdue ≤ 90 days 0.40% general provision on total standard advances.
Sub-Standard Assets NPA for a period ≤ 12 months 15% general provision on total sub-standard portfolio.
Doubtful Assets – D1 Sub-standard for > 12 months (up to 1 yr) 25% of secured portion 100% of unsecured portion
Doubtful Assets – D2 Doubtful from 1 to 3 years 40% of secured portion 100% of unsecured portion
Doubtful Assets – D3 Doubtful for more than 3 years 100% of secured portion 100% of unsecured portion
Loss Assets Identified as uncollectible 100% PROVISION on entire outstanding balance!
Section 5 • Audit Execution

Audit Procedures, Checklists & Fraud Classification

1. Master Audit Checklist for NBFCs

The statutory auditor must execute the following structured verification procedures:

Audit Focus Area Specific Verification Procedures & Auditor Responsibilities
1. CoR & Principal Business Inspect the original Certificate of Registration (CoR) issued by RBI under Section 45-IA. Re-compute the 50-50 Principal Business Test based on audited financial statements. Verify that the company is entitled to continue holding the CoR.
2. NOF Computation Independently re-perform the calculation of Net Owned Funds (NOF) using the statutory formula. Check that NOF meets the prescribed regulatory floor (₹ 2 Cr / ₹ 10 Cr).
3. Lending & Credit Operations Verify loan agreements, board/committee sanction resolutions, borrower KYC documents, CIBIL/credit bureau reports, and title search reports for mortgaged properties.
4. Security Perfection Verify registration of charges with the Registrar of Companies (ROC) within 30 days, registration with CERSAI, and hypothecation endorsement on the Vahan portal for vehicle financing.
5. Public Deposits (NBFC-D) Verify compliance with ceiling on public deposits (up to 1.5 times NOF for rated companies); verify tenure (12 to 60 months only); verify investment in approved liquid assets (15% of public deposits in unencumbered government securities held in designated custodial account).

2. Classification of Frauds by NBFCs & Reporting to RBI

In accordance with RBI Master Directions on Monitoring and Reporting of Frauds in NBFCs:

Fraud Classification & Reporting Timelines
  • Classification: Frauds are categorized into: (a) Misappropriation and criminal breach of trust; (b) Fraudulent encashment / manipulation of books; (c) Unauthorized credit facilities granted for illegal gratification; (d) Cheating and forgery; (e) Fraudulent foreign exchange transactions.
  • Reporting in Form FMR-1: All individual fraud cases involving an amount of ₹ 1 Lakh and above must be reported to the RBI in Form FMR-1 within three (3) weeks of detection.
  • Frauds ≥ ₹ 1 Crore: In addition to FMR-1, a flash report must be sent to the RBI within 48 hours, and statutory auditors must report under Section 143(12) of Companies Act in Form ADT-4 to the Central Government.

Exam Case Scenario (TYU 1)

Delta Ltd. has Total Assets of ₹ 100 Crores, consisting of: (a) Factory Land & Building: ₹ 40 Crores; (b) Plant & Machinery: ₹ 8 Crores; (c) Trade Receivables from manufacturing sales: ₹ 2 Crores; (d) Loans and Advances granted to third parties: ₹ 35 Crores; (e) Investments in corporate debentures and shares: ₹ 15 Crores.

During the year, its Gross Income was ₹ 20 Crores, consisting of: (a) Revenue from sale of manufactured goods: ₹ 9 Crores; (b) Interest earned on loans: ₹ 7 Crores; (c) Dividend and capital gains on debentures/shares: ₹ 4 Crores.

Evaluate whether Delta Ltd.'s principal business is financial in nature, and whether it is mandatorily required to obtain a Certificate of Registration (CoR) from the RBI under Section 45-IA.

ICAI Model Evaluation:

Apply the cumulative 50-50 Twin Criteria:

  1. 1. Financial Assets Test:
    • Financial Assets = Loans & Advances (₹ 35 Cr) + Investments in shares/debentures (₹ 15 Cr) = ₹ 50 Crores.
    • Total Assets = ₹ 100 Crores.
    • Percentage of Financial Assets = (50 / 100) × 100 = 50%.
    • Evaluation: The statutory rule requires Financial Assets to be MORE THAN 50% (> 50.00%) of total assets! Here, financial assets are exactly 50.00%, NOT more than 50%!
  2. 2. Financial Income Test:
    • Financial Income = Interest on loans (₹ 7 Cr) + Dividend/Gains (₹ 4 Cr) = ₹ 11 Crores.
    • Gross Total Income = ₹ 20 Crores.
    • Percentage of Financial Income = (11 / 20) × 100 = 55% (> 50%).
  3. Conclusion: Although Delta Ltd. satisfies the Income Test (55% > 50%), it FAILS the Asset Test because financial assets are exactly 50% (not strictly > 50%). Since both tests are cumulative, Delta Ltd. does NOT meet the 50-50 principal business criteria and is NOT required to register as an NBFC with the RBI!
Section 6 • Statutory Duties

Auditor's Duties: RBI Directions 2016 & CARO 2020 Clause 3(xvi)

1. Non-Banking Financial Companies Auditor's Report (Reserve Bank) Directions, 2016

The statutory auditor of every NBFC has dual reporting responsibilities: (1) The independent auditor's report to shareholders under Section 143; and (2) A Separate Report to the Board of Directors pursuant to the NBFC Auditor's Report (Reserve Bank) Directions, 2016.

Category of NBFC Mandatory Inquiries & Statements in Report to the Board of Directors
Matters for ALL NBFCs
  • Whether the company is engaged in NBFC business and has obtained a valid Certificate of Registration (CoR) from RBI under Section 45-IA.
  • Whether the company is entitled to continue holding the CoR based on the 50-50 Principal Business criteria as of the close of the financial year.
  • Whether the company meets the prescribed minimum Net Owned Fund (NOF) requirement.
Additional Matters for
Deposit-Taking NBFCs
(NBFC-D)
  • Whether public deposits accepted are within the prescribed statutory ceiling.
  • Whether the credit rating obtained from approved credit rating agencies is valid and in force.
  • Whether the bank has defaulted in payment of interest or repayment of principal to any depositor.
  • Whether the company has maintained 15% liquid assets in approved securities in designated bank custody.
  • Whether the annual return on deposits in Form NBS-1 was filed on time.
Additional Matters for
Non-Deposit NBFCs
(NBFC-ND)
  • Whether the Board of Directors has passed a formal resolution for non-acceptance of any public deposits.
  • Whether the company has accepted any public deposits during the year under audit.
  • Whether the company has complied with the prudential norms relating to income recognition, accounting standards, asset classification, and provisioning.
  • Whether CRAR / Leverage Ratio has been correctly determined and reported in annual return (NBS-7 / Form SBR).

2. Exception Reporting Directly to the Reserve Bank of India

The Statutory Duty of Exception Reporting under 2016 Directions

Where in the case of any NBFC, the statement regarding any of the items specified in the report to the Board of Directors is unfavorable or qualified, or in the opinion of the auditor the company has not complied with:

  • The provisions of the RBI Act, 1934 (Sections 45-IA, 45-IB, 45-IC); OR
  • Any Master Direction or Prudential Regulation issued by the RBI;

It shall be the statutory obligation of the auditor to report the fact immediately in writing to the Regional Office of the Reserve Bank of India under whose jurisdiction the registered office of the company is located!

Timing: The auditor cannot wait for the shareholders' meeting or formal presentation of the accounts. The exception report must be submitted to the RBI immediately!

3. Reporting Under CARO 2020 – Clause 3(xvi) (The 4 Sub-Clauses)

Clause 3(xvi) of CARO 2020 imposes specific, detailed reporting duties on statutory auditors of all companies regarding NBFC activities:

CARO Sub-Clause Statutory Reporting Mandate Auditor's Specific Verification Focus
Clause 3(xvi)(a) Whether the company is required to be registered under Section 45-IA of the RBI Act, 1934 and if so, whether the registration has been obtained. Examine 50-50 test. If financial assets > 50% and financial income > 50%, verify whether Certificate of Registration (CoR) is obtained from RBI.
Clause 3(xvi)(b) Whether the company has conducted any Non-Banking Financial or Housing Finance activities without a valid Certificate of Registration (CoR) from the RBI. Detect unauthorized shadow banking. If an unregistered commercial company conducts substantial lending/investing as principal business, report the breach.
Clause 3(xvi)(c) Whether the company is a Core Investment Company (CIC) as defined in the regulations by the RBI, and if so, whether it continues to fulfill the criteria of a CIC, and in case of an exempted/unregistered CIC, whether it continues to fulfill such criteria. Verify 90% net assets invested in group companies and 60% in equity shares; verify whether asset size ≥ ₹ 100 Cr and public funds are accessed.
Clause 3(xvi)(d) Whether the Group has more than one CIC as part of the Group, and if yes, indicate the number of CICs which are part of the Group. Inquire with management, review group corporate structure charts, and state the exact aggregate count of CICs functioning in the corporate group.

Exam Case Scenario (TYU 2 & TYK 7)

During the statutory audit of Zen Finance Ltd., a non-deposit taking NBFC in the Base Layer, the auditor noticed that the company failed to maintain the required minimum Net Owned Fund (NOF) of ₹ 2 Crores as of March 31, 2026 due to operating losses. Furthermore, the company failed to create general provision of 0.40% on standard assets.

The management requested the auditor to state these observations in the separate report to the Board of Directors, but insisted that there is no need to inform the Reserve Bank of India since Zen Finance Ltd. does not accept public deposits. Evaluate the validity of management's contention and explain the auditor's statutory reporting duties.

ICAI Model Evaluation:

  1. Management's contention is completely invalid and contrary to law: The obligation to report non-compliances to the RBI applies to ALL NBFCs, whether deposit-taking or non-deposit-taking!
  2. Mandatory Exception Reporting: Under paragraph 5 of the NBFC Auditor's Report (Reserve Bank) Directions, 2016, if any statement in the report to the Board is unfavorable or qualified, or if the company has failed to comply with NOF requirements or prudential provisioning norms, the auditor has a strict statutory duty to report the matter immediately in writing to the RBI!
  3. Direct Communication: The auditor must send an Exception Report directly to the Regional Office of the Department of Supervision, RBI, detailing the shortfall in NOF and non-provisioning on standard assets.
Section 7 • Accounting Standards

Ind AS Schedule III (Division III), ECL vs. IRAC Prudential Floor

1. Ind AS Financial Statements Presentation (Division III of Schedule III)

All NBFCs covered under the Ind AS roadmap must prepare their Balance Sheet, Statement of Profit and Loss, and Statement of Changes in Equity in accordance with Division III of Schedule III to the Companies Act, 2013:

Key Distinctive Feature of Division III (Order of Liquidity)

Unlike commercial companies under Division II (which classify assets and liabilities into Current and Non-Current), NBFCs under Division III present items in the Balance Sheet in the Order of Liquidity!

Balance Sheet is divided into Financial Assets (Cash, Bank balances, Derivative financial instruments, Receivables, Loans, Investments) and Non-Financial Assets (Inventories, Current tax assets, PPE, Intangible assets). Similarly, liabilities are split into Financial Liabilities and Non-Financial Liabilities.

2. Ind AS 109 Expected Credit Loss (ECL) vs. RBI IRAC Prudential Floor

Under Ind AS 109 (Financial Instruments), NBFCs compute impairment allowances using the forward-looking Expected Credit Loss (ECL) model across three credit quality stages:

Stage 1: Performing

Overdue ≤ 30 days. No significant increase in credit risk since origination. Provision = 12-Month Expected Credit Loss.

Stage 2: Under-Performing (SICR)

Overdue 31 to 90 days. Significant Increase in Credit Risk (SICR). Provision = Lifetime Expected Credit Loss.

Stage 3: Credit-Impaired

Overdue > 90 days. Default occurred / objective evidence of impairment. Provision = Lifetime Expected Credit Loss.

The RBI Prudential Floor & Impairment Reserve

RBI Mandate on Ind AS Implementation – The Prudential Floor Rule

To prevent under-provisioning under statistical ECL models, the RBI mandates that NBFCs must simultaneously compute provisions required under RBI Prudential IRAC Norms:

  • Comparison: Compare aggregate Ind AS 109 Impairment Allowance (ECL) against aggregate RBI IRAC Provisioning requirement.
  • Where Ind AS 109 ECL < RBI IRAC Provision: The shortfall must be transferred from Retained Earnings to a separate "Impairment Reserve" under Equity! No dividend can be paid out of this reserve.
  • Where Ind AS 109 ECL ≥ RBI IRAC Provision: No transfer to Impairment Reserve is needed! The higher Ind AS ECL provision stands in the financial statements.

3. The 5 Mandatory Related Party Loan Disclosures under SBR

To prevent promoter tunneling and unmonitored group fund diversions, the RBI Scale Based Regulation mandates that all NBFCs disclose the following 5 specific related party credit metrics in their financial statement notes:

Item # Mandatory Related Party Loan Disclosure Parameter Regulatory Purpose & Audit Verification Focus
1 Aggregate value of loans sanctioned to related parties during the year. Verify board approval under Section 188 and compliance with arm's length lending rules.
2 Aggregate value of outstanding loans to related parties as on March 31. Confirm closing loan balances and reconciliation with related party master.
3 Percentage of related party loans to total loans and advances of the NBFC. Detect credit concentration risk and excessive promoter-group funding.
4 Aggregate value of outstanding related party loans categorized as SMA and NPA. Identify early stress (SMA-0, SMA-1, SMA-2) and non-performing loans in related party book.
5 Amount of provisions held in respect of loans to related parties. Ensure adequate ECL and IRAC provisioning without under-provisioning related entities.

Exam Case Scenario (TYU 3 & TYK 9)

Omega Capital Ltd., an NBFC in the Middle Layer adopting Ind AS, has computed an Expected Credit Loss (ECL) allowance of ₹ 18 Crores under Ind AS 109 as of March 31, 2026. The statutory auditor, during the course of the audit, independently computed the provision required under the RBI Prudential IRAC Norms, which amounted to ₹ 22 Crores.

The management contends that since Ind AS is notified under the Companies Act, 2013 and prevails over RBI guidelines, only the Ind AS 109 provision of ₹ 18 Crores needs to be recognized in the accounts, and no further action is required. Advise the auditor and evaluate management's contention.

ICAI Model Evaluation:

  1. Management's contention is erroneous: While the accounting provision recognized in the Statement of Profit and Loss must follow Ind AS 109 (₹ 18 Crores), the company is bound by the RBI Prudential Floor Rule.
  2. Computation of Shortfall:
    • Provision required under RBI IRAC Norms = ₹ 22 Crores.
    • Impairment allowance computed under Ind AS 109 = ₹ 18 Crores.
    • Shortfall in Provision = ₹ 22 Crores − ₹ 18 Crores = ₹ 4 Crores.
  3. Creation of Impairment Reserve: Omega Capital Ltd. MUST transfer the shortfall of ₹ 4 Crores from Retained Earnings to a separate "Impairment Reserve" under Equity in the Balance Sheet!
  4. Dividend Restriction: No amount can be distributed as dividend or capitalized from this Impairment Reserve. If management refuses to create this Impairment Reserve, the auditor must issue a qualified audit opinion!

Master SBR 4-Layer Architecture Matrix & Exam Traps

Comparative Synthesis: The 4-Layer Scale Based Regulation (SBR)

SBR Layer Eligibility & Asset Size Capital & Leverage Rules Key Regulatory & Governance Mandates
Base Layer
(NBFC-BL)		 Non-deposit NBFCs < ₹ 1,000 Cr; P2P, AA, NOFHC, Type I. Leverage Ratio ≤ 7 (Total Outside Liabilities / Owned Fund). 50-50 principal business test, NOF glide path to ₹ 10 Cr, 90-day NPA overdue norm.
Middle Layer
(NBFC-ML)		 All Deposit-Taking NBFCs; Non-deposit ≥ ₹ 1,000 Cr; HFCs, CICs, SPDs, IDFs. CRAR min 15% (Tier 1 min 10%). Mandatory Chief Risk Officer (CRO) & CCO, ICAAP, Large Exposure Framework (LEF).
Upper Layer
(NBFC-UL)		 Top 10 eligible NBFCs by size + Parametric scoring model entities. CRAR min 15% + Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) min 9%. Bank-like supervision, mandatory listing on stock exchanges within 3 years of classification.
Top Layer
(NBFC-TL)		 Empty layer (populated if Upper Layer NBFC poses extreme systemic risk). Higher customized capital surcharges. Intrusive supervisory monitoring and higher prudential buffers.
⚠️ High-Frequency NBFC Exam Flash Traps & Pitfalls
  • Trap 1 (50-50 Test Strictly Greater Than): Believing that exactly 50% financial assets qualifies. Fact: The statutory rule requires financial assets to be MORE THAN 50% (> 50.00%)! Exactly 50.00% FAILS the test!
  • Trap 2 (Deposit-Taking SBR Layer): Classifying a small deposit-taking NBFC with ₹ 100 Cr assets into Base Layer. Fact: ALL deposit-taking NBFCs (NBFC-D) are mandatorily placed in the Middle Layer, regardless of asset size!
  • Trap 3 (Exception Reporting to RBI): Assuming exception reporting is only for deposit-taking NBFCs. Fact: The duty to report unfavorable/qualified statements to the RBI applies to ALL NBFCs under the 2016 Directions!
  • Trap 4 (Ind AS ECL vs. IRAC Floor): Overlooking the Impairment Reserve. Fact: If Ind AS 109 ECL is lower than RBI IRAC provision, the shortfall must be transferred to Impairment Reserve from retained earnings!
