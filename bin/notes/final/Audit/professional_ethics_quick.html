Chapter 19: Professional Ethics & Liabilities of Auditors

1. Fundamental Principles & Independence Threats

Every Chartered Accountant must adhere to 5 Fundamental Ethical Principles:

Fundamental Principle Ethical Mandate
1. IntegrityBe straightforward and honest in all professional and business relationships.
2. ObjectivityNot compromise professional judgment because of bias, conflict of interest, or undue influence.
3. Professional Competence & Due CareAttain and maintain professional knowledge and skill; act diligently and in accordance with technical standards.
4. ConfidentialityRespect confidentiality of client information acquired during professional service.
5. Professional BehaviourComply with relevant laws and regulations and avoid any conduct that discredits the profession.
The 5 Threats to Independence

1. Self-Interest Threat: Financial or personal interest in client (e.g., owning shares, fee dependency).
2. Self-Review Threat: Evaluating results of a previous judgment made by the auditor themselves (e.g., preparing accounting records then auditing them).
3. Advocacy Threat: Promoting a client's position to the point that objectivity is compromised (e.g., acting as legal advocate in a lawsuit).
4. Familiarity Threat: Sympathetic to client interests due to close or long relationship (e.g., auditor tenure > 10 years, close relative as CFO).
5. Intimidation Threat: Being deterred from acting objectively by actual or perceived pressures (e.g., threat of replacement, litigation).

2. First Schedule: Master 12 Clauses of Part I & Other Parts

Clause # Subject Matter Key Rules & Exceptions
Part I Cl 1 Allows any person to practice in his name Prohibited unless such person is also a CA in practice and is in partnership with or employed by him.
Part I Cl 2 Pays or shares fee / commission / profits Prohibited to pay/share fees with any non-member. Exceptions: Partner, retired partner, legal heirs of deceased partner, members of recognized bodies (CS, CMA, Bar Council).
Part I Cl 3 Accepts share in profits / fees Prohibited to accept share of fees from non-members (lawyers, brokers, agents).
Part I Cl 4 Enters into partnership Prohibited to partner with anyone other than a CA in practice or member of recognized professional bodies under Regulation 53B.
Part I Cl 5 Secures professional work through non-associates Cannot secure work through agents or persons not qualified to be his partner.
Part I Cl 6 Soliciting clients or professional work STRICTLY PROHIBITED by circular, advertisement, personal communication. Exceptions: Responding to tenders satisfying minimum fee criteria; applying to fellow members for work.
Part I Cl 7 Advertising designations & achievements Cannot advertise other designations (e.g. Income Tax Consultant, Ex-President) on letterheads/signboards. Permitted: Only "Chartered Accountant", academic degrees, networking logo as per guidelines.
Part I Cl 8 Accepting audit without prior communication MANDATORY: Must communicate in writing with previous auditor before accepting appointment. Communication must be via Registered Post AD / Speed Post / Hand delivery with receipt / Valid email.
Part I Cl 9 Verifying compliance of Sec 139 / 140 Must ascertain whether the requirements of Sections 139 and 140 of Companies Act regarding appointment/removal have been complied with.
Part I Cl 10 Contingent Fees / Fees as % of profits Prohibited to charge fees based on % of profits or contingent upon findings. Exceptions: Liquidator, Valuer under Insolvency Code, Debt recovery valuer, Non-assurance services permitted by Council.
Part I Cl 11 Engaging in other business / occupation Requires prior approval of Council under Regulation 190A. General Permission: Director Simpliciter, authoring books, teaching up to 25 hours per week. Specific Permission: Active MD, full-time business.
Part I Cl 12 Allows non-CA / non-partner to sign reports Only a CA in practice and a partner of the firm can sign audit reports on behalf of the firm.
Part II (1 & 2) CAs in Service Misconduct Cl 1: Sharing emoluments with non-CAs; Cl 2: Accepting kickbacks/commissions from suppliers or vendors.
Part III (1–3) Members Generally Cl 1: Not being a fellow, acts as a fellow; Cl 2: Does not supply info called for by ICAI; Cl 3: Gives false information to ICAI.
Part IV (1 & 2) Other Misconduct Cl 1: Conviction for an offence punishable with imprisonment < 6 months; Cl 2: Conduct bringing disrepute to the profession.

3. Second Schedule: Master Breakdown (Parts I, II & III)

Part & Clause Nature of Offence / Professional Misconduct
Part I Cl 1 Disclosing confidential information acquired in professional capacity without consent of client or legal duty.
Part I Cl 2 Certifying report without examination of financial statements by self, partner, or employee.
Part I Cl 3 Vouching prospective financial information (PFI) or estimating earnings contingent on future transactions in a manner leading to belief that he vouches for accuracy of forecast.
Part I Cl 4 Substantial Interest: Expressing opinion on FS of an enterprise in which the CA, partner, or relative has a substantial interest.
Part I Cl 5 Failure to disclose material fact known to him which is not disclosed in FS, but disclosure of which is necessary to prevent FS from being misleading.
Part I Cl 6 Failure to report a material misstatement known to him to appear in FS with which he is concerned in a professional capacity.
Part I Cl 7 Gross Negligence: Does not exercise due diligence, or is grossly negligent in the conduct of his professional duties.
Part I Cl 8 Failure to obtain SAAE sufficient to warrant expression of an opinion.
Part I Cl 9 Failure to invite attention to material departures from generally accepted procedure of audit.
Part I Cl 10 Misuse of client money: Fails to keep moneys of client in separate banking account or uses it for unauthorized purpose.
Part II (1–4) Members Generally: Cl 1: Contravenes Act, regulations, or guidelines; Cl 2: Being an employee, discloses confidential info; Cl 3: Submits false info to ICAI; Cl 4: Defalcates or embezzles money.
Part III Other Misconduct: Conviction by a competent court for an offence punishable with imprisonment exceeding 6 months.

4. Disciplinary Mechanism & High-Yield Council Guidelines

Disciplinary Authority Offences Handled Maximum Punishment Powers
Board of Discipline (Sec 21A) Offences in the First Schedule only. • Reprimand member.
• Suspend from practice up to 3 months.
• Fine up to ₹ 1,00,000.
Disciplinary Committee (Sec 21B) Offences in the Second Schedule OR both Schedules. • Reprimand member.
• Suspend from practice for any period / permanently remove.
• Fine up to ₹ 5,00,000.
Critical High-Yield Council Guidelines

• Tax Audit Limits (Sec 44AB): A CA in practice / partner can accept a maximum of 60 Tax Audits per financial year.
• Statutory Audit Limits (Sec 141(3)(g)): Max 20 company audits per partner (excluding OPC, dormant, small cos, and private cos with paid-up capital < ₹100 Cr without default).
• Fee Ceiling / Independence: A member/firm shall not accept statutory audit of an entity if gross fees from that entity & its group exceed 40% of total gross fees of the firm in that year (does not apply to audits of banks/govt cos/PSUs or firms with gross receipts ≤ ₹50 Lakhs).
