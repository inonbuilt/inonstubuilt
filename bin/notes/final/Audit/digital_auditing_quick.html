1. General IT Controls (GITCs) vs Application Controls

In an automated environment, reliable financial reporting depends on the symbiotic functioning of infrastructure controls and transaction-level controls.

Control Domain Scope & Core Objectives Typical Examples
1. Data Centre & Network Operations Ensure proper job scheduling, backup & recovery, system monitoring, disaster recovery planning (DRP). Automated off-site backups, UPS tests, server logs review.
2. Program Change Management Ensure modifications to applications and systems are authorised, tested, approved, and migrated safely. User Acceptance Testing (UAT), segregation of Dev and Prod environments.
3. Access Security (Physical & Logical) Prevent unauthorized access to systems and data; enforce Segregation of Duties (SoD). Multi-factor authentication (MFA), password complexity, periodic access rights review.
Application Controls (Automated) Enforce completeness, accuracy, and validity of individual business transactions. Input checks (range, format, check digits), automated 3-way matching of PO-GRN-Invoice, duplicate checks.
Golden Rule of IT Auditing

If General IT Controls (GITCs) are ineffective, the auditor cannot rely on automated application controls or system-generated reports (IPE — Information Produced by the Entity) without extensive manual substantive testing!

2. Data Analytics & CAATs Testing

Power of 100% Data Testing

Computer Assisted Audit Techniques (CAATs) and Data Analytics enable auditors to analyze 100% of transactions rather than relying solely on traditional audit sampling.

Audit Area Data Analytics Routine / Procedure
Journal Entry Testing (SA 240) Filter entries posted on weekends/holidays, entries posted by unauthorized users, round-sum entries, entries ending in '999', entries to rarely used suspense accounts.
Revenue & Receivables Three-way matching of Sales Order, Delivery Challan, and Invoice; recalculate sales discounts; analyze price variances; identify unbilled shipments.
Inventory Valuation Recalculate FIFO/Weighted Average cost across millions of SKUs; identify negative stock balances; age inventory dynamically to assess NRV write-downs.
Vendor Payments & Fraud Detect duplicate invoices (same invoice number, amount, or vendor bank account); identify split purchase orders designed to bypass authorization thresholds.

3. Emerging Technologies in Auditing

Technology Role in Audit & Assurance Specific Audit Risk to Evaluate
Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning (AI/ML) Anomaly detection in general ledgers; contract review using NLP (identifying lease terms under Ind AS 116); predictive revenue modeling. "Black-box" algorithm bias, lack of explainability, data poisoning, training data inaccuracy.
Robotic Process Automation (RPA) Automating repetitive audit workflows (extracting trial balances, sending automated bank confirmation requests, reconciling GL with sub-ledgers). Bot credential misuse, lack of change control over bot scripts, unattended bot failure.
Blockchain & Distributed Ledger Tech (DLT) Real-time triple-entry bookkeeping, smart contract execution, immutable transaction timestamps. Smart contract bugs/vulnerabilities, consensus protocol failures, private key security.

4. Cybersecurity & IT Audit Governance

Auditor's Duty Regarding Cyber Risks

Cybersecurity risks directly threaten the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of financial data. The auditor must assess:
1. Incident Response: Does the company have a documented Incident Response Plan tested regularly?
2. Ransomware & Breaches: Have any cyber attacks occurred during the year? Assess financial impact (fines, customer compensation, asset write-downs) and disclosure adequacy under SA 250 & Ind AS 37.
3. IT Governance: Alignment with recognized frameworks such as COBIT, ISO/IEC 27001, and NIST.
