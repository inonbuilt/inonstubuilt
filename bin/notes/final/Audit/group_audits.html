Group Audits & Consolidated Financial Statements

Comprehensive master revision notes covering Standard on Auditing (SA) 600 "Using the Work of Another Auditor", Section 129(3) & (4) of the Companies Act 2013, Rule 6 Exemptions, Ind AS 110 Investment Entities, Permanent vs. Current Period Consolidation Adjustments, CARO 2020 Clause (xxi), and Reporting on Unaudited Components.

Section 1 • Foundations

Introduction & Concept of Consolidated Financial Statements

1. What are Consolidated Financial Statements (CFS)?

Consolidated Financial Statements (CFS) are the financial statements of an entire group presented as those of a single economic entity. While Standalone Financial Statements (SFS) present the financial condition of an isolated legal corporation, CFS combine the assets, liabilities, equity, revenues, expenses, and cash flows of the parent company and all its subsidiaries, joint ventures, and associates.

Core Accounting Definitions & Applicable Standards
  • Parent: An entity that controls one or more entities (Ind AS 110 / AS 21).
  • Subsidiary: An entity that is controlled by another entity. Under Section 2(87) of the Companies Act, 2013, control means controlling the composition of the Board of Directors or exercising/controlling more than 50% of the total voting power.
  • Associate: An entity over which the investor has significant influence (holding 20% or more voting power or contractual participation in financial/operating decisions, but not control). Accounted for via the Equity Method (Ind AS 28 / AS 23).
  • Joint Venture / Joint Arrangement: An arrangement where parties have joint control and rights to net assets (Ind AS 111 / AS 27).

What Comprises a Complete Set of Consolidated Financial Statements?

Under Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013, CFS must follow the exact presentation formats as standalone statements:

  • Consolidated Balance Sheet (Schedule III format)
  • Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss
  • Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity (under Ind AS)
  • Consolidated Cash Flow Statement (AS 3 / Ind AS 7)
  • Explanatory Notes & Accounting Policies (including component share in net assets and P&L under Schedule III general instructions)

Opening Case Scenario: Mudit's Catch-22 (The Unaudited Foreign Subsidiary)

The Practical Dilemma: Mudit is appointed as the statutory auditor of a prominent Indian holding company. The management prepared group consolidated financial statements by combining like items of assets, liabilities, income, and expenses of the Indian parent with those of an overseas foreign subsidiary. No minority interest was involved.

During the course of the consolidation audit, Mudit discovered that the accounts of the foreign subsidiary were completely unaudited! Mudit found himself in a classical Catch-22 situation:

  • Can a parent consolidate unaudited accounts of a component?
  • What are the implications for the group audit report?
  • How does the auditor evaluate materiality and what reporting modification is warranted under SA 705 and SA 600?

→ Keep this scenario in focus! We will resolve Mudit's Catch-22 with precise ICAI regulatory principles in Section 7!

2. Statutory Mandate & Exemption Framework (Section 129 & Rule 6)

Section 129(3) of the Companies Act, 2013 lays down that where a company has one or more subsidiaries (which includes associate companies and joint ventures under the Explanation to Section 129(3)), it shall, in addition to its standalone financial statements, prepare a Consolidated Financial Statement of the company and of all the subsidiaries in the same form and manner as that of its own.

Furthermore, Section 129(4) states that the provisions of the Companies Act applicable to the preparation, adoption, and audit of the financial statements of a holding company shall, mutatis mutandis, apply to its consolidated financial statements.

Exemptions from Preparing CFS under Rule 6 of Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014

Preparation of CFS is mandatory unless an intermediate holding company satisfies all three cumulative conditions under Rule 6:

Condition 1
Wholly-Owned or Consenting Partially-Owned Subsidiary
The company is a wholly-owned subsidiary, or is a partially-owned subsidiary of another company and all its other members (including those not otherwise entitled to vote) have been intimated in writing and have not objected to the company not presenting CFS.
Condition 2
Unlisted Status
The company's securities (equity or debt) are not listed or in the process of being listed on any stock exchange, whether in India or outside India.
Condition 3
Higher Holding Company Files CFS
The ultimate or any intermediate holding company files consolidated financial statements with the Registrar of Companies (ROC) which are in compliance with the applicable Accounting Standards.

The Ind AS 110 "Investment Entity" Exemption

Ind AS 110 Exception for Investment Entities

An entity that qualifies as an Investment Entity under Ind AS 110 is prohibited from consolidating its subsidiaries. Instead, it must measure its investments in subsidiaries at Fair Value Through Profit or Loss (FVTPL) in accordance with Ind AS 109.

Exception to the exception: If an investment entity has a subsidiary that provides investment-related services (such as investment management or advisory services), it MUST consolidate that specific service subsidiary!

Section 2 • Governance & Roles

Responsibilities of Parent Management & Statutory Auditor

1. The 6 Statutory Responsibilities of Parent Management

The primary responsibility for the preparation and fair presentation of consolidated financial statements rests entirely with the management of the parent company:

Parent Responsibility Core Operational Duty under Companies Act & Accounting Standards
1. Component Identification Identifying all components (subsidiaries, associates, joint arrangements) to be included in CFS, and documenting reasons for any permitted statutory exclusions.
2. Accurate Data Gathering Obtaining complete, reliable financial information and audited accounts from all components on a timely basis.
3. Harmonizing Accounting Policies Ensuring that uniform accounting policies are followed across the group for like transactions and other events in similar circumstances. If a component follows different policies, management must make necessary adjustments in CFS.
4. Harmonizing Reporting Dates Ensuring component financial statements are drawn up to the same reporting date as the parent. If different, the gap must not exceed 3 months, and adjustments must be made for significant intervening events.
5. GAAP Conversion Converting financial statements of foreign components prepared under local foreign GAAP into Indian GAAP / Ind AS before consolidation.
6. Consolidation Adjustments Making necessary Permanent Adjustments (Goodwill, Capital Reserve, NCI) and Current Period Adjustments (eliminating intra-group balances, intra-group sales, and unrealized profits).

2. Responsibility of the Auditor of Consolidated Financial Statements

The statutory auditor of the consolidated financial statements (referred to as the Principal Auditor under SA 600) is responsible for:

  • Expressing an independent audit opinion on whether the CFS present a true and fair view in accordance with the applicable Financial Reporting Framework (Ind AS / AS) and statutory requirements.
  • Verifying the completeness of group components consolidated by management.
  • Auditing the consolidation process, including mathematical accuracy of line-by-line additions, equity accounting, and elimination entries.
  • Coordinating with and evaluating the work of Component Auditors in compliance with SA 600.
  • Reporting under Section 143(3) & CARO 2020 Clause (xxi) on qualifications and adverse remarks in component auditor reports.
Section 3 • Standards on Auditing

Audit Considerations & SA 600 "Using the Work of Another Auditor"

1. The SA 600 Framework & Acceptance of Principal Auditor Assignment

When reporting on the consolidated financial statements of a group, the statutory auditor of the parent entity frequently uses the financial information of one or more components that have been audited by other independent auditors. In such circumstances, the auditor must strictly adhere to Standard on Auditing (SA) 600, "Using the Work of Another Auditor".

Core Definitions under SA 600
  • Principal Auditor: The auditor with the reporting responsibility on the financial statements of an entity (the group CFS), when that financial statement includes financial information of one or more components audited by another auditor.
  • Component: A division, branch, subsidiary, joint venture, associated company, or other entity whose financial information is included in the financial statements audited by the principal auditor.
  • Other Auditor / Component Auditor: An auditor, other than the principal auditor, with responsibility for reporting on the financial information of a component included in the financial statements audited by the principal auditor.

Accepting the Assignment: Can the Auditor Act as Principal Auditor?

An auditor should not accept appointment as the principal auditor of consolidated financial statements if their own participation is not sufficient to enable them to act as the principal auditor.

In assessing whether the auditor's own participation is sufficient, the auditor should consider:

Quantitative Criteria
  • The materiality of the portion of the financial statements which the principal auditor audits personally (percentage of total group assets, revenues, and profits).
  • The volume of transactions and turnover handled directly by the parent entity.
Qualitative & Risk Criteria
  • The principal auditor's degree of knowledge regarding the business of the components.
  • The risk of material misstatements in the components audited by other auditors.
  • The performance of additional procedures regarding the components audited by other auditors.

Division of Responsibility & Coordination Procedures

Vital Legal Principle: Division of Responsibility under SA 600

The Principal Auditor does not review the detailed audit working papers of the component auditor! The principal auditor is entitled to rely on the work performed by the other auditor, provided they exercise reasonable professional skill and care.

The principal auditor fulfills their duty by issuing structured instructions, questionnaires, and checklists to the component auditor, and evaluating the component auditor's report and responses.

The Principal Auditor's coordination procedures include:

  1. Advising the other auditor of the use that is to be made of their work and report.
  2. Informing the other auditor of the applicable financial reporting framework and group accounting policies.
  3. Obtaining written confirmation of compliance with independence and professional ethics.
  4. Discussing significant audit findings, litigation exposures, and subsequent events identified by the component auditor.

2. Materiality in the Audit of Consolidated Financial Statements

In a standalone audit, materiality is determined component-wise based on individual metrics. In contrast, during the audit of Consolidated Financial Statements (CFS), the auditor must establish materiality at two interconnected levels:

1. Group-Level Materiality

The principal auditor computes materiality for the group as a whole based on consolidated figures (consolidated revenue, consolidated profit before tax, consolidated net assets).

  • Used to evaluate the appropriateness of consolidation adjustments (permanent and current period adjustments).
  • Used to determine which components are quantitatively significant to the group to scope in additional procedures.

2. Component-Level Materiality

The principal auditor computes and communicates specific materiality thresholds to component auditors for components that are subject to audit for group reporting purposes.

  • Component materiality is set lower than group materiality to reduce the aggregation risk of undetected misstatements across multiple entities.
  • Different components may receive different materiality levels based on their risk profile and relative size.

3. ICAI Announcement on SA 600 (Evaluating Component Auditor Modifications)

ICAI Announcement (May 25, 2017) – Amendment to Guidance Note on CFS

When considering the observations (for instance, a modified opinion under SA 705 or an Emphasis of Matter / Other Matter paragraph under SA 706) in the report of a component auditor on the standalone financial statements, the parent auditor must comply with the requirements of SA 600 and evaluate the observation in relation to group-level materiality!

The 3-Step Evaluation Protocol for Component Modifications:

Step 1
Examine the Nature & Quantum of Component Modification
Identify the specific reason for modification in the component auditor's report (e.g., non-provision for bad debts, non-reconciliation of inventory, going concern uncertainty).
Step 2
Evaluate Materiality from the Perspective of Group CFS
Determine whether the quantified misstatement or uncertainty is material to the consolidated financial statements of the group as a whole. A ₹ 50 Lakh misstatement may be material to a standalone subsidiary with ₹ 2 Crore revenue, but immaterial to a group with ₹ 5,000 Crore consolidated revenue!
Step 3
Determine Impact on Group Audit Report
If Material to Group: The principal auditor MUST modify the group audit opinion on CFS (Qualified or Adverse under SA 705).
If Immaterial to Group: The principal auditor DOES NOT modify the CFS audit opinion. However, the observation may be disclosed or noted if relevant to understanding the group affairs.
Section 4 • Audit Execution

Auditing the Consolidation & Verification of Completeness

1. Planning the Audit of Consolidation

Before commencing the audit of consolidated financial statements, the principal auditor must develop a comprehensive audit plan addressing:

  • Group Structure & Group-Wide Controls: Understanding the legal and operational structure, parent-subsidiary hierarchies, and IT general and application controls over the consolidation process.
  • Accounting Policies: Reviewing the accounting policies of the parent and all components, including foreign currency translation methodologies (AS 11 / Ind AS 21).
  • Risk Assessment: Determining the nature, timing, and extent (NTE) of audit procedures based on the assessed risk of material misstatement in consolidation adjustments.
  • Coordination: Scheduling deadlines for receipt of component financial statements, reporting packages, and component auditor communications.

2. Assessing Completeness of Components (The 8 Mandatory Audit Procedures)

The auditor must verify that all subsidiaries, associates, and joint ventures have been correctly included in CFS unless permitted to be excluded under the applicable reporting framework. The auditor must perform the following 8 structured audit procedures:

Step Mandatory Audit Procedure Auditor's Specific Verification Action & Source of Evidence
1 Prior Year Working Papers Review working papers of prior years to identify the known list of components and historical holding percentages.
2 Parent's Identification System Review and evaluate the parent's internal systems, procedures, and controls for identifying various group components.
3 Management Inquiries Inquire with senior management to identify any newly incorporated or acquired entities, or components that ceased to be part of the group during the year.
4 Investment Portfolio Review Review the investment schedules and ledger records of the parent and its components to determine direct and indirect shareholdings in other corporate entities.
5 Joint Ventures & Arrangements Review joint venture agreements, partnership deeds, and consortium arrangements entered into by the group.
6 Other Unconsolidated Arrangements Scrutinize structured entities, special purpose vehicles (SPVs), or business combinations that management has not included in CFS.
7 Statutory Registers (Sec 186 & 190) Inspect the statutory registers maintained under the Companies Act, 2013, specifically the Register of Loans, Guarantees, Security and Acquisition of Securities (Section 186) and the Register of Contracts (Section 189/190).
8 Shareholding Changes & Board Minutes Inspect share capital changes and review Board of Directors meeting minutes during the year and subsequent to the balance sheet date to detect post-balance-sheet disposals or acquisitions.

3. Exclusions from Consolidation: AS 21 vs. Ind AS 110

Where a parent excludes any component from the consolidated financial statements, the statutory auditor must examine whether such exclusion strictly conforms to the applicable Financial Reporting Framework:

Exclusion Criteria Under AS 21 / AS 23 / AS 27 (Accounting Standards) Under Ind AS 110 / Ind AS 28 / Ind AS 111 (Indian Accounting Standards)
1. Temporary Control Exemption Permitted: A subsidiary is excluded if control is intended to be temporary because it is acquired and held exclusively with a view to its subsequent disposal in the near future (generally within 12 months). STRICTLY PROHIBITED: There is NO exemption for temporary control under Ind AS 110! If the parent controls the investee (power, variable returns, linkage), it MUST consolidate.
2. Severe Long-Term Restrictions Permitted: A subsidiary is excluded if it operates under severe long-term restrictions which significantly impair its ability to transfer funds to the parent (e.g., political sanctions, currency blocks). NO AUTOMATIC EXCLUSION: Restrictions alone do not excuse consolidation. The parent must assess whether restrictions deprive it of power over the investee; if control is retained, consolidation is mandatory.
3. Shares Held as Stock-in-Trade If held exclusively for dealing/trading with intent to sell in near future, treated as temporary control and excluded. Holding shares as stock-in-trade does NOT negate control! The entity MUST be consolidated unless the parent qualifies as an Investment Entity under Ind AS 110.

Exam Case Scenario (TYU 1 & TYK 5)

R Ltd. owns 51% voting power in S Ltd. It, however, holds and discloses all these shares as "Stock-in-trade" in its standalone financial statements. The shares are held exclusively with a view to their subsequent disposal in the near future. R Ltd. represents that while preparing Consolidated Financial Statements, S Ltd. can be excluded from consolidation. As a Statutory Auditor, how would you evaluate and deal with this situation?

Comprehensive ICAI Model Solution:

The statutory auditor must evaluate whether R Ltd. prepares financial statements under Accounting Standards (AS 21) or Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS 110):

  1. Under AS 21 (Accounting Standards):
    • Paragraph 11 of AS 21 explicitly provides that a subsidiary should be excluded from consolidation when control is intended to be temporary because the subsidiary is acquired and held exclusively with a view to its subsequent disposal in the near future.
    • If the auditor verifies sufficient appropriate documentary evidence that the shares are classified as stock-in-trade and an active plan for disposal exists (substantiating temporary control), R Ltd.'s contention of exclusion is valid under AS 21.
  2. Under Ind AS 110 (Indian Accounting Standards):
    • Ind AS 110 does NOT provide any exclusion on the grounds of "temporary control" or "shares held as stock-in-trade".
    • Under Ind AS 110, an investor controls an investee if and only if it has: (a) power over the investee; (b) exposure to variable returns; and (c) the ability to use its power to affect returns. Owning 51% voting power gives R Ltd. control.
    • R Ltd. MUST consolidate S Ltd. under Ind AS 110, unless R Ltd. qualifies as an "Investment Entity" measuring all subsidiaries at FVTPL under Ind AS 109!
Section 5 • Technical Accounting & Audit

Special Considerations: Permanent vs. Current Period Consolidation Adjustments

1. Permanent Consolidation Adjustments

Permanent Consolidation Adjustments are those adjustments that are made on the initial acquisition date or subsequent occasions when there is a change in the parent's shareholding of a consolidated entity. These adjustments establish the opening acquisition baseline and are carried forward in all future consolidations.

Determination of Goodwill or Capital Reserve

The difference between the Cost of Investment and the Parent's Share in Identifiable Net Assets of the subsidiary on the acquisition date:

  • Goodwill on Consolidation: Arises when Cost of Investment > Share of Net Assets. Recognized as an intangible asset in CFS and tested annually for impairment (Ind AS 36 / AS 28).
  • Capital Reserve: Arises when Share of Net Assets > Cost of Investment (bargain purchase). Credited to Capital Reserve under Equity.
  • Fair Valuation (Ind AS 103): Under Ind AS 103, net assets are recognized at acquisition-date fair value rather than historical book value.

Non-Controlling Interest (NCI) / Minority Interest

The portion of equity in a subsidiary not attributable, directly or indirectly, to the parent company:

  • Calculated on acquisition date based on the minority's proportionate share in identifiable net assets.
  • Presented separately within Equity in the Consolidated Balance Sheet.
  • Subsequent period share of profits/losses allocated to NCI, even if it results in a negative NCI balance (Ind AS 110).

2. Current Period Consolidation Adjustments

Current Period Consolidation Adjustments are those recurring adjustments made in each individual financial reporting period for which the consolidation is executed:

Adjustment Type Accounting & Consolidation Requirement Auditor's Specific Verification Procedures
1. Intra-Group Balances Elimination 100% elimination of reciprocal balances between group companies (debtors/creditors, loans/advances, bills receivable/payable). Verify inter-company reconciliation statements; investigate any un-reconciled differences (e.g., cash/goods in transit) and verify adjusting entries.
2. Intra-Group Transactions Elimination Elimination of all intra-group revenues, expenses, and dividends (inter-company sales, management consultancy fees, interest on intra-group loans). Vouch journal vouchers eliminating equal debits and credits from consolidated revenue and cost of sales to prevent artificial grossing up of group turnover.
3. Harmonizing Accounting Policies All group companies must follow uniform accounting policies for like transactions. If a subsidiary uses WDV depreciation while parent uses SLM, or different inventory formulas (FIFO vs Weighted Average), adjustments must be made. Inspect component accounting policies; verify whether management has quantified and adjusted differences in the consolidation worksheets.
4. Harmonizing Reporting Dates (3-Month Gap Rule) Financial statements of components must be drawn up to the same date as the parent. If impracticable, the difference between reporting dates must not exceed 3 months. Verify that reporting date gap is ≤ 3 months; examine whether adjustments have been made for significant intervening events (e.g., major capital expenditure, price shocks).
5. Foreign Currency Translation (Ind AS 21 / AS 11) Converting financial statements of foreign operations into presentation currency (INR): assets/liabilities at closing rate, income/expenses at average rate. Check exchange rates used against RBI/official rates; verify correct allocation of translation differences to Foreign Currency Translation Reserve (FCTR) under OCI.
6. Deferred Tax on Intra-Group Adjustments (Ind AS 12) Recognizing deferred tax assets/liabilities on temporary differences resulting from elimination of intra-group unrealized profits. Verify calculation of deferred tax on unrealized profit elimination at the buyer's local tax rate under Ind AS 12.

3. Elimination of Unrealized Intra-Group Profits & Dividends

Downstream vs. Upstream Sales

  • Downstream Sales (Parent → Subsidiary): The parent sells goods to a subsidiary at a profit. If goods remain unsold at year-end, 100% of the unrealized profit is eliminated against the parent's profit and inventory value.
  • Upstream Sales (Subsidiary → Parent): The subsidiary sells goods to the parent. The unrealized profit is eliminated proportionately between Parent and Non-Controlling Interest (NCI) in ratio of their shareholding.
  • PPE Capitalization: If a subsidiary sells machinery to the parent at a margin, the profit is eliminated from fixed assets and subsequent excess depreciation is adjusted.

Elimination of Intra-Group Dividends

  • Dividends paid or declared by a subsidiary to the parent must be eliminated in CFS.
  • Pre-Acquisition Dividends: Under AS 21, dividends received out of pre-acquisition profits are adjusted against the carrying cost of investment, reducing goodwill/capital reserve.
  • Post-Acquisition Dividends: Eliminated against the consolidated retained earnings and dividend income in P&L to avoid double counting group profits.
Section 6 • Audit Evidence

Management Representations in CFS (SA 580)

1. Written Representations from Parent Management (SA 580)

In accordance with Standard on Auditing (SA) 580, the principal auditor must request written representations from the management of the parent company acknowledging their responsibility for and approval of the consolidated financial statements.

Specific Written Representations Required for CFS:
  1. Completeness of Components: Management has identified and included all components (subsidiaries, associates, JVs) in CFS, and any exclusion strictly satisfies statutory exemption rules.
  2. Accuracy of Consolidation Adjustments: All permanent and current period consolidation adjustments (including intra-group eliminations and unrealized profit removals) are complete and accurate.
  3. Accounting Policy Harmonization: All differences in accounting policies across components have been appropriately quantified and adjusted.
  4. Subsequent Events: All significant intervening events occurring between the reporting dates of components and the parent have been appropriately reflected.

⚠️ If parent management refuses to provide these written representations, the auditor must disclaim an opinion or qualify the audit report on CFS in accordance with SA 580 and SA 705!

Section 7 • Auditor Reporting

Audit Reporting on Consolidated Financial Statements & CARO

1. Reporting When the Parent's Auditor is Also Auditor of All Components

When the auditor of the parent entity personally audits all subsidiaries, joint ventures, and associates included in the CFS, the audit report is straightforward:

  • The auditor has direct personal knowledge and audit evidence across 100% of the group components.
  • No reference to other auditors or SA 600 division of responsibility is required in the auditor's report.
  • The report is issued under SA 700 / SA 705 directly addressing the truth and fairness of the consolidated financial statements.

2. Reporting Under SA 600: The "Other Matter" Paragraph & Magnitude Disclosure

Where the parent's auditor is not the auditor of all components and relies on the reports of other independent auditors, the report must include an Other Matter (OM) Paragraph pursuant to SA 706 and SA 600:

Mandatory Contents of the Other Matter Paragraph (ICAI Wording)
  • Disclosure of Magnitude: The report MUST clearly disclose the magnitude of the portion of financial statements audited by other auditors by stating aggregate rupee amounts and percentages of:
    • Total Assets of the components
    • Total Revenues of the components
    • Net Cash Flows of the components
  • Division of Responsibility: Express statement that the principal auditor's opinion on CFS, in so far as it relates to those components and reports under Section 143(3), is based solely on the reports of such other auditors.
  • Non-Modification Clarification: Explicit confirmation that the group audit opinion is not modified in respect of reliance on other auditors.

3. CARO 2020 Reporting on Consolidated Financial Statements – Clause (xxi)

As a general rule, the Companies (Auditor's Report) Order, 2020 (CARO 2020) does not apply to the auditor's report on consolidated financial statements. However, there is one vital statutory exception: Clause (xxi)!

CARO 2020 Clause 3(xxi) Statutory Mandate

The auditor of CFS must report: "Whether there have been any qualifications or adverse remarks by the respective auditors in the Companies (Auditor's Report) Order (CARO) reports of the companies included in the consolidated financial statements, if yes, indicate the details of the companies and the paragraph numbers of the CARO report containing the qualifications or adverse remarks."

4. Components Not Audited: Resolving Mudit's Catch-22

Returning to our opening scenario: What should Mudit do when he discovers that the foreign subsidiary's accounts included in the group financial statements are completely unaudited?

The Statutory Protocol for Unaudited Components (Guidance Note & SA 705)

Where the financial statements of one or more components continue to remain unaudited, the principal auditor has not been able to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding those consolidated amounts. Mudit must proceed as follows:

  1. Evaluate Materiality & Pervasiveness: Mudit must assess the quantitative size (percentage of group assets, turnover, profits) and qualitative significance of the unaudited component to the group CFS.
  2. If Material but Not Pervasive: Mudit must express a Qualified Audit Opinion under SA 705, stating that except for the possible effects of the matter regarding the unaudited component, the CFS give a true and fair view.
  3. If Material and Pervasive: If the unaudited component represents a massive portion of the group (e.g., 60% of group revenue/assets), the auditor must issue a Disclaimer of Opinion under SA 705!
  4. Report under Section 143(3): State that the auditor was unable to obtain all the information and explanations necessary for the purpose of the audit in respect of the unaudited component.

Exam Problem Formulation (TYU 2 & TYK 16)

You are the statutory auditor of a listed Indian parent company with 4 subsidiaries audited by other auditors. The subsidiaries account for total assets of ₹ 900 Crores, total revenues of ₹ 1,290 Crores, and net cash outflow of ₹ 45 Crores. One subsidiary, Queen Ltd., is located in the UK whose statements were prepared under UK GAAP and audited by local UK auditors. Holding company management converted Queen Ltd.'s accounts into Ind AS, which were audited by your firm. Draft the Other Matter paragraph for the Independent Auditor's Report on CFS.

ICAI Model Draft – Other Matter Paragraph:

Other Matter

We did not audit the financial statements and other financial information, in respect of four (4) subsidiaries, whose financial statements reflect total assets of ₹ 900 Crores as at March 31, 2026, total revenues of ₹ 1,290 Crores and net cash outflows of ₹ 45 Crores for the year ended on that date. These financial statements and other financial information have been audited by other auditors, whose reports have been furnished to us by the management of the Holding Company.

Our opinion on the consolidated financial statements, in so far as it relates to the amounts and disclosures included in respect of these subsidiaries, and our report in terms of sub-section (3) of Section 143 of the Act, in so far as it relates to the aforesaid subsidiaries, is based solely on the reports of such other auditors.

One subsidiary company, Queen Ltd., is located outside India whose financial statements have been prepared in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in its respective country and audited by other auditors under local GAAS. The Holding Company's management has converted the financial statements of Queen Ltd. from UK GAAP to Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS). We have audited these conversion adjustments made by the Holding Company's management. Our opinion in so far as it relates to Queen Ltd. is based on the report of the other auditor and the conversion adjustments audited by us.

Our opinion on the consolidated financial statements is not modified in respect of the above matters with respect to our reliance on the work done and the reports of the other auditors.

Exam Case Scenario (TYU 3)

During the group audit of SuperMega Ltd., the component auditor of an Indian subsidiary, Sub-1 Ltd., issued a qualified opinion on Sub-1's standalone financial statements due to non-provision of doubtful trade receivables amounting to ₹ 80 Lakhs. The consolidated profit before tax of SuperMega Ltd. group is ₹ 1,200 Crores, and overall group materiality is set at ₹ 30 Crores. How should the parent company auditor evaluate this component qualification when drafting the audit report on the consolidated financial statements?

ICAI Model Solution:

In accordance with the ICAI Announcement dated May 25, 2017 and SA 600:

  1. While considering the observations (modifications/emphasis of matter) of the component auditor, the parent auditor must evaluate them in relation to materiality computed for the group as a whole.
  2. In the given case, the misstatement of ₹ 80 Lakhs is material to Sub-1 Ltd. on a standalone basis, but it represents only 0.067% of the group consolidated profit before tax (₹ 1,200 Crores) and is well below the group materiality threshold of ₹ 30 Crores.
  3. Therefore, the misstatement is immaterial to the Consolidated Financial Statements of the group as a whole.
  4. Conclusion: The parent company auditor SHOULD NOT modify the audit opinion on the CFS in respect of this matter! A clean (unmodified) opinion on CFS is appropriate.
Section 8 • ICAI Question Bank

Test Your Knowledge (TYK 1 to 16 – Full Detailed Solutions)

The following section provides comprehensive, 100% complete answers to all 16 Test Your Knowledge questions from the official ICAI Study Material for Chapter 13:

ICAI Model Answer:

Yes, preparation of Consolidated Financial Statements (CFS) is legally mandatory under the Companies Act, 2013:

  • Section 129(3): Where a company has one or more subsidiaries, it shall, in addition to standalone financial statements, prepare a consolidated financial statement of the company and of all the subsidiaries in the same form and manner as that of its own, to be laid before the AGM.

⚠️ High-Frequency CA Final Exam Flash Traps & Pitfalls
  • Trap 1 (Review of Other Auditor's Working Papers): Believing that the Principal Auditor has the right or duty to review detailed working papers of component auditors. Fact: SA 600 does NOT permit the principal auditor to review working papers of other auditors! Reliance is placed on their audit report and questionnaires.
  • Trap 2 (Component Qualification vs. Group CFS): Assuming that if a component auditor qualifies their report, the parent auditor must automatically qualify the group CFS. Fact: Under the ICAI May 2017 Announcement, the parent auditor must evaluate materiality from the perspective of the group as a whole!
  • Trap 3 (CARO on CFS): Assuming CARO applies to CFS. Fact: CARO 2020 does NOT apply to CFS, except for Clause 3(xxi) which requires listing all qualifications in component CARO reports!
  • Trap 4 (Stock-in-Trade Exemption under Ind AS): Excluding a subsidiary because its shares are held as "stock-in-trade". Fact: Under Ind AS 110, holding shares as stock-in-trade does NOT exempt an entity from consolidation!
