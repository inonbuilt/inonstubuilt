Phase 01

Overview & Learning Outcomes

After performing detailed substantive audit procedures (test of details, analytical procedures, substantive tests of transactions and balances), an auditor cannot immediately sign the audit report. There are critical wrap-up and review procedures that must be executed to ensure that the financial statements represent a true and fair view right up to the date of signing the report.

ICAI Prescribed Learning Outcomes
  • Refreshen up your knowledge about procedures relating to completion and review of financial statements.
  • Glance through relevant Standards on Auditing—SA 560 (Subsequent Events), SA 570 (Going Concern), and SA 580 (Written Representations).
  • Gain application-based knowledge of these Standards in practical corporate scenarios and statutory audits.
  • Understand core concepts through detailed analytical case studies, chronological timeline matrices, and exam-level practical problems.
  • Navigate complex real-world dilemmas faced by statutory auditors regarding dual dating, sudden factory disasters, hostile management refusals, and going concern uncertainties.
Case Study

Opening Case: CA Sujatha M (Energy-Saving Lights Factory Fire)

The Real-World Dilemma: Disaster, Forensic Doubt & Resolution

CA Sujatha M was appointed statutory auditor of a company manufacturing energy-saving electric lights. During the financial year, a devastating fire gutted the plant, completely destroying the factory building, plant & machinery, and raw material/finished inventories, resulting in an estimated asset loss of ₹ 50 crores. Operations came to a complete halt.

The company lodged an insurance claim, but no progress on settlement had been achieved by the financial year-end (31st March). The company lacked liquidity to rebuild assets independently. Promoters put personal properties up for distress sale, but the sluggish real estate market prevented any transactions. Concurrently, regular customers began migrating to competitors.

CA Sujatha faced a monumental dilemma: Could the company restart its business and continue as a going concern? With four months remaining before statutory reporting deadlines, she adopted a "Wait and Watch" policy to track insurance settlement progress.

The Forensic Shock: Suddenly, an initial report from the forensic testing lab cast severe doubt on the authenticity of the fire, alleging potential foul play. The insurance company stalled the claim indefinitely. Management submitted a recovery plan and a cash flow forecast, but without insurance proceeds, the plans appeared highly speculative and unrealistic.

The Reversal & Resolution: On rigorous scrutiny, management uncovered glaring technical contradictions and factual errors in the forensic lab's findings. Why would a company earning excellent margins, expanding aggressively, and paying handsome advance taxes commit arson? Management presented a point-wise rebuttal backed by uninterrupted CCTV video recordings and digital heat logs. Convinced by the undeniable proof, the insurance company ordered the forensic lab to re-examine the case. The lab issued a positive revised report clearing the claim for imminent disbursement!

Audit Takeaway: Performing active audit procedures on subsequent events under SA 560 enabled CA Sujatha to identify crucial mitigating factors that resolved the material uncertainty regarding the company's going concern assumption under SA 570!

SA 560

SA 560: Subsequent Events (Comprehensive Master Breakdown)

SA 560 deals with the auditor’s responsibilities relating to subsequent events in an audit of financial statements. Financial statements are prepared as of a specific date (e.g., 31st March), but the audit process and issuance occur several weeks or months later.

Core Statutory Definitions under SA 560
  • Date of Financial Statements: The date of the end of the latest period covered by the financial statements (e.g., 31st March 2026).
  • Date of Approval of Financial Statements: The date on which all the statements that comprise the financial statements (including the related notes) have been prepared and those with recognized authority have asserted that they have taken responsibility for them (e.g., Board of Directors signing on 20th May 2026).
  • Date of the Auditor's Report: The date the auditor signs the report expressing an opinion on the financial statements. It cannot be earlier than the date on which the auditor has obtained sufficient appropriate audit evidence, including approval of financial statements (e.g., 25th May 2026).
  • Date the Financial Statements are Issued: The date that the auditor’s report and audited financial statements are made available to third parties (e.g., published for shareholders, posted on website, or filed with ROC on 15th June 2026).
  • Subsequent Events: Events occurring between the date of the financial statements and the date of the auditor’s report, AND facts that become known to the auditor after the date of the auditor’s report.
🕒 The Three Chronological Time Periods under SA 560

SA 560 divides subsequent events and facts into three distinct chronological phases, each carrying radically different auditor responsibilities and procedural requirements:

Period 01: Active Duty

Between Balance Sheet Date & Date of Auditor's Report

Auditor's Duty: Active affirmative duty to design and perform audit procedures to identify ALL events requiring adjustment or disclosure.

  • Type 1 (Adjusting Events): Provide evidence of conditions that existed at the balance sheet date (e.g., customer insolvency, court decree confirming pre-existing liability, subsequent inventory sale below cost). Requires adjustment in figures.
  • Type 2 (Non-Adjusting Events): Conditions that arose after the balance sheet date (e.g., major factory fire in April, corporate acquisition, loss of plant due to earthquake, proposed dividend). Requires disclosure in notes if material.
Period 02: Responsive Duty

After Report Date but BEFORE Financial Statements are Issued

Auditor's Duty: NO obligation to perform ongoing audit procedures. However, if a fact becomes known that would have caused the auditor to amend the report:

  • Step 1: Discuss with management and TCWG.
  • Step 2: Determine if financial statements need amendment.
  • Step 3: Inquire how management intends to address it.
  • If amended: Extend procedures and either Dual Date or issue a fresh report with EOM/OM.
  • If refused: Modify opinion (if report not yet released) or notify TCWG and take legal steps to prevent reliance.
Period 03: Post-Issuance Action

AFTER Financial Statements Have Been Issued to Public

Auditor's Duty: NO obligation to conduct ongoing procedures. If a material fact becomes known that existed at report date:

  • Step 1: Discuss with management and TCWG.
  • Step 2: Determine if financial statements need revision.
  • Step 3: Inquire management’s plan to inform all recipients of previously issued financial statements.
  • If amended: Issue new amended report with an Emphasis of Matter (EOM) or Other Matter (OM) paragraph.
  • If management refuses: Take appropriate legal steps to prevent public reliance on the audit report.
🔍 Required Audit Procedures for Period 1 (Active Phase)

The auditor shall take into account the risk assessment in determining the nature and extent of procedures, which shall include:

  • Understanding Management's Procedures: Inquiring into procedures established by management to ensure that subsequent events are systematically identified.
  • Inquiry of Management & TCWG: Inquiring whether any subsequent events have occurred affecting the financial statements (e.g., new commitments, borrowings, guarantees, asset sales, share capital increases, government expropriation, litigation settlements).
  • Reading Minutes of Meetings: Examining minutes of meetings of shareholders, Board of Directors, Audit Committee, and executive management held after the balance sheet date, and inquiring about matters discussed in meetings for which minutes are not yet drafted.
  • Reading Interim Financial Statements: Reviewing the entity’s latest available subsequent interim financial statements, management accounts, operating budgets, and cash flow projections.
  • Legal Counsel Inquiries: Inquiring of entity's litigation department or external legal advisers regarding progress of substantial lawsuits and claims.
  • Mandatory Written Representations: Requesting written representations under SA 580 that all events occurring subsequent to the balance sheet date requiring adjustment or disclosure have been adjusted or disclosed.
⚖️ Period 2 & Period 3 Procedures: The Dual Dating Mechanism
How Dual Dating Protects the Auditor

When financial statements are amended after the audit report date solely to incorporate a specific subsequent event, the auditor has two procedural options:

  1. Extend Audit Procedures & Issue a Fresh Report: The auditor extends subsequent event procedures to the new date of approval. The new audit report carries the new date. Risk for Auditor: The auditor becomes legally liable for discovering ALL subsequent events across the entire entity right up to the new date!
  2. Dual Dating the Auditor's Report: Under law and auditing standards, the auditor restricts additional audit procedures solely to the amendment described in the relevant note to the financial statements. The report includes an additional date restricted to that amendment.
Format of Dual Dating:
"May 20, 2026, except as to Note 28, which is as of July 15, 2026."
Legal Significance: The auditor’s responsibility for subsequent events outside Note 28 remains terminated on May 20, 2026!
📝 TEST YOUR UNDERSTANDING 1: Move Fast Limited (Bahadurgarh Flooding & Insurance Claim)
▼

Scenario: “Move Fast Limited” manufactures shoes and slippers in Bahadurgarh, Haryana. In September 2025, unprecedented rains choked sewer systems, inundating the factory premises and damaging stocks. The company lodged an insurance claim of ₹ 1 crore, showing it as a claim receivable as of 31st March 2026 since it remained unsettled at year-end. In April 2026, the insurance surveyor recommended a settlement of ₹ 45 lacs, to which the company consented. On 15th May 2026, ₹ 45 lacs was credited to the company's bank account. You finished substantive procedures by the end of May 2026. What is your responsibility as statutory auditor?

✔ Master Solution & SA 560 Application:

1. Identification of Event Nature: The event occurred between the balance sheet date (31st March 2026) and the date of the auditor's report (late May/June 2026). The flood condition existed at the balance sheet date (September 2025). The subsequent insurance settlement of ₹ 45 lacs in April/May 2026 provides additional objective evidence of the realizable value of the claim asset that existed on 31st March 2026.

2. Accounting Treatment (AS 4 / Ind AS 10 & SA 560): This is a classic Type 1 Adjusting Event. The company cannot continue to show ₹ 1 crore as an asset. It must adjust the financial statements by recognizing the claim receivable at ₹ 45 lacs and writing off the remaining ₹ 55 lacs to the Profit and Loss Statement for the year ended 31st March 2026.

3. Auditor's Duty: Under SA 560, the auditor must perform procedures to ensure that the adjusting event is properly reflected in the financial statements. If management refuses to make the adjustment, the auditor must modify the audit opinion (Qualified or Adverse under SA 705) due to material overstatement of assets and profit.

📝 TEST YOUR UNDERSTANDING 2: CA Anuj (Proposed Dividend of ₹ 1 Crore Declared Post Year-End)
▼

Scenario: CA Anuj is auditing a listed company for the year ending 31st March 2026. At a Board meeting held on 17th April 2026, a dividend of ₹ 1 crore was proposed to equity shareholders (@ ₹ 10 per share), likely to be approved at the ensuing AGM. Audit procedures are nearing completion, and he contemplates finalizing the report by 31st July 2026. Is there any statutory/audit responsibility cast upon him?

✔ Master Solution & SA 560 Application:

1. Accounting Framework (AS 4 Revised / Ind AS 10): Under accounting standards, dividends declared after the reporting period but before the financial statements are approved for issue do not represent an obligation/liability at the balance sheet date. Therefore, no liability is recognized in the Balance Sheet as of 31st March 2026.

2. Type 2 Non-Adjusting Event: The event arose after the balance sheet date. It requires disclosure in the notes to accounts / Director's Report.

3. Auditor's Responsibility: Under SA 560, CA Anuj must verify that:

  • The proposed dividend of ₹ 1 crore has NOT been recognized as a liability in the Balance Sheet as of 31st March 2026.
  • Appropriate disclosure has been made in the notes to the financial statements in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework.
  • If management incorrectly creates a provision/liability for the proposed dividend, CA Anuj must request an adjustment, failing which he must consider the impact on his audit opinion.
SA 570

SA 570: Going Concern (Comprehensive Master Breakdown)

Going concern is one of the fundamental accounting assumptions underlying the preparation of general purpose financial statements. Under the going concern basis of accounting, an entity is viewed as continuing in business for the foreseeable future (defined as a minimum period of 12 months from the balance sheet date). It is assumed that the entity has neither the intention nor the necessity of liquidation or of curtailing materially the scale of its operations.

Division of Statutory Responsibilities under SA 570

👨‍💼 Management's Responsibilities

  • Make a specific assessment of the entity's ability to continue as a going concern for at least 12 months from the reporting date.
  • Prepare financial statements on a going concern basis unless management intends to liquidate or cease operations, or has no realistic alternative.
  • Make full and transparent disclosures in notes to accounts when material uncertainties exist.

🔍 Auditor's Responsibilities

  • Obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the appropriateness of management's use of the going concern basis.
  • Conclude whether a material uncertainty exists regarding entity's survival.
  • Determine the exact implications for the auditor’s report.
  • Caveat: The auditor CANNOT be expected to guarantee the entity's future viability!
⚠️ Indicators of Doubt on Going Concern (Financial, Operating & Other)

During risk assessment and substantive testing, the auditor must remain alert to events or conditions that individually or collectively cast significant doubt on the going concern assumption:

Category Specific Events or Conditions Audit Significance
1. Financial Indicators • Net liability or net current liability position.
• Fixed-term borrowings approaching maturity without realistic prospects of renewal or repayment.
• Excessive reliance on short-term borrowings to finance long-term capital assets.
• Indications of withdrawal of financial support by creditors.
• Negative operating cash flows indicated by historical or prospective financial statements.
• Adverse key financial ratios (e.g., current ratio < 1, interest coverage ratio < 1).
• Substantial operating losses or significant deterioration in the value of assets used to generate cash flows.
• Arrears or discontinuance of dividends.
• Inability to pay creditors on due dates; change from credit to cash-on-delivery (COD) terms by major suppliers.
• Inability to comply with the terms of loan agreements (debt covenant breaches). 		Direct cash insolvency warning signs; triggers mandatory cash flow sensitivity testing.
2. Operating Indicators • Management intentions to liquidate the entity or cease operations.
• Loss of key management without qualified replacement.
• Loss of a major market, key customer(s), franchise, license, or principal supplier.
• Severe labor difficulties or prolonged industrial strikes.
• Shortages of essential raw materials or imported components.
• Emergence of a highly successful direct competitor disrupting pricing. 		Operational paralysis; prevents generation of future revenues even if current debt is settled.
3. Other Indicators • Non-compliance with capital adequacy or statutory regulatory requirements.
• Pending legal or regulatory proceedings that may, if successful, result in claims the entity cannot satisfy.
• Changes in legislation, taxation, or government policy expected to adversely affect the industry.
• Uninsured or severely underinsured catastrophes (e.g., factory fire, tsunami, flood). 		External existential threats; requires legal counsel consultation and regulatory assessment.
🛠️ Auditor's Specific Procedures When Events/Conditions Are Identified

When events or conditions casting significant doubt are identified, the auditor shall perform additional audit procedures to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence:

Core Additional Procedures under SA 570
  1. Request Management Assessment: Where management has not yet performed an assessment of the entity’s ability to continue as a going concern, request management to make its assessment.
  2. Evaluate Management’s Future Action Plans: Inquire about and evaluate management's plans for future actions, including whether the outcome of these plans is likely to improve the situation and whether management’s plans are feasible in the circumstances (e.g., plans to liquidate non-core assets, borrow money or restructure debt, reduce or delay expenditures, or increase capital).
  3. Scrutinize Cash Flow Forecasts: Where the entity has prepared a cash flow forecast, evaluate:
    • The reliability of underlying data used to generate the forecast.
    • Whether there is adequate support for the assumptions underlying the forecast.
    • Comparing previous period forecasts with actual results achieved to evaluate forecasting accuracy.
  4. Consider Subsequent Events: Evaluate whether any additional facts or information have become available since the date on which management made its assessment.
  5. Obtain Written Representations: Request written representations from management and, where appropriate, TCWG regarding their plans for future actions and the feasibility of these plans.
  6. Communicate with TCWG: Promptly communicate identified events or conditions with Those Charged with Governance to discuss mitigating actions.
  7. Delays in Financial Statement Approval: If there is significant delay in the approval of the financial statements, inquire into reasons; if delay relates to going concern assessment, perform additional procedures.
📊 The Master Reporting Decision Tree under SA 570 (Crucial Exam Concept)

The auditor's reporting responsibilities under SA 570 (Revised) depend on the appropriateness of the going concern assumption and the adequacy of disclosures:

Master Decision Matrix: Going Concern Reporting

Case 1: Going Concern Basis is INAPPROPRIATE

If the financial statements have been prepared using the going concern basis of accounting but, in the auditor’s judgment, management’s use of the going concern basis is inappropriate:
👉 The auditor shall express an ADVERSE OPINION under SA 705.
Note: This applies regardless of whether or not the financial statements include disclosure of the inappropriateness of management’s use of the going concern basis!

Case 2: Going Concern Basis is APPROPRIATE BUT a Material Uncertainty Exists

Here, the reporting splits into two sub-cases based entirely on disclosures:

Sub-Case 2A: ADEQUATE DISCLOSURE Made in Financial Statements

• The auditor shall express an UNMODIFIED (CLEAN) OPINION.
• The auditor’s report SHALL include a separate section under the specific mandatory heading:
"Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" (MURGC)

Mandatory Elements of MURGC Section:

  1. Draw attention to the specific Note in the financial statements that discloses the principal events/conditions and management plans.
  2. State that these events or conditions indicate that a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the entity’s ability to continue as a going concern.
  3. State explicitly that the auditor’s opinion is NOT modified in respect of the matter.
Sub-Case 2B: INADEQUATE / NO DISCLOSURE Made in Financial Statements

• The auditor shall express a QUALIFIED OPINION or ADVERSE OPINION in accordance with SA 705.
• In the "Basis for Qualified / Adverse Opinion" section, state clearly that a material uncertainty exists casting significant doubt on going concern and that the financial statements do not adequately disclose this matter.

Case 3: Management Unwilling to Make or Extend Assessment

If management refuses to make or extend its assessment to cover at least 12 months when requested by the auditor:
👉 The auditor shall consider the implications for the auditor’s report—express a Qualified Opinion or Disclaimer of Opinion due to limitation on scope under SA 705.

📝 TEST YOUR UNDERSTANDING 3: CA Somya (Poultry Farm Bird Flu Epidemic & Restructured Loans)
▼

Scenario: CA Somya audits a poultry farming company rearing birds and selling eggs. Incorporated in 2013, it had expanded rapidly to eastern India. However, over the past two years, recurrent bird flu outbreaks wiped out most livestock, collapsing sales from ₹ 50 crores to ₹ 10 crores. Management plans to restart with fresh batches of birds. Bank working capital facilities were restructured into term loans with repayments starting in the ensuing year, but no fresh credit was granted. Management plans longer credit periods from feed suppliers. Villagers have accused the company of spreading air pollution. How should CA Somya evaluate management's action plan and cash flow forecasts?

✔ Master Solution & SA 570 Procedures:

Under SA 570, CA Somya must rigorously test the feasibility and underlying data of management's plans and cash flow forecasts:

  • Livestock Purchase Feasibility: Verify how fresh batches of birds will be financed when the bank has refused fresh credit and existing cash reserves are depleted.
  • Debt Servicing Verification: Review restructured loan sanction letters and amortization schedules to verify that repayments starting in the ensuing year are accurately factored into the cash outflow forecasts.
  • Supplier Credit Reasonableness: Scrutinize the assumption of getting extended credit from animal feed suppliers; suppliers are unlikely to offer longer credit to an entity facing severe livestock wipeouts and financial distress.
  • Preventive Biosecurity Costs: Verify whether cash flow projections include necessary capital and operating expenditure for vaccination, disinfection, quarantine infrastructure, and frequent veterinary check-ups to prevent bird flu recurrence.
  • Environmental & Pollution Liabilities: Assess potential financial and operational fallout from villager protests regarding air pollution, including potential closure notices or penalties by the State Pollution Control Board.
📝 TEST YOUR UNDERSTANDING 4: CFO Disclosing Going Concern Inappropriateness vs Uncertainty
▼

Scenario: The CFO of a company facing severe business distress informs the auditor that management is willing to disclose in the notes that the going concern assumption is inappropriate. The CFO claims that because adequate disclosure is provided in the notes, the auditor must issue an unmodified (clean) report. Is the CFO correct?

✔ Master Solution & SA 570 Rule:

1. CFO's Claim is Completely Erroneous: Under SA 570, if the financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis, but in the auditor's judgment the use of the going concern basis is inappropriate, the auditor SHALL express an ADVERSE OPINION.

2. Inappropriateness cannot be cured by Disclosure: The mandatory requirement to express an adverse opinion applies regardless of whether or not the financial statements include disclosure of the inappropriateness of management’s use of the going concern basis of accounting.

3. Contrast with Material Uncertainty: An unmodified opinion with a "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section is permitted ONLY when the going concern basis is appropriate, but a material uncertainty exists and is adequately disclosed.

SA 580

SA 580: Written Representations (Comprehensive Master Breakdown)

SA 580 deals with the auditor’s responsibility to obtain written representations from management and, where appropriate, Those Charged with Governance (TCWG). In an audit engagement, management acknowledges its fundamental responsibility for the preparation of financial statements and the completeness of information provided.

Definition & Exclusions under SA 580

A Written Representation is defined as:
"A written statement by management provided to the auditor to confirm certain matters or to support other audit evidence."

Critical Statutory Negative Definition: Written representations in this context DO NOT INCLUDE:

  • Financial statements themselves;
  • The assertions contained within the financial statements; or
  • The entity’s supporting books and records.
⚖️ Evidential Quality & Legal Standing of Written Representations
The Fundamental Evidential Rule

Although written representations provide necessary audit evidence, they DO NOT provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence on their own about any of the matters with which they deal!

  • No Substitute: Written representations cannot be used as a substitute for performing other substantive audit procedures. (e.g., obtaining a representation that inventory exists does not eliminate the need for physical count observation under SA 501!).
  • Corroborative Role: They complement and corroborate other audit evidence obtained regarding management's intentions, judgments, and completeness of records.
👥 Management From Whom Representations Are Requested

Written representations are requested from members of management who have appropriate responsibilities for the financial statements and knowledge of the matters concerned. Normally, this includes:

  • The Chief Executive Officer (CEO) / Managing Director, and
  • The Chief Financial Officer (CFO) or individuals occupying equivalent executive positions.
📅 Date of and Period(s) Covered by Written Representations
Strict Chronological Rules for Representation Letters
  1. Timing of Date: The date of the written representations shall be as near as practicable to, but NOT AFTER, the date of the auditor's report on the financial statements!
    Why? The auditor is concerned with events occurring right up to the report date. The opinion cannot be expressed, and the report cannot be dated, prior to receiving the written representations!
  2. Periods Covered: The written representations shall be for ALL periods referred to in the auditor’s report. If comparative figures are reported, management must re-affirm that prior period representations remain valid.
  3. Change in Management: Even if current management was not present during the prior periods under audit, current management remains responsible for the financial statements as a whole and must provide the requested representations.
❓ Inconsistencies & Management Refusal to Provide Representations

When issues arise regarding the reliability of representations or when management refuses to sign:

Circumstance Auditor's Immediate Actions Impact on Audit Opinion (SA 705)
Inconsistency with Other Audit Evidence Perform additional audit procedures to resolve the matter; re-assess competence, integrity, and ethical values of management. If unresolved, evaluate reliability of other representations; determine potential scope limitation.
Doubts Over Management Integrity Conclude that written representations are unreliable; evaluate whether sufficient appropriate audit evidence can be obtained elsewhere. If integrity concerns are pervasive, issue a Disclaimer of Opinion under SA 705.
Management REFUSES to Provide Requested Written Representations 1. Discuss the matter with management.
2. Re-evaluate the integrity of management.
3. Evaluate the effect that this may have on the reliability of representations in general and other audit evidence. 		DISCLAIMER OF OPINION
Under SA 580 & SA 705, refusal constitutes a pervasive limitation on the scope of the audit.
📝 TEST YOUR UNDERSTANDING 5: RES Limited (Representation Letter dated 15.04.2026 vs Report dated 31.07.2026)
▼

Scenario: RES Limited provided a written representation letter to statutory auditors M/s CTK & Associates for the audit of FY 2025-26. The representation letter was dated 15th April 2026 and signed solely by the Chief Financial Officer (CFO). The audit was completed and the audit report was issued and dated 31st July 2026. Point out the severe anomalies in this written representation.

✔ Master Solution & SA 580 Anomalies:

Anomaly 1: Unacceptable Time Lag in Representation Date:

  • Under SA 580, the date of written representations shall be as near as practicable to, but not after, the date of the auditor's report.
  • The audit report is dated 31st July 2026, whereas the representation letter is dated 15th April 2026—a massive gap of 3.5 months!
  • This signifies that the auditor has obtained zero management representations regarding subsequent events (SA 560), lawsuits, contingent liabilities, or fraud occurrences happening between 15th April 2026 and 31st July 2026. The auditor cannot sign the report without an updated representation letter dated on or near 31st July 2026!

Anomaly 2: Deficient Signatory Authorization:

  • The letter is signed solely by the Chief Financial Officer.
  • Under SA 580, representations must be obtained from individuals with overall operational and financial responsibility, specifically including the Chief Executive Officer (CEO) / Managing Director alongside the CFO.
Case Scenario 01

Integrated Case Scenario 1: M/s ABC Stores Ltd (Fire & Fast-Track Lawsuit)

Case Facts & Timeline

CA Sneha, a partner in M/s J & Associates, is statutory auditor of M/s ABC Stores Ltd for FY 2025-26. She was ready to sign the audit report on 01.07.2026, pending receipt of certain written representations from management. She sent Deepak (articled trainee) to collect them.

Upon return, Deepak revealed that on 27.06.2026, a severe fire at the company’s main warehouse destroyed major stock inventories, badly disrupting operations. However, management planned to resume business from an alternate leased location.

CA Sneha postponed signing the report to assess the impact. She requested management to disclose the event in the notes to accounts for 2025-26. Management refused. Consequently, on 15.07.2026, CA Sneha issued her audit report with a Modified Opinion due to non-disclosure of this material non-adjusting event.

On 20.07.2026, management met CA Sneha urgently: on 18.07.2026, a fast-track commercial court ruled against the company in a creditor lawsuit, ordering an immediate payout of ₹ 2 crores. The underlying events causing the lawsuit had occurred after 31.03.2026. The audited financial statements had NOT yet been issued to third parties or filed with any regulator.

CA Sneha required management to amend the financial statements to reflect both the warehouse fire and the lawsuit settlement. This time, management agreed, amended the financial statements, and the Board of Directors approved the amended financial statements on 25.07.2026. CA Sneha now prepares to issue a new audit report.

🎯 Integrated Multiple Choice Questions (Scenario 1)
Q1: What would be the appropriate date of signing of the new audit report?
▼
(a) 20.07.2026
(b) Anytime between 15.07.2026 & 18.07.2026
(c) On or after 25.07.2026 [CORRECT]
(d) Anytime between 15.07.2026 & 25.07.2026
✔ Correct Answer: (c) On or after 25.07.2026

Reason: Under SA 560, the new auditor’s report shall not be dated earlier than the date of approval of the amended financial statements. Since the Board approved the amended accounts on 25.07.2026, the audit report must be dated on or after 25.07.2026.

Q2: Which of the following does NOT fall under subsequent event audit procedures under SA 560?
▼
(a) Obtaining an understanding of management’s procedures set up to identify subsequent events.
(b) Inquiring of management w.r.t the occurrence of any such subsequent events.
(c) Reading the minutes of the meetings of the board held after the balance sheet date during this period.
(d) Getting the Interim financial statements prepared till the date of the audit report mandatorily as a condition to issue the audit report. [CORRECT]
✔ Correct Answer: (d) Getting interim FS prepared mandatorily

Reason: SA 560 requires reading the latest available subsequent interim financial statements, if any. The auditor has no authority to mandatorily demand that interim financial statements be specially prepared up to the date of the audit report as a pre-condition.

Q3: W.r.t the first audit report dated 15.07.2026, which type of opinion was most likely provided by her?
▼
(a) Modified opinion. [CORRECT]
(b) Unqualified opinion.
(c) Disclaimer of opinion.
(d) Including a statement in Emphasis of Matter/Other matters para.
✔ Correct Answer: (a) Modified opinion

Reason: Management refused to disclose the material non-adjusting fire event in the financial statements. Non-disclosure of a material subsequent event violates AS 4 / Ind AS 10, requiring the auditor to modify the opinion under SA 705.

Q4: W.r.t the new audit report issued, which type of opinion is most appropriate?
▼
(a) Disclaimer of opinion.
(b) Unqualified opinion without any extra paragraphs.
(c) Adverse opinion.
(d) Unqualified opinion and a statement in Emphasis of Matter / Other Matter para. [CORRECT]
✔ Correct Answer: (d) Unqualified opinion with EOM/OM para

Reason: Since management amended the financial statements to incorporate proper disclosures for both events, the opinion is unmodified (unqualified). Furthermore, under SA 560, the new report must contain an Emphasis of Matter or Other Matter paragraph drawing attention to the amendment note and prior report.

Q5: The fire event occurring on 27.06.2026 in the company’s plant requires which action by management?
▼
(a) Disclosure in notes to accounts. [CORRECT]
(b) Adjustment in financial statements.
(c) Waiting for the insurance company to settle the claim.
(d) Preparing financial statements afresh.
✔ Correct Answer: (a) Disclosure in notes to accounts

Reason: The fire occurred on 27.06.2026 (after 31.03.2026). The condition did not exist at the balance sheet date. This is a Type 2 Non-Adjusting Event under AS 4 / Ind AS 10 and SA 560, requiring disclosure in the notes to accounts.

TYK Master

Test Your Knowledge (TYK 1 to 12 - Comprehensive Solved Practical Questions)

TYK 1: Ramadhan & Co. / XYZ Company Ltd (Post-Report Approval of ₹ 5,800 Cr Govt Incentive)
▼

Question: Ramadhan & Co. signed the audit report of XYZ Company Ltd for FY 2025-26 on 04/05/2026. The AGM is in August 2026. On 06/05/2026, the company received communication from the Central Government that ₹ 5,800 crore of pending incentives for 2025-26 had been approved for payment before end of May 2026. This had not been recognized earlier due to uncertainty. On 08/05/2026, the Board amended the financial statements, approved them, and requested the auditor to issue a fresh audit report. Can the auditor accede to the Board's request?

✔ Master Solution & SA 560 Application:

1. Chronological Classification: This is a Period 2 Subsequent Event (Fact becoming known after the date of the auditor's report but before the financial statements are issued).

2. Auditor's Procedural Obligations: Under SA 560:

  • The auditor has no obligation to perform ongoing procedures after report date, but when a material fact becomes known that would have caused report amendment, the auditor shall:
    1. Discuss with management / TCWG;
    2. Determine whether financial statements need amendment; and
    3. Inquire how management intends to address it.
  • Since management amended the accounts to recognize the ₹ 5,800 crore incentive and the Board approved them on 08/05/2026, the auditor shall carry out audit procedures on the amendment.

3. Reporting Options Available:

  • Option A (Fresh Audit Report): Issue a new auditor's report on the amended financial statements. Crucial Rule: The new report shall not be dated earlier than 08/05/2026 (the date of approval of the amended financial statements) and must include an Emphasis of Matter (EOM) or Other Matter (OM) paragraph.
  • Option B (Dual Dating): Amend the audit report to include an additional date restricted solely to the incentive amendment (e.g., "May 4, 2026, except as to Note XX, which is as of May 8, 2026").

Conclusion: Yes, Ramadhan & Co. can accede to the Board’s request and issue a new audit report, subject to complying with the dating and procedural rules of SA 560.

TYK 2: M/s Airlift Ltd (Continuous Losses, Grounded Aircraft & Recovery Committee)
▼

Question: M/s Airlift Ltd (passenger air transport) incurs continuous losses and sales drop due to stiff competition and frequent aircraft breakdowns. Management is uncertain about continuing in the near future and has formed a study committee for recovery. Management plans to disclose this uncertainty in the notes to accounts for the year ended 31/03/2026. What are the reporting requirements in the Independent Auditor's Report?

✔ Master Solution & SA 570 Application:

1. Evaluation of Circumstances: Management has used the going concern basis of accounting. Severe operating losses and fleet grounding represent conditions casting significant doubt. However, management is actively formulating recovery plans via a dedicated study committee, making the going concern basis appropriate, though a material uncertainty exists.

2. Reporting Requirement under SA 570 (Revised):

  • Because adequate disclosure of the material uncertainty and management's recovery committee is made in the notes to accounts, the auditor shall express an UNMODIFIED (CLEAN) OPINION.
  • The auditor’s report SHALL include a separate section under the heading: "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" (MURGC).

3. Exact Statutory MURGC Paragraph Requirements:

  • Draw attention to the specific note in the financial statements disclosing the continuous losses, grounded aircraft, and uncertainty.
  • State that these events indicate that a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the entity’s ability to continue as a going concern.
  • State explicitly: "Our opinion is not modified in respect of this matter."
TYK 3: PRSH & Co / Make My Journey Ltd (Verbal vs Written Reps & Date / Period Covered)
▼

Question: PRSH & Co is statutory auditor of Make My Journey Ltd (Turnover ₹ 2,000 cr, Profit ₹ 190 cr). Management argued that representations need not be written because verbal discussions were documented by the audit team. At closure, confusion remains regarding the date of, and period covered by, the written representation letter. Advise in view of SA 580.

✔ Master Solution & SA 580 Rules:

1. Verbal Representations are Inadmissible: Under SA 580, representations must be in written form addressed to the auditor. Verbal discussions documented in audit working papers do NOT satisfy the mandatory statutory requirement of a management representation letter.

2. Date of Written Representations:

  • The date of written representations shall be as near as practicable to, but NOT AFTER, the date of the auditor's report.
  • The audit opinion cannot be expressed, and the report cannot be dated, prior to the date of the written representations.

3. Period Covered:

  • Written representations must cover ALL periods referred to in the auditor's report.
  • Management must reaffirm that representations made with respect to prior comparative periods remain appropriate.
TYK 4: CA Shobit / XYZ Ltd (European Rug Dye Injury Claim & ₹ 4 Cr Out-of-Court Settlement)
▼

Question: CA Shobit audits XYZ Ltd (handicraft exporter to Europe) for 2025-26. In July 2026, he learns that an overseas buyer filed a legal claim on 1st June 2026 for injury caused to a customer by sub-standard toxic dyes in rugs shipped in August 2025. Management agreed to an out-of-court settlement of ₹ 4 crore to protect its reputation. The financial statements are silent on this issue. Discuss how CA Shobit should proceed.

✔ Master Solution & SA 560 / AS 4 Application:

1. Type 1 Adjusting Event: The shipment containing defective rugs was dispatched in August 2025 (during FY 2025-26). Thus, the condition existed at the balance sheet date (31.03.2026). The subsequent legal claim on 01.06.2026 and ₹ 4 crore settlement provide conclusive evidence of the liability that existed at year-end.

2. Accounting Treatment Required: Management must create a provision of ₹ 4 crores in the financial statements for FY 2025-26 as per AS 29 / Ind AS 37 and AS 4 / Ind AS 10.

3. Auditor's Action:

  • CA Shobit must request management to adjust the financial statements by creating a provision of ₹ 4 crores.
  • If management agrees and makes the adjustment, an unmodified opinion may be issued.
  • If management refuses to adjust, CA Shobit must modify his audit opinion (Qualified or Adverse under SA 705) due to material misstatement of profit and liabilities.
TYK 5: CA N / BQR Limited (Restated Financial Statements for 3 Years & Dual Dating)
▼

Question: CA N is auditing restated financial statements of BQR Limited for past 3 financial years (2025-26, 2024-25, 2023-24). The management requests CA N to restrict subsequent event procedures solely to the restatement amendments. Discuss whether CA N can dual date the report.

✔ Master Solution & Dual Dating:

Under SA 560, when an entity amends or restates financial statements for prior years, the auditor has two options:

  • Full Extension: Extend subsequent event procedures to the new approval date (which expands auditor liability across all areas).
  • Dual Dating: If permitted by law and regulation, the auditor may amend the auditor’s report to include an additional date restricted solely to that amendment/restatement.

In such a case, CA N’s audit procedures on subsequent events outside the specific restatement notes are deemed to have terminated on the original audit report date.

TYK 6: Mudit & Associates / GRF Private Limited (Evaluating Going Concern Mitigating Factors)
▼

Question: GRF Private Limited suffered acute liquidity stress. The management presents mitigating plans including promoter equity infusion and renewal of working capital credit lines. How should Mudit & Associates evaluate these mitigating factors under SA 570?

✔ Master Solution & SA 570 Procedures:
  • Promoter Equity Infusion: Verify legally binding commitments, escrow deposits, or bank sanction letters proving the promoters have financial capability and intent to infuse funds.
  • Bank Credit Renewal: Examine bank renewal letters, sanction terms, and compliance with loan covenants to ensure facilities will not be frozen.
  • Cash Flow Forecast Scrutiny: Stress-test cash flow forecasts under adverse assumptions (e.g., lower sales volumes, higher interest rates).
  • Written Representations: Obtain written confirmation from management regarding their intent and feasibility of executing these plans.
TYK 7: SRM & Co. / Rare (P) Ltd (Facts Becoming Known AFTER Financial Statements Are Issued)
▼

Question: Audited financial statements of Rare (P) Ltd were issued to shareholders. Two months later, SRM & Co. discovers that a major product batch was defective and subject to an undisclosed regulatory ban and total recall. What are the auditor’s responsibilities under SA 560 Period 3?

✔ Master Solution & Period 3 SA 560 Procedures:
  1. Discussion: Discuss the matter immediately with management and TCWG.
  2. Determine Amendment Need: Determine whether financial statements require amendment.
  3. Inquire Management Steps: Inquire how management intends to ensure that anyone in receipt of previously issued financial statements is informed of the situation.
  4. If Management Amends & Informs: Issue a new auditor's report containing an Emphasis of Matter (EOM) or Other Matter (OM) paragraph drawing attention to the revision note.
  5. If Management Fails to Act: Notify TCWG that the auditor will take steps to prevent reliance on the audit report. If no action is taken, the auditor shall take appropriate legal steps (e.g., public notice, intimation to ROC/MCA).
TYK 8: Udit & Co. / Fun Journey Ltd (Management Refusal to Provide Written Representations)
▼

Question: Fun Journey Ltd refuses to provide written representations regarding fraud assessment, related party disclosures, and pending litigation. What action must Udit & Co. take under SA 580 and SA 705?

✔ Master Solution & SA 580 / SA 705 Rule:

1. Immediate Steps: Udit & Co. shall discuss the refusal with management, re-evaluate management integrity, and evaluate the effect on reliability of representations and other audit evidence.

2. Mandatory Disclaimer of Opinion: Under SA 580, if management refuses to provide written representations on core responsibilities (preparation of financial statements, fraud, and completeness of information), the auditor SHALL DISCLAIM AN OPINION on the financial statements in accordance with SA 705.

TYK 9: CA Deepa / SGP Textiles Ltd (Going Concern Material Uncertainty Undisclosed by Management)
▼

Question: CA Deepa is statutory auditor of SGP Textiles Ltd for FY 2025-26. A massive fire damaged 60% of the weaving capacity. Operations are severely impaired, and material uncertainty exists regarding going concern. Management refuses to disclose this uncertainty in the notes. Advise CA Deepa on reporting implications.

✔ Master Solution & SA 570 Reporting:

1. Rule under SA 570 (Revised): If the going concern basis of accounting is appropriate but a material uncertainty exists and adequate disclosure is NOT made in the financial statements:

  • The auditor shall express a QUALIFIED OPINION or ADVERSE OPINION as appropriate under SA 705.
  • The auditor CANNOT use a "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" (MURGC) section! (MURGC is restricted solely to cases where adequate disclosure exists).

2. Basis for Opinion Section: CA Deepa must clearly describe in the "Basis for Qualified / Adverse Opinion" section that a material uncertainty exists casting significant doubt on the company's ability to continue as a going concern and that the financial statements do not adequately disclose this matter.

TYK 10: Accounts Receivable Subsequent Collections Verification (SA 560 & SA 500)
▼

Question: How does an auditor use subsequent collections testing up to the date of the audit report as audit evidence for valuation and existence of accounts receivable at the balance sheet date?

✔ Master Solution:

Under SA 560 and SA 500, reviewing subsequent cash receipts from debtors between the balance sheet date and the audit report date provides the most persuasive, reliable audit evidence regarding both the existence and valuation of accounts receivable as of the balance sheet date. If a debtor pays post year-end, it confirms the debt existed and was fully recoverable. Conversely, if a major debtor enters insolvency post year-end, it is a Type 1 adjusting event requiring provision.

TYK 11: SAFE Ltd (Nationwide Strike & Flight Grounding in April 2026 - Risk Assessment)
▼

Question: In April 2026, a nationwide strike and suspension of domestic flight operations occurred, severely impacting SAFE Ltd. How should the auditor design subsequent event procedures to evaluate impact on receivables and inventories for the year ending 31.03.2026?

✔ Master Solution:

Under SA 560, the auditor shall take into account the risk assessment in determining procedures:

  • Understand procedures management established to evaluate strike impact on inventory obsolescence and customer credit defaults.
  • Inquire of management and TCWG whether perishable inventories got spoiled during the strike.
  • Read minutes of Board and executive committee meetings held in April/May 2026.
  • Read subsequent interim management accounts.
  • Determine whether the event is an adjusting or non-adjusting event (operational disruption arising in April is generally non-adjusting requiring disclosure, but inventory deterioration or customer collapse existing earlier may require adjustment).
TYK 12: KIN Ltd (Recurring Losses, Negative Cash Flows & Full Disclosure - MURGC Section)
▼

Question: KIN Ltd incurred recurring operating losses and negative cash flows. Management prepared financial statements on a going concern basis and adequately disclosed all principal events, conditions, liquidity plans, and the existence of material uncertainty in Note 32. The auditor is satisfied that the going concern assumption is appropriate and disclosures are adequate. Draft the reporting requirements under SA 570.

✔ Master Solution & Exact MURGC Draft:

1. Audit Opinion: The auditor shall express an UNMODIFIED OPINION.

2. Mandatory Section in Audit Report: The auditor shall include a separate section immediately after the Basis for Opinion section under the specific heading:

Master Matrix

Chapter 6 Standards at a Glance: SA 560 vs SA 570 vs SA 580

Criteria SA 560 (Subsequent Events) SA 570 (Going Concern) SA 580 (Written Representations)
Core Objective Ensure subsequent events are appropriately adjusted or disclosed in financial statements. Obtain SAAE on appropriateness of going concern basis & whether material uncertainty exists. Obtain written confirmation of management responsibilities and support other evidence.
Auditor's Duty Active Period Active duty up to date of auditor's report; responsive duty thereafter. Continuous duty covering at least 12 months from balance sheet date. Mandatory requirement as near as practicable to, but not after, report date.
Evidential Status Substantive review procedures yield direct audit evidence. Evidence evaluated to judge validity of fundamental assumption. Necessary audit evidence, but NOT SAAE on its own.
Reporting Impact if Non-Compliant Modify opinion (Qualified or Adverse under SA 705) if material event undisclosed. Adverse (if inappropriate) OR Qualified/Adverse (if uncertainty undisclosed) OR MURGC (if disclosed). Disclaimer of Opinion if management refuses to provide representation.
