🏛️ 1. Introduction, Code of Ethics Architecture & Public Interest
Public Interest Foundation

The Oxford Dictionary defines ethics as "the moral principles that govern a person's behaviour or how an activity is conducted." Professional Ethics represents the personal, organizational, and corporate standards of behaviour expected of Chartered Accountants.

🌟 The ICAI Motto: "Ya Esha Supteshu Jagrati"

Adopted from the Kathopanishad, the motto of the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India denotes "Eternal Vigilance" — awakening and guarding when the rest of the world is asleep. The public, investors, banks, revenue authorities, and courts repose unquestioned trust in the signature of a Chartered Accountant; hence, independence and uncompromising ethics form the lifeblood of the profession.

Structure of the ICAI Code of Ethics (Based on IESBA Code)

The Revised Code of Ethics is structured into distinct, mutually reinforcing parts:

Code Part Target Professional Domain Core Scope & Regulatory Focus
Part 1 All Chartered Accountants Complying with the Code, Fundamental Principles, and the Conceptual Framework. Applicable universally to members in practice and in service.
Part 2 CAs in Service (PAIB) Professional Accountants in Business / Employment. Applies to members employed in executive, managerial, or non-executive capacities in corporate or non-profit entities.
Part 3 CAs in Public Practice Sets out professional obligations, client acceptance, engagement management, conflicts of interest, fees, and marketing for practicing members.
Part 4A & 4B Independence Standards Part 4A: Independence for Audit and Review Engagements.
Part 4B: Independence for Assurance Engagements Other than Audit and Review Engagements.
Part 5 Sustainability Assurance Standards Sections 5100 to 5600: Dedicated ethics and independence standards for practitioners providing assurance on non-financial sustainability / BRSR information.
Glossary Defined Terms & Definitions Defines specific terms, acronyms, and operational thresholds used throughout the Code.
⚖️ 2. The 5 Fundamental Principles, Threats & Conceptual Safeguards
Conceptual Framework

A Chartered Accountant is bound to comply with the Five Fundamental Principles of Professional Ethics under Section 110 of the Code:

1. Integrity

To be straightforward and honest in all professional and business relationships. Implies fair dealing, truthfulness, and refusing to be associated with materially false or misleading information.

2. Objectivity

Not to compromise professional or business judgment because of bias, conflict of interest, or undue influence of others. Objectivity ensures decisions are based solely on verifiable evidence.

3. Professional Competence & Due Care

Attain and maintain professional knowledge and skill at the level required to ensure that a client or employer receives competent professional service, acting diligently in accordance with technical and professional standards.

4. Confidentiality

Respect the confidentiality of information acquired as a result of professional relationships. Never disclose such information to third parties without proper authority or legal duty, nor use it for personal advantage.

5. Professional Behaviour

Comply with relevant laws and regulations and avoid any conduct that the chartered accountant knows or should know might discredit the profession (e.g., disparaging claims about other CAs).

The 5 Threats to Independence and Compliance

Compliance with fundamental principles can be compromised by a broad range of circumstances creating Threats:

Nature of Threat Conceptual Definition Practical Exam Scenario Examples
1. Self-Interest Threat The threat that a financial or other personal interest will inappropriately influence a CA's judgment or behaviour. Holding shares in an audit client; undue fee dependence on a single client (>15% gross fees); contingent fees; loans or guarantees to/from client.
2. Self-Review Threat The threat that a CA will not appropriately evaluate the results of a previous judgment made or service performed by the CA or their firm. Performing internal audit or accounting/bookkeeping services and subsequently acting as statutory auditor; designing IT systems and auditing them.
3. Advocacy Threat The threat that a CA will promote a client's or employer's position to the point that their objectivity is compromised. Acting as an advocate for an audit client in litigation or tax appeals before ITAT/Courts; promoting client shares in an initial public offering (IPO).
4. Familiarity Threat The threat that due to a long or close relationship with a client or employer, a CA will be too sympathetic to their interests. Long association of key audit partner with audit client (>7 years); immediate family member or close relative being director or CFO of client.
5. Intimidation Threat The threat that a CA will be deterred from acting objectively because of actual or perceived pressures, including attempts to exercise undue influence. Threat of dismissal or replacement over an audit disagreement; pressure to reduce audit fees unreasonably; litigation threatened by client management.
🛡️ The Conceptual Framework Approach: Identify → Evaluate → Address

When threats are identified, the CA must apply professional judgment to evaluate whether they are at an acceptable level. If not at an acceptable level, the CA must address the threats by:

  • Eliminating the circumstances: e.g., selling financial interests, removing a family member from the audit team.
  • Applying safeguards: e.g., having an independent partner review the work, rotating key audit personnel, involving external experts.
  • Declining or terminating: If effective safeguards cannot reduce threats to an acceptable level, the CA must decline or resign from the engagement.
🚨 3. Non-Compliance with Laws and Regulations (NOCLAR) & SA 250
High-Yield Exam Focus

NOCLAR (Non-Compliance with Laws and Regulations) sets out a comprehensive ethical framework guiding Chartered Accountants when they encounter non-compliance or suspected non-compliance with laws and regulations committed by a client or employing organization.

📌 Core Objective: Overcoming "Turning a Blind Eye"

The core objective of NOCLAR is to ensure that professionals do not simply turn a blind eye to illegal acts. Chartered Accountants have an overarching duty to act in the public interest, help prevent commission of illegal acts, rectify non-compliance, and mitigate adverse consequences.

Scope & Applicability of NOCLAR in India

1. Professional Accountants in Public Practice
  • Applicable currently to Audit Engagements of Listed Entities in India.
  • Covers laws and regulations that have a direct effect on financial statements, as well as laws that are fundamental to the entity's operating aspects (e.g., environmental laws, public health and safety, anti-money laundering, bribery, banking laws, data protection).
  • Does not cover personal misconduct of individuals unrelated to business activities.
2. Professional Accountants in Service (PAIB)
  • Applicable to Senior Professional Accountants in Service employed in listed entities (e.g., CFO, Chief Internal Auditor, Financial Controller, Company Secretary).
  • Senior CAs in service have a special fiduciary responsibility given their operational influence and decision-making roles within the corporate hierarchy.

Detailed Comparison: NOCLAR vs. SA 250

Dimension SA 250: Consideration of Laws & Regs NOCLAR (Code of Ethics)
Applicability Applies to statutory audits of all entities (listed and unlisted). Currently applies to audits of listed entities and senior CAs in service of listed entities.
Target Audience Statutory financial statement auditors only. Both practicing auditors and professional accountants in service (management).
Confidentiality Override Confidentiality precludes reporting to outside regulatory authorities unless required by specific statute (e.g., Sec 143(12) of Companies Act). Explicit Protection: Disclosure to an appropriate external authority in exceptional cases to prevent serious harm to public interest is NOT deemed a breach of confidentiality.
Action Framework Focuses on financial statement misstatements and audit report modification. Focuses on rectifying harm, deterring non-compliance, and escalating to Those Charged with Governance (TCWG).

Step-by-Step Response Hierarchy under NOCLAR

  1. Understand the Matter: Obtain understanding of the nature of the act and the circumstances in which it occurred.
  2. Address with Management: Discuss the matter with immediate management and, where appropriate, Those Charged with Governance (TCWG) to prompt them to investigate and rectify.
  3. Assess Management Response: Evaluate whether management's actions are timely and appropriate.
  4. Determine Need for Further Action: Consider whether to disclose to an appropriate public authority, withdraw from the engagement, or report under statutory provisions (e.g., Section 143(12)).
  5. Documentation: Document all discussions, management responses, and professional judgments exercised.
📜 4. CA Act, 1949: Membership, Fellow (Sec 5) & Disabilities (Sec 8)
Statutory Foundation

The Institute of Chartered Accountants of India was established by Parliament under the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949 as a statutory body corporate to regulate the profession of accountancy in India.

Types of Members: Section 5
  • Associate Chartered Accountant (ACA): Every person whose name is entered in the Register of Members is entitled to use the letters "ACA" after their name.
  • Fellow Chartered Accountant (FCA): An Associate who has been in continuous practice in India for at least 5 years, or an associate of at least 5 years who satisfies equivalent commercial experience prescribed by the Council, is entitled to be admitted as a Fellow and use the letters "FCA".
Register of Members: Sections 19 & 20
  • Section 19: Mandates maintenance of a Register containing full name, DOB, domicile, residential/professional address, date of entry, qualifications, and COP status.
  • Section 20 (Removal of Name): The Council may remove the name of a member upon death, voluntary request, non-payment of prescribed annual fees, incurring any disability under Section 8, or by order of the disciplinary mechanism.

Disabilities for Membership: Section 8 (Zero Tolerance Criteria)

Under Section 8 of the Chartered Accountants Act, a person is permanently or temporarily debarred from having their name entered in or borne on the Register of Members under the following specific disabilities:

Clause of Sec 8 Nature of Statutory Disability Legal Effect & Restoration Criteria
Section 8(i) Age Disability Has not attained the age of 21 years at the time of application. Application rejected until age 21 is attained.
Section 8(ii) Mental Unsoundness Adjudged by a competent court to be of unsound mind. Name cannot be entered or remains removed until declared sound by court.
Section 8(iii) & (iiia) Insolvency / Bankruptcy Undischarged insolvent or undischarged bankrupt declared under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC). Debarred completely until discharged.
Section 8(iv) Discharged Insolvent without Certificate Discharged insolvent who has failed to obtain from the court a certificate stating that insolvency was caused by misfortune without any misconduct on their part.
Section 8(v) Criminal Conviction (Moral Turpitude) Convicted by a competent court (in India or abroad) of an offence involving moral turpitude punishable with imprisonment, or an offence committed in professional capacity, unless pardoned or disability removed by Central Government.
Section 8(vi) Disciplinary Removal Removed from membership of the Institute on being held guilty of professional or other misconduct. Debarred until the expiry of the penalty period ordered by BOD or DC.
⚖️ Section 24: Penalty for Falsely Claiming to be a Member

Any person who, not being a member of the Institute, represents that they are a member, or uses the designation Chartered Accountant, shall be punishable on first conviction with fine extending to ₹1,000, and on any subsequent conviction with imprisonment extending to 6 months, or fine extending to ₹5,000, or both.

💼 5. Certificate of Practice (Sec 6), Deemed in Practice (Sec 2(2)) & Sec 25
Practicing Rights

Merely holding ICAI membership does not confer the legal right to practice. Under Section 6(1) of the Act, no member of the Institute shall be entitled to practice unless they have obtained from the Council a Certificate of Practice (COP).

Deemed to be in Practice: Section 2(2)

A member of the Institute is deemed to be in practice when individually or in partnership with others, in consideration of remuneration:

  • (i) Engages himself in the practice of accountancy; or
  • (ii) Offers to perform or performs services involving auditing, verification of financial statements, or book-keeping; or
  • (iii) Renders professional services or assistance in matters of principle or detail relating to accounting procedures or certification of financial data; or
  • (iv) Renders Management Consultancy and Other Services as permitted by the Council.
Companies Prohibited: Section 25

Section 25 strictly prohibits any company, whether incorporated in India or abroad, from practicing as Chartered Accountants.

  • The audit profession requires personal professional accountability and independence which cannot be sheltered behind the veil of corporate limited liability.
  • Any company violating Section 25 is punishable with a fine extending to ₹1,000 on first conviction, and ₹5,000 on subsequent convictions.
  • Note: Limited Liability Partnerships (LLPs) are expressly permitted under Section 2(1)(eb) of the Act.

Permissible Management Consultancy & Other Services (Sec 2(2)(iv))

Under Council resolution pursuant to Section 2(2)(iv), a practicing CA is authorized to provide a wide spectrum of services:

Financial & Management: Capital structure planning, working capital management, project reports, feasibility studies, business valuations.
Systems & Tech: Design of accounting systems, internal control evaluation, ERP system selection, mystery audits, digital transformation.
Corporate & Legal: Corporate restructuring, mergers & acquisitions, debt syndication, representations before tax authorities, insolvency resolution.
🏢 6. Branch Offices (Sec 27), Designations (Sec 7) & Mandatory KYC
Operational Compliance

To ensure effective supervision and prevent absentee practice, Section 27 of the Act sets out mandatory requirements for maintaining multiple offices.

📌 Section 27: Separate Member in Charge for Each Office

Where a chartered accountant in practice or a firm of chartered accountants has more than one office in India, each one of such offices shall be in the separate charge of a member of the Institute. Failure to place a separate member in charge constitutes professional misconduct under the Act.

Statutory Exemptions from Separate In-Charge Requirement

1. Second Office within Same City / 50 km Limit

A member or firm can open a second office without a separate member in charge, provided:

  • The second office is located within the same city / municipal limits; or
  • It is situated within a radius of 50 km from the municipal limits of the existing office.
  • A name board must be displayed, and one member or partner looks after both offices.
2. Temporary Office in Hill Stations

Practicing members and firms are permitted to open a temporary second office in hill stations under Council guidelines, provided:

  • The temporary office remains open for a period not exceeding 3 months in a financial year during summer.
  • No separate member in charge is required during this limited temporary period.
  • Name board displayed only during operating months; ICAI informed in advance.

Section 7: Restriction on Use of Designations

Under Section 7, a member in practice is strictly prohibited from using any designation other than "Chartered Accountant" on professional documents, letterheads, visiting cards, or signboards.

  • Prohibited Designations: "Tax Consultant", "Cost Consultant", "Corporate Lawyer", "Financial Wizard", "Insolvency Advisor", "GST Specialist".
  • Permitted Designations: Degrees of Universities recognized by law (e.g., B.Com, LL.B., Ph.D.), membership titles of the Institute (ACA, FCA), or recognized foreign accounting bodies (e.g., CPA, ACCA).
  • Insolvency Professional: A CA who is registered as an Insolvency Professional (IP) can mention "Insolvency Professional" only on stationery used exclusively for insolvency assignments under IBC, not on general CA firm letterheads.

Mandatory KYC Norms for CA in Practice

The Council has made it mandatory for every practicing CA to comply with Know Your Customer (KYC) Norms for all clients:

Client Category Identity & Address Verification Business & Ownership Verification
Individuals PAN Card, Passport, Aadhaar, Voter ID, Driving Licence. Proof of residential address, source of wealth/funds.
Corporate Entities Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum & Articles (MOA/AOA), Board Resolution. Identification of Ultimate Beneficial Owners (UBO >10%), list of directors, registered office proof.
Trusts & Societies Trust Deed, Registration Certificate, PAN. Names of Settlors, Trustees, Beneficiaries, and authorized signatories.
⚖️ 7. Disciplinary Mechanism: Director (Discipline), BOD vs. DC (Sec 21–22)
Statutory Judicial Hierarchy

The Chartered Accountants Act establishes a multi-tiered, quasi-judicial enforcement machinery to investigate and adjudicate professional and other misconduct committed by members.

1. Disciplinary Directorate

Section 21: Headed by the Director (Discipline).

  • Receives all complaints (along with prescribed fee of ₹2,500) and information against members.
  • Investigates and arrives at a Prima Facie Opinion.
  • Places First Schedule matters before Board of Discipline; places Second Schedule (or combined) matters before Disciplinary Committee.
2. Board of Discipline (BOD)

Section 21A: Deals with First Schedule Misconduct.

  • Comprises Presiding Officer (Council member) + 2 members.
  • Empowered to award penalties:
    1. Reprimand the member;
    2. Remove name from register up to 3 months;
    3. Impose fine up to ₹1,00,000.
3. Disciplinary Committee (DC)

Section 21B: Deals with Second Schedule Misconduct.

  • President/Vice President + 2 Council members + 2 Central Govt nominees.
  • Empowered to award severe penalties:
    1. Reprimand the member;
    2. Remove name from register permanently or for any period;
    3. Impose fine up to ₹5,00,000.

Appeals to the Appellate Authority: Section 22A & 22G

Any member aggrieved by any order of the Board of Discipline or Disciplinary Committee, or the Director (Discipline), may prefer an appeal within 90 days to the Appellate Authority.

  • The Appellate Authority comprises a retired High Court Judge as Chairperson + 2 Central Govt members + 2 senior ICAI Council nominees.
  • The Authority has powers to confirm, modify, or set aside the order, impose, reduce, or enhance the penalty, or remit the matter back for further enquiry.
  • A further appeal lies before the High Court within 90 days on questions of law.
📋 8. First Schedule, Part I: CAs in Practice – Clauses 1 to 6
Practice Misconduct (Part A)

Part I of the First Schedule governs professional misconduct in relation to Chartered Accountants in practice. Misconduct under this Schedule is placed before the Board of Discipline (BOD) under Section 21A.

Clause (1): Allowing Another Person to Practice in His Name

A CA in practice is guilty of professional misconduct if he: "allows any person to practice in his name as a chartered accountant unless such person is also a chartered accountant in practice and is in partnership with or employed by him."

  • Purpose: To safeguard the public against unqualified persons practicing under the cloak of a qualified CA.
  • Permitted: Only a CA in practice who is a Partner or an Employee working under his direct supervision and control.
  • Prohibited: Allowing non-CAs (e.g., relatives, advocates, tax practitioners) or practicing CAs who are neither partners nor employees to practice in the firm's name (refer to Illustration 5).
Clause (2): Sharing / Paying Share, Commission or Brokerage in Fees

A CA in practice is guilty of misconduct if he: "pays or allows or agrees to pay or allow, directly or indirectly, any share, commission or brokerage in the fees or profits of his professional business, to any person other than a member of the Institute or a partner or a retired partner or the legal representative of a deceased partner, or a member of any other professional body or with such other persons having such qualification as may be prescribed..."

  • General Rule: Sharing professional fees with non-members is strictly forbidden to prevent commercialization and toutism.
  • Statutory Exceptions (Permissible Sharing):
    1. Another member of the Institute (individual CA or CA firm);
    2. A partner or a retired partner;
    3. The legal representative of a deceased partner (goodwill payment / share of profits);
    4. Regulation 53A – Members of Other Professional Bodies:
      • Company Secretary (ICSI);
      • Cost Accountant (ICMAI);
      • Advocate (Bar Council of India);
      • Architect (Indian Institute of Architects);
      • Actuary (Institute of Actuaries of India);
      • Recognized foreign professional accounting bodies under Section 29(2).
    5. Regulation 53B – Prescribed Qualifications in India: CS, CMA, Actuary, Bachelor in Engineering (B.E.), Bachelor in Technology (B.Tech), Bachelor in Architecture (B.Arch), Bachelor in Law (LL.B.), Master in Business Administration (MBA from recognized University).
  • Sale of Goodwill to Widow / Legal Heir: A practicing CA or firm can pay a share of profits or purchase price of goodwill to the widow or legal heirs of a deceased sole proprietor or partner, subject to obtaining prior approval of the Council within one year of death.
Clause (3): Accepting Share of Profits from Non-Members

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "accepts or agrees to accept any part of the profits of the professional work of a person who is not a member of the Institute."

  • While Clause 2 deals with *paying* or sharing firm fees, Clause 3 deals with *receiving* or accepting a cut, kickback, or referral fee from the professional earnings of non-CAs (e.g., accepting referral commission from an advocate, merchant banker, or valuer).
  • Exception: Accepting profits from members of professional bodies prescribed under Regulation 53A / 53B is permitted.
Clause (4): Entering into Partnership with Non-Members

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "enters into partnership, in or outside India, with any person other than a Chartered Accountant in practice or such other person who is a member of any other professional body having such qualifications as may be prescribed..."

  • Permissible Partnerships:
    1. Another Chartered Accountant in practice;
    2. Persons possessing qualifications recognized under Regulation 53B (CS, CMA, Actuary, B.E., B.Tech, B.Arch, LL.B., MBA) forming Multi-Disciplinary Partnerships (MDPs);
    3. Members of recognized foreign accounting bodies entitled to registration under Section 4(1)(v).
  • Partnership with an ordinary non-professional or unqualified person is strictly illegal.
Clause (5): Securing Professional Business through Agents / Non-Open Means

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "secures either through the services of a person who is not an employee of such Chartered Accountant or who is not his partner or by means which are not open to a Chartered Accountant, any professional business."

  • Prohibits paying kickbacks, employing touts, agents, or canvassers to divert clients or secure audits.
  • Agreements permitted under Clauses 2, 3, and 4 (such as fee sharing with CS/CMA under Reg 53A) are expressly saved from this prohibition.
Clause (6): Soliciting Clients or Professional Work

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "solicits clients or professional work either directly or indirectly by circular, advertisement, personal communication or interview or by any other means."

  • The General Ban: Soliciting professional work degrades the dignity of the profession and impairs auditor independence.
  • Statutory Provisos (Permitted Actions):
    1. Proviso (i): Applying for or requesting professional work from another Chartered Accountant in practice (peer-to-peer work sharing).
    2. Proviso (ii): Responding to tenders or enquiries issued by users of professional services.
  • Council Guidelines on Tenders (Crucial Exam Rule):
    • A member in practice shall NOT respond to any tender in areas of services exclusively reserved for Chartered Accountants (e.g., Statutory Audit under Companies Act, Tax Audit u/s 44AB) UNLESS a minimum fee is explicitly prescribed in the tender document itself.
    • Open Areas Exception: Where the scope of service is open to other professionals alongside CAs (e.g., Internal Audit, Concurrent Audit, Consulting, GST advisory), the member is free to respond even if no minimum fee is prescribed!
  • Empanelment Rules: A CA can apply for empanelment only if the organization has maintained a panel or publicly invited applications; sending roving circulars to banks/corporates without invitation violates Clause 6.
  • Books, Articles & Media: Authorship of books or articles is permitted. While publishing, the author may state his name and professional designation ("CA"), but the name of his firm or contact telephone numbers should not be publicized in a manner that solicits work.
📢 9. First Schedule, Part I: Clauses 7 to 12
Practice Misconduct (Part B)
Clause (7): Advertising Professional Attainments or Services

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "advertises his professional attainments or services, or uses any designation or expressions other than Chartered Accountant on professional documents, visiting cards, letter heads or sign boards..."

  • Permissible Designations: Degrees of recognized Universities (B.Com, LL.B., Ph.D.) and recognized Institute titles (ACA, FCA). Expressions such as "Tax Specialist", "Forensic Expert", "GST Guru", or "Management Advisor" are strictly illegal.
  • Write-Up Guidelines:
    • A member or firm may advertise through a write-up setting out services provided and firm particulars.
    • Must be factual, truthful, and dignified; no font larger than normal text.
    • No superlative claims ("leading firm", "best tax auditor"), no fee quotes, and no client lists unless requested by the prospective client.
  • Website Guidelines (Pull vs. Push):
    • Website must run strictly on a Pull basis (information displayed for visitors seeking it, no push popups or unsolicited emails).
    • Domain names must conform to professional dignity (e.g., www.abcandco.com); sensational or superlative domain names (e.g., www.bestca.in, www.cheapesttaxaudit.com) are strictly prohibited.
    • Passport-style photographs of partners are permitted.
    • Names of clients and fees charged cannot be displayed on the public website; they can only be shared upon specific request.
  • Visiting Cards & Letterheads: QR codes on visiting cards are permitted provided they only store basic contact details. Printing vision/mission statements behind visiting cards is prohibited as it amounts to solicitation (ESB Decision).
Clause (8): Communication with Previous Auditor (RPAD Mandatory)

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "accepts a position as auditor previously held by another chartered accountant or a certified auditor... without first communicating with him in writing."

  • Fundamental Professional Courtesy & Duty: Ensures the incoming auditor learns whether there are professional reasons why the appointment should not be accepted (e.g., management interference, scope limitations, fraud reporting under Section 143(12)).
  • Mandatory Mode of Communication:
    • Must be sent via Registered Post with Acknowledgment Due (RPAD) or by hand delivery against written written acknowledgment.
    • Certificate of Posting is NOT valid positive proof: Sending communication under mere Certificate of Posting (ordinary post) does not constitute compliance and the member will be held guilty (Illustration 16).
    • Communication by secure digital means (email) is valid only where positive acknowledgment/delivery proof is verifiable.
  • Timing of Communication: Communication must be initiated PRIOR to accepting the appointment, and the incoming auditor must wait a reasonable time for a reply before taking up the audit.
  • Undisputed Audit Fees (Chapter IX Guidelines): A member shall not accept statutory audit of an entity if the undisputed audit fees of the previous auditor remain unpaid (except in cases of sick industrial units or liquidation).
Clause (9): Compliance with Statutory Appointment Provisions

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "accepts an appointment as auditor of a company without first ascertaining from it whether the requirements of sections 139 to 141 of the Companies Act, 2013... have been duly complied with."

  • The incoming auditor must obtain written confirmation/certificate from the company that all statutory formalities were completed.
  • Special Notice under Section 140(4): Verify whether special notice was required and duly served when replacing a retiring auditor.
  • Removal before Term under Section 140(1): If the previous auditor was removed before expiry of their term, the incoming auditor must verify that prior approval of the Central Government (Form ADT-2) was obtained. Acceptance without Central Government approval is a severe violation!
Clause (10): Restriction on Contingent Fees & Percentage of Profits

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "charges or offers to charge, accepts or offers to accept in respect of any professional employment fees which are based on a percentage of profits or which are contingent upon the findings, or results of such employment..."

  • Contingent fees destroy objectivity and create massive self-interest threats.
  • Exemptions under Regulation 192 (Permissible Percentage / Contingent Fees):
    1. Receiver or Liquidator: Fees based on percentage of realization or disbursement of assets.
    2. Auditor of Co-operative Society: Fees based on percentage of paid-up capital, working capital, gross/net income or profits.
    3. Valuer for Direct Taxes and Duties: Fees based on percentage of property value.
    4. Fund Raising Services: Fees based on percentage of funds raised.
    5. Debt Recovery Services: Fees based on percentage of debt recovered.
    6. Cost Optimization Services: Fees based on percentage of benefit derived.
    7. Insolvency Professional (IBC 2016): Fees based on percentage of recovery/assets.
    8. Non-Assurance Services to Non-Audit Clients: Fees may be charged on a contingent basis!
Clause (11): Engaging in Other Business or Occupation (Regulation 190A)

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "engages in any business or occupation other than the profession of chartered accountant unless permitted by the Council so to engage."

  • Proviso – Director Simplicitor: A CA in practice can be an ordinary Director (Director Simplicitor) in a company without prior permission, provided:
    • He is not a Managing Director or Whole-time Director; and
    • Neither he nor any of his partners is interested in such company as statutory auditor.
  • General Permission (No Prior Application Required):
    1. Employment under another CA in practice or CA firm;
    2. Private tutoring or teaching in university/college up to 25 hours per month;
    3. Authorship of books and articles;
    4. Acting as an Arbitrator, Mediator, Surveyor, Loss Assessor, or Valuer;
    5. Holding life insurance agency for renewal commission on policies taken prior to entering practice;
    6. Attending to own family business without active management.
  • Specific Permission (Prior Council Approval Mandatory):
    1. Full-time or part-time employment in business concerns (e.g., Managing Director / Executive Chairman);
    2. Active partnership in a commercial business firm;
    3. Editorship of journals other than professional journals.
Clause (12): Signing by Unqualified Persons / Non-Partners

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "allows a person not being a member of the institute in practice or a member not being his partner to sign on his behalf or on behalf of his firm, any balance sheet, profit and loss account, report or financial statements."

  • Only a Partner who is a Chartered Accountant in practice can sign financial statements or audit reports on behalf of the firm.
  • Giving a Power of Attorney (POA) to a paid employee CA to sign audit reports is strictly illegal (Illustration 23).
👥 10. First Schedule: Parts II, III & IV (Service & Other Misconduct)
First Schedule Completion
Part II: Professional Misconduct – CAs in Service

A member in employment (other than in practice) is guilty of misconduct if he:

  • Clause (1): Pays or allows or agrees to pay directly or indirectly to any person any share in the emoluments of the employment undertaken by him (kickbacks on salary).
  • Clause (2): Accepts or agrees to accept any part of fees, profits, or gains from a lawyer, CA, broker, agent, or customer engaged by his employer by way of commission or gratification.
Part III: Professional Misconduct – Members Generally

A member, whether in practice or not, is guilty of misconduct if he:

  • Clause (1): Not being a fellow of the Institute, acts as a fellow of the Institute (falsely using "FCA").
  • Clause (2): Does not supply the information called for, or does not comply with the requirements asked for, by the Institute, Council, Director (Discipline), BOD, Disciplinary Committee, QRB, or Appellate Authority.
  • Clause (3): Gives false information knowing it to be false while inviting professional work, responding to tenders, advertising through a write-up, or empanelment.
Part IV: Other Misconduct – Members Generally (First Schedule)

A member of the Institute, whether in practice or not, is deemed guilty of Other Misconduct under the First Schedule if he:

  1. Clause (1): Is held guilty by any civil or criminal court for an offence which is punishable with imprisonment for a term NOT exceeding 6 months.
  2. Clause (2): In the opinion of the Council, brings disrepute to the profession or the Institute as a result of his action, whether or not related to his professional work (e.g., retaining custody of trust funds, issuing bounced cheques, misbehaviour at professional conferences).
⚖️ 11. Second Schedule, Part I: CAs in Practice – Clauses 1 to 10
Severe Practice Misconduct

Part I of the Second Schedule deals with grave professional misconduct in relation to Chartered Accountants in practice. Misconduct under this Schedule falls under the exclusive jurisdiction of the Disciplinary Committee (DC) under Section 21B, empowering it to remove a member's name permanently or for any period and levy fines up to ₹5,00,000.

Clause (1): Breach of Client Confidentiality

A CA in practice is guilty of misconduct if he: "discloses information acquired in the course of his professional engagement to any person other than his client so engaging him, without the consent of his client or otherwise than as required by any law for the time being in force."

  • Fundamental Duty: Absolute confidentiality must be preserved even after the professional relationship ends.
  • Permissible Disclosures:
    1. With the express consent of the client;
    2. When required by law: e.g., summons issued under Section 131 of Income Tax Act, orders of Court, statutory fraud reporting under Section 143(12) of Companies Act, 2013;
    3. Under NOCLAR to prevent imminent and serious harm to public interest;
    4. In response to peer review or quality review by ICAI / QRB / NFRA.
  • Example: An auditor cannot tip off third parties or share prospective merger plans of a client with friends or relatives for insider trading (refer to Illustration 29 & 30).
Clause (2): Certifying Reports without Examination

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "certifies or submits in his name or in the name of his firm, a report of an examination of financial statements unless the examination of such statements and the related records has been made by him or by a partner or an employee in his firm or by another chartered accountant in practice."

  • Prohibits "rubber-stamping" audit reports prepared by unqualified third parties.
  • Permitted reliance: Reliance placed on work performed by partners, qualified employee CAs, branch auditors, or joint auditors in accordance with SA 600 (Using the Work of Another Auditor).
Clause (3): Vouching for the Accuracy of Forecasts (SAE 3400)

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "permits his name or the name of his firm to be used in connection with an estimate of earnings contingent upon future transactions in a manner which may lead to the belief that he vouches for the accuracy of the forecast."

  • Future events are inherently uncertain; an auditor can NEVER guarantee, predict, or vouch for the accuracy of future profits or cash flows.
  • SAE 3400 Examination Permitted: A CA can examine and report on Prospective Financial Information (PFI) under SAE 3400, provided:
    1. He clearly states the sources of information, underlying assumptions, and scope of examination;
    2. He issues negative assurance stating that nothing has come to his attention to suggest assumptions are unreasonable;
    3. He explicitly disclaims vouching for the accuracy of future outcomes.
  • Circulation / Capacity Certificates: Certifying past newspaper circulation figures based on actual past sales books does NOT violate Clause 3 because it relates to historical facts, not future forecasts (Illustration 32).
Clause (4): Expressing Opinion when having Substantial Interest

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "expresses his opinion on financial statements of any business or enterprise in which he, his firm, or a partner in his firm has a substantial interest."

  • Definition of Substantial Interest: Beneficial holding (alone or with relatives) of 20% or more voting power in a company, or entitlement to 20% or more profits in a non-corporate enterprise (under Appendix 9 to CA Regulations).
  • Companies Act Threshold: Section 141(3)(d) disqualifies an auditor if he or his relative holds ANY security or financial interest in the company (with a grace limit of ₹1,00,000 face value held by relatives).
  • Director Simplicitor: A CA who is a director in a company (or whose partner is a director) cannot act as its statutory auditor. Furthermore, after resigning as director, a cooling-off period of 2 years is recommended.
Clause (5): Failure to Disclose a Known Material Fact

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "fails to disclose a material fact known to him which is not disclosed in a financial statement, but disclosure of which is necessary in making such financial statement not misleading where he is concerned with that financial statement in a professional capacity."

  • Applies when the auditor *knows* a material fact that is missing from the accounts (e.g., major post-balance sheet fire destroying un-insured factory, significant contingent liability, default on debt covenants) and fails to report or modify his audit opinion.
Clause (6): Failure to Report a Known Material Misstatement

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "fails to report a material misstatement known to him to appear in a financial statement with which he is concerned in a professional capacity."

  • While Clause 5 deals with *omissions* of material facts, Clause 6 deals with *active falsehoods or misstatements* present in the financial statements (e.g., fictitious sales, under-provision of depreciation, inflated inventory values) which the auditor knows but conceals.
Clause (7): Gross Negligence & Failure to Exercise Due Diligence

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "does not exercise due diligence, or is grossly negligent in the conduct of his professional duties."

  • Core Test of Audit Liability: Gross negligence is a reckless disregard of professional duty and failure to perform standard audit procedures prescribed by ICAI Standards on Auditing (SAs).
  • Examples of Gross Negligence:
    • Failure to attend physical inventory verification without performing alternative audit procedures under SA 501.
    • Failure to obtain external confirmations of bank balances, debtors, and creditors under SA 505.
    • Failing to identify that 100% of sales were made to an undisclosed related party under SA 550 (NFRA Case Precedent).
Clause (8): Failure to Obtain Sufficient Information

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "fails to obtain sufficient information which is necessary for expression of an opinion or its exceptions are sufficiently material to negate the expression of an opinion."

  • Under SA 500, the auditor must obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to draw reasonable conclusions on which to base his opinion.
  • If management imposes severe scope limitations denying basic records or third-party contact details, the auditor cannot issue a clean unqualified report — he must issue a Disclaimer of Opinion or Qualified Opinion under SA 705.
Clause (9): Material Departure from Generally Accepted Audit Procedures

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "fails to invite attention to any material departure from the generally accepted procedure of audit applicable to the circumstances."

  • Mandates strict adherence to ICAI Standards on Auditing (SAs), Guidance Notes, and Accounting Standards.
  • If an audit procedure is omitted without documented justification and alternative testing, the auditor is guilty under Clause 9.
Clause (10): Client Moneys in Separate Bank Account

A CA in practice is guilty if he: "fails to keep moneys of his client other than fees or remuneration or money meant to be expended in a separate banking account or to use such moneys for purposes for which they are intended within a reasonable time."

  • Money received from a client for specific expenses (e.g., advance for payment of advance tax, court fees, registration duties) must be deposited in a separate Client Bank Account.
  • It cannot be mingled with the CA's personal or firm operating bank accounts.
  • Must be utilized promptly for the intended purpose or refunded without delay.

🏛️ 14. Recommended Self-Regulatory Measures & NFRA Disciplinary Case Study
Quality Control & Precedents

To foster internal audit quality and professional excellence, the Council has recommended non-statutory Self-Regulatory Measures:

1. Branch & Joint Audits

Branch audits should preferably be conducted by local practicing CAs. Large public audits should be undertaken jointly by smaller and larger firms to foster professional capacity.

2. Staff Ratio

Maintain a healthy ratio between qualified chartered accountant staff and unqualified audit assistants (recommended benchmark: 1 qualified CA for every 3 unqualified staff).

3. Digital / Banking Audit Fees

All professional audit fees should be collected strictly through digital banking modes or account payee cheques, avoiding cash fee collections.

🚨 Master Case Study: NFRA Disciplinary Order on Gross Negligence (SA 505 Precedent)

Facts of the Case: The statutory Engagement Partner (EP) of a listed company was investigated by the National Financial Reporting Authority (NFRA) for audit failure in FY 2020–21. The EP faced three major charges:

  1. Failure to Attend Physical Inventory Count (SA 501): The EP was appointed on 18.04.2021 and claimed it was impracticable to attend the count as on 31.03.2021. However, the audit file contained zero documentation of alternative audit procedures performed to verify existence and condition of inventory.
  2. Failure to Identify Undisclosed Related Party Transactions (SA 550): The company made almost 100% of its sales to a single party, which was an undisclosed related party previously disclosed in FY 2017–18. The EP relied blindly on management representations without applying professional skepticism.
  3. Failure to Obtain External Confirmations (SA 505): The EP failed to obtain direct balance confirmations from trade debtors and creditors. Management had denied customer contact details claiming fear of losing business secrets. The EP accepted this limitation without evaluating fraud risks or modifying the audit opinion under SA 705.

NFRA Findings & Statutory Holding:

  • Rejection of Scope Limitation Excuse: Management's denial of contact details constituted a clear management-imposed scope limitation. The EP was duty-bound to evaluate fraud risks and consider modifying his opinion under SA 705. Relying on management explanations without independent corroboration violates SA 200, 500, and 505.
  • PCAOB Precedent (Satyam Case): NFRA cited the landmark US PCAOB decision in the Satyam audit failure, where total reliance on management assertions without direct external bank confirmations was held to be gross negligence.
  • Final Disciplinary Order: The Engagement Partner was held guilty of gross negligence and lack of due diligence under Clauses (7), (8), and (9) of Part I of the Second Schedule to the CA Act, 1949. NFRA imposed a heavy monetary penalty and debarred the CA for a period of 2 years from undertaking statutory or internal audits.

⚖️ 16. Legal Liabilities of Auditors: Civil & Criminal Liabilities
Statutory & Common Law Liability

An auditor carries heavy professional accountability. Breach of professional duty exposes the auditor to dual tracks of liability: Civil Liability (damages) and Criminal Liability (imprisonment and fines).

1. Civil Liability for Negligence
  • Breach of Contract: The auditor is liable in damages to the audited company for financial loss caused by failure to exercise reasonable care and professional competence.
  • Liability to Third Parties (Tort of Negligence):
    • Hedley Byrne & Co. Ltd. v. Heller & Partners Ltd.: Established that an auditor can be liable to third parties where a special relationship exists and the auditor knew his statement would be relied upon.
    • Caparo Industries plc v. Dickman (Landmark Precedent): The House of Lords laid down the famous Three-Fold Test:
      1. Foreseeability of damage;
      2. Proximity of relationship; and
      3. Fair, just, and reasonable to impose liability.
      Held: The auditor of a company owes a duty of care to the shareholders as a body, NOT to individual shareholders buying additional shares or prospective takeover bidders!
2. Criminal Liability under Statutes
  • Companies Act, 2013:
    • Section 147: Contravention of Sections 139, 143, 144, 145 — fine from ₹25,000 to ₹5,00,000 (or 4x audit fee). If done knowingly or willfully to deceive: Imprisonment up to 1 year and fine up to ₹25,00,000 (or 8x fee) + refund remuneration and pay damages.
    • Section 447 (Punishment for Fraud): Imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years + fine up to 3 times the amount involved in fraud. If fraud involves public interest, minimum imprisonment is 3 years!
    • Section 448: False statements or deliberate omissions in any return, report, or financial statement.
  • Income Tax Act, 1961 (Section 278): Abetment or inducing another person to deliver false tax returns — rigorous imprisonment from 6 months to 7 years with fine.
  • Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) / IPC: Forgery, falsification of accounts, and criminal breach of trust.

Ill 1: Practicing CA suspended for 6 months – Surrender of COP & Register removal ▼

Facts: A Chartered Accountant in practice was suspended from practice for a period of 6 months. Can he continue to practice or hold COP during this period?

Provisions & Analysis: Under Section 6 and Section 20 of the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949 read with Section 8(vi), when a member is suspended from practice or removed from the Register for any period, his Certificate of Practice (COP) stands cancelled for that duration. He cannot practice accountancy, sign audit reports, or represent himself as a practicing CA.

Conclusion: He must immediately surrender his COP to the Council and cease all professional practice during the 6-month suspension period.

Ill 2: CA selecting and recruiting personnel, conducting training – Is it permitted? ▼

Facts: Mr. A, a practicing CA, agreed to select and recruit personnel, conduct training programs, and carry out executive search for a corporate client. Is this permissible?

Provisions & Analysis: Under Section 2(2)(iv) of the Act read with the Council resolution on Management Consultancy and Other Services, recruitment of personnel, human resource training, and staff selection are recognized management consultancy services permitted to practicing CAs.

Conclusion: Not Guilty. Mr. A is fully permitted to render personnel recruitment and training services.

Ill 3: Two partners in-charge of HO and Branch – Both staying at Head Office ▼

Facts: Mr. X and Mr. Y are partners in a CA firm. Mr. X was in charge of the Head Office and Mr. Y was in charge of the Branch office. However, Mr. Y actually resided and practiced at the Head Office city, leaving the Branch office in charge of an audit assistant. Advise.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Section 27 of the Act, each office of a CA firm must be in the separate charge of a member of the Institute. Merely designating a partner on paper while he actually resides elsewhere without actively managing the branch violates Section 27.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct for failure to maintain a member in separate active charge of the branch office under Section 27.

Ill 4: Sole proprietor opening second office in suburbs of same city without separate in-charge ▼

Facts: Mr. K, a practicing sole proprietor, has an office in the suburbs of Chennai and opened a second office within Chennai city limits without appointing a separate member in charge.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Council Guidelines granting exemption from Section 27, a member or firm is permitted to open a second office without a separate member in charge, provided the second office is located within the same city or within 50 km from the municipal limits of the existing office.

Conclusion: Not Guilty. The second office falls within the municipal limits of the same city.

Ill 5: CA allowing non-CA brother-in-law & CA employee to practice in firm name ▼

Facts: CA C allowed his brother-in-law Mr. P (a non-CA) to practice in his name, and also allowed CA T (a qualified paid employee in his firm) to practice in his name. Evaluate.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clause (1) of Part I of the First Schedule, a CA in practice is guilty if he allows any person to practice in his name unless such person is a CA in practice and is in partnership with or employed by him.

  • Allowing Mr. P (non-CA brother-in-law): Strictly prohibited → Guilty of Misconduct.
  • Allowing CA T (paid employee CA): Expressly permitted under the exception in Clause 1 → Not Guilty.
Ill 6: Deceased CA – Widow proposes to sell goodwill / practice to another firm ▼

Facts: Mr. Qureshi, a practicing CA, died in a road accident. His widow proposes to sell the goodwill and practice of the firm to another CA firm for a lump sum consideration.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Council guidelines framed under Clause (2) of Part I of the First Schedule, the legal representative or widow of a deceased sole proprietor is permitted to sell the goodwill of the firm, provided the sale is completed and permission is obtained from the Council within one year from the death of the member.

Conclusion: Permissible, subject to Council approval within the 1-year timeline.

Ill 7: CA publishing book and stating personal details & firm name ▼

Facts: Mr. S, a practicing CA, published a commentary on GST. On the cover, he stated his name, his designation "Chartered Accountant", his firm's name, and contact details of his office.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clauses (6) & (7) of Part I of the First Schedule, a CA publishing a book is permitted to indicate his name and designation ("Chartered Accountant" / "FCA"). However, mentioning the name of his firm, office address, or contact numbers amounts to indirect solicitation and advertising of his practice.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Clauses (6) and (7) for mentioning firm name and contact details.

Ill 8: CA Firm website with client names, fees charged, and push popups ▼

Facts: M/s XYZ created a website www.xyzindia.com displaying client names, fees charged, and flashing banner advertisements.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Council Website Guidelines pursuant to Clause (7) of Part I of the First Schedule:

  • Websites must operate on a Pull basis; push banners are strictly prohibited.
  • Names of clients and fees charged cannot be displayed publicly.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Clause (7).

Ill 9: Responding to tender for computerization – No minimum fee prescribed ▼

Facts: M/s LMN, a CA firm, responded to a tender issued by a State Government for computerization of accounts where no minimum fee was prescribed.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Council Tender Guidelines (Clause 6, Part I, First Schedule), the prohibition against responding to tenders without minimum fees applies ONLY to areas exclusively reserved for CAs (statutory audit, tax audit). Computerization of accounts is an open consultancy area accessible to IT vendors and non-CAs.

Conclusion: Not Guilty. Responding to tenders in open, non-exclusive areas without minimum fees is permitted.

Ill 10: CA writing letters to another CA firm requesting professional work / partnerships ▼

Facts: Mr. Honest, a CA in practice, wrote letters to another practicing CA firm requesting professional work on a subcontract basis or partnership.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Proviso (i) to Clause (6) of Part I of the First Schedule, an explicit exemption is provided allowing any Chartered Accountant to apply for, request, or invite professional work from another Chartered Accountant in practice.

Conclusion: Not Guilty. Peer-to-peer work requests are statutorily protected.

Ill 11: Visiting card mentioning "Insolvency Professional" alongside CA ▼

Facts: A practicing CA used a visiting card in which he designated himself as "Chartered Accountant" and "Insolvency Professional".

Provisions & Analysis: Under Section 7 and Clause (7) of Part I of the First Schedule, a practicing CA cannot use any designation other than Chartered Accountant on general professional documents. The designation "Insolvency Professional" can be used only on stationery specifically dedicated to insolvency assignments under IBC, not on general visiting cards.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Section 7 and Clause (7).

Ill 12: CA partner in 3 firms – Printing personal letterhead with all 3 firm names ▼

Facts: CA B is a partner in three distinct CA firms. While printing his personal letterhead, he mentions his name and the names of all three firms as a partner.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Council guidelines, a member who is a partner in multiple firms is permitted to mention the names of all the firms in which he is a partner on his letterhead/stationery, provided it is factual and dignified without promotional claims.

Conclusion: Not Guilty.

Ill 13: IPO Offer document containing description of auditor firm's expertise ▼

Facts: In the draft prospectus of a listed company, the auditor Mr. D's firm was described as a "leading international tax firm with premier cross-border expertise".

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clause (7) of Part I of the First Schedule, an auditor must ensure that offer documents disclose only the name, address, and firm registration number. Allowing the company to publish laudatory or promotional write-ups about the firm's expertise violates Clause (7).

Conclusion: Guilty of Misconduct unless the auditor promptly objected and requested the company to remove the promotional description.

Ill 14: Insolvency Professional using separate letterhead for IBC matters ▼

Facts: A practicing CA empanelled as an Insolvency Professional (IP) maintained a separate letterhead mentioning "Insolvency Professional" used solely for IBC corporate insolvency matters.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Council Guidelines, using the title "Insolvency Professional" or "Registered Valuer" on separate stationery dedicated exclusively to assignments under those statutes is permissible.

Conclusion: Not Guilty.

Ill 15: Tax audit u/s 44AB accepted without communicating with previous tax auditor ▼

Facts: Mr. X accepted appointment as tax auditor of a firm under Section 44AB without communicating with the previous tax auditor, arguing that Clause 8 applies only to statutory company audits.

Provisions & Analysis: The Council has clarified that Clause (8) of Part I of the First Schedule applies to ALL recurring audits previously held by another CA, including Tax Audits u/s 44AB, Internal Audits, and Concurrent Audits.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Clause (8).

Ill 16: Communication sent under Certificate of Posting (ordinary post) ▼

Facts: CA W sent a letter to the previous auditor under Certificate of Posting (ordinary postal certificate). The previous auditor denied receiving it.

Provisions & Analysis: A mere "Certificate of Posting" does not provide positive proof of delivery. Council guidelines strictly require communication by Registered Post with Acknowledgment Due (RPAD), hand delivery against written acknowledgment, or secure digital delivery with read receipt.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Clause (8).

Ill 17: Communication sent after accepting audit appointment ▼

Facts: CA Raja received an appointment letter on 10th July, accepted it on 12th July, and posted an RPAD letter to the previous auditor on 15th July.

Provisions & Analysis: Clause (8) explicitly requires the incoming auditor to communicate FIRST in writing BEFORE accepting the appointment, allowing reasonable time for a response. Sending communication after acceptance defeats the statutory intent.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Clause (8).

Ill 18: Accepting company audit without verifying compliance with Section 139 & 140 ▼

Facts: Mr. X accepted appointment as statutory auditor of a company where the previous auditor was removed before term, without verifying whether Central Government approval u/s 140(1) was obtained.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clause (9) of Part I of the First Schedule, an incoming auditor must ascertain from the company whether the requirements of Sections 139 and 140 have been complied with. Removal before expiry of term requires mandatory Central Government approval (Form ADT-2).

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Clause (9).

Ill 19: Liquidator charging fees on percentage of realization of assets ▼

Facts: Mr. P, acting as liquidator of a company, charged his fees based on a percentage of total assets realized.

Provisions & Analysis: While Clause (10) of Part I of the First Schedule generally prohibits fees based on a percentage of profits or contingent outcomes, Regulation 192(a) expressly exempts a receiver or liquidator charging fees on a percentage of realization or disbursement of assets.

Conclusion: Not Guilty. Covered under Regulation 192 statutory exemption.

Ill 20: Practicing CA with 4 articles running full-time manufacturing business ▼

Facts: A practicing CA having four articled assistants engaged himself as a full-time managing director in a family manufacturing company without prior Council approval.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clause (11) of Part I of the First Schedule and Regulation 190A, a member in practice cannot engage in any other business or occupation without prior Council permission. Acting as Managing Director requires Specific Permission, which cannot be granted if it impairs training of articled assistants.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Clause (11).

Conclusion: Not Guilty.

Ill 23: Power of Attorney given to employee CA to sign audit reports ▼

Facts: CA S gave a Power of Attorney (POA) to a qualified employee CA in his firm authorizing him to sign balance sheets and audit reports on behalf of the firm.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clause (12) of Part I of the First Schedule, an audit report can be signed only by a member of the Institute who is a Partner in the firm. An employee CA, even though qualified, cannot be delegated signing authority through a Power of Attorney.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Clause (12).

Ill 24: Partner signing audit reports while sole practicing partner is abroad ▼

Facts: CA Smart, a sole practicing partner, was on a European tour. During his absence, his qualified partner in the firm signed statutory audit reports.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clause 12, any practicing partner of the firm is legally authorized to sign financial statements and reports on behalf of the firm.

Conclusion: Not Guilty. A partner can sign on behalf of the firm.

Ill 25: CA in full-time employment holding COP & recovering fees ▼

Facts: Mr. C holds a COP while in full-time employment with a private company, and also charges fees for tax advice rendered on weekends.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Section 6 and Part II of First Schedule, holding a COP while in full-time commercial employment without specific Council permission is impermissible.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct.

Ill 26: Member failing to supply information requested by Disciplinary Directorate ▼

Facts: Mr. G failed to reply to multiple notices and did not provide information called for by the Director (Discipline).

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clause (2) of Part III of the First Schedule, failure to supply information or comply with requirements of the Institute, Council, or Disciplinary bodies constitutes misconduct.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Part III, Clause (2).

Ill 27: Paid assistant giving false information in firm empanelment profile ▼

Facts: Mr. X, employed as a paid assistant with a CA firm, submitted inflated experience details knowing them to be false in a tender response.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clause (3) of Part III of the First Schedule, giving false information knowing it to be false while responding to tenders or write-ups constitutes misconduct for any member generally.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Part III, Clause (3).

Ill 28: Proprietary CA firm retaining bank audit records and issuing bounced cheques ▼

Facts: YKS & Co. refused to hand over client papers and issued dishonoured cheques to creditors, bringing disrepute to the profession.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clause (2) of Part IV of the First Schedule, bringing disrepute to the profession or Institute, whether related to professional work or personal conduct, is Other Misconduct.

Conclusion: Guilty of Other Misconduct under First Schedule, Part IV, Clause (2).

Ill 29: CA disclosing confidential costing data of client in conference paper ▼

Facts: Mr. P presented a paper at a trade chamber seminar and disclosed specific costing structures and profit margins of his audit client without consent.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clause (1) of Part I of the Second Schedule, disclosing confidential client information without consent or legal obligation is grave misconduct.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Second Schedule, Part I, Clause (1).

Ill 30: Auditor reporting corporate fraud to Central Govt u/s 143(12) – Breach of secrecy? ▼

Facts: CA J reported a ₹5 crore procurement fraud committed by KMP to the Central Government under Section 143(12). Management alleged breach of confidentiality.

Provisions & Analysis: Clause (1) of Part I of Second Schedule expressly permits disclosure when required by any law for the time being in force. Section 143(12) overrides confidentiality.

Conclusion: Not Guilty. Statutory reporting is fully protected.

Ill 31: Representing tax client and certifying financials prepared by client accountant ▼

Facts: A practicing CA certified balance sheets prepared by the client's internal accountant without independent audit examination.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clause (2) of Part I of the Second Schedule, certifying financial statements without examination by the CA, his partner, or employee is prohibited.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Second Schedule, Part I, Clause (2).

Ill 32: Certifying historical circulation figures of daily newspaper based on past records ▼

Facts: CA C certified the circulation figures of a daily newspaper for the past 6 months based on verified dispatch registers.

Provisions & Analysis: Clause (3) of Part I of Second Schedule prohibits vouching for future earnings forecasts. Verifying historical past circulation data relates to past facts, not future forecasts.

Conclusion: Not Guilty. Certification of past historical data is permissible.

Ill 33: CA partner's spouse holding substantial voting interest in audit client ▼

Facts: CA D accepted audit of an entity where his partner's wife held 25% voting power.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clause (4) of Part I of the Second Schedule, an auditor cannot express an opinion on an enterprise in which he or his partner has a substantial interest (>20%).

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Clause (4).

Ill 34: Auditor failing to report omission of unrecorded liability of ₹50 lakhs ▼

Facts: CA A knew of a pending undisputed tax decree of ₹50 lakhs not recorded in accounts and issued a clean report without disclosure.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clauses (5) & (6) of Part I of the Second Schedule, failure to disclose a known material fact or known material misstatement constitutes severe misconduct.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Clauses (5) and (6).

Ill 35: Charitable institution client funds deposited into firm's current account ▼

Facts: A client entrusted ₹10 lakhs with CA firm M/s R & Co. for land purchase stamp duty. The firm deposited it into its own office bank account.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Clause (10) of Part I of the Second Schedule, client moneys must be deposited in a separate dedicated banking account and utilized solely for intended purposes.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Clause (10).

Ill 36: CA not maintaining books of account for professional earnings ▼

Facts: CA L did not maintain a cash book or billing ledger, claiming his income was below the income tax threshold.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Chapter X of Council General Guidelines 2008 read with Clause (1) of Part II of the Second Schedule, every practicing CA must maintain proper professional books of account regardless of tax liability.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Second Schedule, Part II, Clause (1).

Ill 37: Statutory audit of PSU with negative net worth accepted ▼

Facts: Mr. C accepted statutory audit of a State PSU whose net worth was fully eroded, without verifying appointment guidelines.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Council Guidelines, accepting audit appointments in non-compliant government entities without verifying C&AG empanelment violates Council guidelines.

Conclusion: Guilty under Second Schedule, Part II, Clause (1).

Ill 38: Statutory audit fee ₹50 Lakhs – Management consultancy fee ₹1 Crore ▼

Facts: CA A is the auditor of Z Ltd. (turnover ₹300 crore) for an audit fee of ₹50 lakhs. The company offered A a management consultancy assignment for ₹1 crore.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Chapter VI of Council General Guidelines, 2008, for an entity with turnover ≥ ₹250 crore, fees for other assignments cannot exceed the statutory audit fee.

Conclusion: Guilty of Professional Misconduct under Second Schedule, Part II, Clause (1) if he accepts the ₹1 crore assignment.

Ill 39: Tax audit fee of ₹5,50,000 received on progressive basis before signing report ▼

Facts: CA D received ₹5,50,000 audit fees on a progressive basis as work proceeded for a tax audit u/s 44AB before the report was formally signed.

Provisions & Analysis: Under Chapter VII of Council Guidelines, indebtedness exceeding ₹5,00,000 is prohibited. However, the ICAI Research Committee has affirmed that receiving fees on a progressive basis for work actually performed does not constitute indebtedness.

Conclusion: Not Guilty. Progressive fee billing is permissible.
