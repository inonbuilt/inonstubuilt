Phase 01

Overview, Nature of Related Services & The Assurance Spectrum

Under the ICAI Framework for Engagement Standards, professional services rendered by Chartered Accountants fall along a clear spectrum of assurance:

Standard Series Type of Service Assurance Level Reporting Output
SAs (100–700 & 800) Audits of Historical Financial Information Reasonable Assurance (High, but not absolute) Positive opinion (True and fair view / fair presentation)
SREs (2000–2699) Reviews of Historical Financial Information Limited Assurance (Moderate) Negative conclusion ("Nothing has come to our attention")
SAEs (3000–3699) Assurance other than Audits / Reviews (PFI, SOC) Reasonable or Limited Attestation or Direct Reporting conclusion
SRSs (4000–4699) Related Services (Agreed-Upon & Compilations) NO ASSURANCE! Report of Factual Findings or Compilation Report
What Makes "Related Services" Unique?

In a related services engagement, the practitioner does NOT express any audit opinion or review conclusion:

  • SRS 4400 (Agreed-Upon Procedures): The practitioner performs specific procedures agreed upon with the client and specified third parties, and simply delivers a Report of Factual Findings. The users evaluate the findings themselves and draw their own conclusions!
  • SRS 4410 (Compilation Engagements): The practitioner uses accounting and financial reporting expertise to assist management in compiling and presenting financial information without verifying accuracy or gathering evidence.
Case Studies

Opening Scenarios: Trends Multi Products & The Kolkata Dilemma

Scenario A: Trends Multi Products Limited & CA Shaan

Trends Multi Products Limited sells ethnic items across India (brassware, wooden artifacts, handloom home furnishings). Stock is procured from artisans and weavers across dozens of nationwide centers. To mitigate inventory risks, the company requests CA Shaan to undertake an extensive exercise: physically verify inventories at multiple locations and reconcile them with inventory books, offering attractive fees and support staff.

CA Shaan wonders: Does SA 501 apply? No, because SA 501 applies to an audit of financial statements, and he is not the statutory auditor. Can he accept? Yes! This is a classic Agreed-Upon Procedures Engagement under SRS 4400. He will report factual discrepancies without expressing any audit opinion or assurance.

Scenario B: The Sweltering Kolkata Afternoon Compilation

Sitting in his Kolkata office, CA Shaan is approached by another client providing a trial balance and asking him to assist in preparing its financial statements and issue a Compilation Report. Does this report provide assurance? What are his duties?

Answer under SRS 4410: A compilation engagement does not provide any assurance. The practitioner is not required to verify the accuracy of information provided. However, under SQC 1, strict quality control, professional competence, and ethical standards apply. Furthermore, if he notices that the compiled statements are materially misstated or misleading, he MUST propose amendments, and if management refuses, he MUST WITHDRAW!

SRS 4400

SRS 4400: Engagements to Perform Agreed-Upon Procedures

SRS 4400 deals with the auditor’s responsibilities when engaged to perform agreed-upon procedures regarding financial or non-financial information, and to issue a report of factual findings.

Objective under SRS 4400

"The objective of an agreed-upon procedures engagement is for the auditor to carry out procedures of an audit nature to which the auditor and the entity and any appropriate third parties have agreed and to report on factual findings."

No Assurance Expressed: As the auditor simply provides a report of the factual findings of agreed-upon procedures, no assurance is expressed. Instead, users of the report assess for themselves the procedures and findings reported by the auditor and draw their own conclusions from the auditor's work.

⚖️ Audit vs. Agreed-Upon Procedures (SRS 4400)
Basis of Comparison Audit Engagement (SAs) Agreed-Upon Procedures (SRS 4400)
1. Purpose & Scope To express an opinion on whether financial statements give a true and fair view. Scope determined by SAs and law. To perform specific procedures agreed between auditor, entity, and third parties. Scope strictly limited to agreed procedures.
2. Assurance Provided Provides Reasonable Assurance (high level of confidence). NO ASSURANCE is expressed.
3. Nature of Procedures Comprehensive audit procedures selected based on auditor's risk assessment and materiality. Procedures of an audit nature (inquiry, recalculation, comparison, physical observation) specified by client.
4. Reporting Output Independent Auditor's Report containing an opinion (unmodified, qualified, adverse, disclaimer). Report of Factual Findings. Merely describes procedures performed and actual factual results.
5. Distribution of Report Public document available to all shareholders, regulators, and public users. Strictly Restricted to parties who agreed to the procedures (to avoid misunderstanding).
📝 General Principles & Agreeing the Terms of Engagement

The auditor must comply with the Code of Ethics issued by the ICAI (Integrity, Objectivity, Professional Competence & Due Care, Confidentiality, Professional Behavior):

  • Independence Rule: Independence is NOT a mandatory requirement for an agreed-upon procedures engagement (unless specifically required by the terms or contract). However, if the auditor is not independent, a statement to that effect must be made in the report of factual findings!
  • Non-Auditor Permitted: The term "auditor" in SRS 4400 does not imply that the practitioner must be the statutory auditor of the entity. Any practicing Chartered Accountant can accept an SRS 4400 engagement.
Key Matters to Agree in the Engagement Letter (SRS 4400)

The auditor shall ensure there is a clear understanding with client representatives and third parties regarding:

  1. Nature of the engagement, stating that procedures will not constitute an audit or review and no assurance will be expressed;
  2. Stated purpose for which the engagement is performed;
  3. Identification of the specific financial information or elements to which procedures will be applied;
  4. Nature, timing, and extent of the specific procedures to be applied;
  5. Anticipated form of the report of factual findings; and
  6. Clear statement that the report will be restricted solely to the parties who agreed to the procedures.
📢 Reporting Requirements under SRS 4400 (The 4 Negative Disclaimers)
The Four Mandatory Negative Assurance Statements

To ensure report users do not misinterpret factual findings as an audit or review, the report under SRS 4400 SHALL INCLUDE ALL FOUR STATEMENTS:

  1. Disclaimer of Assurance: A statement that the procedures performed do not constitute either an audit or a review and, as such, no assurance is expressed;
  2. Possible Additional Matters: A statement that had the auditor performed additional procedures, an audit or a review, other matters might have come to light that would have been reported;
  3. Restricted Distribution: A statement that the report is restricted to those parties that have agreed to the procedures to be performed; and
  4. Non-Extension to Full Accounts: A statement that the report relates only to the elements, accounts, items or financial/non-financial information specified and that it does not extend to the entity’s financial statements taken as a whole.

SRS 4410 Core

SRS 4410: Compilation Engagements (In-Depth Standard Analysis)

Definitional Architecture of a Compilation Engagement

Under SRS 4410, a Compilation Engagement is defined as:

"An engagement in which a practitioner applies accounting and financial reporting expertise to assist management in the preparation and presentation of financial information of an entity in accordance with an applicable financial reporting framework and issues a report."

SRS 4410 deals with the practitioner’s professional responsibilities when engaged to assist management with the preparation and presentation of historical financial information without obtaining any assurance on that information, and to report on the engagement in accordance with this SRS.

Primary Subject Matter Scope

SRS 4410 primarily applies to compilation engagements for historical financial information.

This includes complete financial statements (Balance Sheet, Statement of Profit and Loss, Cash Flow Statement, Notes) or single financial statements, accounts, or specific elements.

Adaptation to Other Information

Compilation engagements for:

  • Financial information other than historical (e.g., prospective financial information, budgets, pro forma statements), and
  • Non-financial information (e.g., ESG metrics, greenhouse gas data, production statistics)

can also be performed under SRS 4410 after necessary adaptation.

The Strict Non-Assurance Character of SRS 4410

It is critical to distinguish a compilation engagement from an audit or a review:

Dimension Assurance Engagements (Audit / Review) Compilation Engagement (SRS 4410)
Primary Objective Enhance degree of confidence of intended users other than responsible party through independent evaluation against criteria. Assist management in preparing and presenting financial information using practitioner's accounting expertise.
Evidence Gathering Mandatory gathering of sufficient appropriate audit/review evidence (vouching, verification, external confirmations). NO requirement to gather evidence or verify accuracy/completeness of management's data.
Verification of Data Auditor validates underlying transactions, internal controls, and records. Practitioner relies on records, explanations, and judgments provided by management without audit validation.
Form of Conclusion Expresses an Audit Opinion (Reasonable assurance) or Review Conclusion (Limited assurance). ZERO assurance expressed. The report states explicitly that no opinion or conclusion is provided.

ICAI Core Rule: A compilation engagement is not an assurance engagement. It does not require the practitioner to verify the accuracy or completeness of the information provided by management for the compilation, or otherwise to gather evidence to express an audit opinion or a review conclusion.

SRS 4410 Paras 3.1 - 3.3

Objectives, Scope, Intended Purposes & Ethical Principles

Practitioner’s Dual Objectives under SRS 4410 (Para 3.1)

The practitioner’s objectives in a compilation engagement under SRS 4410 are two-fold:

Objective (a): Technical Assistance

Apply accounting and financial reporting expertise to assist management in the preparation and presentation of financial information in accordance with an applicable financial reporting framework based on information provided by management.

Objective (b): Formal Reporting

Report in accordance with the specific requirements of SRS 4410, clearly communicating the nature of the engagement and the absence of any assurance.

Why Entities Request a Compilation Engagement (Exam Question TYK 1 & 12)

Management may request a professional accountant in public practice to assist with financial information for diverse mandatory and non-mandatory purposes:

Category 1: Mandatory Legal / Regulatory Reporting

To comply with mandatory periodic financial reporting requirements established in relevant law or regulation (e.g., statutory filings for certain non-corporate entities, societies, trusts, or regulatory submissions).

Category 2(a): Internal Management / TCWG Use

Prepared on a basis appropriate for their particular internal purposes (e.g., periodic management accounts, divisional performance reports, cash forecasts, internal budgeting).

Category 2(b): External Reporting under Contract / Agreement

Periodic financial reporting undertaken for external third parties under an agreement — such as financial information submitted to a funding body or government authority to support the provision or continuation of a grant, subsidy, or commercial credit line.

Category 2(c): Transactional Purposes (M&A)

To support a major transaction involving changes to the entity’s ownership or financing structure (e.g., mergers, acquisitions, hive-offs, private equity investment pitch, or restructuring of bank loans).

Scope & Management's 4 Inalienable Responsibilities (Para 3.2 & TYK 9)

The scope of a compilation engagement varies based on circumstances — management may provide raw trial balances or preliminary draft financial statements. However, in every case, management retains ultimate responsibility for the financial information.

Management Retains Exclusive Responsibility For:

  1. The Financial Information: For the preparation and presentation thereof in accordance with an applicable financial reporting framework acceptable to intended users.
  2. Internal Control: Design, implementation, and maintenance of such internal control as management determines necessary to enable preparation of financial statements free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error.
  3. Accuracy and Completeness of Data: The accuracy and completeness of records, documents, explanations, and other information supplied to the practitioner.
  4. Management Judgments: All judgments required in preparing the financial statements — including the selection and consistent application of appropriate accounting policies, and developing reasonable accounting estimates (even where the practitioner provides advisory assistance during compilation).

Financial Reporting Framework Spectrum: Management may adopt frameworks ranging from an entity-specific basis of accounting (e.g., cash basis, tax basis) to established standards (Accounting Standards / Ind AS), depending on entity nature and intended usage.

Ethical Requirements & The Independence Dilemma (Para 3.3 & TYK 2)

The practitioner shall strictly comply with relevant ethical requirements established in the ICAI Code of Ethics:

Applicable Fundamental Principles

  • Integrity: Being straightforward and honest.
  • Objectivity: Not compromising professional judgment.
  • Professional Competence & Due Care: Maintaining high technical standards.
  • Confidentiality: Respecting proprietary information.
  • Professional Conduct: Complying with relevant laws.

Independence: Mandatory or Not?

General Rule: Because a compilation engagement is a non-assurance engagement, independence is NOT a requirement under SRS 4410.

Exceptions: If specific laws, regulations, or contractual terms mandate independence, the practitioner must comply. If the practitioner is not independent, this fact may be stated or governed by disclosure rules where applicable.

Crucial Exam Distinction (TYK 2): Non-applicability of independence does NOT exempt the practitioner from the Fundamental Principles of the Code of Ethics! Threats to integrity, objectivity, and competence must still be identified and addressed. If associated with misleading statements without remediation, the practitioner violates the fundamental principle of Integrity.

Quality Control

Applicability of SQC 1 to Compilation Engagements (Para 3 & TYK 6, 14)

SQC 1 Applies to All Engagement Standards — Including SRS 4410!

A frequent exam trap asks whether Quality Control Standards apply to compilation engagements since no assurance is given. ICAI makes this crystal clear:

"SQC 1 (Standard on Quality Control 1) is applicable to ALL Engagement Standards. Since Standards on Related Services (SRS 4400 and SRS 4410) are part of the Engagement and Quality Control Standards suite issued by ICAI, SQC 1 applies to CA firms in respect of their compilation engagements too!"

Firm-Level Quality Control (SQC 1)

The CA firm must establish policies and procedures regarding leadership responsibilities, ethical compliance, acceptance/continuance of client relationships, human resources, engagement performance, and monitoring across all service lines including SRS compilations.

Engagement Partner Responsibilities (Para 3.8)

Under Para 3.8 of SRS 4410, the engagement partner shall take personal responsibility for:
(a) The overall quality of each compilation engagement assigned; and
(b) The engagement being performed in accordance with the firm’s quality control policies and procedures.

SRS 4410 Para 3.4

Engagement Acceptance, Continuance & Terms of Engagement

Pre-Conditions for Acceptance: 6 Mandatory Terms (Para 3.4 & TYK 9, 13, 15)

The practitioner shall NOT accept the compilation engagement unless the practitioner has agreed upon the terms of engagement with management (and the engaging party, if different). The agreed terms must include:

Clause Mandatory Term Detailed Scope & Purpose
(a) Intended Use & Distribution The intended use and distribution of the financial information, and any restrictions on either its use or distribution where applicable.
(b) Financial Reporting Framework Explicit identification of the applicable financial reporting framework (e.g., AS, Ind AS, or a specific cash/tax basis).
(c) Objective & Scope The objective and scope of the compilation engagement (clarifying technical compilation vs assurance).
(d) Practitioner’s Responsibilities Responsibilities of the practitioner, including compliance with relevant ethical requirements and SRS 4410.
(e) Management’s Responsibilities Explicit recording of management’s 4 core responsibilities:
(i) Financial info preparation under acceptable framework;
(ii) Design & maintenance of necessary internal controls;
(iii) Accuracy and completeness of records & documents provided;
(iv) Judgments needed in preparing information.
(f) Expected Form of Report The expected form and content of the practitioner’s compilation report to be issued.

Mandatory Written Engagement Letter: The practitioner shall record the agreed terms in an engagement letter or other suitable form of written agreement prior to performing the engagement.

Recurring Compilation Engagements (TYK 13)

On recurring compilation engagements, the practitioner shall evaluate whether:

  • Circumstances, including changes in engagement acceptance considerations or user requirements, require the terms of engagement to be revised; and
  • There is a need to remind management of the existing terms of engagement (e.g., change in management personnel, scope expansion, legal changes).
SRS 4410 Para 3.5

Performing the Engagement: Fieldwork & Practitioner Responsibilities

The Two Mandatory Understandings (Para 3.5)

The practitioner shall obtain an understanding of the following matters sufficient to be able to perform the compilation:

1. Entity's Business & Operations

Knowledge of the entity’s business and operations, including the entity’s accounting system and accounting records.

2. Applicable Accounting Framework

The applicable financial reporting framework, including its specific application in the entity’s industry.

Key Steps in Performing Compilation:

  • Compile Data: Compile financial information using records, documents, explanations, and judgments provided by management.
  • Discuss Judgments: Discuss with management or TCWG those significant judgments for which the practitioner provided assistance during compilation.
  • Read Compiled Statements: Prior to completion, the practitioner shall read the compiled financial information in light of their understanding of the entity's business, operations, and applicable framework.

Final Management Acknowledgement

The practitioner shall obtain an acknowledgement from management or those charged with governance, as appropriate, that they have taken responsibility for the final version of the compiled financial information.

Communication with TCWG (Para 3.6)

The practitioner shall communicate with management or TCWG on a timely basis during the course of the engagement all matters that, in professional judgment, are of sufficient importance to merit attention.

SRS 4410 Para 3.7

Form, Elements & Structure of the Practitioner's Compilation Report

The 13 Prescribed Elements of a Compilation Report (Para 3.7 & TYK 12)

The practitioner’s report issued for a compilation engagement shall be in writing and contain the following 13 statutory elements:

# Report Element Standard Requirement & ICAI Specific Guidance
(a) Report Title Clearly titled: "Practitioner’s Compilation Report" (distinguishing it from an independent auditor's report).
(b) Addressee Addressee as required by the terms of the engagement (ordinarily management or TCWG).
(c) Statement of Compilation A statement that the practitioner has compiled the financial information based on information provided by management.
(d) Management's Responsibilities A description of the responsibilities of management or TCWG in relation to the compilation engagement and the financial information.
(e) Applicable Framework Identification of the applicable financial reporting framework (and description/reference if a special purpose framework is used).
(f) Identification of Financial Info Identification of the financial information, including the title of each element (e.g. Balance Sheet, P&L) and the date or period to which it relates.
(g) Practitioner's Responsibilities A description that the engagement was performed in accordance with SRS 4410 and that the practitioner complied with relevant ethical requirements.
(h) Nature of Compilation A description of what a compilation engagement entails in accordance with SRS 4410.
(i) Dual Negative Explanations Mandatory Non-Assurance Statements:
(i) Since a compilation is not an assurance engagement, the practitioner is not required to verify accuracy or completeness of info provided; and
(ii) Accordingly, the practitioner does not express an audit opinion or a review conclusion on whether the statements are prepared in accordance with framework.
(j) Special Purpose Warning Paragraph If prepared under a special purpose framework:
(i) Describes purpose & intended users (or refers to note); and
(ii) Draws attention of readers that info is prepared in accordance with special purpose framework and may not be suitable for other purposes.
(k) Date of Report The date of the practitioner’s report (cannot predate date management took responsibility).
(l) Practitioner's Signature Signature in the personal name of the CA / firm name along with Membership Number & FRN.
(m) Place of Signature The specific city/place where the report is signed.

Engagement Documentation Requirements (Para 3.9 & TYK 4, 11, 14)

The practitioner shall maintain engagement documentation that provides a sufficient and appropriate record of the work performed:

Mandatory Inclusions in Working Papers

  • Significant Matters: Significant matters arising during the engagement and how those matters were addressed by the practitioner (e.g. resolution of inventory valuation doubts).
  • Reconciliation Record: A record of how the compiled financial information reconciles with underlying records, documents, explanations, and other information provided by management.
  • Acknowledged Copy & Report: A copy of the final version of the compiled financial information for which management/TCWG acknowledged responsibility, and the practitioner’s report.

Optional Items & File Assembly (SQC 1)

  • Optional Records: Copy of the entity’s trial balance, summary of significant accounting records, or schedules used during compilation.
  • Assembly of Final File (Case MCQ 5): In accordance with SQC 1, the final engagement file should be assembled on a timely basis after the report has been finalised, within the time limits established by the firm’s quality control policies.
ICAI TYU 2

Test Your Understanding 2: Unrecorded Theft Loss of ₹ 100 Lacs in Compilation

Practical Case Problem (ICAI Study Material)

Scenario: Repudiated Insurance Claim of ₹ 100 Lacs Kept under Current Assets

During the course of performing a compilation engagement in accordance with SRS 4410, it becomes known to you that the client had suffered a theft loss of ₹ 100 lacs of its inventories over a period of time at a storage location visited infrequently.

A claim was lodged by the client with the insurance company which was repudiated due to certain technical reasons relating to policy coverage. The client has not preferred a complaint or an appeal against said repudiation.

The amount of ₹ 100 lacs is reflected under the head "Current Assets" in the trial balance of the client.

Question: Discuss how you should proceed to deal with the matter under SRS 4410?

Step-by-Step Resolution under SRS 4410:

Phase Applicable Principle & Practical Action
1. Nature of the Item Since the insurance claim has been repudiated and no appeal or legal challenge has been preferred, the amount of ₹ 100 lacs ceases to be a recoverable receivable or asset. It represents an irrecoverable loss that should be written off to the Statement of Profit and Loss. Retaining it under "Current Assets" renders the compiled financial statements materially misstated and misleading.
2. Practitioner's Duty to Propose Amendments Under SRS 4410, if the practitioner becomes aware during the engagement that:
• Amendments to compiled financial information are required for information not to be materially misstated; or
• The compiled financial information is otherwise misleading,
The practitioner SHALL PROPOSE THE APPROPRIATE AMENDMENTS TO MANAGEMENT (i.e. propose writing off ₹ 100 lacs as a loss).
3. If Management Accepts If management permits the amendment, the practitioner compiles the financial statements incorporating the write-off and completes the engagement normally.
4. If Management Declines (Crucial Step) If management declines or does not permit the practitioner to make proposed amendments, the practitioner SHALL WITHDRAW FROM THE ENGAGEMENT and inform management and those charged with governance (TCWG) of the reasons for withdrawing!
5. If Withdrawal Not Possible If withdrawal is not legally possible, the practitioner shall determine applicable professional and legal responsibilities in the circumstances.

Exam Key Takeaway: In a compilation engagement, the practitioner cannot issue a "qualified" compilation report on disagreement. If management refuses to rectify a material misstatement, WITHDRAWAL IS MANDATORY!

Integrated Case Scenario: KM Limited (Rourkela Unit Compilation)

ICAI Integrated Case Scenario (Exam Standard)

Background & Engagement Observations: KM Limited

KM Limited has engaged your CA firm for the compilation of financial statements in accordance with the requirements of SRS 4410. You also come to know that the company is setting up a new unit in Rourkela, Odisha. The company management has provided you with a draft trial balance and requires assistance in the preparation and presentation of its financial statements for the year ended 31st March, 2026. The management requires such preparation and presentation for its internal use.

During the course of the engagement, the following specific matters are noticed by the engagement team:

  • [Observation 1 - Opening Balance Swaps]: There are apparent errors in a few opening balances brought forward from the previous year relating to some outstanding incentives receivable from government authorities. These have been swapped with some other balances in the trial balance. However, there are no credit transactions in such incentive accounts or accounts whose balances have been swapped during the year.
  • [Observation 2 - Revenue Verification Suggestion]: One of the team members suggests that it is one of the team's duties to ensure that revenue figures stated in the trial balance, at least, are verified to ensure that all revenues required to be booked by the company have, in fact, been booked.
  • [Observation 3 - Quality Control Applicability]: It is also suggested by this team member that even though it is a compilation engagement, quality control aspects like adhering to appropriate Standards need to be followed.
  • [Observation 4 - Omission of Rourkela Unit Expenditure]: Before signing and issuing the report under SRS 4410, you once again read the financial information. It comes to your notice that figures relating to setting up the new unit in Rourkela, Odisha have not been properly disclosed in the compiled financial statements. The expenditure was incurred from a bank account maintained in Rourkela and was omitted to be shown under appropriate heads. You are vacillating regarding this matter considering the non-assurance scope of a compilation engagement.
  • [Observation 5 - Engagement Documentation]: The engagement team has maintained detailed documentation during the course of the compilation engagement.

Multiple Choice Questions (Solved with ICAI Rationale)

Question 1 SRS 4410 Para 3.5 • Incomplete Records

1. In respect of errors in some of the opening balances noticed as described in the case, which of the following statements is most appropriate?

(a) Such errors should straight away lead to the qualification of a report to be issued.
(b) Such errors should be brought to the knowledge of management.
(c) Knowledge of such type of errors has no effect on compliance of ethical requirements by the firm.
(d) The above said matter relates to SA 510 Initial Audit Engagements-Opening Balances and has no effect on reporting obligations in a compilation engagement.
Correct Answer: (b)
ICAI Rationale: Under SRS 4410, if the practitioner becomes aware that the records, documents, explanations, or other information provided by management are incomplete, inaccurate, or otherwise unsatisfactory, the practitioner shall bring that to the attention of management and request additional or corrected information. A compilation report cannot be "qualified" — if management fails/refuses to correct, withdrawal is required. SA 510 does not apply to non-assurance compilations.
Question 2 SRS 4410 • Non-Assurance Character

2. As regards the suggestion of one of the team members regarding the verification of all revenues of the company, which of the following statements is most appropriate?

(a) Suggestion of team member is proper as such verification is part and parcel of such an engagement.
(b) Suggestion of team member is proper as the absence of such verification may make financial statements misleading.
(c) Suggestion of team member is not proper as verifying the accuracy or completeness of the information provided by management is not required in such engagement.
(d) Suggestion of team member is not proper as compliance with qualitative requirements is not required in such engagement.
Correct Answer: (c)
ICAI Rationale: A compilation engagement is not an assurance engagement. Under SRS 4410, the practitioner is not required to verify the accuracy or completeness of the information provided by management for the compilation, or otherwise to gather evidence to express an audit opinion or review conclusion. Applying audit vouching to revenues misinterprets the fundamental scope of SRS 4410.
Question 3 Quality Control Standards • SQC 1 Scope

3. In view of the team member’s suggestion relating to adherence to appropriate Standards for quality control, which of the following statements is relevant in the context of the above said engagement?

(a) SA 220 is applicable in this engagement and has to be followed by the engagement partner meticulously.
(b) SQC 1 is applicable in this engagement.
(c) Both SA 220 and SQC 1 are applicable in this engagement.
(d) SA 220 and SQC 1 are not applicable in this engagement. However, SRS 4410 lays down detailed quality control requirements for such type of engagement.
Correct Answer: (b)
ICAI Rationale: SQC 1 is applicable to all Engagement Standards issued by ICAI. Because SRS 4410 is an Engagement Standard, SQC 1 applies to the firm in respect of compilation engagements. SA 220 applies specifically to audits of historical financial statements and is not applicable to compilations.
Question 4 Ethics & Integrity • Misleading Financial Statements

4. Which of the following statements is most appropriate as regards omission of expenditure under appropriate heads pertaining to the Rourkela unit in compiled financial statements?

(a) The above-noted omission can be misleading. By disregarding such an omission, the fundamental principle of integrity is violated, and engagement cannot be performed in accordance with ethical requirements.
(b) The above-noted omission can be misleading. By disregarding such an omission, the fundamental principle of objectivity is violated, and engagement cannot be performed in accordance with ethical requirements.
(c) The above noted omission has no effect on performing such compilation engagement.
(d) The above-noted omission has no effect on performing and issuing reports under such compilation engagement.
Correct Answer: (a)
ICAI Rationale: Even in non-assurance engagements, a Chartered Accountant must comply with the Fundamental Principles of the Code of Ethics. Knowingly associating oneself with materially false or misleading information violates the fundamental principle of Integrity. The practitioner must propose amendments to properly classify the expenditure; if refused, withdrawal is mandatory.
Question 5 Documentation • File Assembly under SQC 1

5. The detailed documentation is maintained during the course of compilation engagement. Which of the following statements is most appropriate regarding the assembly of the final engagement file?

(a) Final engagement file should be assembled in not more than 60 days after the date of the report.
(b) Final engagement file should be assembled in not more than 120 days after the date of the report.
(c) Final engagement file should be assembled on a timely basis after the engagement report has been finalised in accordance with the time limits set by the firm.
(d) There is no requirement of assembling of final engagement file in a compilation engagement.
Correct Answer: (c)
ICAI Rationale: Under SQC 1, the firm must establish policies and procedures for engagement teams to complete the assembly of final engagement files on a timely basis after the engagement reports have been finalised, in accordance with the time limits established by the firm. (While SQC 1 provides an illustrative limit ordinarily not more than 60 days for audits, for engagements generally it is governed by the firm's established time limits).

TYK 1: Intended Purposes of a Compilation Engagement

Theory
Question: List out a few intended purposes of a "compilation engagement."

ICAI Model Answer:

As per SRS 4410, financial information that is the subject of a compilation engagement may be required for various purposes, including:

  1. Mandatory Reporting: To comply with mandatory periodic financial reporting requirements established in relevant law or regulation, if any (e.g. regulatory submissions by trusts, societies, or non-corporate entities).
  2. Unrelated to Mandatory Reporting:
    • Internal Management Use: For management or those charged with governance (TCWG), prepared on a basis appropriate for their particular internal purposes (e.g., periodic management accounts, operational cash flows).
    • Contractual External Reporting: For periodic financial reporting undertaken for external parties under a contract or agreement — such as financial information provided to a funding body to support the provision or continuation of a government grant or credit facility.
    • Transactional Purposes: For transactional purposes, for example to support a transaction involving changes to the entity’s ownership or financing structure (such as for a merger, acquisition, or restructuring).

TYK 2: Ethical Requirements & Code of Ethics in Compilation

Ethics
Question: A Chartered Accountant is offered appointment for a compilation engagement to be performed under SRS 4410. Is he required to comply with the ethical requirements of the Code of Ethics? Discuss briefly.

ICAI Model Answer:

As per Para 3.3 of SRS 4410, the practitioner SHALL COMPLY with relevant ethical requirements established in the ICAI Code of Ethics:

  • Fundamental Principles: The practitioner must adhere to Integrity, Objectivity, Professional Competence and Due Care, Confidentiality, and Professional Conduct. Threats to compliance must be identified and appropriately addressed.
  • Independence Nuance: Being in the nature of a non-assurance engagement, independence requirements DO NOT apply to compilation engagements under SRS 4410. However, if specific laws, regulations, or client agreements specify independence rules, they must be complied with.
  • Integrity Principle Crux: Non-applicability of independence does not permit the practitioner to compromise on Integrity. A practitioner cannot knowingly be associated with materially false or misleading information.

TYK 3: Related Services vs. Assurance Engagements

Distinction
Question: How do "related services" differ from assurance engagements?

ICAI Model Answer:

Feature Assurance Engagements (Audits / Reviews) Related Services (SRS 4400 / SRS 4410)
Definition & Intent Practitioner expresses a conclusion designed to enhance user confidence by evaluating a subject matter against criteria. Practitioner performs agreed-upon procedures or compiles financial information without expressing any conclusion.
Level of Assurance Reasonable assurance (Audit) or Limited assurance (Review). ZERO Assurance provided.
Evidence Gathering Systematic evidence gathering through testing, verification, external confirmation, and analytical procedures. No evidence gathered to form an opinion. Only factual findings or compilation from management data.
User Conclusion Users rely on the practitioner's independent professional opinion/conclusion. Users assess findings and procedures themselves and draw their own independent conclusions.

TYK 4: Documentation Requirements under SRS 4410

Documentation
Question: Discuss main documentation requirements to be taken care of by a practitioner while performing a compilation engagement under SRS 4410.

ICAI Model Answer:

As per Para 3.9 of SRS 4410, the practitioner shall include in the engagement documentation:

  1. Significant Matters: Significant matters arising during the compilation engagement and how those matters were addressed by the practitioner.
  2. Reconciliation Record: A record of how the compiled financial information reconciles with the underlying records, documents, explanations, and other information provided by management.
  3. Final Acknowledged Copy & Report: A copy of the final version of the compiled financial information for which management or TCWG has acknowledged their responsibility, and a copy of the practitioner’s compilation report.

Additional Inclusions: The practitioner may also include a copy of the entity’s trial balance, summary of significant accounting records, or schedules used to perform the compilation.

TYK 5: CA P / X Limited — Audit Procedures vs. Compilation Approach

Case Application
Question: CA P has been appointed to compile the financial information of X Limited. CA P is confused whether he should apply the same procedures which are required to be applied to conduct an audit or there are some other procedures to discharge the duties under such an engagement. Define the characteristics of Compilation Engagement. What should be the approach of CA P for performing the Engagement?

ICAI Model Answer:

1. Characteristics of a Compilation Engagement (Para 3):
A compilation engagement is an engagement in which a practitioner applies accounting and financial reporting expertise to assist management in preparing and presenting financial information under an applicable framework and issues a report. It is NOT an assurance engagement. It does not require CA P to verify accuracy or completeness of data provided, or gather evidence to express an audit opinion.

2. CA P’s Approach for Performing the Engagement (Para 3.5):
CA P should NOT apply audit procedures (like vouching, verification, debtor circularisation). Instead, CA P's approach must comprise:

  • Obtain Understanding: Gain knowledge of X Limited’s business, operations, accounting systems/records, and the applicable financial reporting framework.
  • Compile from Records: Compile financial statements using records, documents, explanations, and judgments provided by management.
  • Discuss Judgments: Discuss significant accounting judgments with management where assistance was provided.
  • Read Financial Statements: Prior to completion, read the compiled statements in light of understanding of entity and framework to assess if they appear sensible.
  • Address Deficiencies: If records are incomplete/inaccurate, request additional information. If statements are materially misstated or misleading, propose amendments. If management refuses, WITHDRAW from the engagement!

TYK 6: MNC Limited / CA Lalit — Guidance to Team & SQC 1 Applicability

QC & Guidance
Question: MNC Limited has engaged CA Lalit to help the company in compilation of financial information. CA Lalit explained to his team members the scope of work and responsibilities under this engagement. The team members have done mostly audit engagements and do not have exposure to compilation engagements. Discuss the key issues that CA Lalit should deliberate and guide his team members with respect to this engagement and the manner it differs from assurance engagements. Give your views on the applicability of SQC 1 to this engagement.

ICAI Model Answer:

1. Key Guidance to Team Members:
CA Lalit should guide his team on the fundamental differences between audit and compilation under SRS 4410:

  • Assistance vs. Verification: The role is to assist management using accounting expertise, not to audit or verify accuracy/completeness of management records.
  • Intended Purposes: Explain that compilation may be for internal management use, funding grant support, or transactional M&A purposes.
  • No Opinion Expressed: Highlight that the report explicitly states that no audit opinion or review conclusion is expressed.

2. Applicability of SQC 1:
SQC 1 is fully applicable to this engagement! SQC 1 applies to all Engagement Standards issued by ICAI. Since SRS 4410 is an Engagement Standard within the ICAI framework, the firm's quality control policies regarding leadership, ethics, human resources, engagement performance, and monitoring strictly govern CA Lalit's compilation engagement.

TYK 7: Brown Enterprises Limited / CA Kamna — ₹ 100 Cr Trade Receivables Verification

SRS 4400 Case
Question: Brown Enterprises Limited has huge funds locked up in trade receivables standing at ₹ 100 crores as on 31st December, 2025. Management wants to evaluate validity of trade receivables to ensure reliability of reporting. The accounts department provided a list of 1000 debtors across the country. Management requested CA Kamna to take up this assignment and prepare a report under professional standards. Despite not being statutory auditor of the company, she accepts the engagement.
(a) Discuss whether it was proper for her to accept such engagement.
(b) While reporting, which precautions should be taken by her so that readers of report do not misunderstand its scope?

ICAI Model Answer:

(a) Propriety of Acceptance under SRS 4400:
The engagement involves performing specified procedures on specified subject matter (trade receivables) and issuing a report of factual findings. This falls squarely within the purview of SRS 4400 ("Engagements to Perform Agreed-Upon Procedures Regarding Financial Information").
Acceptance is completely proper! SRS 4400 clarifies that the reference to "auditor" in the Standard does not mean that the practitioner performing related services must be the statutory auditor of the entity’s financial statements. A non-statutory auditor can lawfully accept an agreed-upon procedures engagement.

(b) Reporting Precautions to Avoid Misunderstanding (The 4 Disclaimers):
To prevent readers from misunderstanding the scope of the report, CA Kamna must include the following mandatory precautions in her report of factual findings:

  1. No Audit/Review Disclaimer: A statement that the procedures performed do not constitute either an audit or a review, and as such, no assurance is expressed.
  2. Additional Procedures Disclaimer: A statement that had the auditor performed additional procedures, an audit or a review, other matters might have come to light that would have been reported.
  3. Restricted Distribution: A statement that the report is strictly restricted to those parties that have agreed to the procedures to be performed (management).
  4. Relates Only to Specified Elements: A statement that the report relates only to trade receivables specified and does not extend to the financial statements of Brown Enterprises Limited taken as a whole.

TYK 8: Kings & Company — Understated Inventory & Refusal to Amend

Crucial Case
Question: You have been appointed to compile financial statements of Kings & Company (partnership firm) for tax purposes. During work, you discover that inventory is grossly understated and the company failed to apply applicable standards. On pointing this out, partners inform you that it is outside your scope since you are not conducting an audit and said figures duly certified by firm should be accepted. Comment.

ICAI Model Answer:

1. Evaluation of Partners' Contention:
The contention of the partners that the matter is "outside scope" because it is a compilation and not an audit is COMPLETELY UNTENABLE AND REJECTED under professional standards.

2. Legal & Professional Duty under SRS 4410 (Para 3.5):
Under SRS 4410, if the practitioner becomes aware during the engagement that:

  • The compiled financial information does not adequately refer to or describe the applicable framework;
  • Amendments to compiled information are required for it not to be materially misstated; or
  • The compiled financial information is otherwise misleading,

The practitioner SHALL PROPOSE APPROPRIATE AMENDMENTS to management.

3. Mandatory Action on Refusal:
If the partners decline or do not permit the practitioner to make the proposed amendments to rectify the understated inventory, THE PRACTITIONER SHALL WITHDRAW FROM THE ENGAGEMENT and inform management and TCWG of the reasons for withdrawing. A Chartered Accountant cannot be associated with misleading financial statements as it violates the Fundamental Principle of Integrity under the Code of Ethics.

TYK 9: Coastal Ventures Ltd / CA X — Loan Application & Management Responsibilities

Case Application
Question: Coastal Ventures Ltd. engages CA X to compile Ind AS financial statements for a bank loan application. CA X intends to record agreed terms in an engagement letter prior to performance.
• CA X is of view that independence requirements will not be applicable for above assignment. Do you agree?
• Suggest focus points in respect of management responsibilities that will be recorded in agreed terms in engagement letter.

ICAI Model Answer:

1. Independence Requirements View:
Yes, we agree with CA X. Under SRS 4410, being in the nature of a non-assurance engagement, independence requirements do not apply to compilation engagements. (However, if specific laws, regulations, or bank lending terms prescribe independence disclosure, those rules must be honored).

2. Focus Points on Management Responsibilities in Engagement Letter (Para 3.4):
CA X must record management's explicit agreement to the following four core responsibilities:

  1. Financial Information Preparation: Responsibility for the financial information, and for preparation/presentation thereof in accordance with Ind AS (acceptable in view of intended bank use).
  2. Internal Control: Responsibility for design, implementation, and maintenance of internal controls necessary to ensure statements are free from material misstatement from fraud or error.
  3. Accuracy & Completeness of Data: Responsibility for accuracy and completeness of records, documents, explanations, and other information provided to CA X.
  4. Management Judgments: Responsibility for judgments needed in preparation/presentation, including those where CA X provides compilation assistance.

TYK 10: ATP Limited / RS Ltd — Takeover Due Diligence & SRS 4400 Engagement Letter

SRS 4400 Terms
Question: ATP Limited plans to invest in RS Ltd with a vision of takeover. ATP Limited engages you to perform agreed-upon procedures for Accounts Receivable and Inventory of RS Ltd and submit a report. Enumerate key matters that need to be agreed to form a clear understanding, and matters to include in engagement letter addressed to ATP Ltd.

ICAI Model Answer:

As per Para 2.3.1 of SRS 4400, the auditor should ensure with representatives of the entity and specified parties who will receive copies that there is a clear understanding regarding agreed procedures and conditions of engagement.

Matters to Include in the Engagement Letter Addressed to ATP Limited:
  1. Nature & Objective: Clear statement of the nature of the engagement, confirming it is an agreed-upon procedures engagement regarding accounts receivable and inventory.
  2. Subject Matter Specified: Specific identification of the accounts receivable ledger balances and inventory physical counts/valuation sheets of RS Ltd to which procedures will be applied.
  3. Specific Procedures: An exhaustive, detailed listing of the specific procedures agreed to be performed (e.g. circularisation of debtors above ₹ 10 lacs, test counting 20% inventory lines).
  4. Anticipated Format of Report: The anticipated format and content of the report of factual findings.
  5. Non-Assurance Statement: Explicit statement that the procedures performed will not constitute an audit or review and accordingly, no assurance will be expressed.
  6. Restricted Distribution: Statement that the distribution of the report will be strictly restricted to ATP Limited and management, and cannot be used by or disclosed to other third parties.

TYK 11: M/s RK Industries / CA Pawan — Inventory Doubts & Documentation

Final Exam Traps & Memory Shortcuts for Chapter 9

Trap 1: Independence in SRS 4400 & 4410

Independence is NOT required for Related Services. However, in SRS 4400, if not independent, you MUST state this fact in the report. In SRS 4410, you must still adhere to the Fundamental Principles of the Code of Ethics (especially Integrity).

Trap 2: Qualified Report in Compilation

A practitioner CANNOT issue a "qualified compilation report". If management refuses proposed amendments to fix material misstatements or misleading disclosures $implies$ WITHDRAWAL IS MANDATORY!

Trap 3: SQC 1 Applicability

Students often assume SQC 1 only applies to audits. SQC 1 applies to ALL Engagement Standards, including SRS 4400 and SRS 4410.

Trap 4: Who Can Perform SRS 4400

The practitioner performing an agreed-upon procedures engagement does NOT need to be the statutory auditor of the entity (TYK 7 - CA Kamna).
