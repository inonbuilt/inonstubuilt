1. SA 500 "Audit Evidence"

SA 500 establishes the auditor's responsibility to design and perform audit procedures in such a way as to enable the auditor to obtain Sufficient Appropriate Audit Evidence (SAAE) to be able to draw reasonable conclusions on which to base the auditor's opinion.

🔍 Core Dimensions of Audit Evidence: Sufficiency vs. Appropriateness
Dimension Core Concept Influencing Factors
Sufficiency The measure of the quantity of audit evidence. Affected by:
• The auditor's assessment of the risks of material misstatement (higher assessed risk $implies$ more evidence required); and
• The quality of such audit evidence (higher quality $implies$ less quantity may be required).
Caution: Merely obtaining more evidence cannot compensate for its poor quality!
Appropriateness The measure of the quality of audit evidence; that is, its Relevance and its Reliability in providing support for conclusions. • Relevance: Deals with the logical connection with, or bearing upon, the purpose of the audit procedure and the assertion under consideration.
• Reliability: Influenced by its source and nature, and the circumstances under which it is obtained.
🧠 Universal Generalizations on Reliability of Audit Evidence
  1. External Sources > Internal Sources: Evidence obtained from independent sources outside the entity (e.g. bank confirmations) is more reliable than evidence generated internally.
  2. Effective Controls > Deficient Controls: Internal evidence is more reliable when the related controls, including controls over its preparation and maintenance, are effective.
  3. Direct Auditor Knowledge > Indirect / Inferred: Evidence obtained directly by the auditor (e.g. observation of control application) is more reliable than evidence obtained indirectly or by inference (e.g. inquiry about application of control).
  4. Documentary / Written > Oral: Evidence in documentary form (whether paper, electronic, or other medium) is more reliable than oral representations.
  5. Original Documents > Photocopies / Facsimiles: Evidence provided by original documents is more reliable than evidence provided by photocopies, facsimiles, or digitized scans prone to alteration.
🛠️ 7 Audit Procedures to Obtain Audit Evidence

Audit evidence is obtained by performing Risk Assessment Procedures (RAP) and Further Audit Procedures (TOC and Substantive tests) using the following methods:

  1. Inspection: Examining records or documents (internal or external, paper or electronic) or physical examination of an asset.
  2. Observation: Looking at a process or procedure being performed by others (e.g. auditor's observation of inventory counting by entity personnel).
  3. External Confirmation: Direct written response to the auditor from a third party in paper, electronic, or other medium (SA 505).
  4. Recalculation: Checking the mathematical accuracy of documents or records (can be performed manually or electronically).
  5. Reperformance: Independent execution by the auditor of procedures or controls that were originally performed as part of entity's internal control.
  6. Analytical Procedures: Evaluating financial information through analysis of plausible relationships among both financial and non-financial data (SA 520).
  7. Inquiry: Seeking information of knowledgeable persons, both financial and non-financial, within the entity or outside the entity.

1.1 Evaluating the Work of a Management's Expert

A Management's Expert is an individual or organization possessing expertise in a field other than accounting or auditing, whose work in that field is used by the entity to assist the entity in preparing the financial statements (e.g. actuaries calculating employee gratuity/leave encashment liabilities, geologists valuing mineral reserves, professional valuers valuing real estate).

⚡ Three Mandatory Steps When Using Work of Management's Expert (SA 500)
  1. 1. Evaluate Competence, Capabilities and Objectivity of the Expert:
    • Inquire into professional certifications, licensing, membership in professional bodies, and market reputation.
    • Objectivity is threatened if the expert is employed by the entity, financially dependent, or related to management.
  2. 2. Obtain an Understanding of the Work of that Expert:
    • Understand the expert's field of specialization, scope of assignment, models, and assumptions used.
  3. 3. Evaluate the Appropriateness of the Expert's Work as Audit Evidence:
    • Evaluate the relevance and reasonableness of findings and conclusions.
    • Review significant assumptions and methods used for relevance and reasonableness.
    • Test the relevance, completeness, and accuracy of source data provided by management to the expert!
TYU 1 BBNS Ltd: Actuary Valuation of Gratuity Liability (Management's Expert)
▼
Question: BBNS Ltd. accepted the gratuity liability valuation based on a certificate issued by an actuary. The statutory auditor simply placed reliance on the certificate without performing further procedures, contending that an actuary is a qualified professional expert. Evaluate whether the auditor's approach is appropriate under SA 500.

1. Legal Position under SA 500:

The auditor's approach is inappropriate. As per SA 500, when information used as audit evidence has been prepared using the work of a management's expert, the auditor cannot accept the expert's certificate at face value.

2. Required Audit Procedures:

  • Evaluate Competence, Capabilities and Objectivity: The auditor must assess the actuary's professional qualifications, licensing with the Institute of Actuaries of India, experience, and independence from BBNS Ltd.
  • Understand the Scope of Work: Review terms of engagement, valuation date, and applicable accounting standard (AS 15 / Ind AS 19).
  • Evaluate Assumptions & Methods: Review whether significant actuarial assumptions (discount rate based on government bond yields, salary escalation rate, attrition rate, mortality tables) are relevant and reasonable.
  • Verify Source Data Completeness & Accuracy: Test the employee census data provided by BBNS Ltd to the actuary (employee age, length of service, basic salary, qualifying past service) against payroll and HR records.

2. SA 501 "Audit Evidence - Specific Considerations for Selected Items"

SA 501 deals with specific considerations by the auditor in obtaining sufficient appropriate audit evidence with respect to:
  1. Existence and condition of inventory;
  2. Completeness of litigation and claims involving the entity; and
  3. Presentation and disclosure of segment information.

2.1 Existence and Condition of Inventory

When inventory is material to the financial statements, the auditor shall obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the existence and condition of inventory by:

📦 Four Core Duties during Physical Inventory Count Attendance
  1. 1. Evaluate Management's Instructions & Procedures: Assess whether instructions cover control activities (collection of used count sheets, accounting for unused tags, movement cut-off, estimating liquid/bulk quantities, identification of damaged/slow-moving goods).
  2. 2. Observe Performance of Management's Count Procedures: Observe whether staff follow instructions properly and execute independent dual counts.
  3. 3. Inspect the Inventory: Assist in identifying obsolete, damaged, deteriorating, or aging stock to assess valuation issues (AS 2 / Ind AS 2 NRV write-downs).
  4. 4. Perform Test Counts: Trace items from physical floor to management count sheets (completeness) and from count sheets to physical floor (existence).
⚡ Physical Counting on a Date Other Than Balance Sheet Date

If physical inventory counting is conducted at a date other than the date of the financial statements, the auditor shall, in addition to attending the count, perform audit procedures to obtain evidence about whether changes in inventory between the count date and the date of the financial statements (roll-forward or roll-back period) are properly recorded.

Procedures for Intervening Period: Test sales and purchase cut-offs, verify gross profit percentages, examine goods received and dispatched registers during the intervening period.

⚠️ Attendance at Physical Inventory Count is Impracticable

In some cases, attendance may be impracticable due to factors such as nature and location of inventory (e.g. inventory held in a location posing threats to safety). General inconveniences or cost constraints do not justify impracticability!

Where attendance is impracticable, the auditor shall perform alternative audit procedures (e.g. inspecting documentation of subsequent sales of specific inventory items acquired prior to count). If it is not possible to obtain SAAE via alternative procedures, the auditor shall modify the opinion under SA 705.

🏭 Inventory Under Custody & Control of a Third Party

When inventory in the custody of a third party is material, the auditor shall obtain SAAE by performing one or both of:

  • (a) Request Confirmation: Direct confirmation from the third party regarding quantities and condition of inventory held.
  • (b) Perform Inspection or Other Procedures:
      • Attending, or arranging for another auditor to attend, the third party's physical counting;
      • Obtaining another auditor's report or service auditor's report on adequacy of controls;
      • Inspecting warehouse receipts, consignment agreements, or shipping documents.
    Crucial Rule: Where information is obtained that raises doubt about the integrity and objectivity of the third party (e.g. tax evasion raids, DGGI investigations), confirmation alone is NOT reliable—physical attendance or inspection is mandatory!

2.2 Completeness of Litigation and Claims

The auditor shall design and perform audit procedures to identify litigation and claims involving the entity that may give rise to a risk of material misstatement:

  1. Inquiry of Management: Inquiring of management and, where applicable, in-house legal counsel.
  2. Reviewing Minutes: Reviewing minutes of meetings of Those Charged with Governance and correspondence between entity and external legal counsel.
  3. Reviewing Legal Expense Accounts: Scrutinizing legal and professional expense accounts to identify payments to lawyers and ongoing litigations.
⚖️ Communication with External Legal Counsel (Letter of Inquiry)

If the auditor assesses a risk of material misstatement regarding litigation, the auditor shall seek direct communication with the entity's external legal counsel through a letter of inquiry prepared by management and sent by the auditor.

Reporting Implications: If management refuses permission to communicate with external legal counsel, or the external legal counsel refuses to respond or is prohibited from responding, and the auditor is unable to obtain SAAE by alternative procedures, the auditor shall modify the opinion under SA 705! A written representation from management alone is NOT a sufficient substitute for legal confirmation.

2.3 Presentation & Disclosure of Segment Information

The auditor shall obtain SAAE regarding presentation and disclosure of segment information in accordance with the applicable FRF (AS 17 / Ind AS 108) by:

  • Understanding Methods Used by Management:
    • Sales, transfers, and charges between segments, and elimination of inter-segment amounts;
    • Comparisons with budgets and other expected results (e.g. operating profit as % of sales);
    • The allocation of assets and costs among segments; and
    • Consistency with prior periods and adequacy of disclosures regarding inconsistencies.
  • Performing Analytical Procedures: Or other audit procedures appropriate in the circumstances.

TYU 2 Auditor Inability to Attend Inventory Count on Scheduled Date
▼
Question: An auditor was unable to attend the physical inventory count scheduled by the client on 31st March due to personal unforeseen circumstances. The auditor decided to skip attendance entirely, claiming attendance was impracticable. Evaluate the auditor's decision under SA 501.

1. Legal Position under SA 501:

The auditor's decision is untenable and incorrect. Personal reasons or general scheduling conflicts do not constitute "impracticability".

2. Required Action:

SA 501 explicitly provides that if the auditor is unable to attend physical inventory counting due to unforeseen circumstances, the auditor shall:
(a) Make or observe some physical counts on an alternative date; and
(b) Perform audit procedures on intervening transactions (roll-forward or roll-back procedures) to verify changes between the count date and the balance sheet date.

TYU 3 Direct Unilateral Inquiry to External Legal Counsel
▼
Question: An auditor directly issued a letter to the company's external advocate seeking details of all pending litigations without first making inquiries of management or reviewing legal expense accounts. Evaluate the propriety of this procedure under SA 501.

1. Flawed Sequence of Procedures:

The auditor's approach is not correct. As per SA 501, seeking direct communication with external legal counsel is not a standalone starting procedure.

2. Preliminary Mandatory Steps:

The auditor must first perform preliminary procedures:
  • Inquire of management and in-house legal counsel;
  • Review board and committee minutes; and
  • Review legal and professional expense accounts.
Only when these procedures identify litigations, or when the auditor assesses a risk of material misstatement, does the auditor arrange for a letter of inquiry prepared by management and sent by the auditor to external legal counsel.

TYU 4 Third Party Warehouse Facing DGGI Raids (SA 501)
▼
Question: A company holds material inventory in a third-party warehouse. During the year, the third party was subjected to searches and raids by the Directorate General of GST Intelligence (DGGI) for issuing fake invoices. The auditor merely obtained a written confirmation from the third party and closed the audit file. Evaluate the adequacy of audit evidence under SA 501.

1. SA 501 Mandate on Third Party Integrity:

Under SA 501, where inventory under custody of a third party is material, the auditor normally requests confirmation. However, where information raises doubt about the integrity and objectivity of the third party, confirmation alone is NOT sufficient or appropriate!

2. Required Additional Procedures:

Given the DGGI raids on the warehouse for fake invoicing, the third party's integrity is severely compromised. The auditor must perform other procedures:
• Attending, or arranging for another auditor to attend, physical inventory counting at the third-party warehouse;
• Inspecting warehouse storage agreements and transport delivery records;
• Verifying goods movements subsequent to count.

3. SA 505 "External Confirmations"

SA 505 deals with the auditor's use of external confirmation procedures to obtain audit evidence in accordance with the requirements of SA 330 and SA 500.

✉️ Definition of External Confirmation

"Audit evidence obtained as a direct written response to the auditor from a third party (the confirming party), in paper form, or by electronic or other medium."

External confirmations are frequently used for: bank balances and loan agreements, accounts receivable balances, accounts payable balances, stock held by third parties, property title deeds held by lawyers/bankers, and investments held for safekeeping.

3.1 Positive vs. Negative Confirmation Requests

Confirmation Type ICAI Definition Reliability & Audit Evidence Weight
Positive Confirmation Request A request that the confirming party respond directly to the auditor indicating whether the confirming party agrees or disagrees with the information in the request, or providing the requested information. Highly Reliable audit evidence. A response provides explicit direct verification. If no response is received, it represents a non-response requiring alternative procedures.
Negative Confirmation Request A request that the confirming party respond directly to the auditor ONLY if the confirming party disagrees with the information provided in the request. Less Persuasive evidence. A failure to respond does NOT explicitly verify agreement; the debtor may have simply discarded or overlooked the request!
⚡ Strict 4 Conditions for Using Negative Confirmations as Sole Substantive Procedure

Negative confirmation requests shall NOT be used as the sole substantive procedure unless ALL four of the following conditions are met:

  1. The auditor has assessed the Risk of Material Misstatement as LOW and has obtained sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the operating effectiveness of controls relevant to the assertion;
  2. The population of items subject to negative confirmation procedures comprises a large number of small, homogeneous account balances, transactions, or conditions;
  3. A very low exception rate is expected; and
  4. The auditor is not aware of circumstances or conditions that would cause recipients of negative confirmation requests to disregard such requests.

3.2 Management's Refusal to Allow Confirmation & Reliability Assessment

Step 1
Inquire & Evaluate Reasons

Inquire as to management's reasons for refusal; seek audit evidence as to their validity and reasonableness (e.g. existing legal dispute).

Step 2
Evaluate Fraud & RMM Impact

Evaluate the implications of management's refusal on assessed risks of material misstatement, including fraud risk, and on the NTE of other procedures.

Step 3
Alternative Procedures

Perform alternative audit procedures designed to obtain relevant and reliable audit evidence (e.g. subsequent cash receipts, shipping docs).

Step 4
Reporting under SA 705

If refusal is unreasonable or unable to obtain SAAE $implies$ communicate with TCWG and modify the opinion under SA 705!

TYU 5 Confirmation Responses from Cancelled GST Concerns (SA 505 / SA 500)
▼
Question: An auditor received debtor confirmation responses from 25 concerns. However, audit verification revealed that the GST registrations of these 25 concerns had been cancelled during the year, indicating that businesses at those addresses were closed. Further, the responses were forwarded indirectly to the auditor. How should the auditor proceed?

1. Doubts Over Reliability of Confirmation Responses:

Under SA 505, if the auditor determines that a response to a confirmation request is not reliable, the auditor shall evaluate the implications on the assessment of RMM, including the risk of fraud.

2. Evaluation of Circumstances:

  • The cancellation of GST registrations proves that these commercial entities were non-operational or fictitious.
  • SA 500 states that evidence is less reliable if received indirectly or appears not to come from the intended confirming party.
  • Responses received indirectly from closed concerns indicate fictitious sales, dummy debtors, or fraud.

3. Required Auditor Action:

The auditor cannot rely on these confirmations. He must treat them as unreliable, expand fraud risk procedures under SA 240, verify bank realization vouchers, and perform extensive tests of details on sales invoices and transport dispatch records.

4. SA 510 "Initial Audit Engagements - Opening Balances"

SA 510 applies when conducting an initial audit engagement (prior period not audited, or audited by a predecessor auditor). The auditor's objective is to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence about whether:
  (a) Opening balances contain misstatements that materially affect the current period's financial statements; and
  (b) Appropriate accounting policies reflected in opening balances have been consistently applied in current period, or changes properly accounted and disclosed.

📋 Audit Procedures Regarding Opening Balances
  1. Read the most recent financial statements and predecessor auditor's report (if any).
  2. Determine whether the prior period's closing balances have been correctly brought forward or restated.
  3. Determine whether opening balances reflect the application of appropriate accounting policies.
  4. Perform specific audit procedures:
    • Current Assets & Liabilities: Collections or payments in the current period provide evidence on existence, rights/obligations, completeness, and valuation.
    • Non-Current Assets (Fixed Assets, Investments, Long-Term Debt): Examining records underlying opening balances, inspecting title deeds, or obtaining external confirmations.
⚡ Opening Inventory Procedures & Reporting Impact (Split Opinion)

For inventory, current period procedures on closing inventory provide very little audit evidence regarding opening inventory. The auditor must:
  1. Observe a current physical inventory count and reconcile it back to opening inventory quantities;
  2. Perform audit procedures on the valuation of opening inventory items; and
  3. Perform audit procedures on gross profit and sales/purchase cut-offs.

Dual / Split Opinion Concept: If the auditor is unable to obtain SAAE regarding opening inventory, the auditor may issue a Qualified or Disclaimer of Opinion on the Statement of Profit and Loss and Cash Flows, while expressing an Unmodified (Clean) Opinion on the Balance Sheet (since closing inventory is fully verified)!

TYU 6 Initial Audit Engagement: Opening Inventory Audit Procedures (SA 510)
▼
Question: In an initial audit engagement, the predecessor auditor had not attended physical inventory counting. Explain the procedures the successor auditor must undertake to verify opening inventory.

1. Audit Procedures under SA 510:

As per SA 510, current period audit procedures on closing inventory provide little evidence regarding opening inventory. Therefore, additional procedures are necessary:

  • Roll-Back Reconciliation: Observing a current physical inventory count and reconciling it back to opening inventory quantities.
  • Testing Valuation: Verifying opening inventory unit costs against previous year purchase invoices and testing Lower of Cost and Net Realizable Value.
  • Testing Cut-Off & Gross Margin: Performing analytical procedures on gross profit margins and verifying sales and purchase cut-offs around opening dates.

5. SA 530 "Audit Sampling"

SA 530 applies when the auditor has decided to use audit sampling in performing audit procedures. The objective is to provide a reasonable basis for the auditor to draw conclusions about the population from which the sample is selected.

🎲 5 Sample Selection Methods
  1. 1. Random Selection: Applied through random number generators or tables, giving each sampling unit an equal probability of selection.
  2. 2. Systematic Selection: The number of sampling units in the population is divided by sample size to give a sampling interval ($n$). A random start is chosen, and every $n^{ ext{th}}$ item selected.
  3. 3. Monetary Unit Sampling (MUS): Value-weighted selection in which sample size, selection, and evaluation result in a conclusion in monetary amounts.
  4. 4. Haphazard Selection: The auditor selects the sample without following a structured technique, but avoiding conscious bias or predictability.
  5. 5. Block Selection: Selection of a contiguous block of items. Rarely appropriate in auditing because most populations are structured chronologically or geographically.
⚖️ Sampling Risk Matrix (Type I vs. Type II Errors)

Sampling Risk is the risk that the auditor's conclusion based on a sample may be different from the conclusion if the entire population were subjected to the same audit procedure.

Audit Area Type I Error (Efficiency Risk)
(Auditor does unnecessary work)		 Type II Error (Effectiveness Risk)

TYU 7 Sampling Risk Analysis (SA 530)
▼
Question: An auditor selected 50 invoices out of 5,000 sales invoices and concluded that controls over credit approval were operating effectively. Subsequent investigations by the management revealed widespread unauthorized credit sales. Name and explain the risk illustrated.

1. Risk Identification:

The described risk is Sampling Risk—specifically the Risk of Over-Reliance in Tests of Controls.

2. Consequences:

The auditor concluded that controls were more effective than they actually were. This error affects audit effectiveness and is more serious because it leads the auditor to erroneously reduce substantive procedures, culminating in an inappropriate audit opinion.

6. SA 550 "Related Parties"

Related party relationships and transactions present higher risks of material misstatement due to potential complexity, lack of arm's length terms, dominance, and potential for fraudulent financial reporting.

6.1 Procedures When Undisclosed Related Parties are Identified

If the auditor identifies arrangements or information that suggest the existence of related party relationships or transactions that management has not previously identified or disclosed, the auditor shall:

  1. 1. Communicate within Engagement Team: Promptly communicate the relevant information to other members of the engagement team;
  2. 2. Request Management Identification: Request management to identify all transactions with the newly identified related party;
  3. 3. Inquire on Control Failures: Inquire as to why the entity's controls failed to identify or disclose the related party;
  4. 4. Substantive Procedures: Perform appropriate substantive audit procedures on newly identified transactions;
  5. 5. Reconsider Risk: Reconsider the risk that other undisclosed related parties or significant transactions exist; and
  6. 6. Evaluate Fraud Risk: If non-disclosure appears intentional, evaluate the implications for the audit and management integrity under SA 240.
TYU 8 Living Well Pvt Ltd: Interest-Free Loan of ₹10 Cr to Director Relative
▼
Question: Living Well Private Limited granted an unsecured interest-free loan of ₹10 crores to a partnership firm in which the brother of the Managing Director is a majority partner. The transaction was not disclosed in the draft financial statements. How should the auditor proceed under SA 550?

1. Significant Transaction Outside Normal Course of Business:

Granting an unsecured interest-free loan of ₹10 crores to a director's relative's firm is a significant related party transaction outside the normal course of business.

2. Required Auditor Actions under SA 550:

  • Evaluate Business Rationale: The auditor must seek to understand the business rationale (or lack thereof). An interest-free loan without collateral strongly suggests an intention to siphoning funds or fraudulent financial reporting.
  • Inspect Contracts & Authorization: Verify loan agreements and board/shareholder approvals under Section 185 and 188 of the Companies Act, 2013.
  • Evaluate Intentional Non-Disclosure: Intentional non-disclosure indicates a significant fraud risk. Re-evaluate management integrity.
  • Reporting: Insist on full disclosure under AS 18 / Ind AS 24 and report non-compliance in the Auditor's Report and under CARO 2020 Clause (iv) & (xiii).

🧪 Case Study 1: Chemical Manufacturing Company

• The company imports key raw materials and is exposed to high risk of currency volatility and import regulations.
• Working capital is 60% blocked in inventory and 40% in receivables. High inventory carried due to long import cycles.
• Huge plant and machinery financed through term loans from financial institutions.
• Bought import licenses from the open market; received notices from Customs for using fake licenses.
• Liquid chemicals stored in huge tanks; powdered chemicals in standard drums; coal and sulphur stored in open yards.
• Huge domestic sales on a consignment basis; vast quantities lying with agents.
• Order from National Green Tribunal (NGT) to pay a fine of ₹1.5 crores for emissions.
• Continuous process plant running 24x7; raw materials stored in tanks 2 km from plant transported through pipelines.
• Inventory details prepared by involving a Management's Expert.
• Previous auditor resigned; new auditor appointed.

Case 1 • Q1 Audit Procedures for Existence & Condition of Chemical Inventory (SA 501)
▼
Question: Which audit procedures are required for verifying the existence and condition of the company's complex inventory?

1. Attendance at Physical Counting (SA 501):

  • Liquid Chemicals in Tanks: Evaluate dipping/sounding methods, calibration charts of tanks, temperature and specific gravity adjustments.
  • Powdered Chemicals in Drums: Count sealed drums; perform test opening and chemical sampling to verify contents.
  • Coal & Sulphur in Open Yards: Volumetric surveys, geometric measurement, density estimation by technical experts.
  • Continuous Process WIP: Test laboratory analysis reports on purity and percentage of completion.
  • Consignment Stock with Agents: Obtain direct external confirmations under SA 505 and perform physical inspection of major agent locations.
Case 1 • Q2 Evaluation of Management's Inventory Expert (SA 500)
▼
Question: The company prepared inventory details by involving a management's expert. What must the auditor evaluate?

1. Three Mandatory Inquiries under SA 500:

  • Evaluate the competence, capabilities, and objectivity of the expert (objectivity is impaired if the expert is an employee).
  • Obtain an understanding of the expert's work (dip charts, mathematical formulas for pipeline stock).
  • Evaluate the appropriateness of the work as audit evidence (relevance and reasonableness of assumptions, test accuracy of source data).
Case 1 • Q3 Stocks Lying with Consignment Agents (SA 501)
▼
Question: What procedures must the auditor carry out in respect of stocks lying with consignment agents?

As per SA 501, request direct confirmation from consignment agents regarding quantities and condition. In addition, inspect consignment agreements, reconcile proforma invoices with actual sales returns, inspect documentation of subsequent sales, and conduct physical inspection where quantities are material.

Case 1 • Q4 Litigation Matters: Customs Notices & NGT ₹1.5 Cr Fine (SA 501)
▼
Question: What procedures should the auditor undertake for litigation matters (Customs fake license notices and NGT fine)?
  • Inquire of management and legal counsel regarding status of appeals.
  • Review minutes of the Board of Directors and inspect notices from the Customs Department and the NGT order.
  • Send a letter of direct inquiry to external legal counsel handling the Customs and NGT disputes.
  • Evaluate whether provisions or contingent liabilities are required under AS 29 / Ind AS 37.

  2. Which matter is irrelevant in planning attendance at physical inventory counts? (a) Nature of inventory, (b) Timing of counting, (c) Internal control related to inventory, (d) Whether 100% of inventory is covered in count. [Answer: (d)]

🏢 Case Study 2: Mr. Bharose Lal & Real Estate SPV Fraud Risks

• Mr. Bharose Lal, promoter of a major real estate firm, formed a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) with an overseas investor.
• Foreign partner is completely dependent on Mr. Bharose Lal and has zero knowledge of local Indian laws and municipal approvals.
• Mr. Bharose Lal enjoys an extravagant personal lifestyle, but his personal businesses face a severe liquidity crunch and heavy bank dues.
• He was required to infuse ₹5 crores as equity into the SPV; instead, ₹20 crores of SPV funds were transferred to undisclosed entities under the guise of land development expenses.

Case 2 • Q1 Engagement Team Discussion on Related Party Fraud Risks (SA 550)
▼
Question: Guide the engagement team on the further course of action and team discussion under SA 550 and SA 240.

1. SA 550 / SA 240 Team Discussion:

  • Emphasize professional skepticism regarding transactions with entities controlled by Mr. Bharose Lal.
  • Consider how SPVs might be used to siphon funds or misappropriate cash.
  • Exchange ideas on where financial statements are most susceptible to fraud.
  • Evaluate the risk of management override of controls.
Case 2 • Q2 Fraud Risk Factors Classified under Fraud Triangle (SA 240)
▼
Question: Identify and classify the fraud risk factors in the given case scenario under Incentive/Pressure, Opportunity, and Rationalization.
  • 1. Incentive / Pressure: Severe financial crunch in personal businesses, heavy vendor/bank dues, expensive personal lifestyle, urgent pressure to contribute ₹5 crores equity.
  • 2. Opportunity: Foreign partner's complete dependence and lack of local legal knowledge, dominant personality of Managing Director, complex land aggregation contracts, and lack of oversight.
  • 3. Rationalization: Justifying diversion of SPV funds as "routine business advances" or "land procurement costs".
🎯 Case 2: Multiple Choice Questions & Answer Keys

TYK 1 Coccyx Ltd: Navy Uniforms & Impracticable Physical Counting (SA 501)

TYK 15 Electric Corporation Ltd: Government Related Entities Disclosure under SA 550
▼
Question: Electric Corporation Limited, a Government Company, entered into material transactions with PCDC India Limited, another Government-owned Corporation. Financial statements complied with Ind AS 24. How should CA Chirag analyse this issue?

1. Government-Related Entities Exemption under Ind AS 24 / SA 550:

Under Ind AS 24 / AS 18, a reporting entity is exempt from the detailed disclosure requirements of related party transactions and outstanding balances with:
(a) A government that has control or joint control of, or significant influence over, the reporting entity; and
(b) Another entity that is a related party because the same government has control or joint control of both.

2. Required Disclosures & Audit Verification:

  • The entity must disclose the name of the government and nature of relationships, along with sufficient detail of significant transactions (individually or collectively).
  • CA Chirag must verify that transactions were at arm's length or in accordance with government tariff notifications and that statutory exemption criteria were satisfied. An unmodified opinion is appropriate if proper disclosures are made.
