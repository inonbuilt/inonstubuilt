Chapter 15: Audit of Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs)

1. Constitutional Role of C&AG (Articles 148 to 151)

The Comptroller and Auditor General of India (C&AG) is an independent constitutional authority appointed by the President of India.

Constitutional Article Mandate / Provision
Article 148 Appointment, oath, and conditions of service of C&AG (tenure of 6 years or age 65; removal on same grounds as Supreme Court Judge).
Article 149 Duties and powers of C&AG prescribed by Parliament under the CAG's (DPC) Act, 1971 (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service).
Article 150 Form of accounts of the Union and the States prescribed by President on advice of C&AG.
Article 151 C&AG submits audit reports relating to Union to the President (placed before Parliament) and relating to States to Governors (placed before State Legislatures).

2. Appointment of Auditor & C&AG Audit Mechanism

Government Company Definition (Sec 2(45))

Any company in which not less than 51% of the paid-up share capital is held by the Central Govt, or by any State Govt(s), or partly by Central and partly by State Govt(s), and includes a subsidiary of a Government Company.

Statutory Mechanism Section & Specific Mandate
Appointment of Auditor • Sec 139(7) First Auditor: Appointed by C&AG within 60 days of registration. If C&AG fails, Board appoints in next 30 days, else EGM in 60 days.
• Sec 139(5) Subsequent Auditor: Appointed by C&AG within 180 days from commencement of financial year.
C&AG Directions (Sec 143(5)) C&AG directs statutory auditor on the manner in which accounts shall be audited. Auditor submits report to C&AG including directions compliance.
Supplementary Audit (Sec 143(6)) C&AG has the right to conduct a supplementary audit within 60 days from the date of receipt of statutory audit report. C&AG comments are placed before AGM.
Test Audit (Sec 143(7)) C&AG may, by general or special order, cause a test audit to be conducted on company accounts.

3. Comprehensive Audit & The 3Es Framework

Comprehensive audit evaluates the overall efficiency, operational performance, and public objectives achievement.

Pillar of 3Es Core Audit Question Focus Area
Economy Were inputs acquired at the lowest possible cost without compromising quality? Procurement procedures, tender negotiations, minimizing input costs, timely acquisition.
Efficiency Was the maximum possible output achieved with the minimum input resources? Capacity utilization, plant productivity, reducing downtime, optimizing labor and material usage.
Effectiveness Did the program/activity achieve its intended social and financial objectives? Outcomes vs targets, delivery of public benefits, fulfillment of socio-economic objectives.

4. Propriety Audit & Parliamentary Committees

Propriety Audit Concept

Propriety audit goes beyond mere vouchers and legality to examine whether public expenditure was incurred with wisdom, faithfulness, and economy. It checks: (1) Expenditure is not prima facie more than the occasion demands; (2) Authority does not exercise power to pass an order directly or indirectly to its own advantage; (3) Public funds not utilized for the benefit of a particular person or community unless recognized by law.

Parliamentary Committee Role in Scrutinizing C&AG Reports
Public Accounts Committee (PAC) Examines appropriation accounts and annual financial accounts of Union, and C&AG audit reports on civil/defense expenditures.
Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU) Examines reports and accounts of Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) and C&AG reports on PSUs to evaluate commercial soundness and efficiency.
