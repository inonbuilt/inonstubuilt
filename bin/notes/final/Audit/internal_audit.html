🔍 1. Meaning, Evolution & Definition of Internal Audit
Governance & Assurance

In modern commercial enterprises, operations have become highly complex with massive transaction volumes across multi-locational units, increasing decentralization of operational powers, and deep dependency on enterprise ERPs and IT infrastructure.

💡 Modern Definition of Internal Audit (ICAI & IIA)

"Internal auditing is an independent, objective assurance and consulting activity designed to add value and improve an organization's operations. It helps an organization accomplish its objectives by bringing a systematic, disciplined approach to evaluate and improve the effectiveness of risk management, control, and governance processes."

Evolution of the Internal Audit Mandate in India

Traditional vs. Modern Internal Audit
  • Traditional Role: Routine clerical vouching, verification of mathematical accuracy, post-payment audit of vouchers, and passive policing of accounting entries.
  • Modern Transformative Role: A strategic, Risk-Based Internal Audit (RBIA) function acting as an internal consultant and value-adding partner to the Board and Audit Committee.
  • Focuses on risk governance, operational efficiencies, fraud deterrence, process re-engineering, and IT controls.
Internal Audit Standards Board (IASB) of ICAI

Constituted by the ICAI on February 5, 2004 (originally known as the Committee on Internal Audit).

  • Formulates comprehensive Standards on Internal Audit (SIAs) to harmonize internal audit practices across industries.
  • Issues Technical Guides, Practice Insights, and Guidance Notes on modern risk management, operational audits, and forensic review.
📜 2. Statutory Provisions of Internal Audit under Companies Act, 2013
Section 138 & Rule 13

Under Section 138(1) of the Companies Act, 2013 read with Rule 13 of the Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014, specific classes of companies are statutorily mandated to appoint an internal auditor.

Mandatory Applicability Thresholds Matrix

Class of Company Statutory Threshold Criteria (Preceding Financial Year) Key Exam Observations & Traps
1. Listed Companies Every Listed Company is mandatorily required to appoint an internal auditor. No monetary thresholds! Mandatory regardless of paid-up capital, turnover, or borrowing levels.
2. Unlisted Public Companies Required to appoint an internal auditor if it satisfies ANY ONE of the following four criteria during the preceding financial year:
1. Paid-up Share Capital: ≥ ₹ 50 Crore; OR
2. Turnover: ≥ ₹ 200 Crore; OR
3. Outstanding Loans/Borrowings from banks or public financial institutions: > ₹ 100 Crore (exceeding ₹ 100 Cr) at any point of time during the preceding FY; OR
4. Outstanding Deposits: ≥ ₹ 25 Crore at any point of time during the preceding FY. 		Memory Shortcut: D-T-L-P
• Deposits: ≥ ₹ 25 Cr
• Turnover: ≥ ₹ 200 Cr
• Loans: > ₹ 100 Cr (Notice: strictly exceeding ₹ 100 Cr, exactly ₹ 100 Cr does not trigger!)
• Paid-up Capital: ≥ ₹ 50 Cr
Loans and Deposits are tested "at any point of time" during preceding year!
3. Private Companies Required to appoint an internal auditor if it satisfies EITHER of the following two criteria during the preceding financial year:
1. Turnover: ≥ ₹ 200 Crore; OR
2. Outstanding Loans/Borrowings from banks or public financial institutions: > ₹ 100 Crore (exceeding ₹ 100 Cr) at any point of time during preceding FY. 		Private companies are exempt from Paid-up Share Capital and Deposit thresholds! Only Turnover (≥ 200 Cr) and Borrowings (> 100 Cr) apply.
⚠️ Critical Statutory Wordings & Exam Traps
  • Preceding Financial Year: Applicability for the current financial year (e.g., FY 2025-26) is determined strictly on the figures of the preceding financial year (FY 2024-25)! Current year expansions or contractions do not alter applicability for the current year.
  • Loans > ₹ 100 Cr: The statute states "exceeding one hundred crore rupees". If outstanding loans from banks/PFIs touch exactly ₹ 100.00 Crore, Section 138 is NOT triggered! It must be at least ₹ 100.01 Crore.
  • At Any Point of Time: For Loans/Borrowings and Public Deposits, the condition is satisfied if the threshold is breached at any point of time during the preceding financial year, even if repaid or reduced before March 31st!

Who Can Be Appointed as Internal Auditor?

According to Section 138(1):

  • A Chartered Accountant (whether engaged in practice or not); OR
  • A Cost Accountant; OR
  • Such other professional as may be decided by the Board of Directors.
  • Status: The internal auditor may or may not be an employee of the company! (An in-house employee with professional credentials or an outside professional firm can be appointed).

Formulation of Scope, Functioning & Methodology (Rule 13(2))

Under Rule 13(2) of Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014:

"The Audit Committee of the company or the Board shall, in consultation with the Internal Auditor, formulate the scope, functioning, periodicity and methodology for conducting the internal audit."

⚙️ 3. Management Functions & 7 Core Scope Areas of Internal Auditing
Systemic Operational Oversight

Management is the process by which an enterprise conducts its affairs to achieve stated goals through optimum resource utilization within legal, social, economic, and environmental constraints. The internal auditor independently appraises management controls across seven core functional areas:

(i) Review of Internal Controls & Procedures

Assessing the design and operating effectiveness of the internal control environment to reduce inherent risk to an acceptable residual level.

  • 3-Way Matching Control: Automated or manual matching of Purchase Order (PO), Goods Receipt Note (GRN), and Vendor Invoice before payment sanction to prevent over-invoicing or duplicate disbursements.
  • Segregation of Duties: Clear segregation between authorization, custody of assets, transaction recording, and periodic reconciliation.
  • Credit Control: Independent evaluation of credit limits and customer aging prior to goods dispatch.
(ii) Review of Custodial Controls & Assets

Ensuring physical and digital safeguarding of enterprise assets against theft, misappropriation, damage, or unauthorized deployment:

  • Physical verification of property, plant, equipment, and inventories at regular intervals.
  • Review of insurance coverage adequacy against fire, transit, and business interruption risks.
  • Verification of title deeds, lease agreements, and intellectual property registrations.
(iii) Review of Statutory Compliances

Reviewing systems established by management to track, record, and satisfy complex legal, tax, and environmental obligations:

  • Timely deposit of statutory dues (GST, TDS, Provident Fund, ESI, Custom duties).
  • Adherence to factory safety rules, labor codes, and pollution control boards norms.
  • Ensuring proper compliance tracking software without internal auditor assuming executive liability!
(iv) Operational Efficiency & Resource Utilization

Evaluating whether enterprise resources are deployed economically and efficiently:

  • Comparing actual resource consumption with scientific operating standards and norms.
  • Identifying Idle Assets: Underutilized plant & machinery, unoccupied warehouse space, surplus cash balances, or idle workforce.
  • Scrutinizing job descriptions to eliminate non-productive, redundant processes.
(v) Review of Accomplishment of Goals & Objectives

Examining whether enterprise objectives are clearly stated, attainable, and quantified in monetary and non-monetary metrics:

  • Reviewing budgetary control mechanisms to detect revenue variances early.
  • Ensuring plans anticipate potential supply chain bottlenecks or competitive disruptions.
  • Evaluating flexibility in operating plans to adapt to changing market dynamics.
(vi) Accounting Functions & Financial Records

Assisting management in ensuring reliability, integrity, and compliance of accounting records:

  • Review of automated data interfaces between operational modules and general ledger.
  • Detection of unauthorized manual journals, post-closing entries, and suspense balances.
  • Verification of bank reconciliations, inventory valuations, and inter-branch balances.
💡 (vii) Advisory Inputs on Governance, Risk Management & Ethics

Beyond historical assurance, modern internal auditors provide forward-looking advisory inputs on Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) frameworks, business process automation, ethics codes, whistle-blower mechanisms, and business continuity planning (BCP) – provided they do not assume executive decision-making responsibilities!

⚖️ 4. Integrity, Objectivity & Independence of the Internal Auditor
Core Ethical Pillar

The internal auditor must remain free from any undue pressures, threats, or executive influences that could compromise his objectivity, impartiality, and allegiance to the truth. Independence is required both in mind and in appearance.

The Ideal Dual Reporting Structure

To ensure structural and operational independence, the reporting relationships of the Chief Internal Auditor must be bifurcated into Functional and Administrative channels:

1. Functional Reporting → Audit Committee / Board

The internal auditor must report functionally to the Audit Committee (or Board of Directors). This includes:

  • Approval of the Internal Audit Charter and Risk-Based Annual Audit Plan.
  • Appointment, evaluation, compensation, and removal of the Chief Internal Auditor.
  • Direct submission of internal audit reports, critical findings, and executive summaries.
  • Holding periodic private executive sessions without management presence.
2. Administrative Reporting → CEO / MD

For day-to-day administrative coordination, the internal auditor reports to the Chief Executive Officer (CEO) or Managing Director:

  • Human resource administration, leave approvals, and operational budgeting.
  • Internal departmental communications and facilities support.
  • FATAL TRAP: Never Report to the CFO! Reporting to the Chief Financial Officer (CFO) completely destroys independence, as the Finance and Accounts functions headed by the CFO are themselves subject to internal audit review!

Safeguarding Objectivity & Managing Conflicts of Interest

🚫 Prohibitions on Operational & Management Roles
  • No Operational Responsibilities: The internal auditor must not assume executive accountability for designing, installing, or operating internal control systems, accounting systems, or process re-engineering. Assuming operational duties creates an irrecoverable self-review threat!
  • Cooling-off Period for Past Operations: The internal auditor should not audit any operational area or department for which he was previously responsible within a preceding period (recommended at least one year).
  • Refusal of Conflicting Assignments: If management requests the internal auditor to manage day-to-day compliance tracking or negotiate with tax/statutory regulators, the auditor must politely decline, as direct regulatory representation is an executive management function.
🧠 5. Interactive Test Your Understanding 1 & 2 (Practical Case Studies)
Case Challenges
📝 TYU 1: Appointment of Retired IA&AS Officer as Chief Internal Auditor (Parteek's Case) −

Scenario:

After an illustrious career of 25 years in the Indian Audit & Accounts Service (IA&AS), Parteek, a post-graduate in law, took voluntary retirement from government service. Attracted by compensation packages in the corporate sector, he applied for the position of Chief Internal Auditor in a leading listed company engaged in oil refining. The Board of Directors is very keen to appoint him due to his extensive audit credentials and leadership experience.

Can Parteek be appointed as the Chief Internal Auditor of this listed company under the Companies Act, 2013?

✅ ICAI Official Solution & Statutory Analysis

1. Statutory Legal Provisions:

  • Under Section 138(1) of the Companies Act, 2013, an internal auditor shall either be:
    1. A Chartered Accountant (whether in practice or not); OR
    2. A Cost Accountant; OR
    3. Such other professional as may be decided by the Board.
  • The section further provides that the internal auditor may or may not be an employee of the company.

2. Evaluation of Parteek's Eligibility:

  • Although Parteek is neither a Chartered Accountant nor a Cost Accountant, he possesses a post-graduate qualification in law and over 25 years of specialized audit experience in the Supreme Audit Institution (IA&AS).
  • The third category under Section 138 explicitly grants discretionary statutory authority to the Board of Directors to appoint "such other professional as may be decided by the Board".
  • Conclusion: If the Board of Directors evaluates his qualifications and decides that his 25 years of audit experience and law background qualify him as a competent professional for the organization, Parteek can validly be appointed as the Chief Internal Auditor of the listed company!
📝 TYU 2: Internal Auditor Managing SEBI Compliance Tracking System (CA Deva's Case) −

Scenario:

CA Deva is the internal auditor of a listed company. To ensure continuous compliance with SEBI LODR Regulations, the company management asks CA Deva to actively manage its compliance tracking software system, including directly corresponding with SEBI in this regard. The scope of internal audit agreed upon at the time of appointment included "review of compliance with laws and regulations."

Can CA Deva perform such activities in his capacity as the internal auditor of the company?

✅ ICAI Official Solution & Governance Analysis

1. Independent Assurance vs. Executive Management Duties:

  • While reviewing whether the company has an effective framework to monitor statutory compliances falls squarely within the scope of internal audit, actively managing the compliance tracking system and directly corresponding with regulators is an operational management responsibility!
  • An internal auditor provides independent, objective assurance on the design and operating effectiveness of controls. He cannot step into the shoes of management.

2. Impairment of Independence & Self-Review Threat:

  • If CA Deva manages the compliance tracking system and interacts directly with SEBI, he becomes an operational participant in the executive compliance process.
  • When subsequently auditing statutory compliance, he would be placed in the untenable position of auditing his own operational actions and communications (severe self-review threat).
  • Conclusion: CA Deva CANNOT perform such executive activities in his capacity as internal auditor. He must advise the company to assign compliance tracking and regulatory correspondence to the Company Secretary or Compliance Officer, while he retains the independent role of reviewing the effectiveness of such tracking mechanisms.
🌟 6. Qualities & Specialised Competencies of the Internal Auditor
Professional Toolkit

To perform diverse operational and governance appraisals effectively, an internal auditor must possess a multi-disciplinary blend of technical expertise and interpersonal leadership:

Technical Expertise
  • Financial & Accounting Competence: Mastery over Ind AS / AS, financial statement analysis, and management accounting controls.
  • Operational Controls Knowledge: In-depth understanding of engineering, production, logistics, procurement, and commercial trade practices.
  • Commercial Laws & Taxation: Thorough working knowledge of Companies Act, GST, Direct Taxes, SEBI LODR, labor laws, and environmental codes.
  • IT Systems & ERP Proficiency: Practical understanding of enterprise ERPs (SAP, Oracle), automated controls, database queries, and cybersecurity basics.
Behavioral & Professional Qualities
  • Professional Skepticism & Due Care: A questioning mind that critically verifies underlying evidence rather than taking management statements at face value.
  • Communication & Soft Skills: Persuasive presentation skills, diplomatic tact in conducting interviews, and clarity in drafting actionable audit findings.
  • Management Principles: Understanding organizational structures, delegation matrix, and human behavior dynamics.
  • Strict Confidentiality: Absolute assurance to management and TCWG that confidential trade secrets, strategic data, and pricing formulas will remain secure.
🔄 7. Performing the Internal Audit Engagement (Lifecycle & Steps)
Methodology & Execution

A systematic, disciplined internal audit engagement progresses through a rigorous four-phase lifecycle:

Phase Core Operational Activities Key Documentation Output
Phase 1: Planning & Scoping • Define Audit Universe (all auditable entities/processes).
• Conduct risk assessment to prioritize high-risk processes.
• Formulate the Risk-Based Internal Audit (RBIA) Plan.
• Issue engagement notification and conduct Entry Conference with process owners. 		• Internal Audit Charter.
• Approved Annual Audit Plan.
• Process Understanding Document (PUD).
Phase 2: Fieldwork & Testing • Conduct end-to-end process walkthroughs.
• Test design and operating effectiveness of key controls (Test of Controls).
• Perform substantive analytical procedures, sample testing, and data analytics on full populations.
• Identify control exceptions, operational bottlenecks, and compliance deviations. 		• Risk and Control Matrix (RCM).
• Detailed Audit Programs & Working Papers.
• Exception Sheets with data evidence.
Phase 3: Root Cause & Exit Meeting • Perform Root Cause Analysis (RCA) on identified gaps (asking "Why" to trace human, systemic, or policy origin).
• Quantify business impact (financial exposure, operational delay).
• Conduct formal Exit Conference with auditee management to discuss draft findings and obtain committed management responses. 		• Draft Audit Findings Sheet.
• Minutes of Exit Conference.
• Management Response Sheets with agreed target dates and action owners.
Phase 4: Reporting & Follow-up • Issue final formal Internal Audit Report to Audit Committee and MD/CEO.
• Track remediation progress through a systematic Follow-up Tracking Matrix until all agreed action plans are implemented. 		• Final Internal Audit Report.
• Quarterly Audit Committee Dashboards.
• Issue Closure Validation Reports.
📑 8. The Internal Audit Report & The 5Cs Observation Model
High-Impact Reporting

The Internal Audit Report is the primary tangible product of the internal audit engagement. It is addressed primarily to the Audit Committee and Top Management, serving as an authoritative basis for corrective action and governance enhancement.

Essential Characteristics of an Outstanding Internal Audit Report

1. Objectivity & Fairness

Factual, balanced, and free from bias. Acknowledges satisfactory performance while objectively presenting gaps.

2. Clarity & Conciseness

Uses plain, precise business language avoiding unnecessary audit jargon. Highlights executive takeaways upfront.

3. Action-Oriented Tone

Constructive rather than fault-finding. Offers practical, cost-effective solutions to mitigate identified residual risks.

The Standard 5Cs Model for Structuring Audit Observations

Under modern internal auditing standards (SIA 300 series), every audit observation should be structured using the 5Cs Framework:

The 5C Element Analytical Definition Illustrative Case Example (Procurement)
1. Condition "What is?" – The factual situation or deficiency observed during audit testing. 35 purchase orders totaling ₹ 4.2 Crore were issued without obtaining the mandatory 3 competitive vendor quotations.
2. Criteria "What should be?" – The governing benchmark, policy, rule, standard, or expectation. Company Procurement Policy Clause 4.2 mandates minimum 3 competitive bids for all purchases exceeding ₹ 5 Lakhs.
3. Cause "Why did it happen?" – The underlying root cause, lack of training, or control breakdown. Procurement module in ERP allowed manual bypass of the mandatory quotation attachment validation due to poor IT configuration.
4. Consequence / Effect "What is the impact?" – The actual or potential risk, financial loss, or operational exposure. Risk of non-competitive price discovery, vendor favoritism, and potential financial loss estimated at ₹ 38 Lakhs.
5. Corrective Action "How to fix it?" – Concrete remediation plan with assigned ownership and target date. Recommendation: Re-configure ERP to enforce mandatory 3-quotation validation before PO generation.
Management Response: ERP patch deployed by IT Head; target completion 15th May 2026.
👥 9. Relationship Between Internal and External Auditors
Role Comparison & Synergy

While both internal and external auditors use similar testing techniques, their roles, objectives, reporting lines, and statutory mandates are fundamentally distinct:

Basis for Comparison Internal Audit External (Statutory) Audit
1. Performed By An internal auditing department within the organization or an outsourced professional firm. An independent Chartered Accountant in practice, completely outside and separate from the entity.
2. Scope & Examination Examines design and operating adequacy of operational controls, risk management, and governance systems. Examines accuracy, validity, and regulatory compliance of the Financial Statements.
3. Appointment Authority Appointed by the Audit Committee or Board of Directors under Section 138. Appointed by the Shareholders (Members) in AGM under Section 139 (or C&AG for PSUs).
4. Primary Users of Report Used primarily by Top Management, Board of Directors, and Audit Committee. Used by Shareholders, Lenders, Investors, Regulators, and Public.
5. Reporting Objective Identifies deficiencies in internal controls and evaluates operational efficiency (constructive guidance). Expresses an independent statutory opinion on the True and Fair view of financial statements.
6. Employment Status Can be an employee of the company or an external professional firm. Mandatorily NOT an employee of the company (independent practitioner).
🔍 10. Evaluation of the Internal Audit Function by External Auditor (SA 610 Revised)
Using the Work of Internal Auditors

Under SA 610 (Revised) – "Using the Work of Internal Auditors", the external statutory auditor may consider using the work of the internal audit function to modify the nature, timing, or extent of statutory audit procedures. However, before relying on such work, the external auditor must determine whether the internal audit function is adequate for the purposes of the statutory audit.

The Three Foundational Criteria to Evaluate (SA 610)

1. Objectivity of the Function

The external auditor evaluates:

  • Organizational status of internal audit and whether it reports directly to the Audit Committee.
  • Whether the internal auditor is free from conflicting operational responsibilities.
  • Whether TCWG oversees employment, compensation, and removal decisions of the Chief Internal Auditor.
2. Level of Competence

The external auditor evaluates:

  • Professional qualifications (CA, CMA, CIA) and industry experience of internal audit staff.
  • Adequacy of technical resources, data analytics tools, and staffing strength.
  • Existence of ongoing training and Continuing Professional Development (CPD) programs.
3. Systematic & Disciplined Approach

The external auditor evaluates:

  • Existence and adequacy of documented audit plans, programs, and standardized working papers.
  • Adherence to Standards on Internal Audit (SIAs) issued by ICAI.
  • Implementation of an internal Quality Assessment and Improvement Program (QAIP).
⚠️ Non-Reliance Rule

If the external auditor determines that the internal audit function lacks sufficient objectivity, technical competence, or a systematic approach, the external auditor shall NOT use the work of the internal audit function in any area of the statutory audit!

🤝 11. Using Direct Assistance of Internal Auditors under SA 610 (Revised)
Direct Supervision & Prohibited Traps

Direct Assistance refers to the use of internal auditors to perform audit procedures under the direction, supervision, and review of the external statutory auditor.

Mandatory Pre-Conditions & Written Agreements Prior to Use

Prior to using internal auditors to provide direct assistance, the external auditor must:

  1. Evaluate the existence and significance of threats to the objectivity and the level of competence of the specific internal auditors who will be providing direct assistance.
  2. Obtain written agreement from an authorized representative of the entity confirming that the internal auditors will be allowed to follow the external auditor's instructions, and that the entity will not intervene in the work the internal auditor performs for the external auditor.
  3. Obtain written agreement from the internal auditors that they will keep confidential specific matters as instructed by the external auditor and will inform the external auditor of any threat to their objectivity.

Prohibited Areas for Direct Assistance (SA 610 Red Lines)

🚫 Absolute Prohibitions: External Auditor CANNOT Use Direct Assistance In:
  1. Procedures that involve making significant judgments in the audit:
    • Assessing the risks of material misstatement (ROMM).
    • Evaluating the appropriateness of management's use of the Going Concern assumption.
    • Evaluating significant accounting estimates (impairment, provisions, fair values).
    • Evaluating the adequacy of financial statement disclosures.
  2. Procedures relating to higher assessed risks of material misstatement where the judgment required in performing relevant procedures is more than limited.
  3. Procedures relating to activities in which the internal auditors have been involved (e.g., auditing an operational system they helped design).
  4. Decisions on external confirmation requests and evaluating the results of external confirmation procedures!

Mandatory Audit Documentation under SA 610 & SA 230

When direct assistance is used, the statutory auditor must document in the statutory audit file:

  • The evaluation of the internal auditors' objectivity and competence.
  • The basis for the decision regarding the nature and extent of work performed.
  • The signed written agreements from the entity and the internal auditors.
  • THE AUDIT WORKING PAPERS TRAP: The working papers prepared by the internal auditors providing direct assistance MUST be included in the statutory auditor's working papers file! The external auditor cannot exclude them, because the external auditor assumes full sole responsibility for the audit opinion expressed!
🚀 12. Internal Audit as a Strategic Management Function
Value Creation & Catalyst

Modern internal auditing transcends routine voucher checking to serve as an indispensable management instrument for good corporate governance:

  • Catalyst for Operational Improvement: Identifies unutilized production capacities, bottlenecks in supply chains, and opportunities for cost reduction without compromising quality.
  • Proactive Risk Advisory: Evaluates emerging enterprise risks (cyber threats, geopolitical disruptions, regulatory changes) before they materialize into financial losses.
  • Fraud Deterrence & Detection: Analyzes transactional anomalies, red flags, and segregation of duties breakdowns, creating a robust fraud deterrent environment.
  • Strengthening Governance Architecture: Serves as the eyes and ears of the independent directors and Audit Committee, providing unvarnished insights into operational realities.
🛡️ 13. Audit Trail (Edit Log) & Rule 11(g) Statutory Compliance
Digital Transparency Mandate

To eliminate corporate data manipulation, clandestine back-dated entries, and unauthorized alterations in books of account, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) introduced mandatory statutory provisions concerning Audit Trail (Edit Log):

Statutory Reference Regulatory Requirement Auditor & Management Duties
Proviso to Rule 3(1)
Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014		 Every company which uses accounting software for maintaining its books of account shall use only such accounting software which has a feature of recording Audit Trail of each and every transaction, creating an edit log of each change made in books of account along with the date when such changes were made, and ensuring that the audit trail cannot be disabled. Primary Management Duty: Ensure accounting software has audit trail enabled at database and application level continuously throughout the year without disabling.
Rule 11(g)
Companies (Audit & Auditors) Rules, 2014		 The statutory auditor must state in his audit report whether:
1. Company used accounting software with audit trail (edit log) feature enabled throughout the year.
2. The audit trail feature has operated throughout the year for all transactions recorded in software.
3. The audit trail feature has not been tampered with.
4. The audit trail has been preserved by the company as per statutory requirements for record retention (min 8 years under Sec 128(5)). 		Statutory Reporting Duty: Test whether audit trail was enabled, evaluate access controls over edit logs, verify preservation, and issue a modified report if audit trail was disabled at any point!

Key Internal Controls Required to Demonstrate Audit Trail Functionality

Technical Controls
  • Continuous Logging: Audit trail feature permanently enabled at both application software and back-end database level.
  • Tamper-Evident Storage: Edit logs stored in read-only tables or immutable media where even database administrators (DBAs) cannot delete or alter logs.
  • Time Synchronization: System clocks synchronized across all servers with an authoritative Network Time Protocol (NTP).
Access & Procedural Controls
  • No Shared User IDs: Unique user ID assigned to each employee; strict prohibition on generic or shared logins.
  • Privileged Access Management: Direct access to back-end database strictly restricted, authenticated via multi-factor authentication (MFA), and logged.
  • Periodic Review of Logs: Management routinely reviews audit trail exception logs for unauthorized configuration changes.
🧠 14. Interactive Test Your Understanding 3 (Audit Trail Implementation Review)
Digital Control Audit
📝 TYU 3: Reviewing Audit Trail Feature in Accounting Software −

Scenario:

During the audit of a manufacturing company, the auditor notices that the company uses customized ERP software. Although the edit log feature exists in the software, it can be switched off by the system administrator during month-end batch processing to "improve processing speed." Furthermore, certain user IDs in the accounts department are shared among temporary data entry clerks.

Evaluate the statutory implications under Rule 3(1) of Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014 and Rule 11(g) of Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014.

✅ ICAI Official Solution & Statutory Analysis

1. Violation of Rule 3(1) of Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014:

  • The law explicitly mandates that the accounting software must ensure that the audit trail cannot be disabled.
  • Allowing the system administrator to switch off the audit trail feature during month-end processing violates this statutory requirement. Even if disabled for a few hours for "performance speed," the continuity of the audit trail is broken, creating an opportunity for unauthorized or unrecorded alterations.

2. Breakdown of User Identification Control:

  • The edit log must record who made the change along with date and time. Sharing user IDs makes it impossible to establish individual accountability for changes, defeating the core purpose of an audit trail.

3. Reporting Implication under Rule 11(g):

  • The statutory auditor cannot confirm that the audit trail operated continuously throughout the year without being disabled.
  • The auditor is required to modify his audit report under Rule 11(g), clearly disclosing that the audit trail feature was capable of being disabled, was switched off during batch processing, and that shared user IDs compromised log attribution!

SIA Series Classification Domain Core Subject Matter Covered

📝 16. Test Your Knowledge (TYK Questions 1 to 8)
Statutory & Technical Applications
Q1: Short Note on Internal Audit Report +

Question: Write a short note on the Internal Audit Report.

✅ ICAI Solution

The Internal Audit Report is the primary tangible output of the internal audit engagement, communicating findings and recommendations to the Audit Committee and Top Management.

Key Characteristics & Structure:

  • Objectivity & Tone: Factual, unbiased, and constructive. Identifies control deficiencies while acknowledging positive management initiatives.
  • Standard 5Cs Observation Structure: Every observation details: (1) Condition (factual state); (2) Criteria (benchmark/policy); (3) Cause (underlying root reason); (4) Consequence/Effect (financial or operational risk); and (5) Corrective Action Plan (management response, target date, action owner).
  • Executive Summary: Summarizes critical high-risk observations, systemic themes, and remediation status for swift Board-level review.
  • Follow-up Action: Mandates systematic follow-up tracking to verify whether agreed management actions were implemented.
Q2: Important Aspects Considered by External Auditor in Evaluating Internal Audit Function +

Question: State the important aspects to be considered by the External auditor in the evaluation of the Internal Audit Function under SA 610 (Revised).

✅ ICAI Solution

Under SA 610 (Revised), before using the work of the internal audit function, the external auditor must evaluate three core aspects:

  1. Objectivity of the Internal Audit Function: Whether the function reports directly to TCWG (Audit Committee); whether internal auditors are free from conflicting operational duties; whether management places undue scope restrictions.
  2. Level of Competence: Professional qualifications (CA, CMA, CIA), hiring policies, continuing professional education (CPE), and technical resources available to the internal audit team.
  3. Systematic and Disciplined Approach: Whether internal audit applies documented audit methodologies, standardized risk-based programs, quality control policies, and complies with Standards on Internal Audit (SIAs).
Q3: AB Pvt. Ltd. Borrowings Exceeding ₹ 100 Crore (Applicability & Appointee) +

Question: AB Pvt. Ltd. company has outstanding loans or borrowings from banks exceeding one hundred crore rupees. It wants to appoint an internal auditor. Please guide the company regarding applicability, who can be appointed, and what work would be reviewed.

✅ ICAI Solution

1. Statutory Applicability under Section 138:

  • Under Rule 13 of Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014, every private company having outstanding loans or borrowings from banks or public financial institutions exceeding ₹ 100 Crore at any point of time during the preceding financial year is mandatorily required to appoint an internal auditor.
  • Since AB Pvt. Ltd.'s borrowings exceed ₹ 100 Crore, Section 138 is applicable.

2. Who Can Be Appointed:

  • A Chartered Accountant (in practice or not); OR a Cost Accountant; OR such other professional as decided by the Board. May or may not be an employee of the company.

3. Work to Be Reviewed (Scope formulated under Rule 13(2)):

  • Review of internal controls, loan utilization, debt-servicing capability, safeguarding of assets, compliance with bank covenants, and operational efficiency.
Q4: Factors Weighing Considerations in Using Direct Assistance (Moon Ltd) +

Question: Moon Ltd., of which you are the Statutory Auditor, has an internal audit being conducted by an outside agency. State the factors that weigh considerations in opting to make use of direct assistance of the internal auditors for the purpose of statutory audit.

✅ ICAI Solution

Under SA 610 (Revised), in deciding whether to use internal auditors to provide direct assistance, the external auditor considers:

  • Amount of Judgment Involved: Direct assistance cannot be used in planning procedures or testing where significant judgment is required.
  • Assessed Risk of Material Misstatement: Higher risk areas require external auditor's direct personal performance.
  • Objectivity and Competence: The significance of threats to objectivity and technical skills of the specific internal audit personnel.
  • Prior Written Agreements: Must obtain written agreements from the entity (non-intervention) and internal auditors (confidentiality & objectivity).
  • Prohibitions: Direct assistance is strictly barred in significant estimates, going concern, risk assessment, and external confirmations.
Q5: Direct Assistance in Accounting Estimates & External Confirmations (XYZ Ltd) +

Question: Mr. A is appointed as statutory auditor of XYZ Ltd. XYZ Ltd. appointed Mr. B as internal auditor under Section 138. External auditor Mr. A asked internal auditor Mr. B to provide direct assistance regarding:
(a) Evaluating significant accounting estimates by management and assessing risk of material misstatements.
(b) External confirmation requests and evaluation of confirmation results.

✅ ICAI Solution

(a) Evaluating Accounting Estimates & Risk Assessment:

  • Under SA 610 (Revised), the external auditor SHALL NOT use internal auditors to provide direct assistance to perform procedures that involve making significant judgments in the audit.
  • Assessing the risks of material misstatement and evaluating significant accounting estimates are core areas of professional auditor judgment. Therefore, Mr. A cannot ask Mr. B for direct assistance in these areas.

(b) External Confirmations & Evaluation:

  • SA 610 explicitly prohibits using internal auditors to provide direct assistance with respect to decisions on external confirmation requests and evaluating the results of external confirmation procedures!
  • Therefore, Mr. A cannot use Mr. B for direct assistance in confirmation procedures. The answer remains that such direct assistance is strictly prohibited.
Q6: Ideal Reporting Structure of Chief Internal Auditor (XYZ Ltd) +

Question: XYZ Ltd. has to appoint Mr. A as Chief Internal Auditor. The Managing Director asked the HR head to define the reporting structure so that Mr. A can discharge his duties objectively. Suggest the ideal reporting structure.

✅ ICAI Solution

The HR head should propose a Dual Reporting Framework:

  1. Functional Reporting → Audit Committee / Board of Directors:
    • Approval of internal audit charter and annual risk-based audit plan.
    • Approval of Chief Internal Auditor's appointment, appraisal, and remuneration.
    • Direct reporting of all audit findings, internal control deficiencies, and investigation results.
    • Periodic executive sessions between Audit Committee and Chief Internal Auditor without management.
  2. Administrative Reporting → Managing Director / CEO:
    • Day-to-day administrative matters, operational budget approvals, and routine communications.
  3. Crucial Negative Directive: The Chief Internal Auditor must NEVER report to the CFO or Finance Director, as doing so introduces a severe conflict of interest and compromises independence over financial auditing.
Q7: Expense Analysis & Gap Identification Steps (Zero Ltd / Mr. Shubh) +

Question: Zero Ltd. appointed Mr. Shubh as internal auditor. The Audit Committee requested him to perform a detailed analysis of previous year expenses and report on all risks and underlying gaps. Elucidate the steps the internal auditor should take.

✅ ICAI Solution

Mr. Shubh should adopt a systematic risk-based analytical methodology:

  1. Data Extraction & Stratification: Extract the entire expense general ledger and categorize expenses by head, department, and vendor volume.
  2. Budget vs. Actual Variance Analysis: Compare actual expenses with approved budgets to flag significant cost overruns and unexplained variations.
  3. Test of 3-Way Matching & Delegation of Financial Powers (DoFP): Verify that purchase orders, delivery challans/GRNs, and invoices match, and that approvals adhered strictly to authorized spending limits.
  4. Analysis of Non-Routine & High-Risk Expenses: Scrutinize legal expenses, consultancy fees, travel expenses, promotional spends, and round-sum disbursements.
  5. Vendor Concentration & Related Party Testing: Identify unusual vendor concentrations, split purchase orders designed to evade tender limits, and payments to related parties.
  6. Root Cause Identification & 5Cs Reporting: Formulate findings identifying root causes (lack of ERP validation, missing review controls) and provide actionable remediation plans.
Q8: Section 138 Applicability Evaluation for Group Companies +

Question: Evaluate the applicability of internal audit under Section 138 of the Companies Act, 2013 for the following group companies based on previous year data (₹ in Crore):
• XYX Limited (Listed): Capital 100, Turnover 230, Loans 20, Deposits 48.
• MNM Limited (Unlisted Public): Capital 60, Turnover 100, Loans 50, Deposits 24.
• GFG Limited (Unlisted Private): Capital 70, Turnover 180, Loans 80, Deposits Nil.

✅ ICAI Solution

1. XYX Limited (Listed Company):

  • Under Section 138 read with Rule 13, internal audit is MANDATORY for every listed company without any monetary threshold. Hence, XYX Ltd must appoint an internal auditor.

2. MNM Limited (Unlisted Public Company):

  • Criteria: Capital ≥ 50 Cr; OR Turnover ≥ 200 Cr; OR Loans > 100 Cr; OR Deposits ≥ 25 Cr.
  • MNM Ltd has Paid-up Capital of ₹ 60 Crore, which exceeds the threshold of ₹ 50 Crore!
  • Since meeting any ONE criterion triggers applicability, internal audit is MANDATORY for MNM Limited.

3. GFG Limited (Unlisted Private Company):

  • Criteria: Turnover ≥ 200 Cr; OR Loans > 100 Cr. (Capital and Deposits thresholds do NOT apply to private companies!).
  • GFG Ltd has Turnover of ₹ 180 Cr (below ₹ 200 Cr) and Loans of ₹ 80 Cr (below ₹ 100 Cr). Its Capital of ₹ 70 Cr is irrelevant for private companies.
  • Hence, internal audit is NOT APPLICABLE to GFG Limited!

Q10: Direct Assistance for Newly Assessed High Risk Assertions (First and Last Ltd) +

Question: Statutory auditor CA K designed substantive procedures for assertions not examined in previous years due to higher assessed risk of material misstatement. A team member decides to engage internal auditor Mr. X for direct assistance. Comment under SA 610.

✅ ICAI Solution

Under SA 610 (Revised):

  • Direct assistance cannot be used for procedures relating to higher assessed risks of material misstatement where the judgment required in performing the relevant procedures is more than limited.
  • Because these assertions involve higher assessed risks and were newly targeted in response to identified vulnerabilities, delegating substantive testing on these assertions to internal auditors is inappropriate.
  • CA K must perform these procedures directly or through his own statutory engagement team members.
Q11: Purpose & Importance of Audit Trail (Edit Log) under Rule 3(1) & Rule 11(g) +

Question: Explain the meaning, purpose, and statutory importance of an Audit Trail (Edit Log) in a computerized accounting system.

✅ ICAI Solution

An Audit Trail (Edit Log) is a chronological, step-by-step digital record providing documentary evidence of the sequence of activities that have affected a specific transaction or data element in accounting software.

Statutory Purpose & Importance:

  • Tracking Modifications: Records who made the entry, date and exact time of creation, subsequent alterations, and deletion attempts.
  • Fraud Deterrence: Prevents unauthorized back-dated journal vouchers, suppression of sales, and manipulation of ledger balances.
  • Data Integrity: Ensures financial statements are based on transparent, immutable accounting records.
  • Mandatory Preservation: Companies must preserve books of account and edit logs for at least 8 financial years under Section 128(5).

Q13: Shared IDs & Unreviewed Logs in ERP (LED Ltd) +

Question: LED Ltd. implemented a new ERP. Auditors notice shared User IDs among employees, uncertainty whether audit trail is active, and unreviewed access logs. Advise the key internal controls required to be implemented.

✅ ICAI Solution

The company must establish the following essential internal controls over its ERP:

  1. Unique User Access Control: Discontinue shared logins immediately; enforce unique user credentials for every user with multi-factor authentication.
  2. Continuous Audit Trail Configuration: Lock ERP configuration so that audit trail (edit log) is enabled 24x7 at both application and database levels without ability to disable.
  3. Independent Log Review: Establish periodic management review of audit trail exception reports to detect unauthorized alterations.
  4. Privileged User Restrictions: Segregate duties between system administrators (who maintain IT) and business users (who enter transactions).
Q14: Statutory Auditor Excluding Direct Assistance Working Papers (UV Ltd) +

Question: CA L used direct assistance of internal auditors in UV Ltd. He instructed his audit assistant not to document the working papers prepared by the internal auditors in statutory files, arguing they do not form part of statutory audit. Is CA L right?

✅ ICAI Solution

CA L IS COMPLETELY WRONG!

Mandatory Documentation under SA 610 (Revised) & SA 230:

  • The external auditor has sole responsibility for the audit opinion expressed and cannot dilute that responsibility by using direct assistance.
  • SA 610 explicitly requires that the statutory audit documentation SHALL INCLUDE the working papers prepared by the internal auditors providing direct assistance, including signatures indicating who reviewed their work and the date of review!

Q15: Activities Performed Prior to Using Direct Assistance (Ish and Vish Ltd) +

Question: Statutory auditor of Ish and Vish Ltd. plans to use internal auditor Mr. Yug for direct assistance. Indicate the activities to be performed by the statutory auditor prior to using internal auditor for providing direct assistance.

✅ ICAI Solution

Prior to using Mr. Yug for direct assistance, the statutory auditor must:

  1. Evaluate Threats to Objectivity: Assess whether Mr. Yug has personal bias, operational conflicts, or financial interests that could impair objectivity.
  2. Evaluate Level of Competence: Confirm Mr. Yug has requisite technical training, proficiency, and experience to execute the specific assigned audit tasks.

Q16: Internal Controls to Demonstrate Continuous Audit Trail (Shine Appliances Ltd) +

Question: Shine Appliances Ltd. seeks guidance from statutory auditor CA M on internal controls that should be implemented to demonstrate that the Audit Trail feature was continuously enabled and not disabled at any point of time under Rule 11(g).

✅ ICAI Solution

CA M should advise management to implement the following controls:

  1. Database & Application Level Enabler: Configure software parameters to enable edit logging across all transaction tables permanently.
  2. Restricted DBA Rights: Prohibit database administrators from altering logging tables; ensure change-log tables are append-only.
  3. Automated Heartbeat & Alert System: Implement automated alerts that trigger immediately if audit trail logging stops or fails.
  4. Periodic Independent Log Scrutiny: Have internal audit or an independent IT auditor review log continuity certificates periodically.
  5. Secure Archival & Backup: Ensure daily encrypted backups of audit logs are stored securely to comply with the 8-year statutory retention rule under Section 128(5).
