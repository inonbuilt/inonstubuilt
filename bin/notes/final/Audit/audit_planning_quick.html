1. SA 300: Audit Strategy vs. Audit Plan (NET)

📊 Interrelationship: Strategy Sets the Scope, Plan Guides Execution

Overall Audit Strategy: Sets the scope, timing, and direction of the audit, guiding the development of the audit plan (resource allocation, industry factors, reporting deadlines).
Audit Plan: More detailed than the strategy; includes the Nature, Timing, and Extent (NET) of risk assessment procedures, further audit procedures (tests of controls & substantive procedures).

  • Dynamic Nature: Planning is not a discrete phase; it is a continuous and iterative process that often begins shortly after completion of the previous audit and continues until completion.

2. SA 315: The 5 Components of Internal Control (COSO)

COSO Component Core Internal Control Function Exam Testing Angle
1. Control Environment Governance and management functions; tone at the top, ethical values, organizational structure, HR policies. Sets foundation; weak environment increases fraud risk across all accounts.
2. Risk Assessment Entity's process for identifying business risks relevant to financial reporting objectives and deciding actions. Failure to identify new digital or regulatory risks.
3. Information System Procedures and records established to initiate, record, process, and report transactions and maintain accountability. Audit trail testing and IT general controls.
4. Control Activities Policies and procedures that ensure management directives are carried out: segregations of duties, approvals, reconciliations. Preventive vs detective automated controls.
5. Monitoring of Controls Ongoing evaluation of whether internal controls are functioning as intended (internal audit, periodic reviews). Management review of exception reports.

3. SA 320: Materiality Hierarchy & Benchmarks

Level of Materiality Definition & Purpose Standard Benchmark Range
Overall Materiality Materiality for financial statements as a whole; threshold where misstatements influence economic decisions of users. • Profit before tax: 5% – 10%
• Total Revenue: 0.5% – 1%
• Total Assets: 1% – 2%
Performance Materiality Amount set below overall materiality to reduce to acceptably low level the probability that aggregate uncorrected misstatements exceed overall materiality. Typically 60% – 75% of Overall Materiality depending on risk assessment.
Clearly Trivial Threshold Matters that are clearly inconsequential; below which misstatements need not even be accumulated under SA 450. Typically 1% – 5% of Overall Materiality.

4. Significant Risks under SA 315

A Significant Risk is an identified and assessed risk of material misstatement that, in the auditor's judgment, requires special audit consideration.

  • Statutory Determination Factors: Risk of fraud, significant economic or accounting developments, complexity of transactions, significant transactions with related parties, high degree of estimation uncertainty, transactions outside normal course of business.
  • Mandatory Audit Procedure: If the auditor identifies a significant risk, substantive procedures directly responsive to that risk are mandatory. If relying on controls, testing in the *current period* is mandatory (cannot rely on prior-period testing!).

5. High-Frequency Exam Traps

⚠️ Examiner Traps Alert
  • Trap 1 (Revision of Materiality): If the auditor revises overall materiality to a lower amount during the audit, he MUST ALSO REVISE performance materiality and re-evaluate whether the nature, timing, and extent of audit procedures remain appropriate.
  • Trap 2 (Qualitative Materiality): An uncorrected misstatement below the quantitative threshold can still be material qualitatively if it affects compliance with loan covenants, changes a loss into a profit, or increases director bonuses.
