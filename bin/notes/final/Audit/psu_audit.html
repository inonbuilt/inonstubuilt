Overview of Audit of Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs)

Master the constitutional mandate of the Comptroller & Auditor General of India (C&AG) under Articles 148–151, the C&AG (DPC) Act 1971, statutory audits under Companies Act 2013 (Sections 139 & 143), Audit Boards, Parliamentary Committees (COPU, PAC), and the five pillar audits: Financial Audit, Compliance Audit, Performance Audit (The 3Es), Comprehensive Audit, and Propriety Audit with all TYU & TYK solutions!

🏛️ 1. Introduction & 3 Organizational Categories of PSUs
Socio-Economic Engine

In India, Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) are enterprises fundamentally owned or controlled by the Central Government, or any State Government(s), or partly by the Central Government and one or more State Governments.

💡 Strategic Role of Public Enterprises

Public enterprises serve as the backbone of India's socio-economic development, controlling key strategic sectors that provide basic industrial and agricultural inputs: coal, crude oil, steel, minerals and metals, cement, chemicals and fertilizers, and heavy capital equipment. They also operate vital national public utilities: Indian Railways, postal services, telecommunications, electricity generation and transmission, and state road transport. While the private sector now participates actively in power and telecom, government enterprises continue to occupy commanding heights in public interest infrastructure.

The Three Organizational Categories of PSUs

Public Sector Undertakings in India are organized into three distinct constitutional and legal forms:

1. Departmental Undertakings

Enterprises managed directly by government departments, forming an integral part of executive government machinery. Funded directly via annual budget appropriations from the Consolidated Fund of India.

  • Indian Railways
  • Department of Posts (India Post)
  • Security Printing & Minting Press
  • Canteen Stores Department (CSD)
2. Government Companies

Entities registered under the Companies Act, 2013 (or earlier Acts) where ≥ 51% of paid-up share capital is held by Central Govt, State Govts, or jointly, including subsidiaries under Section 2(45).

  • Coal India Ltd (CIL)
  • Steel Authority of India Ltd (SAIL)
  • NTPC Ltd, ONGC, BHEL, GAIL
  • Deemed Government Companies
3. Statutory Corporations

Corporate entities established under specific Acts of Parliament or State Legislatures. Their powers, duties, audit provisions, and governance structures are defined solely by their parent statutes.

  • Life Insurance Corporation (LIC Act 1956)
  • State Bank of India (SBI Act 1955)
  • Reserve Bank of India (RBI Act 1934)
  • Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC)
📜 2. Framework for Government Audit & Constitutional Mandate (Articles 148–151)
Supreme Constitutional Authority

Government audit in India is entrusted exclusively to an independent constitutional authority: the Comptroller and Auditor General of India (C&AG), who heads the Indian Audit and Accounts Department (IAAD). Together, they constitute the Supreme Audit Institution of India (SAI India).

Article Constitutional Provision Significance & Safeguards of Independence
Article 148 Appointment, Tenure & Removal of C&AG • Appointed by the President of India by warrant under his hand and seal.
• Removal Safeguard: Can be removed from office only on the grounds of proven misbehaviour or incapacity, in like manner and on like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court (requires 2/3rd majority of members present and voting in both Houses of Parliament).
• Salary and service conditions determined by Parliament and cannot be varied to his disadvantage after appointment.
• Ineligible for further office under the Government of India or any State Government after ceasing to hold office.
• Administrative expenses of the office of C&AG (including salaries, allowances, and pensions) are charged upon the Consolidated Fund of India (non-votable).
Article 149 Duties & Powers of C&AG • C&AG shall perform such duties and exercise such powers in relation to the accounts of the Union and of the States and of any other authority or body as may be prescribed by or under any law made by Parliament.
• Exercised in detail under the C&AG's (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971.
Article 150 Form of Accounts of Union & States • The accounts of the Union and of the States shall be kept in such form as the President may, on the advice of the C&AG, prescribe.
Article 151 Submission of Audit Reports to Legislature • Union Accounts: Audit reports submitted to the President, who causes them to be laid before each House of Parliament.
• State Accounts: Audit reports submitted to the Governor of the State, who causes them to be laid before the State Legislative Assembly/Council.
⚖️ 3. The C&AG (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971
Statutory Powers

The C&AG (DPC) Act, 1971 codifies the tenure, terms of office, and extensive audit powers of the Comptroller & Auditor General:

Tenure & Resignation

• The C&AG holds office for a term of six (6) years or up to the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier.
• He may at any time resign his office by writing addressed under his hand to the President of India.
• His independence is constitutionally insulated from executive interference.

Core Statutory Audit Mandates

• Sec 10–12: Compilation and keeping of accounts of Union and States.
• Sec 13: Audit of all expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of India and States.
• Sec 14–15: Audit of bodies or authorities substantially financed by grants/loans from Consolidated Fund.
• Sec 16: Audit of all receipts payable into the Consolidated Fund.
• Sec 17: Audit of stores, stocks, and inventories of government departments.
• Sec 19: Audit of Government companies and statutory corporations.

🏢 4. Commercial Audit of Government Companies under Companies Act, 2013
Sections 139 & 143 Protocol

For Government companies and deemed government companies (where government equity participation is ≥ 51%), the audit regime combines independent Chartered Accountant firms with statutory oversight by the C&AG:

Section Legal Mandate Statutory Auditor & C&AG Interplay
Section 139(5) / 139(7) Appointment of Statutory Auditors • In the case of a Government company, the auditor is appointed by the C&AG of India within 180 days from the commencement of the financial year.
• First Auditor: Appointed by C&AG within 60 days of incorporation; if not appointed, the Board of Directors within next 30 days; failing which, members within 60 days at an EGM.
Section 143(5) C&AG Directions to Statutory Auditors • C&AG has statutory authority to issue directions to the appointed auditor on the manner in which the accounts shall be audited.
• The auditor must submit a copy of the audit report to the C&AG, which shall specifically incorporate:
  1. The directions issued by the C&AG;
  2. The action taken thereon by the auditor; and
  3. Its financial impact on the accounts and financial statements of the company.
Section 143(6) Supplementary Audit by C&AG • C&AG has the right to conduct a Supplementary Audit of the financial statements within 60 days from the date of receipt of the audit report from the statutory auditor.
• C&AG may comment upon or supplement such audit report.
• Laying at AGM: Such comments or supplement must be sent by the company to every person entitled to receive financial statements and placed before the AGM at the same time and in the same manner as the audit report.
Section 143(7) Test Audit Ordered by C&AG • Without prejudice to Section 143(6), the C&AG may, by an order, cause a Test Audit to be conducted of the accounts of such company.
• The provisions of Section 19A of the C&AG (DPC) Act 1971 apply to such test audit.
⚠️ Exam Alert: Disinvestment & Government Company Status

If during a financial year, the Central or State Government disinvests its shareholding in a PSU such that the government holding falls below 51% (e.g., drops to 48%), the company ceases to be a Government Company under Section 2(45). Consequently, the C&AG loses jurisdiction under Section 139(5) and Section 143(5)–(7) for subsequent periods; the appointment of auditors and reporting reverts to standard shareholder governance under Section 139(1) of the Companies Act, 2013!

👥 5. Audit Board Setup & Parliamentary Oversight Committees (COPU & PAC)
Checks & Balances

Audit Board Setup in Commercial Audit

A unique feature of the commercial audit conducted by the IAAD is the constitution of Audit Boards, established in 1969 pursuant to recommendations of the Administrative Reforms Commission:

  • Purpose: To conduct Comprehensive Audit Appraisals of public sector enterprises operating in diverse sectors of the national economy.
  • Composition:
    • Chairman: Deputy C&AG or Additional Deputy C&AG (Commercial).
    • Two Whole-Time Members: Senior IAAD officials (Director General / Principal Director of Commercial Audit).
    • Two Part-Time Members: Technical experts, economists, or specialists from outside the IAAD nominated by C&AG in consultation with the administrative ministry concerned.
  • Synergy: Associates external sector expertise with audit acumen to evaluate operational performance objectively.

Parliamentary Financial Committees

Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)

Established in 1964. Consists of 22 Members (15 elected from Lok Sabha and 7 from Rajya Sabha according to proportional representation).

Primary Functions:

  • To examine the reports and accounts of public undertakings specified in the Fourth Schedule to the Rules of Procedure.
  • To examine C&AG audit reports on public undertakings.
  • To examine whether the affairs of public undertakings are being managed in accordance with sound business principles and prudent commercial practices.
  • To exercise functions vested in PAC and Estimates Committee concerning PSUs.
  • Limitation: Cannot examine matters of major government policy or day-to-day administrative matters.
Public Accounts Committee (PAC)

Consists of 22 Members (15 Lok Sabha + 7 Rajya Sabha). Headed by an Opposition leader.

Primary Functions:

  • Examines the Appropriation Accounts showing expenditure sanctioned by Parliament vs. actual spend.
  • Examines the Annual Finance Accounts of the Union Government.
  • Scrutinizes C&AG audit reports on civil ministries, defence, railways, and revenue receipts.
  • Ascertains whether moneys shown in accounts were legally available, applied for intended purposes, and conform to authorizing sanctions.
  • Brings out cases of financial irregularities, waste, loss, and extravagance.
🧠 6. Interactive Test Your Understanding 1 (COPU Functions)
Case Challenge
📝 TYU 1: Functions and Limitations of Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU) −

Scenario:

Explain the constitution, primary functions, and statutory limitations of the Parliamentary Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU) in relation to the examination of Public Sector Enterprises and C&AG audit reports.

✅ ICAI Official Solution & Statutory Analysis

The Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU) was introduced in Parliament in 1964. It consists of 22 members (15 from Lok Sabha and 7 from Rajya Sabha).

Four Mandated Functions of COPU:

  1. To examine the reports and accounts of the public undertakings specified in the Fourth Schedule to the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in Lok Sabha.
  2. To examine the reports, if any, of the Comptroller and Auditor General on the public undertakings.
  3. To examine, in the context of the autonomy and efficiency of the public undertakings, whether the affairs of the public undertakings are being managed in accordance with sound business principles and prudent commercial practices.
  4. To exercise such other functions vested in the Public Accounts Committee and the Estimates Committee in relation to public undertakings as are allotted to the Committee by the Speaker from time to time.

Statutory Limitations (Matters Excluded from COPU Scrutiny):

  • Matters of major government policy as distinct from business or commercial functions of the undertaking.
  • Matters of day-to-day administration.
  • Matters for the consideration of which machinery is established by any special statute under which a particular public undertaking is established.
🎯 7. Objective, Scope & Basic Elements of Public Enterprises Audit
Public Accountability Paradigm

Public sector auditing augments public confidence by providing independent, objective assessments concerning deviations from accepted standards, legal regulations, or principles of good governance.

Fiscal Accountability vs. Managerial Accountability

In the broad constitutional context, Government audit encompasses two fundamental dimensions:

1. Fiscal Accountability (Regularity & Propriety)

Focuses on the strict legality and financial prudence of public transactions. It includes:

  • Audit of Provision of Funds: Ensuring money spent was legally voted and appropriated by the legislature under the Appropriation Act.
  • Audit of Sanctions: Ensuring expenditure conforms to general or specific sanctions of the competent authority.
  • Audit of Compliance: Adherence to statutory rules, regulations, service codes, and contractual terms.
  • Audit of Propriety: Ensuring expenditure respects canons of public finance and is not wasteful.
2. Managerial Accountability (The 3Es)

Focuses on operational results, resource productivity, and program effectiveness. It includes:

  • Audit of Economy: Minimising input costs of resources without compromising quality.
  • Audit of Efficiency: Maximising outputs from given inputs or minimising inputs for targeted outputs.
  • Audit of Effectiveness: Evaluating whether policy goals and socio-economic outcomes were achieved.
  • Often designated as: Efficiency-cum-Performance Audit.

The Three Basic Elements of PSU Audits

Every public sector audit conducted under the auspices of the Supreme Audit Institution (SAI India) consists of three foundational elements:

Element Definition & Role Application in PSU Auditing
(a) The Three Parties 1. Auditor: The Supreme Audit Institution (C&AG of India and IAAD, represented by Accountants General).
2. Responsible Party: The auditable entity, its management, and those charged with governance (TCWG).
3. Intended Users: The legislature (Parliament/State Assemblies), citizens, taxpayers, and oversight bodies. 		The C&AG provides independent assurance to the legislature and citizens regarding the conduct and stewardship of public resources by executive management.
(b) Subject Matter, Criteria & Info • Subject Matter: The condition, activity, transaction, or financial information evaluated.
• Criteria: The benchmarks, standards, laws, or performance targets used for evaluation.
• Subject Matter Information: The outcome of evaluating the subject matter against criteria. 		In financial audit, subject matter is the financial position; criteria are Ind AS / AS. In performance audit, subject matter is program delivery; criteria are policy targets and best practices.
(c) Types of Engagement • Attestation Engagements: The responsible party measures the subject matter against criteria and presents the subject matter info; auditor gathers evidence to express a conclusion.
• Direct Reporting Engagements: The auditor directly measures or evaluates the subject matter against criteria and presents the findings. 		• Financial Audits: Always Attestation Engagements.
• Performance & Compliance Audits: Generally Direct Reporting Engagements.
📐 8. Principles of PSU Audits (General & Process Principles)
SAI India Standards

The principles of PSU Audits constitute the overarching auditing standards applicable to all SAI India personnel and appointed chartered accountant firms. They are classified into two distinct groups:

Group I: General Principles
  1. Ethics & Independence: Compliance with ethical rules, integrity, objectivity, political neutrality, and constitutional independence.
  2. Professional Judgement, Due Care & Skepticism: Exercising critical assessment, alertness to conflicting evidence, and due diligence.
  3. Quality Control: Implementing robust internal quality control systems in line with ISSAI / C&AG Quality Assurance Framework.
  4. Audit Team Management & Skill: Ensuring the engagement team collectively possesses necessary technical knowledge, competencies, and sector expertise.
  5. Audit Risk: Managing risk of expressing inappropriate conclusions (inherent, control, and detection risks).
  6. Materiality: Determining materiality thresholds by value, nature, context, and public interest sensitivity.
  7. Documentation: Preparing contemporaneous audit files enabling an experienced auditor to understand evidence and conclusions.
  8. Communication: Maintaining constructive two-way communication with auditable entities and TCWG at entry, conduct, and exit conferences.
Group II: Principles Related to Audit Process
  1. Planning the Audit:
    • Establish terms, understanding of entity & operating environment.
    • Conduct risk assessment, identify fraud risks.
    • Formulate audit strategy, plan, and Audit Design Matrix (ADM).
  2. Conducting the Audit (Performing & Gathering Evidence):
    • Execute planned procedures (inspection, observation, inquiry, confirmation, analytical procedures).
    • Obtain sufficient, appropriate, and persuasive audit evidence.
    • Continually update planning assumptions upon discovery of red flags.
  3. Reporting & Follow-up:
    • Evaluate evidence, draw objective conclusions against criteria.
    • Issue clear, constructive, balanced reports incorporating entity responses.
    • Conduct rigorous follow-up on corrective actions taken by management.
📊 9. Financial Audit in Public Sector Undertakings
Attestation of True & Fair View

Financial Audit in the public sector is primarily conducted to:

  • Express an independent audit opinion on the financial statements of the government enterprise; and
  • Enhance the degree of confidence of intended users (Parliament, Government, lenders, public) in the financial statements.
📋 Objective of Financial Audit by C&AG

The C&AG or the appointed statutory auditor shall express an opinion on whether the financial statements are prepared, in all material respects, in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework (Ind AS / AS / Schedule III). In the case of financial statements prepared under a fair presentation framework, the auditor assesses whether the financial statements give a true and fair view of the financial position and financial performance.

🔍 10. Compliance Audit (Regularity & Propriety Dimensions)
Rules, Authority & Ethics

Compliance Audit is the independent assessment of whether a given subject matter is in compliance with the applicable governing criteria. In public sector entities, this audit evaluates whether activities, financial transactions, and operational information comply, in all material respects, with the regulatory rules, laws, policies, budgetary sanctions, and contractual covenants governing the entity.

The Twin Perspectives of Compliance Auditing

1. Regularity Dimension

Strict adherence of the subject matter to formal criteria emanating from:

  • Enacted laws, statutes, and parliamentary enactments.
  • Subordinate regulations, statutory orders, and administrative notifications.
  • Terms of agreements, contracts, and scheme guidelines.
  • Budgetary authorizations and appropriation laws.
2. Propriety Dimension

Observance of the general principles governing sound financial management and ethical conduct of public officials:

  • Exercising financial prudence and avoidance of waste.
  • Ensuring public funds are not used for personal aggrandizement.
  • Meeting legitimate public expectations of integrity, fairness, and morality in public spending.

Three Operational Perspectives of Compliance Audit

Compliance auditing in government undertakings is rarely isolated; it is conducted from three operational perspectives:

  1. In relation with the audit of financial statements: Reviewing legal and regulatory compliance impacting balance sheet and profit & loss figures.
  2. Separately as individual compliance audits: Standalone thematic compliance reviews of specific government schemes, procurement contracts, or departments.
  3. In combination with performance auditing: Where compliance is treated as an integral facet of economy, efficiency, and effectiveness. Non-compliance often serves as the root cause or consequence of performance failures!
🔄 The 5-Stage Compliance Audit Process

1. General Principles & Annual Plan: Consider ethics, identify auditable units, develop annual plan.
2. Planning Compliance Audits: Determine objective, identify subject matter & criteria, understand entity & controls, assess risk & materiality.
3. Performing & Evidence Gathering: Gather evidence via inspections, interviews, documentation; detect suspected unlawful acts.
4. Evaluating Evidence & Conclusions: Assess sufficiency, consider reporting materiality, formulate balanced conclusions.
5. Reporting & Follow-up: Issue comprehensive report with management responses, monitor remedial implementation.

🧠 11. Interactive Test Your Understanding 2 (C&AG Directions under Sec 143(5))
Statutory Reporting Duties
📝 TYU 2: Statutory Auditor Reporting on IT Systems, Loan Restructuring & Central Schemes −

Scenario:

Under Section 143(5) of the Companies Act, 2013, the C&AG issued specific directions to the statutory auditor of a Central PSU requiring examination and reporting on three specific operational areas:

  1. Whether the company has a system in place to process all accounting transactions through an IT system/ERP without manual intervention?
  2. Whether there is any restructuring of an existing loan or cases of waiver/write-off of debts/loans/interest?
  3. Whether funds (grants/subsidy etc.) received/receivable for specific schemes from Central/State Government were properly accounted for and utilized?

Explain the audit procedures and reporting duties of the statutory auditor in addressing each of these three C&AG directions.

✅ ICAI Official Solution & Step-by-Step Reporting Framework

1. IT System & Accounting Processing without Manual Intervention:

  • The auditor must examine whether the company processes transactions through an integrated ERP (e.g., SAP, Oracle) or legacy software.
  • Evaluate automated internal controls, segregation of duties, role-based access, and automated data flow from sub-ledgers to the general ledger.
  • Identify any manual journals, offline adjustments, or bypass of IT controls. If manual intervention exists, report the nature, extent, risks, and financial implications.

2. Loan Restructuring & Debt Waiver / Write-off:

  • Scrutinize loan files, restructuring agreements, waiver proposals, and minutes of Board of Directors / delegated committees.
  • Verify whether debt waivers or write-offs were approved by the competent authority in accordance with delegated financial powers (DoFP).
  • Ensure proper accounting treatment in books of account and disclose total quantum restructured, waived, or written off during the year along with justifications.

3. Grants / Subsidies for Specific Schemes:

  • Verify terms and sanction orders of grants/subsidies received from Central/State Governments.
  • Verify whether funds were deposited in designated bank accounts, utilized exclusively for the sanctioned purpose, and whether Utilization Certificates (UCs) were furnished in time.
  • Verify physical creation/existence of scheme assets and check whether any unspent funds, diversion of grants, or interest earned on scheme funds were refunded or credited in accordance with scheme guidelines.
⚡ 12. Performance Audit & The 3Es (Economy, Efficiency, Effectiveness)
Value for Money (VFM) Audit

A Performance Audit is an objective and systematic examination of evidence for the purpose of providing an independent assessment of the performance of a government organization, program, activity, or function in order to provide information to improve public accountability and facilitate decision-making by parties with responsibility to oversee or initiate corrective action.

🎯 Core Purpose of Performance Auditing

Performance auditing promotes good governance by evaluating whether:

  • Decisions by the legislature or executive are efficiently and effectively prepared and implemented; and
  • Taxpayers, citizens, and intended beneficiaries have received Value for Money (VFM) from public funds deployed.

The 3Es Framework: Detailed Analysis

1. Economy (Input Cost Optimization)

Minimising the cost of resources deployed for an activity, having regard to appropriate quantity, quality, and at the best available price.

Auditor Evaluates:

  • Were human, material, and financial resources acquired in due time, in right quantities, at lowest cost?
  • Have the best competitive prices been obtained for procurement and consultancy?
  • Can transport, logistics, or administration costs be reduced?
  • Has there been wasteful acquisition of idle assets?
2. Efficiency (Input-Output Ratio)

The relationship between inputs and outputs. Achieved when maximum output is delivered from given inputs, or minimum inputs are consumed for a given quantity and quality of output.

Auditor Evaluates:

  • Focuses on processes by which an organization transforms inputs into outputs.
  • Are sound procurement and maintenance practices followed?
  • Are equipment, plant facilities, and staff deployed optimally without idle time?
  • How does the cost per unit output compare with industry benchmarks?
3. Effectiveness (Outcome & Goal Achievement)

The extent to which program objectives are achieved and the relationship between the intended impact and the actual observed impact of an activity.

Auditor Evaluates:

  • Did the program achieve its desired socio-economic outcomes?
  • Were the policy means and financial resources suitable for the goal?
  • Did the benefits reach the targeted underprivileged beneficiaries without leakage?
  • Were there unintended negative side effects or environmental damages?
📐 13. Planning for Performance Audit & Audit Design Matrix (ADM)
Methodology & Criteria

Planning is the most vital phase of a performance audit, establishing the blueprint for execution, data collection, and evidentiary rigor.

Key Steps in Planning a Performance Audit

  1. Understanding the Entity / Programme: Reviewing enabling legislation, policy statements, scheme guidelines, budget documents, organizational hierarchy, internal control manuals, and previous audit findings.
  2. Defining Audit Objectives & Scope: Articulating what the audit intends to accomplish, the time period covered, geographic locations, and auditable units to be sampled.
  3. Determining Audit Criteria: Establishing reasonable and attainable standards of performance against which operations will be judged.
  4. Formulating the Audit Design Matrix (ADM): A structured analytical framework linking objectives with criteria and data sources.
  5. Determining Data Collection & Analysis Methods: Surveys, quantitative sampling, qualitative interviews, physical site inspections, focus group discussions, and forensic data analytics.
  6. Risk Assessment & Materiality: Factoring financial magnitude, political sensitivity, public interest visibility, and vulnerability to systemic mismanagement.
📏 Audit Criteria: Definition & Sources

Audit Criteria are the benchmarks used to evaluate the subject matter. They represent "what should be" under ideal or reasonable conditions, against which actual performance ("what is") is compared to identify performance gaps.

Core Sources of Audit Criteria:

  • Statutory laws, acts of legislature, regulations, and cabinet decisions.
  • National and international standards, technical codes, and industry benchmarks.
  • Performance targets set by management in MOUs, project feasibility reports, or scheme guidelines.
  • Historical performance data of the entity or comparable domestic/international peers.
  • Professional consensus, scientific literature, and legitimate citizen expectations.

The Audit Design Matrix (ADM) Structure

Audit Objective Sub-Objective / Question Audit Criteria Data Sources & Collection Expected Output
To assess efficiency of solar power plant setup. Were procurement and installation completed within budgeted time and cost? Detailed Project Report (DPR) timelines, approved budget caps, CERC benchmark capital costs. Tender documents, contract awards, vendor payment records, site measurement logs, plant commissioning certificates. Quantified cost overruns, delay analysis, identification of bottleneck causes, and financial impact.
🏗️ 14. Comprehensive Audit (The 10 Focus Areas of Audit Boards)
Enterprise-Wide Appraisal

Comprehensive Audit is an efficiency-cum-performance appraisal of public enterprises as commercial business concerns. Conducted through the mechanism of Audit Boards, it synthesizes financial, regularity, and performance data into a holistic operational evaluation to determine whether the enterprise is fulfilling the strategic socio-economic objectives for which it was created.

🔍 The 10 Core Focus Areas Examined in Comprehensive Audit
  1. Project Formulation & Capital Expenditure: Was project preparation adequate? Were there delays, faulty technical designs, inadequate feasibility studies, or massive capital cost overruns?
  2. Capacity Utilization: Are installed production capacities fully utilized? What are the reasons for idle capacity (power cuts, raw material shortage, machine breakdowns, or lack of demand)?
  3. Production Planning & Operational Efficiency: Comparison of actual production with budgeted targets and standard norms. Are rejects, scrap, and rework within permissible limits?
  4. Inventory Management & Procurement: Are stock levels of raw materials, spares, and finished goods excessive? Are procurement procedures transparent and competitive? Is there obsolete or surplus inventory?
  5. Financial Management & Working Capital: Review of capital structure, liquidity management, debt servicing capability, timely billing, and prompt recovery of sundry debtors.
  6. Marketing & Sales Policy: Pricing strategies, trade discounts, credit policies, competitive positioning, and customer service satisfaction.
  7. Human Resource & Manpower Planning: Assessment of staffing ratios, surplus manpower, overtime payments, absenteeism, industrial relations, and training effectiveness.
  8. Plant Maintenance & Equipment Health: Adequacy of preventive maintenance schedules, frequency of unscheduled downtime, and availability of critical spare parts.
  9. Research & Development (R&D): Investments in innovation, indigenous technology adoption, product quality improvement, and patent filings.
  10. Internal Control & Internal Audit: Independence and adequacy of the internal audit department, compliance with Audit Committee recommendations, and IT general controls.
⚖️ 15. Propriety Audit & Canons of Financial Prudence
Morality, Wisdom & Public Interest

In Propriety Audit, the auditor moves beyond mechanical conformity with rules and regulations to examine transactions on the touchstone of public interest, financial prudence, morality, and ethical conduct.

💡 Crucial Conceptual Distinction: Regularity vs. Propriety

A transaction may satisfy all the formal requirements of a Regularity Audit (proper sanction, correct budget head, valid voucher, authorized signature), yet remain highly improper, wasteful, extravagant, or infructuous! Propriety audit exists precisely to bring such wasteful decisions to public light.

The Four Canons of Financial Propriety (The Golden Rules)

The C&AG has laid down four cardinal standards of financial propriety that must govern all public expenditure:

Canon The Principle of Prudence Practical Audit Verification
Canon 1: Ordinary Prudence The expenditure should not be prima facie more than the occasion demands. Every public officer is expected to exercise the same vigilance in respect of expenditure incurred from public money as a person of ordinary prudence would exercise in respect of expenditure of his own money. Auditor checks for extravagant hospitality, luxury vehicles, five-star hotel stays, or unwarranted first-class air travel when economical alternatives were readily available.
Canon 2: No Personal Advantage The authority should not exercise its powers of sanctioning expenditure to pass an order which will be directly or indirectly to its own advantage. Auditor checks whether sanctioning officials awarded contracts, leased properties, or sanctioned perks to themselves, their relatives, or entities in which they hold personal interests.
Canon 3: Public Benefit Only Public funds should not be utilized for the benefit of a particular person or section of the people, unless: (a) the claim is enforceable in a court of law; or (b) the expenditure is in pursuance of a recognized policy or custom. Auditor verifies that grants, subsidies, or welfare concessions are distributed strictly under transparent policy guidelines, without partisan favoritism.
Canon 4: Allowance Not a Source of Profit The amount of allowances granted to meet expenditure of a particular type (e.g., travelling allowance, daily allowance) should be so regulated that the allowances do not on the whole become a source of profit to the recipients. Auditor checks that DA, per diems, and transport reimbursements reflect realistic expenditure and do not serve as disguised salary increments.

Auditor's Functions & Dilemmas in Propriety Audit

Auditor's Core Functions
  • Ensure all expenditure incurred is properly planned.
  • Ensure the size and channels of expenditure are rightful and yield optimum results.
  • Appraise whether substitute plans of action could yield better operational results or capital returns.
  • Examine managerial decisions to verify they are conducive to public interest and satisfy high standards of ethical conduct.
The Hindsight Bias & Subjectivity Dilemma

• Inherent Subjectivity: Propriety is a moral concept. Standards of public interest vary over time and context.
• No Hindsight Bias: Commercial decisions taken under dynamic business environments must be evaluated with reference to the circumstances prevailing at the time the decision was taken, not with the benefit of hindsight!
• Preserving Initiative: Auditor must exercise balanced discretion; hyper-critical propriety audit can stifle managerial bold risk-taking and paralyze decision-making!

📑 16. Audit Reports of the Comptroller & Auditor General
Parliamentary Scrutiny

To facilitate orderly parliamentary examination, the audit reports of the C&AG on Public Sector Undertakings are presented to Parliament in multiple parts:

Part Report Component Core Contents & Findings
Part (a) General Review of Working Results Comprehensive macroeconomic and financial overview of the working results of all Government companies, deemed government companies, and statutory corporations (investments, profitability, dividend yields, net worth).
Part (b) Results of Comprehensive Appraisals Detailed reports of comprehensive appraisals of selected undertakings conducted by the Audit Board with technical experts.
Part (c) Resume of Auditors' Reports & C&AG Comments Summary resume of reports submitted by statutory company auditors under Section 143(5) directions, along with significant comments issued by the C&AG under Section 143(6) supplementary audits.
Part (d) Significant Individual Audit Findings Specific material instances of financial irregularities, infructuous expenditure, loss of revenue, and system deficiencies in undertakings not covered by comprehensive appraisals.

State Audit Reports: For specified states, a separate Commercial Audit Report is presented to the State Legislature; for others, a dedicated commercial chapter is included in the State Civil Audit Report.

To facilitate orderly consideration by Parliament and COPU, the C&AG reports on PSUs comprise four distinct parts:

Q2: Factors to Consider While Planning Performance Audit +

Question: ABG & Co., a Chartered Accountant firm, has been appointed by C&AG for conducting a performance audit of a government organization. Discuss the factors to be considered by the firm while planning the performance audit.

✅ ICAI Solution

The auditor should consider the following critical factors during the planning stage of a performance audit:

  • Understanding the Entity/Programme: Review policy objectives, enabling legislation, administrative guidelines, funding patterns, and governance structures.
  • Audit Objectives & Scope: Explicitly define what the audit aims to establish (Economy, Efficiency, or Effectiveness) and boundaries of time, geography, and programs.
  • Determination of Audit Criteria: Identify realistic, measurable benchmarks and standards against which performance will be evaluated.
  • Audit Design Matrix (ADM): Develop an ADM linking questions, criteria, required evidence, data sources, and analytical tools.
  • Audit Methodology: Plan qualitative and quantitative data collection methods (surveys, interviews, sample testing, site inspections, benchmarking).
  • Materiality & Risk Assessment: Focus on areas of highest financial outlay, public vulnerability, fraud risk, or prior systemic failure.
  • Team Composition & Skills: Ensure the team possesses requisite sector expertise, data analytics proficiency, and statistical sampling knowledge.
Q3: Comprehensive Audit of a Solar Power PSU (Sunlight Ltd) +

Question: Sunlight Limited is a public sector undertaking engaged in electricity generation from solar energy. The C&AG has decided to conduct a comprehensive audit of the company. Identify the key areas that should be examined.

✅ ICAI Solution

In conducting a Comprehensive Audit of Sunlight Ltd, the auditor through the Audit Board examines whether the enterprise is fulfilling its commercial and socio-economic mandate across key areas:

  • Project Formulation & Capital Outlay: Adequate feasibility studies for solar parks, land acquisition delays, cost overruns, and adherence to commissioning schedules.
  • Capacity Utilization & Generation Efficiency: Plant Load Factor (PLF) compared with rated capacity and solar radiation benchmarks; causes of generation shortfalls.
  • Procurement & Inventory Management: Competitive bidding for solar panels, inverters, and power evacuation equipment; warranty terms and quality checks.
  • Maintenance & Downtime: Frequency of inverter failures, panel cleaning efficiency, grid downtime, and availability of spares.
  • Commercial Management & PPA Compliance: Execution of Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs), billing timeliness, recovery of dues from State DISCOMs, and dispute resolution.
  • Financial Health: Debt-service coverage ratio (DSCR), capital structure, working capital liquidity, and cost per unit generated.
  • R&D & Environmental Impact: Investments in solar storage battery technology, panel recycling, and environmental impact assessments.
Q4: Definition & Objectives of Performance Audit +

Question: "A performance audit is an objective and systematic examination of evidence for providing an independent assessment of the performance of a government organization, program, activity, or function." Discuss the objectives and significance of performance audit.

✅ ICAI Solution

Performance audit evaluates whether public resources are deployed in accordance with the principles of Economy, Efficiency, and Effectiveness (the 3Es):

  1. Assisting Governance & Oversight: Providing actionable, objective insights to Parliament and executive authorities to correct operational bottlenecks.
  2. Ensuring Value for Money (VFM): Confirming that taxpayers and citizens receive optimal quality and quantity of public services for funds expended.
  3. Enhancing Public Accountability: Promoting transparency in executive decision-making and project implementation.
  4. Identifying Opportunities for Reform: Evaluating systemic flaws, institutional deficiencies, and suggesting concrete remedial actions.
Q5: Disinvestment & Status of Government Company (BT Ltd) +

Question: BT Ltd., a company wholly owned by the Central Government, was disinvested during the financial year, resulting in Central Government holding being reduced to 45%. Does the C&AG continue to hold jurisdiction to appoint auditors and conduct supplementary audits?

✅ ICAI Solution

Statutory Analysis under Companies Act, 2013:

  • Under Section 2(45), a "Government Company" is defined as a company in which not less than 51% of the paid-up share capital is held by the Central Government, or State Government(s), or jointly.
  • Upon disinvestment, the Central Government's equity holding in BT Ltd dropped to 45% (less than 51%). Consequently, BT Ltd ceases to be a Government Company.
  • Therefore, Section 139(5) (appointment of auditor by C&AG) and Section 143(5)–(7) (C&AG directions, supplementary audit, test audit) no longer apply to BT Ltd for subsequent periods.
  • The statutory auditor of BT Ltd will be appointed by its shareholders under Section 139(1) of the Companies Act, 2013.

Q6: Determination of Audit Criteria in Performance Audit (AKY Ltd) +

Question: You have been appointed as auditor of AKY Ltd. After determining audit objectives, you are required to establish audit criteria. Discuss the meaning and sources of audit criteria.

✅ ICAI Solution

Audit Criteria are reasonable and attainable standards of performance against which the economy, efficiency, and effectiveness of activities are assessed. They represent "what should be".

Sources for Determining Audit Criteria:

  1. Enacted statutory legislation, governing rules, and parliamentary mandates.
  2. Objectives and performance milestones set out in scheme guidelines, Detailed Project Reports (DPRs), and MOUs.
  3. Technical standards and codes prescribed by national/international standard-setting bodies (e.g., BIS, ISO, CERC).
  4. Historical performance data and trends of the company in previous years.
  5. Benchmarks established by comparable peer enterprises in the same industry.
  6. Best management practices and scientific management literature.
Q7: C&AG Appoints CA Firm for Performance Audit of State Road Transport PSU +

Question: C&AG appointed Verma & Associates, a Chartered Accountant firm, to conduct a performance audit of a State Road Transport Corporation. Outline the audit scope and reporting framework for this assignment.

✅ ICAI Solution

The firm acts on behalf of the Supreme Audit Institution (SAI India) and must adhere to C&AG Performance Auditing Guidelines:

  • Economy: Bus fleet procurement prices, fuel purchasing terms, tyre retreading costs, financing costs.
  • Efficiency: Fleet utilization ratio, vehicle-staff ratio, fuel consumption per kilometre (KMPL), preventive maintenance downtime.
  • Effectiveness: Punctuality of services, route coverage in rural/remote areas, passenger satisfaction, accident rates.
  • Reporting: Findings must be structured under the Audit Design Matrix, supported by verifiable quantitative evidence, incorporating management's explanations obtained during the exit conference.
Q8: Performance vs. Regularity Audit in State Electricity Distribution Co +

Question: The audit of a State Electricity Distribution Company was undertaken to evaluate transmission & distribution (T&D) losses, billing efficiency, and revenue collection. Distinguish whether this is a Performance Audit or Regularity Audit.

✅ ICAI Solution

This is primarily a Performance Audit (Efficiency-cum-Performance Audit). While Regularity Audit checks whether billing transactions comply with approved tariff orders, the audit of aggregate technical and commercial (AT&C) losses, feeder metering efficiency, and recovery ratios assesses the input-output efficiency and operational effectiveness of the utility in managing resources and curbing distribution leakages!

Q9: State Government Department Procurement Audit (Compliance Audit) +

Question: During an audit of a state department, the Office of C&AG observed widespread non-compliance with public procurement rules, tender splitting to evade higher sanction thresholds, and unauthorized advance payments. Classify the audit and explain its nature.

✅ ICAI Solution

This is a Compliance Audit. It evaluates whether the procurement activities adhered to formal criteria (State Financial Rules, Public Procurement Act, Delegation of Financial Powers). It covers both Regularity (following formal statutory rules) and Propriety (preventing favoritism and ensuring competitive price discovery in spending public money).

Q10: Performance Audit of Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) Scheme +

✅ ICAI Solution

Likely Audit Objectives:

  • To evaluate whether beneficiary databases are accurate, deduplicated, and seeded with Aadhaar.

Q11: Thermal Power Plant Setup Delay (Audit Methods) +

Question: A state government PSU setting up a thermal power plant experienced delays due to indecision regarding water treatment in the cooling plant and frequent layout changes. Identify the kind of audit and describe methods used in conducting such audits.

✅ ICAI Solution

This is a Performance / Comprehensive Audit focusing on project management efficiency and capital cost overruns.

Audit Methods Applied:

  • Documentary Scrutiny: Detailed review of Board agenda, minutes of technical committees, contract milestone charts, and consultant reports.
  • Comparative Timeline Analysis: Benchmarking actual milestone completion dates against approved Detailed Project Report (DPR) timelines.
  • Physical Inspection & Measurement: Site visits to inspect cooling towers, water intake systems, and soil stabilization works.
  • Expert Consultation: Interacting with technical specialists regarding soil re-testing and water chemistry requirements.
  • Financial Impact Quantification: Calculating idle interest during construction (IDC), escalation claims by contractors, and revenue lost from delayed commissioning.
Q12: Five-Year Operational Review of RST Ltd (Comprehensive Audit) +

Question: RST Ltd., a power generation PSU, completes five years. The Board requests an audit of financial accuracy, operational efficiency, internal controls, capacity underutilization, and fuel consumption overruns. Identify the audit and suggest key focus areas.

✅ ICAI Solution

The suitable audit is a Comprehensive Audit (Efficiency-cum-Performance Appraisal) conducted under the aegis of the Audit Board.

Key Issues to Examine:

  1. Capital Cost Overruns: Causes of variance between sanctioned project cost and actual capital capitalization.
  2. Capacity Underutilization: Technical and commercial bottlenecks causing plants to run below rated Megawatt capacity.
  3. Fuel & Raw Material Consumption: Station Heat Rate (SHR), coal transit losses, specific oil consumption compared with CEA/CERC standard norms.
  4. Inventory Management: Coal stock pile aging, non-moving spares, and inventory holding costs.
  5. Internal Financial Controls: Operational efficacy of IT automation in billing, weighbridge integration, and SCADA systems.
Q13: Rural Development Scheme Funds Utilization (Propriety Audit) +

Question: Funds provided by the Central Government to a State Government for building rural hospitals, schools, and roads are suspected of being subjected to wasteful and infructuous expenditure. Outline the auditor's functions in conducting a Propriety Audit.

✅ ICAI Solution

The auditor conducting a Propriety Audit must examine transactions beyond mechanical vouchers to verify public interest:

  • Proper Planning: Ensure all civil construction works were preceded by genuine need assessment and demographic surveys.
  • Rightful Size & Channels: Verify whether expenditure was justified by occasion and yielded optimum community benefits.
  • Alternative Feasibility: Examine whether lower-cost building models or better road alignments could have achieved equivalent utility.
  • Identification of Infructuous Expenditure: Highlight half-constructed structures abandoned due to lack of staff, substandard roads washed away in first rain, and schools constructed without access roads.
  • Public Interest & Ethical Conduct: Verify that tenders were not rigged to favor politically connected contractors.
Q14: LNG Corporation Compliance with Safety & Environment Standards +

Question: LNG Corporation, a government-owned entity, operates liquefied natural gas terminals. Audit is required to assess adherence to safety guidelines, environmental clearances, and hazard protocols. Classify the audit and explain its scope.

✅ ICAI Solution

This is a Compliance Audit focusing on regulatory and environmental governance.

The auditor assesses compliance with Petroleum and Natural Gas Regulatory Board (PNGRB) safety codes, Ministry of Environment & Climate Change (MoEFCC) clearances, Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) norms, and disaster management drills. Non-compliance exposes the entity to severe legal penalties, environmental liabilities, and catastrophic operational risks.

✅ ICAI Solution

This is a Performance Audit with central focus on Effectiveness. It examines the relationship between intended social impact (reduction in maternal mortality) and actual observed outcomes, verifying whether program benefits reached rightful beneficiaries without diversion.

Q16: Ministry of IT Software Project Audit (Performance Audit – Efficiency) +

Question: The Ministry of IT initiated an e-governance portal. An audit was commissioned to evaluate whether the portal delivers services at minimum cost per transaction, with optimal server utilization and user satisfaction. Classify the audit dimension.
