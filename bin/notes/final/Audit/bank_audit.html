Special Features of Audit of Banks

Comprehensive master revision notes covering the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, Third Schedule Forms A & B, RBI IRAC Prudential Norms on Income Recognition, Asset Classification & Provisioning, CRR/SLR Compliance, Basel III Capital Framework, Long Form Audit Report (LFAR), Concurrent Audit, and RBI Fraud Reporting.

Section 1 • Regulatory Landscape

Regulatory Framework & Peculiarities of Banking

1. Legal & Regulatory Framework Governing Banks in India

Banking institutions in India operate under a comprehensive, multi-tiered regulatory architecture overseen by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI):

Key Enactments Governing Banks

  • Banking Regulation Act, 1949 (BR Act): Primary statute governing licensing, management, accounting formats, and audit of banks.
  • Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 (RBI Act): Central banking functions, monetary policy, and Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR).
  • State Bank of India Act, 1955: Specific governance for SBI.
  • Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Acts, 1970/1980: Governing Nationalized Banks.
  • Companies Act, 2013: Applies to banking companies to the extent not inconsistent with the BR Act.
  • Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007: Electronic clearing, RTGS, NEFT, UPI systems.

Peculiar Operating Features of Banking Business

  • Voluminous Transactions: Massive volume of daily financial transactions processed electronically via Core Banking Solutions (CBS).
  • Wide Geographical Spread: Vast network of branches and automated service delivery channels (ATMs, mobile banking).
  • High Fiduciary Responsibility: Public deposits represent the overwhelming majority of funding, demanding extreme prudence and safety.
  • Stringent Prudential Norms: Stringent RBI directives on asset classification, provisioning, liquidity maintenance, and capital adequacy.

3. Audit of Accounts & Appointment of Auditors (Section 30, BR Act)

Section 30 of the BR Act governs the audit of banking companies. Key legal rules governing statutory auditor appointment and powers:

Appointment of Statutory Central Auditors (SCAs)
  • Banking Companies (Private Banks): Appointed by shareholders in AGM with prior approval of RBI under Section 30(1A) of the BR Act.
  • Nationalized Banks: Appointed by the Board of Directors with prior approval of RBI.
  • State Bank of India (SBI): Appointed by the Comptroller and Auditor General (C&AG) in consultation with the Central Government under Section 41 of the SBI Act.
Statutory Branch Auditors (SBAs) & Powers
  • Branch auditors are appointed to audit specific branches in accordance with RBI norms.
  • Right of Access: Auditors have full right of access at all times to books, accounts, and vouchers at head office and branches.
  • Special Audit: RBI has the power to order a special audit of any banking company under Section 30(1B) if considered in public interest or depositor interest.
Section 2 • Internal Controls

Conducting Bank Audit & Controls in Selected Areas

1. Internal Control Evaluation Across Key Operational Cycles

Due to the massive decentralization and volume of transactions, statutory auditors must evaluate specific internal controls across high-risk operational areas:

Operational Area Key Internal Control Procedures Expected in a Bank Branch
1. Cash & Currency Chests Joint custody of cash vault by Branch Manager and Head Cashier; dual-key mechanism; daily surprise cash counts; mandatory insurance coverage; insurance limit vs cash holding limits.
2. Clearing Operations Segregation of inward and outward clearing; Cheque Truncation System (CTS) security; immediate reconciliation of clearing suspense accounts with RBI/clearing house.
3. Bills for Collection Maintenance of separate registers for outward and inward bills; distinct control numbers; physical safe custody of commercial documents; release of documents strictly against payment (D/P) or acceptance (D/A).
4. Credit Card Operations Strict physical custody and dual control over unissued credit cards; immediate destruction of returned/unclaimed cards; automated fraud detection algorithms for anomalous card swipes.
5. Security Stationery Dual custody over blank Demand Drafts, Cheque books, Fixed Deposit Receipts (FDRs), and Banker's Cheques; daily stock verification against issuance register.
6. Loans and Advances Credit appraisal independent of sanctioning authority; verification of title deeds; perfection of security charge (hypothecation/mortgage); regular inspection of stocks; periodic review of drawing power (DP); monitoring compliance with sanction terms.
Section 3 • Statutory Ratios

Compliance with CRR & SLR Requirements (NDTL Computation)

1. CRR vs. SLR – Conceptual & Legal Framework

Commercial banks are statutorily required to maintain adequate liquid reserves with the central bank and in specified liquid assets to protect depositor interests and facilitate monetary policy transmission:

Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)

Section 42 of RBI Act, 1934: Banks must maintain with RBI an average daily cash balance equal to a specified percentage of their Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL).

  • Maintained entirely in cash balances with the RBI.
  • No interest is paid by RBI on CRR balances maintained by banks.
  • Daily minimum maintenance requirement (e.g., 90% of required CRR).

Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)

Section 24 of BR Act, 1949: Banks must maintain assets in specified liquid forms equal to a prescribed percentage of their NDTL as of the last Friday of the second preceding fortnight.

  • Maintained in: (a) Cash in hand; (b) Gold valued at market rates; (c) Unencumbered approved Central/State Government Securities.
  • Banks earn interest yield on approved government securities.

Understanding Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL)

NDTL Formula Architecture

NDTL = Demand Liabilities + Time Liabilities + Other Demand & Time Liabilities − Eligible Inter-Bank Assets

  • Demand Liabilities: Current deposits, demand liabilities of savings bank accounts, demand drafts, unpaid balances in cash credit/overdraft accounts.
  • Time Liabilities: Fixed deposits, recurring deposits, cash certificates, staff security deposits.
  • Liabilities not included in NDTL: Paid-up capital, statutory reserves, credit balance in P&L account, refinance loans from RBI/NABARD/SIDBI/EXIM Bank.

Auditor's Verification of CRR & SLR (Statutory Central Auditor's Role)

The Statutory Central Auditor is required to verify and issue an independent certificate/report on SLR compliance on 12 alternate Fridays throughout the year:

  1. Testing the NDTL Computation: Sample-check the calculation of NDTL on selected reporting alternate Fridays (Form A / Form VIII).
  2. Verification of Eligible Assets: Verify book values and market values of unencumbered government securities, physical cash counts, and RBI statement balances.
  3. Checking for Encumbrances: Ensure that securities pledged for borrowing (e.g., Repo under LAF or MSF) are excluded from eligible SLR assets!
  4. Penalties for Default: Check whether the bank defaulted on any day and whether penal interest under Section 42(3) of RBI Act or Section 24 of BR Act was appropriately provided for.
Section 4 • Asset Verification

Verification of Cash, Bank Balances & Investments

1. Cash and Balances with RBI (Schedule 6) & Other Banks (Schedule 7)

Cash and liquid balances form the primary defense for bank liquidity. The auditor must execute strict substantive procedures:

Cash & Currency Chest Balances (Schedule 6)

  • Physical Cash Count: Mandatory physical cash count at the close of business on March 31 simultaneously across all tills, vault, and ATMs.
  • Discrepancies: Cash shortages/surpluses must be investigated, booked to Cash Suspense, and reported in the LFAR.
  • Insurance Limits: Verify that cash holding limits stipulated by Head Office and cash transit insurance limits have not been breached.
  • RBI Balances: Obtain independent balance confirmation certificates from the RBI and test bank reconciliations.

Balances with Other Banks & Call Money (Schedule 7)

  • Direct Confirmations: Obtain direct external balance confirmations from correspondent banks in India and abroad.
  • Nostro & Vostro Accounts: Verify monthly reconciliation of foreign currency Nostro accounts; investigate long-pending un-reconciled debit entries (which may indicate unauthorized fund siphoning or un-booked charges).
  • Money at Call & Short Notice: Verify call money borrowing/lending receipts, interest rates, and compliance with RBI prudential limits on call money borrowing.

2. Verification of Investments (Schedule 8) & RBI Categorization

In accordance with RBI Master Directions on Prudential Norms for Classification, Valuation, and Operation of Investment Portfolios, all bank investments must be classified into three distinct categories on acquisition date:

Investment Category Characteristics & Regulatory Ceiling Valuation Rules & Accounting Treatment
1. Held to Maturity (HTM) Securities acquired with the positive intention to hold until maturity. Maximum ceiling prescribed by RBI (e.g., eligible SLR securities up to prescribed cap). Carried at acquisition cost / amortized cost. Premium on acquisition is amortized over the remaining maturity period. Discount is ignored. Tested for permanent impairment.
2. Available for Sale (AFS) Securities not classified under HTM or HFT. Held for liquidity and strategic portfolio management. Marked-to-market (MTM) at least quarterly. Valuation is done category-wise. Net depreciation is provided in P&L; net appreciation is ignored.
3. Held for Trading (HFT) Securities acquired with the intention to trade and take advantage of short-term price/interest rate movements. Must be liquidated within 90 days. Marked-to-market at least monthly (or daily). Valuation is done category-wise. Net depreciation charged to P&L; net appreciation ignored.

Critical Treasury & Investment Audit Checks

  • Shifting Between Categories: Shifting from AFS/HFT to HTM is permitted only once a year, normally at the beginning of the financial year, with the prior explicit approval of the Board of Directors. Shifting is done at the lower of acquisition cost or market value, and any depreciation is charged immediately to P&L!
  • Bank Receipts (BRs): BRs must be issued on special security paper; valid for 30 days only. BRs outstanding for more than 30 days are irregular and require immediate investigation!
  • Physical Verification / Demat Holding: Verify physical certificates or demat holding statements from PDO (Public Debt Office of RBI), NSDL, and CDSL.
  • Segregation of Treasury Duties: Ensure strict Chinese walls between the Front Office (dealing/trading), Mid Office (risk management/monitoring), and Back Office (settlement, confirmations, and accounting).
Section 5 • Core Lending Operations

Verification of Advances (Schedule 9) & RBI IRAC Norms

1. Structure of Advances: Funded vs. Non-Funded

Advances represent the largest asset class on a bank's balance sheet and carry the highest credit risk:

Funded Credit Facilities
  • Cash Credits & Overdrafts (CC/OD): Running revolving working capital limits against hypothecation of inventory and book debts.
  • Term Loans: Fixed tenure loans for capital expenditure, repayable in installments over multiple years.
  • Bills Purchased & Discounted: Financing genuine commercial trade bills of exchange.
Non-Funded Facilities
  • Letters of Credit (LCs): Financial commitments to pay sellers upon delivery of compliant shipping documents.
  • Bank Guarantees (BGs): Financial guarantees and Performance guarantees issued on behalf of customers.
  • Risk: Non-funded limits crystallize into funded advances if the borrower defaults or invokes the guarantee!

2. RBI Prudential Norms on Income Recognition, Asset Classification & Provisioning (IRAC)

Formal Definition of Non-Performing Asset (NPA)

A Non-Performing Asset (NPA) is a loan or an advance where it ceases to generate income for the bank. Once an account becomes an NPA, interest income CANNOT be recognized on an accrual basis; it can be recognized strictly on actual cash realization basis!

The 90-Day Overdue Criteria Across Credit Facilities

Facility Type Exact Trigger Point for Classification as Non-Performing Asset (NPA)
Term Loans Interest and/or installment of principal remains overdue for more than 90 days.
Cash Credit / Overdraft
("Out of Order")		 The account remains "Out of Order" if:
  • The outstanding balance remains continuously in excess of the sanctioned limit or Drawing Power (DP) for more than 90 days; OR
  • There are no credits in the account for more than 90 days; OR
  • Credits are insufficient to cover the interest debited during the previous 90-day period.
Bills Purchased & Discounted The bill remains overdue and unpaid for more than 90 days from due date.
Agricultural Advances
  • Short Duration Crops: Interest and/or installment remains overdue for two (2) crop seasons.
  • Long Duration Crops (> 1 yr crop season): Overdue for one (1) crop season.
Derivative Transactions Overdue receivables representing positive mark-to-market value remain unpaid for more than 90 days.
⚠️ Drawing Power (DP) & Old Stock Statements Rules
  • 3-Month Stock Statement Limit: Stock statements must not be older than 3 months. If stock statements are older than 3 months (even if renewal is under process), the drawing power becomes NIL, and if the account is running with a debit balance, it becomes an Out of Order NPA!
  • 180-Day Limit for Renewal: If credit limits are due for annual renewal and are not renewed within 180 days (6 months) from the due date, the account must be mandatorily classified as an NPA!

3. Master Classification Categories & RBI Provisioning Matrix

Banks must classify their loan assets into four progressive categories and make mandatory provisions:

Asset Category Classification Criteria Secured Portion Provision Unsecured Portion Provision
1. Standard Assets
(Performing)		 Account does not disclose any problems and does not carry more than normal banking risk. Specific General Provisions:
• Direct Advances to Agriculture & SME: 0.25%
• Commercial Real Estate – Residential (CRE-RH): 0.75%
• Commercial Real Estate (CRE): 1.00%
• Restructured Standard Accounts: 5.00%
• All other Standard Advances: 0.40%
2. Sub-Standard Assets
(NPA)		 Classified as an NPA for a period ≤ 12 months. 15% provision on total outstanding. 25% provision on unsecured ab-initio advances.
(20% for infrastructure loans).
3. Doubtful Assets – D1 Remained in Sub-standard category for 12 months (Doubtful up to 1 year). 25% of secured portion. 100% of unsecured portion.
3. Doubtful Assets – D2 Doubtful for 1 year to 3 years. 40% of secured portion. 100% of unsecured portion.
3. Doubtful Assets – D3 Doubtful for more than 3 years. 100% of secured portion. 100% of unsecured portion.
4. Loss Assets Identified as uncollectible by bank, auditors, or RBI inspectors, but not wholly written off. 100% PROVISION on entire outstanding balance!
Calculation of Provision on Doubtful Advances: Dual-Tier Rule

For all Doubtful advances (D1, D2, D3), the provision is calculated in two separate steps:

  1. First, determine the Realizable Value of Security. That portion is the "Secured Portion", provisioned at 25%, 40%, or 100% based on the age of doubtful status.
  2. The remaining excess of total outstanding over the realizable security value is the Unsecured Portion, which attracts a flat 100% PROVISION across ALL doubtful categories!

4. Special Advance Categories & Unique Classification Protocols

Special Case Mandatory RBI IRAC Classification & Income Recognition Rule
1. Consortium Advances Each member bank must classify the borrower account and recognize income based strictly on its own record of recovery, regardless of whether the Lead Bank has classified the account as standard or performing!
2. Central Government Guaranteed Advances The advance is treated as a Standard Asset for classification purposes so long as the guarantee has not been repudiated. However, interest income CANNOT be recognized on accrual basis if overdue > 90 days; income is recognized strictly on cash realization basis!
3. State Government Guaranteed Advances If interest and/or principal remains overdue for more than 90 days, the advance becomes a Non-Performing Asset (NPA) for BOTH asset classification and provisioning purposes (no sovereign exemption)!
4. Advances Against FDRs, NSCs, Life Policies Advances against term deposits, National Savings Certificates, and surrender value of LIC policies need NOT be treated as NPAs, provided adequate margin is available. Income can be recognized on accrual basis.
5. Upgradation of NPAs An account classified as NPA can be upgraded to standard category ONLY IF the entire arrears of interest and principal are fully paid by the borrower! Partial recovery does not entitle upgradation.
6. Restructuring of Advances (Prudential Framework June 7, 2019) Any standard advance that is restructured (granting financial concessions due to borrower's financial difficulty) must be immediately downgraded to Sub-Standard upon restructuring, and provided for at 5% minimum!

Exam Case Scenario (TYU 1)

During the statutory branch audit of a nationalized bank, the auditor examined 5 major loan accounts. All 5 accounts were operating positively with timely servicing of interest and principal. However, the auditor noticed that the loan application forms were filled in incompletely, financial statements were not signed by borrowers, and there were no records of assessment of enhanced working capital requirements in the credit files. What action should the auditor take? Does this warrant classifying the accounts as NPA?

ICAI Model Evaluation:

  1. No Change in Asset Classification: An account becomes an NPA only when it ceases to generate income. Since all 5 accounts are operating satisfactorily with prompt recoveries, there is no reason to classify them as NPAs. They remain Standard Assets.
  2. Severe Deficiencies in Internal Controls: The incomplete loan documentation and absence of working capital assessments reflect critical deficiencies in the bank's Credit Appraisal Mechanism.
  3. LFAR Reporting: The branch auditor must highlight these operational and credit appraisal lapses in the Long Form Audit Report (LFAR) under the Advances section for corrective action by higher management.
Section 6 • Balance Sheet Integrity

Other Assets & Non-Banking Assets (Section 9, BR Act)

1. Non-Banking Assets Acquired in Satisfaction of Claims (Section 9, BR Act)

When borrowers default on secured loans, banks frequently enforce mortgages and acquire properties, land, or machinery in settlement of their outstanding claims:

Section 9 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 – Statutory Holding Cap

No banking company shall hold any immovable property, howsoever acquired (except such as is required for its own use), for any period exceeding seven (7) years from the date of acquisition!

Extension: The RBI may grant an extension for a further period not exceeding 5 years if satisfied that disposal within 7 years would cause severe financial loss.

Auditor's Verification Procedures for Non-Banking Assets:

  • Balance Sheet Classification: Recorded under Schedule 11 (Other Assets) as "Non-Banking Assets acquired in satisfaction of claims" (NOT under Schedule 10 Fixed Assets!).
  • Valuation at Lower of Cost or Market Value: Recorded at the lower of the net book value of the loan or estimated net realizable/market value. If market value falls below book value, write-down to P&L is mandatory.
  • Legal Title & Dispute Disclosures: Check title deeds and examine if third-party litigation or ownership disputes exist. Any title dispute must be disclosed in the financial statement notes as a contingent liability or matter of significance.
  • Enforcing the 7-Year Disposal Period: Verify the acquisition date. If held for > 7 years without RBI extension approval, report the non-compliance under the BR Act!
Section 7 • Solvency & Capital

Verification of Capital, Liabilities & BASEL III Framework

1. Capital (Schedule 1), Stress Testing & BASEL III Framework

Capital represents the foundational shock-absorber for commercial banks against unanticipated credit, market, and operational losses:

Stress Testing Framework

Banks are required by RBI to conduct rigorous, regular Stress Tests under plausible extreme economic scenarios (e.g., sharp spike in NPAs, sudden interest rate spikes, massive liquidity outflows).

  • Assesses the resilience of bank capital and liquidity under adverse market conditions.
  • Forms an integral component of the Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP).

The 3 Pillars of Basel III Framework

  • Pillar 1: Minimum Capital Requirements: Prescribes capital ratios against Credit, Market, and Operational Risk.
  • Pillar 2: Supervisory Review Process: RBI evaluates internal risk assessments and ICAAP of banks.
  • Pillar 3: Market Discipline: Comprehensive public disclosures regarding risk exposures and capital computation.

Basel III Capital Ratios Mandated by RBI:

Capital Tier / Buffer Minimum Ratio to RWA Eligible Capital Instruments & Function
Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) 5.50% Paid-up equity share capital, share premium, statutory reserves, capital reserves, and audited retained earnings. Highest loss-absorbing capability.
Tier 1 Capital (CET1 + AT1) 7.00% CET1 plus Additional Tier 1 (AT1) capital instruments (Perpetual Non-Cumulative Preference Shares and Perpetual Debt Instruments with loss-absorption triggers).
Total Capital (Tier 1 + Tier 2) 9.00% Tier 1 plus Tier 2 capital (General provisions/loss reserves up to 1.25% of credit RWA, Revaluation reserves at 55% discount, Subordinated term debt).
Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB) 2.50% Composed entirely of CET1 capital. Designed to absorb losses during periods of severe financial and economic stress.
Total Capital + CCB (Regulatory Minimum) 11.50% Minimum overall capital requirement that Indian commercial banks must maintain against Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA).
Countercyclical Capital Buffer (CCCB) 0% to 2.50% Activated by RBI during periods of excessive credit growth to build buffers against cyclical downturns.

2. Reserves & Surplus (Schedule 2) & Deposits (Schedule 3)

Statutory Reserve (Section 17, BR Act)

Every banking company incorporated in India must create a reserve fund and transfer a sum equivalent to not less than 20% of its net profits of each year (before declaring any dividend) to this fund.

RBI Enhancement: To strengthen bank balance sheets, RBI guidelines mandate that banks transfer at least 25% of net profits to the Statutory Reserve!

Inoperative Accounts & DEAF Scheme

  • Inoperative / Dormant Accounts: A savings or current account is classified as inoperative if there are no customer-induced transactions for more than 2 years. Interest credited by the bank is not a customer transaction. Segregated ledger and dual authorization for activation required.
  • DEAF Transfer (Section 26A, BR Act): Any deposit account that remains unclaimed or inoperative for ten (10) years or more must be transferred to the Depositor Education and Awareness Fund (DEAF) within 3 months.

3. Contingent Liabilities (Schedule 12) & Claims Not Acknowledged

Schedule 12 covers obligations that may crystallize depending on future uncertain events. The auditor must verify the following distinct categories:

Contingent Liability Head Auditor's Verification Procedures & Risk Considerations
1. Claims Not Acknowledged as Debts Review pending court cases, disputed customer claims, consumer disputes, ex-employee wage litigations, and disputed tax assessments (Direct/Indirect taxes). Verify legal opinions from bank's counsel; ensure adequate provision if liability is probable under AS 29 / Ind AS 37.
2. Guarantees Given on Behalf of Customers Distinguish between Financial Guarantees (direct credit substitutes) and Performance Guarantees (bid bonds, EPC contracts). Verify margin money held in term deposits, counter-indemnities signed by customers, and ensure expired guarantees are promptly reversed from off-balance-sheet ledgers.
3. Letters of Credit (LCs) & Acceptances Verify outstanding documentary credits, underlying trade contracts, bills accepted on behalf of customers, and verify that non-funded LC limits are included in total borrower exposure calculations.
4. Forward Exchange Contracts Examine outstanding foreign currency forward sale and purchase contracts; verify mark-to-market valuations; check compliance with FEDAI guidelines and RBI limits on Net Open Positions (NOP).
Section 8 • Reporting Mandates

Auditor's Report, Long Form Audit Report (LFAR) & Fraud Reporting

1. Statutory Audit Report & Reporting under Section 30 of BR Act

The statutory auditor of a banking company must report in accordance with Section 30 of the BR Act, 1949 and Section 143 of the Companies Act, 2013:

  • Whether the Balance Sheet presents a true and fair view of the state of affairs of the bank as of March 31, and the P&L Account shows a true balance of profit or loss for the year.
  • Whether transactions of the bank that have come to notice are within the powers of the bank.
  • Whether proper returns adequate for the purposes of audit have been received from branches.
  • Whether the financial statements exhibit a true and fair view in accordance with the accounting standards and RBI rules.

2. Long Form Audit Report (LFAR) – The Comprehensive Questionnaire

In addition to the main statutory audit report, the RBI requires both Statutory Central Auditors (SCAs) and Statutory Branch Auditors (SBAs) to submit a detailed Long Form Audit Report (LFAR):

Operational Architecture of LFAR
  • Questionnaire Format: The LFAR is structured as a detailed questionnaire designed by the RBI covering all key operational and risk facets of branch and bank operations.
  • Coverage in Branch LFAR: Cash and security items, clearing operations, deposits, detailed scrutiny of advances (credit appraisal, sanctioning, documentation, post-disbursement supervision, NPAs, stock audits, restructuring), non-funded facilities, foreign exchange, internal controls, housekeeping, and fraud occurrences.
  • Specific Comments vs. General Observations: The auditor must not give vague or evasive replies (like "refer branch records" or "satisfactory"). Specific instances, account numbers, amounts, and dates must be cited!
  • Submission Deadlines: Branch LFAR must be submitted to the Branch Manager and SCAs within the stipulated time frame to enable consolidation at the Head Office level.

3. Reporting of Frauds to RBI & Red Flagged Accounts (RFAs)

Statutory auditors have distinct statutory and regulatory reporting duties regarding frauds detected during bank audits:

RBI Framework for Classification & Reporting of Frauds
  • Reporting to Central Board / Management: If an auditor notices any fraud or suspects fraudulent transactions, they must immediately report the full details to the Chairman/MD, CEO, and the Audit Committee of the Board (ACB).
  • Section 143(12) of Companies Act: For frauds involving an amount of ₹ 1 Crore or more, report to the Central Government (Ministry of Corporate Affairs) in Form ADT-4 within 60 days.
  • Early Warning Signals (EWS) & Red Flagged Accounts (RFAs): An account where an early warning signal of fraud is detected must be classified as an RFA on the Central Fraud Registry (CFR) portal. A forensic audit must be commissioned within 15 days, and if fraud is confirmed, it must be reported to the RBI within 7 days of confirmation!
Section 9 • Real-Time Assurance

Concurrent Audit System in Banks & Audit Committee

1. Concurrent Audit System in Commercial Banks (RBI Guidelines)

Concurrent Audit is a systematic and timely examination of financial transactions on a continuous or real-time basis. Unlike post-audit, concurrent audit aims to shorten the time lag between transaction occurrence and its independent verification, catching irregularities before damage occurs.

Mandatory Scope & Branch Coverage

  • Branches covering at least 70% of total advances of the bank.
  • Branches covering at least 70% of total deposits of the bank.
  • 100% of Treasury operations, Dealing Rooms, and Forex branches.
  • 100% of Stressed Asset Management Branches and specialized large corporate lending hubs.

Core Functional Activities Covered

  • Cash & Vault: Daily physical verification, adherence to retention limits.
  • Investments: Verification of purchase/sale deals, MTM valuations.
  • Advances: Pre-disbursement checks, sanction adherence, title deeds, stock inspections.
  • Forex: NOOP limits, import/export remittances, Nostro reconciliations.

2. Role of the Audit Committee of the Board (ACB)

The Audit Committee of the Board (ACB) operates as the premier governance body in a commercial bank:

  • Reviews the half-yearly and annual financial statements before recommending them to the Board.
  • Monitors the compliance with statutory audit observations, LFAR findings, and RBI Annual Financial Inspection (AFI) reports.
  • Oversees the functioning of the internal inspection machinery, risk-based internal audit (RBIA), and concurrent audit system.
  • Reviews serious irregularities and frauds exceeding specified thresholds.

Exam Case Scenario (TYU 2)

You have been appointed to conduct a revenue audit / fee leakage verification of a large commercial branch of a nationalized bank. Enumerate the critical areas of advances and fees that you must scrutinize to ensure the bank has correctly levied and recovered its income without revenue leakages.

ICAI Model Audit Verification Checklist:

  1. Interest Rates Fed in CBS: Verify that interest rates entered into the core banking system match the sanction letters, and verify proper compounding intervals.
  2. Processing & Upfront Fees: Check that upfront processing fees, appraisal charges, and annual renewal charges were levied in strict accordance with bank circulars.
  3. Penal Interest on Stock Statements: Check whether penal interest was levied on borrowers who failed to submit monthly stock statements within the stipulated deadlines.
  4. Overdue Interest on Export Credit: Verify levy of overdue interest on export packing credit and export bills purchased running beyond the concessional period.
  5. Commission on LCs and Bank Guarantees: Verify that commitment fees and guarantee commissions were collected upfront for the entire validity period.

Exam Case Scenario (TYU 3)

CA Neha was appointed by a bank to conduct a stock audit of an agro-processing firm enjoying a Cash Credit facility of ₹ 10 Crores against hypothecation of rice stocks. Neha verified the physical count of gunny bags in the warehouse, counted 50,000 bags matching the stock statement, and certified the drawing power.
