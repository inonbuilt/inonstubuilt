Opening Scenario: CA A at WIRC Seminar on Audit Conclusions & Reporting

CA A attended a prestigious national seminar on “Audit Conclusions and Reporting—Responsibilities of Auditors in the Context of SA 700 Series” organized by the local branch of the Western India Regional Council (WIRC) of ICAI. Realizing the heightened regulatory glare (NFRA, SEBI, MCA, RBI), he noted the following vital takeaways from eminent experts:

  • Paramount Significance: The Audit Report is the formal document by means of which an auditor communicates the opinion formed on the financial statements. Its importance is paramount in capital markets.
  • No Hesitation on Adverse Opinion: If evidence reveals misstatements that are both material and pervasive, the auditor must not hesitate to express an Adverse Opinion in terms of SA 705.
  • Challenging Going Concern Assumptions: Auditors must rigorously challenge management’s key assumptions. If the use of the going concern assumption is inappropriate, an adverse opinion is non-negotiable under SA 570.
  • EOM is No Substitute for Modification: As highlighted by SA 706, an Emphasis of Matter (EOM) paragraph can NEVER be used for matters requiring an audit modification. It is strictly a pointer to an already appropriately disclosed matter of fundamental importance.
  • Communicative Value via Key Audit Matters (KAM): Under SA 701, listed entity audit reports must transparently share matters of most significance and describe exactly how the auditor addressed them.
SA 700 (Revised)

SA 700 (Revised): Forming an Opinion & Reporting on Financial Statements

SA 700 (Revised) deals with the auditor’s responsibility to form an opinion on the financial statements based on an evaluation of the audit conclusions drawn from the audit evidence obtained, and to issue a clearly written report that describes the basis for that opinion.

Core Objectives of the Auditor under SA 700
  1. To form an opinion on the financial statements based on an evaluation of the conclusions drawn from the audit evidence obtained; and
  2. To express clearly that opinion through a written report that also describes the basis for the opinion.

To form that opinion, the auditor must conclude whether reasonable assurance has been obtained that the financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, taking into account materiality, accounting policies consistency, reasonableness of estimates, and adequate disclosures.

📋 Basic Elements of the Independent Auditor's Report (All 12 Elements)

SA 700 (Revised) mandates a standardized structure for the independent auditor's report to promote consistency and user understanding across global and domestic markets:

Basic Element Statutory Requirements & Contents Exam / Practical Significance
1. Title Must clearly have a title that indicates it is the "Independent Auditor’s Report". Affirms the auditor’s independence and objective professional standing.
2. Addressee Addressed as required by the engagement circumstances. In a company audit, addressed to the "Members of the Company" (Shareholders). Reports u/s 143 are for shareholders, not directors or management.
3. Auditor's Opinion Placed in the first section of the report under the heading "Opinion". Identifies:
• The entity whose financial statements have been audited;
• State that the financial statements have been audited;
• Title of each statement comprising the financial statements;
• Specify the date or period covered;
• State whether statements give a true and fair view in accordance with applicable financial reporting framework (Ind AS / AS). 		Opinion is brought right to the front to provide immediate clarity to stakeholders.
4. Basis for Opinion Immediately follows the Opinion section under the heading "Basis for Opinion":
• States audit was conducted in accordance with Standards on Auditing (SAs);
• Refers to auditor’s responsibilities section;
• Includes independence statement under ICAI Code of Ethics & relevant statutory ethical requirements;
• States evidence obtained is sufficient and appropriate. 		Mandatory ethical and professional benchmark grounding the audit opinion.
5. Going Concern Where applicable, reporting in accordance with SA 570 (Revised) under the specific heading "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" (MURGC). Draws attention to note disclosures without modifying the opinion.
6. Key Audit Matters (KAM) For audits of complete sets of general purpose financial statements of listed entities, report in accordance with SA 701 under the heading "Key Audit Matters". Mandatory for listed entities; prohibited in Disclaimers unless required by law.
7. Other Information Where applicable, reporting in accordance with SA 720 (Revised) covering financial and non-financial information included in the Annual Report. Separate section detailing auditor and management responsibilities for Annual Report.
8. Responsibilities of Management & TCWG Describes management's responsibility for:
• Preparation of financial statements giving true and fair view;
• Maintenance of adequate accounting records and internal controls;
• Assessing going concern ability and making disclosures. Identifies TCWG oversight. 		Reinforces that financial statements are primarily the responsibility of management.
9. Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit States objectives of the auditor (obtain reasonable assurance, issue report). Explains:
• Reasonable assurance is high but not a guarantee;
• Materiality definition;
• Exercise of professional judgment and professional skepticism;
• Procedures performed, internal control consideration, communication with TCWG. 		Can be presented in body, appendix, or by specific cross-reference to website.
10. Other Reporting Responsibilities Addresses statutory duties under Section 143(1), 143(3), Rule 11, and CARO, 2020 under the heading "Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements". Separates SAs reporting from statutory/legal reporting requirements in India.
11. Signature of the Auditor Signed in the personal name of the Chartered Accountant and in the name of the audit firm, along with Membership Number and firm Registration Number. Mandatory mention of UDIN (Unique Document Identification Number).
12. Place & Date of the Report • Place: City where report is signed.
• Date: Date when auditor obtained SAAE, including approval of financial statements by TCWG. 		Audit report cannot pre-date the approval of financial statements by Board!
📝 TEST YOUR UNDERSTANDING 1: Inclusion of Internal Control Communication in Audit Report
▼

Scenario: The auditors of a listed company have affirmed in their audit report communication of significant audit findings, including significant deficiencies in internal control of the company identified to those charged with governance. Where are such matters included in the audit report of a listed company? Also dwell upon the importance of such communication.

✔ Master Solution & SA 700 Application:

1. Location in the Audit Report:

  • Such matters are in the nature of auditor's responsibilities and are formally stated in “The Auditor’s Responsibilities for the Audit of the Financial Statements” section of the auditor's report in accordance with SA 700 (Revised).
  • In this section, the auditor explicitly states: "We also communicate with those charged with governance regarding, among other matters, the planned scope and timing of the audit and significant audit findings, including any significant deficiencies in internal control that we identify during our audit."

2. Importance of Such Communication (SA 260 & SA 265):

  • Communication of significant audit findings and internal control deficiencies to TCWG is a cornerstone of corporate governance oversight.
  • It assists Those Charged with Governance in fulfilling their statutory and supervisory responsibility to oversee the financial reporting process and take timely corrective remedial measures against operational vulnerabilities.
SA 701

SA 701: Communicating Key Audit Matters in the Independent Auditor's Report

SA 701 deals with the auditor’s responsibility to communicate Key Audit Matters (KAM) in the auditor’s report. It is intended to address both the auditor’s judgment as to what to communicate and the form and content of such communication.

Definition & Mandatory Applicability of Key Audit Matters

Key Audit Matters (KAM): Those matters that, in the auditor’s professional judgment, were of most significance in the audit of the financial statements of the current period. Key audit matters are selected from matters communicated with Those Charged with Governance.

Statutory Applicability:

  • Mandatory for audits of complete sets of general purpose financial statements of listed entities.
  • Also applicable when the auditor is required by law or regulation to communicate KAM, or when the auditor decides to communicate KAM voluntarily for unlisted public or private entities.
🔍 Determining & Communicating Key Audit Matters

The auditor determines KAM through a structured three-step funnel:

The Three-Tier KAM Funnel
  1. Step 1: Universe of Matters Communicated with TCWG: The entire set of significant audit findings, risks, and governance discussions under SA 260.
  2. Step 2: Matters That Required Significant Auditor Attention: The auditor considers:
    • Areas of higher assessed risk of material misstatement or significant risks identified under SA 315.
    • Significant auditor judgments relating to areas in the financial statements that involved significant management judgment, including accounting estimates identified as having high estimation uncertainty (SA 540).
    • The effect on the audit of significant events or transactions that occurred during the period (e.g., major acquisitions, restructuring).
  3. Step 3: Matters of MOST Significance: From the matters requiring significant attention, the auditor selects those of most significance in the audit of the financial statements of the current period $implies$ Key Audit Matters!
✍️ How Key Audit Matters are Described in the Report

For each Key Audit Matter communicated in the KAM section, the auditor must clearly provide:

  • An introductory reference to the related disclosure(s) in the financial statements, if any.
  • Why the matter was considered to be one of most significance in the audit (the rationale/complexity).
  • How the matter was addressed in the audit (a summary of the auditor’s response, key substantive procedures performed, and outcome).
What KAM is NOT (Strict Prohibitions under SA 701)

Communicating Key Audit Matters is NOT:

  1. A substitute for disclosures in the financial statements that the applicable financial reporting framework requires management to make;
  2. A substitute for the auditor expressing a modified opinion when required by the circumstances of a specific audit engagement in accordance with SA 705 (Revised);
  3. A substitute for reporting in accordance with SA 570 (Revised) when a material uncertainty exists relating to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on an entity’s ability to continue as a going concern; or
  4. A separate opinion on individual matters!

Absolute Prohibition: When the auditor disclaims an opinion on the financial statements in accordance with SA 705, the auditor’s report SHALL NOT include a Key Audit Matters section unless required by law or regulation!

📝 TEST YOUR UNDERSTANDING 2: Ind AS 115 Complex Revenue Recognition as KAM
▼

Scenario: Below is a draft extract of an audit report of a listed company. Para (A) reflects certain matters communicated with the CFO and Para (B) is the auditor’s response:
(A) The Company recognizes revenues when control of goods is transferred to the customer at the net consideration expected to be received in accordance with contracts terms. Terms of sales arrangements and nature of discounts/rebates create complexities requiring judgment in determining revenues.
(B) We read the Company’s revenue recognition policy and assessed compliance with Ind AS 115. We assessed design and tested operating effectiveness of internal controls related to sales and rebates/discounts. We tested on a sample basis that revenue has been recognized in the proper period with supporting customer confirmations.
Identify what auditors are trying to report and under what heading such matter should be reflected.

✔ Master Solution & SA 701 Identification:

1. Identification of Matter: The matter described is a classic Key Audit Matter (KAM) under SA 701.

2. Section Heading in Report: It must be reported under the dedicated section heading: “Key Audit Matters”.

3. SA 701 Rationale:

  • Para (A) explains why the matter was considered to be one of most significance (complex terms of sales contracts, discounting mechanisms, and estimation judgments under Ind AS 115).
  • Para (B) explains how the matter was addressed in the audit (testing design and operating effectiveness of internal controls, sample substantive verification, customer confirmations, and evaluating accounting policy compliance).
SA 705 (Revised)

SA 705 (Revised): Modifications to the Opinion in the Independent Auditor's Report

SA 705 (Revised) deals with the auditor’s responsibility to issue an appropriate report in circumstances when, in forming an opinion in accordance with SA 700 (Revised), the auditor concludes that a modification to the auditor’s opinion on the financial statements is necessary.

Types of Modified Opinions under SA 705

There are three types of modified opinions:

  1. Qualified Opinion: Expressed when misstatements or lack of evidence are material but NOT pervasive.
  2. Adverse Opinion: Expressed when misstatements are BOTH material AND pervasive to the financial statements.
  3. Disclaimer of Opinion: Expressed when inability to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence is BOTH material AND pervasive, or in extremely rare cases of multiple uncertainties.
⚖️ Circumstances When Modification is Required

The auditor shall modify the opinion in the auditor’s report when:

  • Scenario A: The auditor concludes that, based on the audit evidence obtained, the financial statements as a whole are not free from material misstatement (accounting policy violation, misapplication, or inadequate disclosure); OR
  • Scenario B: The auditor is unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to conclude that the financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement (limitation on scope).
📊 The Master Pervasiveness Decision Matrix
Definition of "Pervasive" under SAs

Pervasive effects on the financial statements are those that, in the auditor’s judgment:

  1. Are not confined to specific elements, accounts or items of the financial statements;
  2. If so confined, represent or could represent a substantial proportion of the financial statements; or
  3. In relation to disclosures, are fundamental to users’ understanding of the financial statements.
Nature of Matter Giving Rise to Modification Material but NOT Pervasive Material AND Pervasive
Financial Statements are Materially Misstated
(Departure from AS/Ind AS, wrong valuation, omitted notes) 		Qualified Opinion

📝 Form and Content of the Auditor’s Report When Opinion is Modified

When the auditor modifies the opinion, specific structural heading and text changes are strictly mandatory:

Mandatory Heading & Wording Changes
  • Opinion Section Heading: Changed to "Qualified Opinion", "Adverse Opinion", or "Disclaimer of Opinion".
  • Basis Section Heading: Changed to "Basis for Qualified Opinion", "Basis for Adverse Opinion", or "Basis for Disclaimer of Opinion".
  • Quantification Requirement: If there is a material misstatement of the financial statements that relates to specific amounts, the auditor shall include in the Basis section a quantification of the financial effects of the misstatement. If not practicable to quantify, the auditor shall explicitly state so!
  • Amendments in Disclaimer of Opinion:
    • The Opinion section states that the auditor does not express an opinion on the financial statements because of the significance of the matter(s).
    • The Auditor’s Responsibilities section is substantially truncated to state only: (a) that the auditor’s responsibility is to conduct an audit under SAs and issue a report; and (b) because of the matter(s) described in the Basis for Disclaimer section, the auditor was not able to obtain SAAE.
    • NO Key Audit Matters (KAM) section shall be included unless required by law!
🚪 Inability to Obtain SAAE Due to Management Restriction & Resignation
Management Imposed Limitation After Acceptance

If, after accepting the engagement, the auditor becomes aware that management has imposed a limitation on the scope of the audit that is likely to result in a Qualified or Disclaimer of Opinion:

  1. Request management to remove the limitation.
  2. If management refuses, communicate the matter to Those Charged with Governance (TCWG) and determine whether alternative procedures can be performed.
  3. If unable to obtain SAAE and effects are both material and pervasive:
    • Withdraw from the audit where practicable and permitted by applicable law or regulation.
    • If withdrawal is not practicable or possible before issuing report (e.g., public sector auditor or advanced stage): Express a Disclaimer of Opinion!
SA 706 (Revised)

SA 706 (Revised): Emphasis of Matter (EOM) & Other Matter (OM) Paragraphs

SA 706 (Revised) deals with additional communication in the auditor’s report when the auditor considers it necessary to draw users’ attention to a matter or matters presented or disclosed in the financial statements, or to matters other than those presented or disclosed.

Core Definitions under SA 706
  • Emphasis of Matter (EOM) Paragraph: A paragraph included in the auditor’s report that refers to a matter appropriately presented or disclosed in the financial statements that, in the auditor’s judgment, is of such importance that it is fundamental to users’ understanding of the financial statements.
  • Other Matter (OM) Paragraph: A paragraph included in the auditor’s report that refers to a matter other than those presented or disclosed in the financial statements that, in the auditor’s judgment, is relevant to users’ understanding of the audit, the auditor’s responsibilities, or the auditor’s report.
⚠️ The Three Iron-Clad Conditions for Using EOM Paragraphs
Mandatory Pre-Conditions for EOM

The auditor shall include an Emphasis of Matter paragraph in the auditor’s report ONLY IF:

  1. The matter is appropriately presented or disclosed in the financial statements (it points directly to a Note in the accounts);
  2. The auditor would NOT be required to modify the opinion in accordance with SA 705 (Revised) as a result of the matter; AND
  3. The matter has NOT been determined to be a Key Audit Matter (KAM) to be communicated in the auditor's report under SA 701!

Golden Exam Rule: An Emphasis of Matter paragraph is NO SUBSTITUTE for a modified opinion under SA 705, nor is it a substitute for disclosures that management is required to make!

🎯 Examples of Circumstances Where EOM is Appropriate
  • An uncertainty relating to the future outcome of exceptional litigation or regulatory action.
  • A significant subsequent event that occurs between the date of the financial statements and the date of the auditor’s report.
  • Early application (where permitted) of a new accounting standard that has a material effect on the financial statements.
  • A major catastrophe (e.g., massive fire, flood, cyclone) that has had, or continues to have, a significant effect on the entity’s financial position.
📄 Placement and Structure of EOM and OM Paragraphs
Structural Drafting Rules

When including an EOM paragraph, the auditor shall:

  • Include the paragraph within a separate section with an appropriate heading that includes the term "Emphasis of Matter".
  • Include a clear reference to the matter being emphasized and to the related disclosure in the financial statements where the matter is discussed.
  • Indicate clearly: "Our opinion is not modified in respect of this matter."

When including an OM paragraph:

  • Include within a separate section with the heading "Other Matter".
  • Common uses: Predecessor auditor audited prior period figures (SA 710), prior period un-audited, reporting on multiple financial reporting frameworks, restrictions on report distribution.
📝 TEST YOUR UNDERSTANDING 3: PTD Ltd Construction Claims, Arbitration & EOM Para
▼

Scenario: PTD Limited executes construction contracts. Non-current receivables outstanding for more than 3 years include claims under arbitration. The auditor relied solely on a management representation letter stating that the claims will be fully realized. Without performing any other procedures (verifying contracts, arbitration progress, legal counsel opinions), the auditor proposes to issue an unmodified report with an Emphasis of Matter paragraph drawing attention to the non-current receivables note. Is the auditor's proposal justified?

✔ Master Solution & SA 706 / SA 580 Application:

1. Auditor's Action is Unjustified & Legally Flawed:

  • Under SA 580, written representations do NOT provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence on their own. The auditor cannot substitute management representations for independent substantive verification of arbitration claims, contract terms, and legal status.
  • Under SA 706, an Emphasis of Matter paragraph can be included ONLY IF the auditor concludes that the matter does not require a modified opinion under SA 705.
  • Because the auditor failed to perform essential audit procedures, there is an inability to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence. Performing necessary procedures might reveal that the claims are doubtful or bogus, requiring a provision and a Modified Opinion.

Conclusion: An EOM paragraph cannot be used to bypass audit verification or hide a scope limitation. The auditor must first perform independent procedures to obtain SAAE. Only if no modification is warranted may an EOM paragraph be considered.

Core Concepts

Distinctions: Notes on Accounts vs Qualifications & Report vs Certificate

📌 Distinction Between Notes on Accounts and Qualifications
Basis of Distinction Notes on Accounts Audit Qualifications
1. Authorship & Responsibility Drafted, finalized, and issued by Management / Board of Directors as an integral part of financial statements. Formulated, written, and issued solely by the Statutory Auditor in the independent audit report.
2. Purpose To clarify, provide detailed schedules, explain significant accounting policies, or disclose contingent liabilities. To formally express a reservation or disagreement regarding misstatements or scope limitation affecting true & fair view.
3. Legal Consequence Merely informative; does not constitute a defect in reporting by itself. Triggers statutory requirements: Board must give explanation in Board's Report u/s 134(5); scrutinized by regulators.
4. Substitution Rule Management cannot use notes to cure an accounting misstatement. Auditor cannot accept a note in lieu of qualifying if the accounting treatment is wrong!
📜 Distinction Between Audit Report and Certificate
Basis of Distinction Audit Report Audit Certificate
1. Nature of Work Expression of a professional opinion based on examination, test checks, sampling, and professional judgment. Written factual confirmation of the accuracy and truth of facts without any expression of opinion.
2. Level of Assurance Provides reasonable assurance (high, but not absolute). Provides absolute assurance regarding the specific certified facts/figures.
3. Scope of Examination Extensive; covers true and fair view of the entire set of financial statements. Narrow and specific (e.g., certificate of turnover, net worth certificate for bank, export realization).
4. Basis of Verification Based on sampling, materiality, and selective testing under SAs. Based on 100% verification of relevant records and vouchers.
🗣️ SA 260 & SA 265: Communication with Management & TCWG
Mandatory Governance Communications
  • Planned Scope and Timing (SA 260): Communicating an overview of the planned scope and timing of the audit, including significant risks identified. (Auditor must take care not to compromise audit effectiveness by giving exact sample details!).
  • Significant Findings from the Audit (SA 260):
    • The auditor’s views about significant qualitative aspects of accounting practices (accounting policies, estimates, disclosures).
    • Significant difficulties encountered during the audit (management delays, unavailability of personnel).
    • Material weaknesses in internal controls.
    • Circumstances affecting form and content of auditor's report (e.g., modifications, KAM).
  • Auditor Independence (SA 260): For listed entities, written confirmation that the engagement team complies with relevant ethical independence requirements, disclosing all relationships and total fees.
  • Communicating Deficiencies in Internal Control (SA 265):
    • The auditor shall communicate in writing significant deficiencies in internal control identified during the audit to Those Charged with Governance on a timely basis.
    • Other deficiencies in internal control of sufficient importance shall be communicated to appropriate management.
SA 710 & SA 720

SA 710 (Comparative Information) & SA 720 (Other Information)

📊 SA 710: Comparative Information (Corresponding Figures vs Comparative FS)

SA 710 identifies two distinct frameworks for comparative information:

Framework Key Features Auditor's Opinion Scope
Corresponding Figures
(Standard framework in India)		 Comparative information where amounts and other disclosures for the prior period are included as an integral part of the current period financial statements. The auditor’s opinion refers to the current period only; it does not explicitly mention corresponding figures unless specific reporting triggers arise.
Comparative Financial Statements Comparative information where amounts and other disclosures for prior period(s) are included for comparison with current period, presented at same detail. The auditor’s opinion refers to each period for which financial statements are presented and audited.
Reporting Scenarios for Corresponding Figures under SA 710
  1. Prior Period Report was Modified & Unresolved: If prior period audit report contained a modification and the matter remains unresolved: modify current report. (If resolved: no modification needed).
  2. Prior Period Misstatement Discovered in Current Year: If prior period FS had material misstatement not previously dealt with, and prior FS have not been amended/restated: express a Qualified or Adverse Opinion on current FS with respect to corresponding figures.
  3. Prior Period Audited by Predecessor Auditor: If prior period FS were audited by another auditor, the current auditor shall state in an Other Matter (OM) paragraph:
    • That the financial statements of the prior period were audited by a predecessor auditor;
    • The type of opinion expressed by the predecessor auditor (and reasons if modified); and
    • The date of that report.
  4. Prior Period Financial Statements Unaudited: The auditor shall state in an Other Matter paragraph that corresponding figures are unaudited. (Such statement does not relieve the auditor of obtaining SAAE regarding opening balances under SA 510!).
📰 SA 720 (Revised): The Auditor’s Responsibilities Relating to Other Information

SA 720 (Revised) deals with the auditor’s responsibilities relating to other information, whether financial or non-financial (other than financial statements and the auditor’s report thereon), included in an entity’s Annual Report (e.g., Director's Report, Management Discussion and Analysis (MD&A), Chairman's Statement, Corporate Governance Report).

Core Auditor Duties under SA 720
  1. Obtain the Other Information: Make appropriate arrangements with management to obtain other information prior to the date of the auditor’s report (or as soon as practicable thereafter).
  2. Read & Consider: Read the other information and consider whether there is a material inconsistency between:
    • The other information and the financial statements; and
    • The other information and the auditor’s knowledge obtained in the audit.
  3. Remain Alert for Misstatement of Fact: Remain alert for indications that other information not related to FS appears to be materially misstated.
  4. Dedicated Reporting Section: The auditor's report SHALL include a separate section with a heading "Other Information":
    • Identify the other information obtained prior to report date (and expected to be obtained after report date for listed entities).
    • State that management is responsible for other information.
    • State that auditor’s opinion does NOT cover other information and does not express audit assurance thereon.
    • State whether the auditor has anything to report regarding uncorrected material misstatements of other information.
Responding to Uncorrected Material Misstatements in Other Information

If the auditor concludes that a material misstatement exists in the other information and management refuses to correct it:

  • If Prior to Report Date: Communicate with TCWG. If still uncorrected: (a) describe the uncorrected misstatement in the "Other Information" section of the audit report; or (b) withhold the report / withdraw from the audit where permitted by law.
  • If After Report Date: Communicate with TCWG and take appropriate legal steps to bring the uncorrected misstatement to the attention of users (e.g., notification to shareholders, intimation to ROC).
Companies Act, 2013

Duties of Auditors Under Section 143 & Rules

The Companies Act, 2013 prescribes comprehensive statutory powers, duties, and responsibilities for the independent auditor of a company under Section 143. These duties cannot be curtailed, restricted, or waived by the Articles of Association or any agreement.

🔎 Section 143(1): Duty of Auditor to Inquire on Six Specific Matters

Section 143(1) imposes a statutory duty on the auditor to inquire into six specific matters during the course of the audit:

Clause Specific Matter for Inquiry Reporting Norm
Sec 143(1)(a) Whether loans and advances made by the company on the basis of security have been properly secured and whether the terms on which they have been made are prejudicial to the interests of the company or its members. Reporting by Exception Only!
The auditor is NOT required to state these matters in the audit report if satisfied with the inquiry.
Report ONLY IF the inquiry yields an unfavorable, adverse, or prejudicial answer!
Sec 143(1)(b) Whether transactions of the company which are represented merely by book entries are prejudicial to the interests of the company.
Sec 143(1)(c) Where the company not being an investment company or a banking company, whether so much of the assets of the company as consist of shares, debentures and other securities have been sold at a price less than that at which they were purchased by the company.
Sec 143(1)(d) Whether loans and advances made by the company have been shown as deposits.
Sec 143(1)(e) Whether personal expenses have been charged to revenue account.
Sec 143(1)(f) Where it is stated in the books and documents of the company that any shares have been allotted for cash, whether cash has actually been received in respect of such allotment, and if no cash has actually been so received, whether the position as stated in the account books and the balance sheet is correct, regular and not misleading.
📝 Section 143(3) & Rule 11: Matters to be Stated in Auditor's Report

Unlike Section 143(1), the matters under Section 143(3) and Rule 11 of Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014 must be affirmatively stated in every company audit report:

Section 143(3) Statutory Clauses
  • (a) Information & Explanations: Whether he has sought and obtained all the information and explanations which to the best of his knowledge and belief were necessary for the purpose of his audit.
  • (b) Proper Books of Account: Whether proper books of account as required by law have been kept by the company, and proper returns adequate for the purposes of audit received from branches.
  • (c) Branch Audit Report: Whether the report on the accounts of any branch office audited by a person other than the company’s auditor has been sent to him and the manner in which he has dealt with it.
  • (d) Balance Sheet & P&L Agreement: Whether the company’s balance sheet and profit and loss account dealt with in the report are in agreement with the books of account and returns.
  • (e) Accounting Standards Compliance: Whether the financial statements comply with the accounting standards prescribed under Section 133.
  • (f) Adverse Observations: The observations or comments of the auditors on financial transactions or matters which have any adverse effect on the functioning of the company.
  • (g) Director Disqualification: Whether any director is disqualified from being appointed as a director under sub-section (2) of Section 164.
  • (h) Maintenance of Accounts Remark: Any qualification, reservation or adverse remark relating to the maintenance of accounts and other matters connected therewith.
  • (i) Internal Financial Controls (IFC): Whether the company has adequate internal financial controls with reference to financial statements in place and the operating effectiveness of such controls.
    (Exemptions for Private Companies: OPC, Small Company, or Private company having Turnover < ₹ 50 Cr AND Aggregate Borrowings from banks/FIs/body corporate < ₹ 25 Cr at any time during the financial year).
Rule 11 Prescribed Reporting Matters (Including Audit Trail)
  1. Rule 11(a) Pending Litigations: Whether the company has disclosed the impact, if any, of pending litigations on its financial position in its financial statements.
  2. Rule 11(b) Foreseeable Losses: Whether the company has made provision, as required under any law or accounting standards, for material foreseeable losses on long-term contracts including derivative contracts.
  3. Rule 11(c) IEPF Delays: Whether there has been any delay in transferring amounts, required to be transferred, to the Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF) by the company.
  4. Rule 11(e) Intermediary Advances & Ultimate Beneficiaries:
    • Whether management has represented that no funds have been advanced/loaned/invested (from borrowed funds or share premium) to intermediaries with understanding that the intermediary shall lend/invest in other persons/entities identified as Ultimate Beneficiaries or provide any guarantee/security.
    • Whether management has represented that no funds have been received by the company from any funding party with understanding that the company shall lend/invest in Ultimate Beneficiaries.
    • Whether the auditor has performed procedures to verify that nothing has come to notice causing belief that representations are materially misstated.
  5. Rule 11(f) Dividend Compliance: Whether the dividend declared or paid during the year by the company is in compliance with Section 123 of the Companies Act, 2013.
  6. Rule 11(g) Audit Trail / Edit Log Feature:
    • Whether the company has used accounting software for maintaining books of account which has a feature of recording audit trail (edit log) facility.
    • Whether the audit trail feature has operated throughout the year for all transactions recorded in the software.
    • Whether the audit trail feature has not been tampered with and has been preserved as per statutory requirements for record retention.
🚨 Section 143(12) & Rule 13: Reporting on Frauds (Form ADT-4)

If an auditor of a company, in the course of the performance of his duties as auditor, has reason to believe that an offence of fraud involving such amount or amounts as may be prescribed, is being or has been committed in the company by its officers or employees, the auditor shall report the matter to the Central Government.

The Strict Fraud Reporting Mechanism

Case A: Fraud Involving ₹ 1 Crore or Above $implies$ Report to Central Government

Under Rule 13, if fraud involves an amount of ₹ 1 crore or above, the auditor must follow the strict statutory timeline:

  1. Step 1 (Within 2 Days): Forward report to the Board of Directors or the Audit Committee immediately, but not later than 2 days of knowledge, seeking their reply or observations within 45 days.
  2. Step 2 (Within 15 Days of Reply): On receipt of reply/observations, forward the report with comments and Board reply to the Secretary, Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) in Form ADT-4 within 15 days.
  3. Step 3 (If No Reply within 45 Days): If no reply/observations are received within 45 days, forward the report in Form ADT-4 to the Central Government along with a note that no reply was received within the stipulated time.

Case B: Fraud Involving LESS Than ₹ 1 Crore $implies$ Report to Board / Audit Committee

• Report the matter to the Board of Directors or Audit Committee within 2 days of knowledge.
• The Board is required to disclose details of such frauds in the Board’s Report under Section 134(3)(ca) (nature of fraud, amount involved, parties involved if not remediated, and remedial action taken).

Penalty for Default u/s 143(15): In case of non-compliance, in listed company: ₹ 5 Lakhs; in unlisted company: ₹ 1 Lakh!
👔 Section 197(16): Reporting on Managerial Remuneration

The auditor of a public company must make a statement in his report under Section 197(16) whether the managerial remuneration paid by the company to its directors is in accordance with the provisions of Section 197, whether remuneration paid to any director is in excess of the limits laid down, and give other details as prescribed.

CARO, 2020

Companies (Auditor’s Report) Order, 2020: Applicability & All 21 Clauses

CARO, 2020 is an additional reporting order issued by the Central Government under Section 143(11) of the Companies Act, 2013. It applies to every company including a foreign company as defined in Section 2(42), with specific statutory exemptions.

🛡️ Statutory Exemptions from CARO, 2020
Entities Specifically Exempt from CARO, 2020
  1. Banking Company as defined in the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.
  2. Insurance Company as defined in the Insurance Act, 1938.
  3. Section 8 Company (licensed companies operating for charitable/social objects).
  4. One Person Company (OPC) as defined in Section 2(62).
  5. Small Company as defined in Section 2(85) (Paid-up capital $le$ ₹ 4 Cr and Turnover $le$ ₹ 40 Cr).
  6. Private Limited Company (not being a subsidiary or holding of a public company) that satisfies ALL THREE CUMULATIVE CONDITIONS:
    • Condition 1 (Capital Limit): Paid-up share capital + Reserves & Surplus (including capital reserves, revenue reserves, securities premium, less debit balance of P&L) $le$ ₹ 1 Crore as at the balance sheet date.
    • Condition 2 (Borrowings Limit): Total borrowings from banks or financial institutions $le$ ₹ 1 Crore at ANY point of time during the financial year.
    • Condition 3 (Revenue Limit): Total Revenue (including revenue from operations, other income, and discontinued operations as per Schedule III) $le$ ₹ 10 Crore during the financial year.

Crucial Rule: If a private company breaches EVEN ONE of these three cumulative conditions, CARO 2020 becomes instantly applicable!

TYK Master Suite

TYK 1: Circumstances Requiring Modification of Audit Opinion (SA 705)
▼

Question: Under the applicable Standards on Auditing, in what circumstances does the report of the statutory auditor require modification?

✔ Master Solution & SA 705 Provisions:

Under SA 705 (Revised), the auditor shall modify the opinion in the auditor’s report when:

  1. The auditor concludes that, based on the audit evidence obtained, the financial statements as a whole are not free from material misstatement (due to appropriateness of selected accounting policies, their application, or adequacy of disclosures); OR
  2. The auditor is unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to conclude that the financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement (scope limitation).
TYK 2: Short Note on Emphasis of Matter (EOM) Paragraph in Audit Reports
▼
✔ Master Solution & SA 706 Provisions:

An Emphasis of Matter (EOM) paragraph is a paragraph included in the auditor’s report that refers to a matter appropriately presented or disclosed in the financial statements that, in the auditor’s judgment, is of such importance that it is fundamental to users’ understanding of the financial statements.

Key Requirements:

  • Must not be used for matters requiring modification under SA 705 or Key Audit Matters under SA 701.
  • Must refer to the specific Note in the financial statements.
  • Must explicitly state that the auditor’s opinion is not modified in respect of the matter.
TYK 3: Certificate for Special Purpose vs. Audit Report
▼
✔ Master Solution:

Audit Report: An expression of a professional opinion regarding true and fair view of financial statements based on selective verification, test checks, sampling, and professional judgment. It provides reasonable assurance.

Certificate for Special Purpose: A written factual confirmation of the mathematical or factual accuracy of specific facts/figures without expressing an opinion. It is based on 100% verification and provides absolute assurance.

TYK 4: Reporting to Shareholders vs. TCWG & Audit Report vs. Board's Report
▼
✔ Master Solution:

(i) Reporting to Shareholders vs TCWG: Reporting to shareholders is a public, statutory duty expressing an opinion on the true and fair view of financial statements under Section 143 and SA 700. Reporting to TCWG (SA 260/265) is a confidential internal governance communication addressing audit planning, significant audit findings, and internal control deficiencies.

(ii) Audit Report vs Board’s Report: The Audit Report is an independent assessment prepared by the statutory auditor under Section 143. The Board’s Report is prepared by the company’s directors under Section 134 explaining the state of company affairs, operations, dividends, and explanations on auditor qualifications.

TYK 5: Headings Used in Modified Opinions (SA 705)
▼
✔ Master Solution & SA 705 Rules:

When the auditor modifies the opinion, the headings in the report must be specifically titled:

  • Opinion section: "Qualified Opinion", "Adverse Opinion", or "Disclaimer of Opinion".
  • Basis section: "Basis for Qualified Opinion", "Basis for Adverse Opinion", or "Basis for Disclaimer of Opinion".
TYK 6: ADKS & Co. LLP / PKK Ltd (Material Misstatement Not Pervasive $implies$ Qualified Opinion)
▼

Scenario: ADKS & Co. LLP are statutory auditors of PKK Ltd. Inventories are overstated by ₹ 2.5 crores due to non-provision of obsolete items. Profit before tax is ₹ 20 crores. Management refuses to adjust. What type of opinion should the auditors express?

✔ Master Solution & SA 705 Application:

The overstatement of inventory by ₹ 2.5 crores is material (constitutes 12.5% of profit before tax). However, the misstatement is confined solely to inventory and does not render the entire financial statements misleading (not pervasive). Therefore, under SA 705, ADKS & Co. LLP shall express a Qualified Opinion using the phrase "except for the effects of the matter described in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section...".

When supplementary information is presented with the financial statements, the auditor evaluates whether it is clearly differentiated from audited financial statements. If cross-referenced, the auditor’s opinion covers it. If not covered, the auditor must ensure it is clearly marked as unaudited.

TYK 26: KYM Pvt. Ltd (CARO, 2020 Applicability Limits Computation)
▼

Scenario: You are statutory auditor of KYM Pvt. Ltd. Paid-up capital is ₹ 80 Lakhs, Reserves & Surplus ₹ 30 Lakhs (total ₹ 1.10 Cr). Bank borrowings peak at ₹ 95 Lakhs during the year. Total revenue is ₹ 8 Crores. Is CARO, 2020 applicable?

✔ Master Solution & Limit Evaluation:

  • Paid-up capital + Reserves $le$ ₹ 1 Crore $implies$ Here, ₹ 80 L + ₹ 30 L = ₹ 1.10 Crore (EXCEEDS ₹ 1 Crore!).
  • Borrowings $le$ ₹ 1 Crore $implies$ Satisfied (₹ 95 Lakhs).
  • Revenue $le$ ₹ 10 Crore $implies$ Satisfied (₹ 8 Crores).

Conclusion: Because the capital and reserves limit of ₹ 1 Crore has been breached, KYM Pvt. Ltd. is NOT exempt. CARO, 2020 is strictly applicable!

TYK 31: All in One Ltd / CA Kabir (Management Scope Limitation & Withdrawal / Disclaimer)
▼
✔ Master Solution:

Refusal of access to warehouse inventories is a management-imposed scope limitation. Under SA 705, if unable to obtain SAAE and effects are pervasive, CA Kabir must withdraw from audit or express a Disclaimer of Opinion.

TYK 32: Megh & Sons (Partnership Firm - Books Destroyed $implies$ Disclaimer of Opinion)
▼
✔ Master Solution:

Inability to obtain SAAE due to total loss of records in a non-corporate audit: Express a Disclaimer of Opinion in accordance with SA 705.

TYK 33: Sober Pvt. Ltd / CA Prashant (Going Concern Uncertainty Indicators under SA 570)
▼
✔ Master Solution:

Net liability position, loss of major customer, negative operating cash flows are going concern indicators requiring additional procedures and reporting under SA 570.

TYK 34: DCD Pvt. Ltd (Resignation of Statutory Auditor under CARO Clause (xviii))
▼
✔ Master Solution:

Under Clause (xviii) of CARO, 2020, the incoming auditor must report whether issues, objections or concerns raised by the outgoing auditor have been considered.

TYK 35: Trikon Ltd / R & J Associates (Determining Key Audit Matters under SA 701)
▼
✔ Master Solution:

Determine matters of most significance from communications with TCWG, considering higher RMM areas and estimation uncertainty under SA 701.

TYK 36: Pari Ltd / CA Krish (Going Concern Material Uncertainty Disclosed $implies$ MURGC Section)
▼
✔ Master Solution:

Because material uncertainty is adequately disclosed in notes, CA Krish expresses an Unmodified Opinion and includes a separate "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section under SA 570.

TYK 37: Rise Ltd / ABC & Associates (Communication with TCWG under SA 260)
▼
✔ Master Solution:

Communicate significant qualitative aspects of accounting practices, accounting estimates, and auditor independence in writing to TCWG under SA 260.

TYK 38: PKT Limited (De-recognition of Financial Liability without Legal Extinguishment)
▼
✔ Master Solution & SA 705:

A financial liability cannot be de-recognized unless extinguished by discharge, cancellation, or expiry. De-recognizing a disputed liability without settlement violates Ind AS 109, resulting in material misstatement requiring a Qualified or Adverse Opinion under SA 705.

Master Matrix

Master Reporting Standards Matrix: SA 700 vs 701 vs 705 vs 706

Standard Core Objective Applicability Relationship with Other SAs
SA 700 (Revised) Forming an opinion and standard report architecture. All audits of general purpose financial statements. Foundation for all other 700-series standards.
SA 701 (KAM) Communicating matters of most significance in audit. Mandatory for listed entities; voluntary for others. Cannot be used in Disclaimers; no substitute for modification.
SA 705 (Revised) Modifying opinion (Qualified, Adverse, Disclaimer). When FS misstated or inability to obtain SAAE. Overrides SA 700; dictates modification headings.
SA 706 (Revised) Emphasis of Matter & Other Matter paragraphs. Where fundamental matter is appropriately disclosed. Prohibited if matter requires SA 705 modification or SA 701 KAM.
