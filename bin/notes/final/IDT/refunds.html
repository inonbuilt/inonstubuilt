Refunds under GST

Comprehensive Master Revision Notes: CGST Sections 54 to 58, 9 Ground Scenarios for Refund Claims, Exhaustive 2-Year Relevant Date Determination Matrix, Detailed Arithmetic Computations under Rule 89(4) (1.5x Domestic Turnover Cap) and Rule 89(5) (Inverted Duty Structure), Section 56 Interest Mechanisms (6% vs 9%), Unjust Enrichment Exceptions, and Step-by-Step Solved ICAI Test Your Knowledge Problems.

1. Statutory Framework for Refunds

Timely refund of unutilized input taxes and taxes erroneously paid is indispensable to maintain working capital liquidity and international export competitiveness. The refund regime in GST is standardized under Sections 54 to 58 of the CGST Act read with Rules 89 to 96B of the CGST Rules, 2017.

General Rule

Application & 2-Year Limitation [Sec 54(1)]

Any person claiming refund of tax, interest, or unutilized ITC must file an application in Form GST RFD-01 electronically before the expiry of 2 years from the "relevant date".

De Minimis Rule

â‚¹1,000 Minimum Threshold [Sec 54(14)]

No refund shall be paid to an applicant if the amount of refund claim is less than â‚¹1,000. This â‚¹1,000 cap is evaluated separately for each tax head (CGST, SGST, IGST, Cess).

2. Statutory Situations Permitting Refund Claims

Refund claims under GST arise under the following recognized legal scenarios:

Category of Refund Governing Section Operational Mechanism
1. Zero-Rated Supplies without payment of tax (under LUT/Bond) Section 54(3)(i) read with Rule 89(4) Refund of unutilized ITC accumulated on inputs and input services used in making zero-rated exports or supplies to SEZ.
2. Inverted Duty Structure (Rate on inputs > Rate on output) Section 54(3)(ii) read with Rule 89(5) Refund of unutilized ITC accumulated on inputs where input tax rate is higher than output tax rate (other than nil-rated/fully exempt).
3. Export of Goods with payment of IGST Section 54 read with Rule 96 Shipping Bill itself treated as refund application; automatically processed via ICEGATE customs system upon matching with GSTR-1 & 3B.
4. Export of Services with payment of IGST Section 54 read with Rule 96(2) Application in Form GST RFD-01 after receipt of convertible foreign exchange (FIRC/BRC).
5. Deemed Exports (EOU / Advance Authorisation) Section 147 read with Rule 89(2)(g) Claimed either by the recipient EOU or by the supplier (upon obtaining a waiver disclaimer from recipient).
6. Excess Balance in Electronic Cash Ledger Section 49(6) read with Sec 54(1) Can be claimed through monthly Form GSTR-3B or Form GST RFD-01 at any time.
7. Taxes Paid Wrongfully (CGST/SGST instead of IGST or vice versa) Section 77 CGST & Sec 19 IGST Refund of wrongly paid tax without payment of any interest on correct tax subsequently deposited.
8. Tax paid on supply not provided / Refund Voucher issued Section 54(8)(c) Where advance was received, tax paid, but supply cancelled and refund voucher issued u/s 31(3)(e).
9. Favourable Appellate Order / Court Decree Section 54(1) read with Sec 54(8)(e) Pre-deposit or tax deposited during litigation refunded along with Section 56 interest.
Two Absolute Bars on Refund of Unutilized ITC [Provisos to Section 54(3)]:
  1. Export Duty Goods: No refund of unutilized ITC is allowed if the goods exported out of India are subjected to export duty (e.g., iron ore, certain tobacco items).
  2. Duty Drawback / IGST Refund Availed: No refund of unutilized ITC is allowed if the supplier avails drawback in respect of central tax or claims refund of the integrated tax paid on such supplies.

4. Rule 89(4): Refund of Unutilized ITC on Zero-Rated Supplies

Where zero-rated supplies are made without payment of tax under Letter of Undertaking (LUT), refund of unutilized ITC is calculated as per the statutory formula under Rule 89(4):

Statutory Formula [Rule 89(4)]:
$$\text{Refund Amount} = \frac{\text{Turnover of Zero-Rated Supply of Goods} + \text{Turnover of Zero-Rated Supply of Services}}{\text{Adjusted Total Turnover}} \times \text{Net ITC}$$
Net ITC

Inputs & Input Services Only

Includes ITC availed on inputs and input services during the relevant period.
EXCLUDES: Capital Goods ITC!

1.5x Cap Rule

Turnover of Zero-Rated Goods

Value of zero-rated goods is LOWER of:
(a) Actual zero-rated export value; OR
(b) 1.5 × Value of like goods domestically supplied by the similarly placed supplier.

Adjusted Total TO

Denominator Scope

Total turnover in a State/UT, excluding exempt supplies (other than zero-rated exempt supplies) and turnover of inward RCM supplies.

5. Rule 89(5): Refund on Inverted Duty Structure

Where credit accumulates because the rate of tax on inputs is higher than the rate on output supplies (e.g., fabric @ 18% → apparel @ 5%), refund is granted under Rule 89(5). The formula was amended to ensure proportional reduction between inputs and input services:

Amended Formula for Inverted Duty Refund [Rule 89(5)]:
$$\text{Maximum Refund} = \left[\frac{\text{Turnover of Inverted Rated Supply of Goods \& Services}}{\text{Adjusted Total Turnover}} \times \text{Net ITC}\right] - \left[\text{Tax Payable on Inverted Supply} \times \frac{\text{Net ITC}}{\text{ITC availed on Inputs and Input Services}}\right]$$

• Net ITC: Under Rule 89(5), Net ITC means ITC availed on INPUTS ONLY during the relevant period.
• The second term proportionally reduces the output tax liability paid by utilizing input credit vs input service credit, eliminating the anomaly struck down in VKC Footsteps.

Negative List of Inverted Duty Supplies: The Government has notified items on which NO inverted duty refund is permissible under Section 54(3)(ii):
• Pan masala, tobacco products, gutkha.
• Edible oils (mustard oil, soyabean oil, palm oil).
• Coal, lignite, and peat.
• Woven fabrics of silk, wool, cotton, corduroy, velvet.
• Rail locomotives and rolling stock.

6. Doctrine of Unjust Enrichment & Consumer Welfare Fund

Under Section 54(5), every refund sanctioned is ordinarily credited to the Consumer Welfare Fund [Section 57] based on the presumption that the incidence of tax has been passed on to the buyer. However, Section 54(8) lists 6 vital exceptions where the refund is paid DIRECTLY TO THE APPLICANT:

Clause Exceptions under Section 54(8) (Refund Paid Directly to Applicant)
(a) Refund of tax paid on export of goods or services or both, or on inputs/input services used in making such exports.
(b) Refund of unutilized Input Tax Credit under Section 54(3) (Zero-rated supplies or Inverted Duty Structure).
(c) Refund of tax paid on a supply which is not provided, either wholly or partially, or for which invoice has not been issued, or where a refund voucher has been issued.
(d) Refund of tax paid in pursuance of Section 77 (tax wrongfully collected and paid to wrong Government).
(e) The tax and interest, or any other amount paid by the applicant, if he had not passed on the incidence of such tax and interest to any other person. (Certified by Chartered Accountant/Cost Accountant if refund claim ≥ â‚¹2 Lakhs).
(f) Such other class of persons who has borne the incidence of tax as the Government may notify.

7. Procedural Timelines: Provisional & Final Refund Orders

Provisional Refund (90%)

Section 54(6) & Rule 91: 7-Day Sanction

In case of refund claims of zero-rated supplies of goods/services (and inverted duty structure as amended by Finance Act 2026), the Proper Officer shall sanction 90% of the refund claimed on a PROVISIONAL basis within 7 DAYS of issuance of acknowledgement in Form GST RFD-02.
Condition: Applicant must not have been prosecuted for any offence involving tax evasion exceeding â‚¹2.5 Crore in the preceding 5 years.

Final Order (60 Days)

Section 54(7) & Rule 92: Order in RFD-06

The Proper Officer shall issue the final refund order in Form GST RFD-06 within 60 DAYS from the date of receipt of the complete refund application. If a deficiency memo is issued in Form GST RFD-03, the 60-day period begins afresh from the date of the re-filed application.

Deficiency Memo Modality [Rule 90(3)]: If deficiencies are noticed in the refund application, the Proper Officer must issue a Deficiency Memo in Form GST RFD-03 within 15 days. Upon issuance of RFD-03, the ITC debited from the Electronic Credit Ledger is automatically re-credited, and the applicant must submit a fresh refund application.

8. Interest on Delayed Refunds [Section 56]

To ensure accountability, Section 56 mandates interest payment where the Government delays sanctioning refunds beyond statutory deadlines:

Circumstance Trigger Point Statutory Rate of Interest
Normal Refund under Section 54(1) If refund is not sanctioned within 60 days from the date of receipt of complete application → Interest payable from the day immediately after the expiry of 60 days till the date of actual refund. 6% per annum
Refund arising from Appellate Authority / Tribunal / Court Order If refund ordered by an appellate forum/court is not refunded within 60 days from the date of receipt of application filed consequent to such order → Interest payable from the day after expiry of 60 days till actual refund. 9% per annum

9. Withholding of Refunds [Section 54(10) & 54(11)]

Section 54(10)

Withholding for Return Defaults & Dues

The Proper Officer may withhold refund or deduct outstanding tax dues where the applicant:
• Has failed to furnish any return till he files the return; or
• Is required to pay any tax, interest, or penalty which has not been stayed by an appellate authority/court.

Section 54(11)

Withholding during Pending Appeals

Where an order giving rise to refund is subject matter of an appeal/proceeding and the Commissioner considers that grant of refund is likely to adversely affect revenue on account of malfeasance/fraud, he may withhold refund after giving an opportunity of being heard. If taxpayer succeeds later, interest @ 6% p.a. is payable!

10. ICAI Test Your Knowledge (TYK) - Complete Solved Problems

ICAI TYK 1 (Refund Sanction Time Limit)

Question:

Is there any time limit for sanctioning of refund under Section 54?

Answer:

Yes. Under Section 54(7) of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 92, the proper officer shall issue the final order sanctioning the refund in Form GST RFD-06 within 60 days from the date of receipt of application complete in all respects.

If the refund is not sanctioned within the said period of 60 days, interest at the rate of 6% per annum will have to be paid in accordance with Section 56 from the date immediately after the expiry of 60 days till the date of actual refund.

Provisional Refund Exception: Under Section 54(6), in case of zero-rated supplies, a provisional refund to the extent of 90% of the amount claimed shall be sanctioned within 7 days from the date of the acknowledgement (RFD-02).

ICAI TYK 2 (Advance Tax Refund for Casual Taxable Person)

Question:

Discuss the provisions relating to refund of the amount of advance tax deposited by a casual taxable person under Section 27(2).

Answer:

Under Section 27(2), a Casual Taxable Person (CTP) makes an advance deposit of tax equivalent to the estimated tax liability for the period of registration. Any unutilized excess advance tax can be claimed as refund under Section 54(1).

Mandatory Condition [Second Proviso to Section 54(1)]: The second proviso to Section 54(1) provides that the amount of advance tax deposited by a casual taxable person (or non-resident taxable person) under Section 27(2) shall not be refunded unless such person has, in respect of the entire period for which the certificate of registration granted to him had remained in force, furnished all the returns required under Section 39.

Therefore, a CTP can claim refund of excess advance tax only after filing all monthly/periodic GSTR-3B returns for the registration tenure.

ICAI TYK 3 (BRC / FIRC for Export of Goods)

Question:

In case of refund under exports of goods, whether BRC/FIRC is necessary for granting refund?

Answer:

No, Bank Realization Certificate (BRC) or Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) is NOT required for claiming refund on export of goods.

As per Circular No. 125/44/2019-GST and Circular No. 37/11/2018-GST, for export of goods, realization of proceeds is not an upfront prerequisite for sanctioning refund (since shipping bills and export general manifests are tracked digitally via ICEGATE). However, under Rule 96B, if foreign exchange is not realized within the FEMA timeline (9 months), the exporter must deposit back the refund along with Section 50 interest within 30 days.

Contrast with Services: In case of export of services, realization of convertible foreign exchange is an essential statutory prerequisite under Section 2(6); hence, FIRC / BRC is mandatory for refund on export of services.

ICAI TYK 4 (Deficiency Memo Modality)

Question:

When is a deficiency memo issued in respect of a refund claim made under Section 54?

Answer:

Under Rule 90(3) of the CGST Rules, 2017, where any deficiencies are noticed in the refund application submitted in Form GST RFD-01 upon preliminary scrutiny, the proper officer shall issue a Deficiency Memo in Form GST RFD-03 within 15 days of the filing of the application.

Legal Implications:

  • Upon issuance of RFD-03, any ITC debited from the Electronic Credit Ledger at the time of filing the refund application is automatically re-credited to the ledger.
  • The applicant is required to file a fresh refund application after rectifying the deficiencies.
  • The statutory 60-day period for sanctioning refund begins afresh from the date of receipt of the re-filed complete application.
ICAI TYK 5 (Exceptions to Unjust Enrichment)

Question:

State the exceptions to the principle of unjust enrichment as applicable to refund claims.

Answer:

Under Section 54(8) of the CGST Act, 2017, the doctrine of unjust enrichment is NOT applicable, and the refundable amount is paid directly to the applicant (instead of being credited to the Consumer Welfare Fund), in the following cases:

  1. Refund of tax paid on export of goods or services or both, or on inputs or input services used in making such exports;
  2. Refund of unutilized input tax credit under Section 54(3) in respect of zero-rated supplies without payment of tax or inverted duty structure;
  3. Refund of tax paid on a supply which is not provided, either wholly or partially, and for which invoice has not been issued, or where a refund voucher has been issued;
  4. Refund of tax in pursuance of Section 77 (tax wrongfully collected and paid to Central/State Govt);
  5. Tax or interest paid by the applicant where the incidence of tax has not been passed on to any other person;
  6. Such other class of persons as the Government may notify.

ICAI TYK 8 (Circumstances of Unutilized ITC Refund)

Question:

With reference to Section 54(3), mention the cases where refund of unutilised input tax credit is allowed.

Answer:

Under Section 54(3) of the CGST Act, 2017, refund of unutilised input tax credit is allowed ONLY in the following two cases:

  1. Zero-rated supplies made without payment of tax: Where zero-rated supply of goods or services or both is made under a Letter of Undertaking or Bond in terms of Section 16(3) of the IGST Act;
  2. Inverted Duty Structure: Where credit has accumulated on account of the rate of tax on inputs being higher than the rate of tax on output supplies (other than nil-rated or fully exempt supplies).

Exceptions: No refund of unutilised ITC is permissible where: (i) goods exported are subject to export duty; (ii) supplier claims drawback of central tax or refund of IGST; or (iii) output supplies are specifically notified as ineligible for inverted duty refund.

ICAI TYK 9 (Refunds Paid to Applicant vs Consumer Fund)

Question:

State few cases where the refundable amount shall be paid to the applicant instead of being credited to the Consumer Welfare Fund under the CGST Act, 2017.

Answer:

Under Section 54(8), the refundable amount is disbursed directly to the applicant's bank account in the following cases:

  • Refund of tax paid on export of goods or services or both, or on inputs/input services used in making such exports;
  • Refund of unutilized ITC under Section 54(3) (zero-rated supplies without tax or inverted duty structure);
  • Refund of tax paid on a supply not provided or where refund voucher has been issued;
  • Refund of tax wrongfully collected and paid under Section 77;
  • Tax/interest paid where the applicant establishes that incidence of tax was not passed on to any other person.
ICAI TYK 10 (Deficiency Memo & Interest Computation u/s 56)

Problem Statement:

Suraj Pvt. Ltd. (Delhi) filed a refund application for unutilised ITC of â‚¹20,00,000 on 1st May. Proper officer issued a deficiency memo on 10th May. Assessee re-filed fresh refund application on 15th May. The proper officer rejected the claim on 10th July.

Assessee filed an appeal on 1st September. Appellate Authority allowed the refund and the refund was released on 20th September.

Suraj Pvt. Ltd. claims it is entitled to interest, while the Proper Officer contends refund was granted within specified time of the appellate order. Examine who is correct and compute interest payable.

Detailed Solution & Statutory Computations:

1. Analysis of Legal Provisions [Section 54, 56 & Rule 90]:

  • When a deficiency memo is issued under Rule 90(3), a fresh application must be submitted. The date of filing the fresh application (15th May) is the legally valid application date.
  • Under Section 54(7), refund must be sanctioned within 60 days from the date of receipt of the complete application. The 60-day period expires on 14th July.
  • Under Section 56, if refund is not sanctioned within 60 days from the date of receipt of application, interest @ 6% per annum is payable for the period commencing from the day immediately after the expiry of 60 days (15th July) till the date of actual refund (20th September).
  • The fact that the refund was initially rejected and later allowed in appeal does not deprive the taxpayer of interest under Section 56. The Supreme Court in Ranbaxy Laboratories Ltd. settled that liability to pay interest arises from the expiry of 60 days from the original complete application.

2. Computation of Period of Delay:

  • Expiry of 60 days from 15th May: 14th July.
  • Delay Period: 15th July to 20th September (both inclusive):
    • July: 17 days (31 − 14)
    • August: 31 days
    • September: 20 days
    • Total Days = 17 + 31 + 20 = 68 days
$$\text{Interest Payable under Section 56} = \text{Principal Amount} \times 6\% \times \frac{\text{Number of Days}}{365}$$ $$\text{Interest} = â‚¹20,00,000 \times 6\% \times \frac{68}{365} = \mathbf{â‚¹22,356.16 \text{ or } â‚¹22,356}$$

Conclusion: The contention of Suraj Pvt. Ltd. is CORRECT. Suraj Pvt. Ltd. is entitled to receive statutory interest of â‚¹22,356.
