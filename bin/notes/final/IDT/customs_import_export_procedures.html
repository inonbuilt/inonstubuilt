Importation, Exportation & Clearance Procedures

Exhaustive analysis of Sections 29 to 51, 52 to 56, 77 to 90 of the Customs Act, 1962: Arrival Manifests (IGM), Entry Inwards, Custodian Liabilities u/s 45(3), Electronic Bill of Entry Filing & Late Fees u/s 46, Self-Assessment u/s 17, Provisional Assessment u/s 18 & 2025 Regulations, Deferred Duty Facility, Transit vs Transhipment, Postal Goods, Stores, and Baggage Rules.

1. Statutory Architecture of Customs Clearance

Customs procedural law establishes statutory supervision over goods and conveyances from the moment they enter Indian territorial waters until final clearance into the domestic market, bonded warehousing, or export out of India:

2. Arrival of Conveyance & Manifest Delivery (Sections 29 to 30A)

A. Arrival of Vessels & Aircraft in India [Section 29]

The person-in-charge of a vessel or aircraft entering India from abroad shall NOT call or land at any place other than a notified customs port or customs airport, unless expressly permitted by CBIC.

Emergency Landing Exception & Obligations

Where compelled by accident, severe stress of weather, or unavoidable cause to call/land at a non-customs station:

  • Person-in-charge must immediately report arrival to the nearest customs officer or police station officer-in-charge and produce logbook.
  • No cargo unloading or passenger/crew departure is permitted without permission, except where strictly necessary for reasons of health, safety, or preservation of life/property.

B. Delivery of Arrival Manifest / Import Manifest (IGM) [Section 30]

3. Entry Inwards & Regulated Unloading (Sections 31 to 35)

A. Grant of Entry Inwards [Section 31]

The master of a vessel shall NOT permit unloading of imported cargo until an order granting ENTRY INWARDS has been issued by the proper officer:

  • Significance: Entry Inwards is official acknowledgment that the Customs Department is ready to supervise cargo unloading and assess duties.
  • Section 15 Impact: The date of Entry Inwards is the crucial benchmark for determining the applicable rate of duty where an advance Bill of Entry was filed!
  • Exemptions from Entry Inwards: Passenger accompanied baggage, mail bags, animals, perishable goods, and hazardous cargo may be unloaded prior to Entry Inwards.

B. Unloading Safeguards (Sections 32 to 35)

  • Manifested Cargo Only [Sec 32]: Unloading is barred unless cargo is specifically mentioned in the IGM for landing at that particular port.
  • Approved Wharfs Only [Sec 33]: Cargo must be unloaded only at places approved under Section 8(a).
  • Customs Supervision [Sec 34]: Unloading must proceed under continuous supervision of customs officers deployed at wharfs.
  • Restrictions on Water-Borne Cargo [Sec 35 - Boat Notes]: Where ships anchor mid-sea, cargo ferried in lighters/barges must be covered by a statutory Boat Note under the Boat Notes Regulations, 1976.

4. Custody of Imported Goods & Custodian Liability (Section 45)

Upon unloading, imported goods remain in the custody of an approved Custodian (Port Trust, Airport Authority of India, or Container Corporation of India) until cleared for home consumption, warehoused, or transshipped.

Custodian Duty Liability for Pilfered Goods [Section 45(3)]

Notwithstanding anything in any other law, if any imported goods are pilfered after unloading while in the custody of the custodian, the custodian is legally liable to pay customs duty on such pilfered goods!

  • Rate of Duty: Duty is payable at the rate prevailing on the date of delivery of the Import General Manifest (IGM) under Section 30.
  • Major Port Authorities Distinction: In Board of Trustees v. UOI (2009) Bom HC, it was held that statutory major port trusts acting as bailees under the Major Port Authorities Act are liable to the importer for the value of the goods, but customs duty recovery u/s 45(3) applies primarily to approved custodians.

5. Filing of Import Bill of Entry & Late Presentation Charges (Section 46)

A. Types of Bill of Entry

1. Home Consumption

Filed for direct clearance into domestic market upon immediate payment of all duties.

2. Into-Bond Warehousing

Filed for depositing goods into a customs bonded warehouse; duty collection is deferred under bond.

3. Ex-Bond Clearance

Filed for clearing warehoused goods from bond for home consumption upon duty discharge.

B. Statutory Timelines & Advance Filing

Mandatory Time Limit for Filing [Section 46(3)]

The importer shall present the Bill of Entry before the end of the day (including holidays) preceding the day on which the vessel/aircraft/vehicle carrying the goods arrives at the customs station.

  • Advance Bill of Entry: An importer may present a Bill of Entry at any time up to 30 days prior to the expected arrival of the vessel/aircraft.
  • Late Filing Charges: Where a Bill of Entry is filed belatedly without sufficient cause:

C. "Warehousing Without Warehousing" [Section 49]

Where an importer is unable to furnish full particulars for filing a complete Bill of Entry pending documents/invoices, he may request permission to deposit the goods in a public bonded warehouse under Section 49 for up to 30 days (extendable by 30 days by Commissioner). Such goods are not deemed warehoused goods, and warehousing provisions do not apply.

6. Self-Assessment & Re-Assessment (Section 17)

  • Self-Assessment [Sec 17(1)]: Importers and exporters must self-assess duty, classification, valuation, and exemptions on the Bill of Entry / Shipping Bill.
  • Risk Management System (RMS): Proper officer selects consignments for verification/testing primarily based on automated risk evaluation. Green Channel importers receive clearance without routine physical examination.
  • Speaking Order on Re-Assessment [Sec 17(5)]: Where the proper officer re-assesses duty contrary to self-assessment (and the importer does not confirm acceptance in writing), the officer must pass a speaking order within 15 days.

Faceless Assessment [Section 143AA]

CBIC introduced Faceless Assessment under the 'Turant Customs' initiative. Bills of Entry filed at any port in India are assigned electronically and anonymously to Faceless Assessment Groups (FAGs) across different customs zones, ensuring specialization, transparency, and elimination of physical interface.

7. Provisional Assessment & 2025 Regulations (Sections 18 & 18A)

A. Four Statutory Grounds for Provisional Assessment [Section 18(1)]

  1. Importer or exporter is unable to make self-assessment and requests provisional assessment in writing.
  2. Proper officer deems it necessary to subject goods to chemical or other laboratory testing.
  3. Importer produced all documents, but the proper officer deems it necessary to make further inquiry.
  4. Necessary documents have not been produced or information has not been furnished by the importer.

Customs (Finalisation of Provisional Assessment) Regulations, 2025

Requisition of Documents Within 15 days of assessment Proper officer specifies missing information in writing.
Submission by Importer Within 2 months (+ 2 months extension) Maximum outer limit for extensions = 14 months.
Conclusion of Inquiry Within 14 months Inquiry report transferred to proper officer.
Finalization of Assessment Within 2 Years Extendable by 1 additional year by Commissioner (total 3 yrs).

B. Interest Mechanics under Section 18

  • Interest Payable by Importer [Sec 18(3)]: Interest @ 15% p.a. is payable on differential duty from the first day of the month in which provisional assessment was made till the actual date of payment.
  • Interest on Delayed Refund [Sec 18(4)]: If refund consequent to final assessment is not disbursed within 3 months from final order date, interest @ 6% p.a. is payable to the importer.
  • Unjust Enrichment: All refunds are subject to the doctrine of unjust enrichment and credited to Consumer Welfare Fund unless falling under Section 18(5) exceptions (personal imports, duty borne without passing on).

C. Voluntary Revision of Entry Post-Clearance [Section 18A]

Importers can voluntarily revise cleared entries to pay short-paid duty with interest, or treat excess duty as a refund claim u/s 27. Barred if audit, search, seizure, or summons has already been initiated.

8. Clearance for Home Consumption & Deferred Duty Payment (Section 47)

  • Duty Payment Timelines: Importer must pay duty electronically on the same day in self-assessment, or within 1 day (excluding holidays) of receipt of a re-assessed Bill of Entry. Default attracts interest @ 15% p.a. under Section 47(2).
  • Out of Charge (OOC) Order: The proper officer issues an OOC order permitting clearance from the customs area.
  • Auction of Uncleared Goods [Section 48]: If imported goods are not cleared, warehoused, or transshipped within 30 days of unloading, the custodian (with customs permission) may sell them by public auction.

"Clear First - Pay Later": Deferred Duty Payment Scheme

Available to Authorized Economic Operators (AEO Tier-2 and Tier-3). Instead of paying duty on each consignment:

  • Goods cleared from 1st to 15th of a month: Duty payable by 16th of that month.
  • Goods cleared from 16th to end of month (except March): Duty payable by 1st of following month.
  • Goods cleared from 16th to 31st March: Duty payable by 31st March itself.

9. Clearance of Export Goods (Sections 50 & 51)

  1. Filing Shipping Bill [Section 50]: Exporter presents electronic Shipping Bill (for vessel/aircraft) or Bill of Export (for vehicle) on ICEGATE and self-assesses duty.
  2. Entry Outwards [Section 39]: The master of a vessel shall not permit loading of export cargo until the proper officer grants ENTRY OUTWARDS.
  3. Let Export Order (LEO) [Section 51]: Issued by the proper officer upon verification that export duty (if any) is paid and goods are not prohibited.
  4. Export General Manifest (EGM) [Section 41]: Person-in-charge delivers departure manifest / EGM before the conveyance departs from the customs station.

10. Transit of Goods (Sec 53) vs Transhipment of Goods (Sec 54)

Distinguishing Feature Transit of Goods [Section 53] Transhipment of Goods [Section 54]
Conveyance Identity Goods remain in the same conveyance throughout voyage in India. Goods are transferred from one conveyance to another conveyance.
Unloading at Port Goods are NOT unloaded at the intermediate Indian port. Goods are unloaded at first port and re-loaded on second conveyance.
Documentation Mentioned in IGM as transit goods; no separate Bill of Entry or bond needed. Importer/carrier files Bill of Transhipment / transhipment declaration.
Bond & Undertaking No bond required. Transhipment bond with surety/guarantee executed to ensure arrival at destination.
Customs Duty No duty payable at transit port; assessed and paid at final destination port. No duty payable at transhipment port; assessed and paid at destination customs station.

B. Ship Stores [Sections 85 to 90]

  • "Stores" includes fuel, spare parts, and provisions for use on a vessel or aircraft (not vehicles).
  • Stores on a foreign-going vessel or aircraft may be consumed duty-free during the voyage outside India [Sec 87].
  • Stores may be warehoused without assessment of duty under Section 85.

12. Passenger Baggage & Allowances (Sections 77 to 81 & Baggage Rules)

A. Statutory Framework

  • Declaration [Sec 77]: Passenger declares contents of baggage before customs; Green Channel (nothing to declare) vs Red Channel (dutiable goods).
  • Rate of Duty: Flat concessional rate of 35% + 10% SWS = 38.5% (Notfn 26/2016-Cus) on dutiable goods exceeding the General Free Allowance.
  • Temporary Detention [Sec 80]: Passenger may request detention of dutiable baggage at the airport for re-export upon departure.

ICAI Question 1

Vessel 'Queen Marry': Relevant Date for Rate of Duty

'Queen Marry' entered territorial waters on 24th May. IGM delivered on 25th May. Importer XML Ltd filed Bill of Entry on 27th May (duty @ 8%). Duty increased to 10% on 28th May. Entry Inwards granted on 29th May. At what rate should XML Ltd pay customs duty?
Statutory Resolution:

XML Ltd must pay customs duty @ 10%.

  1. Legal Provision: Under Section 15(1)(a) of the Customs Act, 1962, the relevant date for determining the rate of duty for goods entered for home consumption is the date of presentation of the Bill of Entry OR the date of grant of Entry Inwards to the vessel, whichever is LATER.
  2. Application: Bill of Entry filed on 27th May; Entry Inwards granted on 29th May. The later date is 29th May. Since the duty increased to 10% on 28th May, the rate prevalent on 29th May (10%) applies.
