Advance Ruling under GST

Comprehensive Master Revision Notes: CGST Chapter XVII (Sections 95 to 106), The 7 Exhaustive Statutory Questions under Section 97(2), Absolute Statutory Bar on Place of Supply, AAR Application & 90-Day Pronouncement Timeline, Appellate Authority for Advance Ruling (AAAR) Mechanism & Disagreement Protocols, Binding Effect under Section 103 (In Personam Nature), Void Ab-Initio Annulment under Section 104, and Complete Solved ICAI Test Your Knowledge Problems.

1. Statutory Architecture & Commercial Philosophy

Commercial enterprises require tax certainty and predictability before entering into long-term capital investments and complex transactional agreements. The mechanism of Advance Ruling under Chapter XVII of the CGST Act [Sections 95 to 106] provides binding tax determinations on proposed or ongoing transactions, eliminating long-drawn litigation, fostering Foreign Direct Investment (FDI), and ensuring administrative transparency.

Twin Avenues

Proposed & Ongoing Transactions

Under GST, an advance ruling is not restricted solely to future contracts. An applicant can seek an advance ruling on a transaction being undertaken (ongoing) as well as an activity proposed to be undertaken in the future.

Unregistered Applicants

No Prior Registration Needed!

Under Section 95(c), an "applicant" includes not only registered persons but also ANY PERSON DESIROUS OF OBTAINING REGISTRATION. An entrepreneur can obtain binding clarity before commencing operations!

2. Statutory Definitions [Section 95]

Term & Section Statutory Legal Scope
Advance Ruling [Section 95(a)] A decision provided by the Authority (AAR) or Appellate Authority (AAAR) to an applicant on matters/questions specified in Section 97(2) or Section 100(1) in relation to the supply of goods or services or both being undertaken or proposed to be undertaken.
Applicant [Section 95(c)] Any person registered OR desirous of obtaining registration under this Act.
Authority [Section 95(e)] The Authority for Advance Ruling (AAR) constituted under the SGST Act / UTGST Act in each State/UT, consisting of one Central GST member and one State GST member (officers not below Joint Commissioner).
Appellate Authority [Section 95(b)] The Appellate Authority for Advance Ruling (AAAR) constituted in each State/UT, consisting of the Chief Commissioner of Central Tax and Commissioner of State Tax.

3. The 7 Questions on Which Advance Ruling Can Be Sought [Section 97(2)]

An application for advance ruling can be filed ONLY in respect of the following 7 specific statutory questions:

Clause Statutory Question [Section 97(2)] Practical Scope & Real-World Examples
(a) Classification of any goods or services or both Determining correct HSN / SAC code (e.g., whether flavoured milk is beverage or milk product).
(b) Applicability of a notification issued under the Act Determining eligibility for exemption notifications or concessional rate notifications.
(c) Determination of time and value of supply Valuation of subsidies, discounts, mixed supplies, or time of supply for milestone contracts.
(d) Admissibility of input tax credit (ITC) Whether ITC on solar power plant, lift installation, or canteen services is blocked u/s 17(5).
(e) Determination of the liability to pay tax on goods or services Whether supply attracts reverse charge (RCM), composite supply rates, or zero rate.
(f) Whether applicant is required to be registered Examining aggregate turnover thresholds (â‚¹20L/â‚¹40L) or compulsory registration u/s 24.
(g) Whether any particular activity amounts to or results in a supply Examining Schedule I, Schedule II, or Schedule III (e.g., director remuneration, liquidated damages).
The Absolute Statutory Bar: PLACE OF SUPPLY CANNOT BE RAISED! AAR and AAAR are State-level authorities. Consequently, the question of "Determination of Place of Supply" is NOT included in Section 97(2)!
• Neither AAR nor AAAR has statutory jurisdiction to decide whether a supply is intra-State or inter-State or determine place of supply.
• Any application seeking determination of place of supply shall be rejected at the threshold.

Stage Procedural Mandates & Statutory Rules

2. Scrutiny & Admission Copy sent to concerned officer.
Ground for Non-Admission [Sec 98(2)]: Application SHALL NOT BE ADMITTED if the question raised is already pending or decided in any proceedings in the case of the applicant under any provisions of the Act!
Natural Justice: No application shall be rejected without giving an opportunity of personal hearing. Rejection must be through a speaking order.
3. Pronouncement Timeline The AAR shall pronounce its ruling in writing within 90 DAYS from the date of receipt of the application [Section 98(6)].
4. Difference of Opinion at AAR Since AAR consists of two members (one Central, one State), if there is a difference of opinion between the two members, they shall refer the point(s) of difference to the Appellate Authority (AAAR) [Section 98(5)].

6. Applicability & Binding Nature of Advance Ruling [Section 103]

In Personam Character

Strictly Binding on Two Parties Only

Under Section 103(1), an advance ruling pronounced by AAR or AAAR is BINDING ONLY ON:

  1. The Applicant who sought the advance ruling; and
  2. The Concerned Officer or Jurisdictional Officer in respect of the applicant.

Implication: An advance ruling has NO PRECEDENTIAL VALUE for third parties! Another taxpayer in the same trade cannot rely on it as a matter of right, nor can the department enforce it against third-party competitors.

Period of Validity

Perpetual until Law or Facts Change

Under Section 103(2), the advance ruling remains binding indefinitely unless the law, facts, or circumstances supporting the original ruling have undergone a change.

There is NO REQUIREMENT to apply for renewal of an advance ruling every year!

7. Advance Ruling Void Ab-Initio [Section 104]

Under Section 104, where the Authority (AAR) or Appellate Authority (AAAR) finds that an advance ruling was obtained by the applicant or appellant by:

  • Fraud; or
  • Suppression of material facts; or
  • Misrepresentation of facts;
Consequence of Annulment: The Authority may, by order, declare such advance ruling to be VOID AB-INITIO (null and void from the very beginning)!
• Thereupon, all provisions of the GST Act (including normal demand proceedings under Section 73/74/74A, interest under Section 50, and penalties under Section 122) shall apply to the applicant as if such advance ruling had never been made.
• Natural Justice: An opportunity of being heard must be provided before passing an order under Section 104.

8. Powers of Civil Court & Rectification of Errors

Section 105: CPC Powers

Powers of Civil Court

AAR and AAAR possess powers of a Civil Court under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (CPC) regarding: (a) discovery and inspection; (b) enforcing attendance and examining on oath; and (c) compelling production of books. Proceedings are deemed judicial proceedings under IPC/BNS.

Section 102: Rectification

Rectification of Mistake Apparent

AAR or AAAR may amend any order to rectify any mistake apparent from the record within 6 MONTHS from the date of the order. If an amendment enhances liability or reduces input tax credit, an opportunity of being heard is mandatory.

9. ICAI Test Your Knowledge (TYK) - Complete Solved Problems

ICAI TYK 1 (The 7 Eligible Questions)

Question:

Which are the questions for which an advance ruling can be sought under Section 97(2)?

Answer:

Under Section 97(2) of the CGST Act, 2017, an advance ruling can be sought on the following 7 questions:

  1. Classification of any goods or services or both;
  2. Applicability of a notification issued under the provisions of the CGST Act;
  3. Determination of time and value of supply of goods or services or both;
  4. Admissibility of input tax credit of tax paid or deemed to have been paid;
  5. Determination of the liability to pay tax on any goods or services or both;
  6. Whether applicant is required to be registered;
  7. Whether any particular thing done by the applicant regarding any goods or services or both amounts to or results in a supply of goods or services or both.

Crucial Exclusion: Place of Supply is not covered under Section 97(2); hence, no advance ruling can be sought on determination of place of supply.

ICAI TYK 2 (Objectives of Advance Ruling)

Question:

What is the objective of having a mechanism of Advance Ruling under GST?

Answer:

The broad statutory objectives for setting up the mechanism of Advance Ruling are:

  • Provide Certainty: To provide certainty in tax liability in advance in relation to an activity proposed to be undertaken by the applicant;
  • Attract Foreign Direct Investment (FDI): By ensuring legal clarity and transparency in taxation aspects of international commercial contracts;
  • Reduce Litigation: By avoiding expensive, long-drawn disputes with tax authorities at a later date;
  • Expeditious & Inexpensive Justice: To pronounce authoritative rulings in a transparent, expeditious, and inexpensive administrative manner.
ICAI TYK 3 (To Whom Advance Ruling is Applicable)

Question:

To whom will the Advance Ruling be applicable?

Answer:

Under Section 103(1) of the CGST Act, 2017, an advance ruling pronounced by the AAR or AAAR shall be binding ONLY ON:

  1. The Applicant who had sought it in respect of any matter referred to in Section 97(2); and
  2. The Concerned Officer or the Jurisdictional Officer in respect of the said applicant.

The advance ruling operates strictly in personam. It is not binding on any other taxable person, nor does it operate as a binding precedent on other tax officers for different assessees.

ICAI TYK 4 (Period of Applicability)

Question:

What is the time period for applicability of an Advance Ruling?

Answer:

Under Section 103(2) of the CGST Act, 2017, the advance ruling shall be binding unless the law, facts or circumstances supporting the original advance ruling have changed.

Therefore, an advance ruling continues to remain valid and binding perpetually so long as the underlying statutory provisions and the factual matrix remain identical. There is no requirement in law to seek an advance ruling on an annual basis.

ICAI TYK 5 (Nullification of Advance Ruling)

Question:

Can an advance ruling given be nullified?

Answer:

Yes. Under Section 104(1) of the CGST Act, 2017, where the Authority (AAR) or Appellate Authority (AAAR) finds that an advance ruling has been obtained by the applicant or appellant by fraud, or suppression of material facts, or misrepresentation of facts, it may, by order, declare such advance ruling to be VOID AB-INITIO.

Thereupon, all provisions of the Act shall apply to the applicant as if such advance ruling had never been made. However, no order declaring a ruling void shall be passed without giving the applicant an opportunity of being heard.

ICAI TYK 6 (Comprehensive Practical Scenario - Ranjan & Sambhav)

Problem Statement:

Ranjan intends to start selling certain goods in Delhi with contentious classification. Tax advisor advises him to apply for advance ruling. Advise Ranjan on:

  • (i) Advisor says Ranjan must get registered under GST before applying. Does Ranjan need to register?
  • (ii) Ranjan fears he has to seek advance ruling every year. Is his apprehension correct?
  • (iii) Advisor says AAR order is final and non-appealable. Is this view correct?
  • (iv) Sambhav (registered supplier in Delhi) wants to apply the classification decided in Ranjan's advance ruling to his own identical goods. Can Sambhav do so?

Detailed Legal Advice & Solutions:

  • (i) Need for Registration [Section 95(c)]: Advisor's advice is INCORRECT. Section 95(c) explicitly defines an applicant as "any person registered OR desirous of obtaining registration under this Act." Hence, an unregistered person intending to commence business can validly apply for an advance ruling without obtaining prior registration.
  • (ii) Annual Renewal Apprehension [Section 103(2)]: Ranjan's apprehension is INCORRECT. Under Section 103(2), an advance ruling remains binding perpetually as long as the law, facts, and circumstances supporting the ruling remain unchanged. He does not need to apply every year.
  • (iii) Appealability of AAR Order [Section 100]: Advisor's view is INCORRECT. Under Section 100, an order of the AAR is appealable before the Appellate Authority for Advance Ruling (AAAR) within 30 days (+ 30 days condonation) in Form GST ARA-02.
  • (iv) Reliance by Third Party Sambhav [Section 103(1)]: Sambhav CANNOT do so. Under Section 103(1), an advance ruling is binding strictly in personam on the applicant (Ranjan) and his jurisdictional officer. It has no binding legal effect on third-party suppliers like Sambhav. If Sambhav desires certainty, he must file an independent application in his own name.
ICAI TYK 7 (Procedure before AAR under Sec 98)

Question:

Briefly explain the procedure to be followed by the Authority for Advance Ruling on receipt of an application under Section 98.

Answer:

Under Section 98 of the CGST Act, 2017:

  1. Upon receipt of application in Form GST ARA-01, AAR forwards a copy to the concerned officer and calls for relevant records.
  2. Admission / Rejection: AAR examines records and passes order admitting or rejecting the application. Bar on Admission: Application cannot be admitted if the question is already pending or decided in any proceedings in the applicant's case. Hearing must be given before rejection.
  3. Pronouncement: On admission, AAR provides opportunity of personal hearing and pronounces its ruling in writing within 90 days from the date of receipt of the application.
  4. Deadlock: If members of AAR differ in opinion, they refer the point(s) to AAAR.
  5. Certified copy sent to applicant, concerned officer, and jurisdictional officer.
ICAI TYK 8 (Appeals to AAAR under Sec 100 & 101)

Question:

Briefly explain whether an appeal could be filed before the Appellate Authority against an order of the AAR, with reference to Sections 100 and 101.

Answer:

Yes. Under Section 100 of the CGST Act, 2017:

  • The applicant, or the concerned officer/jurisdictional officer aggrieved by an advance ruling of the AAR, may appeal to the Appellate Authority for Advance Ruling (AAAR).
  • Time Limit: Within 30 days from the date of communication of the ruling (condonable by further 30 days on sufficient cause).
  • Order under Section 101: AAAR shall pass an order confirming or modifying the ruling within 90 days of filing the appeal, after granting an opportunity of being heard.
  • Difference of Opinion: If the members of AAAR differ on any point, it shall be deemed that no advance ruling can be issued in respect of the question under appeal.
