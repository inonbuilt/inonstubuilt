1. Introduction: Exempt Supply vs. Non-Taxable Supply

Just because a transaction qualifies as a "Supply" (Chapter 1) and falls within the "Charging Section" (Chapter 2) does not automatically mean you must pay GST! You must check whether an exemption saves the transaction from tax.

📖 Key Statutory Definitions (Section 2 CGST Act, 2017)
  • Exempt Supply Sec 2(47): Means supply of any goods or services or both which:
    1. attracts nil rate of tax, OR
    2. may be wholly exempt from tax under Section 11 CGST / Section 6 IGST, AND
    3. includes non-taxable supply.
  • Non-Taxable Supply Sec 2(78): Means a supply of goods or services or both which is not leviable to tax under the CGST Act or IGST Act.
    Examples: Alcoholic liquor for human consumption; 5 specified petroleum products (Petroleum Crude, High Speed Diesel, Motor Spirit/Petrol, Natural Gas, Aviation Turbine Fuel); and un-denatured extra neutral alcohol or rectified spirit used for manufacture of alcoholic liquor for human consumption.

How Exemptions Can Be Granted (4 Architectural Patterns)

In GST law, an exemption notification grants relief in one of four ways. Paying attention to who or what triggers the exemption makes solving exam questions effortless:

2. Power to Grant Exemption from Tax Sec 11 CGST / Sec 6 IGST

⚖️ Statutory Architecture of Section 11 CGST (Parallel to Section 6 IGST)
Sub-section Mechanism & Powers Key Legal Rules
Sec 11(1)
General Exemption		 By Notification in Official Gazette on recommendation of GST Council, in public interest. May exempt generally, either absolutely (unconditional) or subject to conditions, goods or services or both from whole or part of tax, effective from date specified in notification.
Sec 11(2)
Special Order		 By Special Order in each case on recommendation of GST Council, in public interest. Issued only under circumstances of an exceptional nature (reasons must be stated in the order).
Sec 11(3)
Retrospective Explanation		 Insert an Explanation in any notification u/s 11(1) or order u/s 11(2) within 1 year of issue. Clarifies scope or applicability. Every such explanation takes effect retrospectively as if it had always been part of the original notification/order!
Explanation to Sec 11 Mandatory Rule for Absolute Exemption Where exemption from whole or part of tax is granted absolutely, the registered supplier SHALL NOT collect tax in excess of effective rate.
🚨 Exam Trap: Mandatory vs. Optional Exemption + Retrospective Explanation
  • Absolute (Unconditional) Exemption = MANDATORY: If a supply is unconditionally exempt from whole of tax, a supplier has NO option to charge GST and pass on ITC. If exempt from part of tax (say, reduced rate of 5%), supplier cannot collect more than 5%.
  • Conditional Exemption = OPTIONAL: Where exemption is subject to specified conditions (e.g., "provided no ITC is taken"), the registered person has an option either to fulfil the condition and avail exemption, OR pay full tax and claim ITC.
  • Circular No. 120/39/2019-GST (Retrospective Effect of Sec 11(3)): An explanation inserted within 1 year of an entry's insertion applies from the date of inception of that entry, NOT from the date the amending notification becomes effective!
💡 Example 9 • Retrospective Date of Explanation (Circular 120/39/2019)

Facts: Principal Notification 11/2017-CT(R) took effect on 01.07.2017. A new Entry 3(vi) was inserted w.e.f. 21.09.2017. An explanation clarifying Entry 3(vi) was issued on 26.07.2018 (within 1 year of 21.09.2017), stating effective date 27.07.2018.

Result: The explanation takes effect from 21.09.2017 (inception of Entry 3(vi)), NOT 27.07.2018!

3. Goods Exempt from Tax

Under Section 11(1) of CGST Act / Section 6(1) of IGST Act, everyday essential items used by the common man have been wholly exempted from GST (e.g., Notification No. 2/2017-CT(R)). Note that these are exempt unconditionally.

🥛 Illustrative List of Exempt Goods (ICAI Study Material)
  • Daily Food Essentials: Unbranded atta/maida/besan, unpacked food grains, milk (Fresh Milk excluding UHT Milk — HSN 0401), eggs, curd, lassi, fresh vegetables.
  • Produce & Livestock: Potatoes (HSN 0701), Live fish (HSN 0301), Grapes (HSN 0806).
  • National & Cultural Items: Indian National Flag (HSN 63), Plastic Bangles (HSN 3926).

Note: Only services exemption entries under Notification No. 12/2017-CT(R) (and 9/2017-IT(R)) are tested exhaustively in CA Final; the CBIC website maintains the full tariff schedule of exempt goods.

All services exemptions stem from Notification No. 12/2017-CT(R) dated 28.06.2017 (and parallel Notification No. 9/2017-IT(R) for IGST — entry numbers are for reference only and not strictly required to be memorized in exams, though knowing key ones helps).

📜 Relevant Entries: Entries 1, 13, 60 & 80
EntryExempt Service Description
Entry 1 Services by an entity registered u/s 12AA / 12AB of the Income-tax Act, 1961 by way of "charitable activities".
Entry 13 Services by a person by way of:
(a) conduct of any religious ceremony;
(b) renting of precincts of a religious place meant for general public, owned/managed by an entity registered u/s 12AA/12AB/10(23C)(v) or body u/s 10(23BBA) of IT Act.

(a) recreational activities relating to arts or culture, by an individual; OR
(b) sports by charitable entities registered u/s 12AA or 12AB of IT Act.

A. Meaning of "Charitable Activities" under Entry 1

To get Entry 1 exemption, BOTH conditions must be met: (1) entity is registered u/s 12AA/12AB IT Act, AND (2) activity falls strictly within the 4-limb statutory definition of "charitable activities":

  1. Public Health by way of: (A) Care/counseling of (I) terminally ill or severe physical/mental disability, (II) HIV/AIDS afflicted, (III) drug/alcohol addicts; OR (B) public awareness of preventive health, family planning or HIV prevention.
  2. Advancement of Religion, Spirituality or Yoga (e.g. Example 12: Bhavyajyoti Foundation meditation camp for old age people = Exempt).
  3. Advancement of Educational Programmes / Skill Development relating to ONLY: (A) abandoned, orphaned or homeless children; (B) physically/mentally abused & traumatized persons; (C) prisoners; or (D) persons over 65 years residing in a RURAL area.
  4. Preservation of Environment including watershed, forests & wildlife.
🚨 Exam Traps on Charitable & Religious Entities
  • Example 10 (Shiksha Academy): Skill development for persons aged 65+ residing in Bangalore (urban area) → TAXABLE! Limb (iii)(D) requires them to reside in a rural area (village per land revenue records outside municipal/urban limits).
  • Hostel Accommodation by Trusts: Does NOT fall under "charitable activities" u/s Entry 1 → check Entry 12A (₹20,000/90 days) or Entry 66 if run by an educational institution itself.
  • Advertising / Event Tickets by Trust: Granting advertisement space on trust premises/publications or selling event tickets = TAXABLE (not charitable activity).
  • General Religious Yatras: Taxable! Only Haj Committee & KMVN (Example 11: medical, catering, security, accommodation by KMVN for Kailash-Mansarovar = Exempt under Entry 60).
  • Circular 66/40/2018-GST (Camps & Fitness): Residential yoga/meditation/spiritual camps (incl. lodging & boarding) = EXEMPT if predominant purpose is spirituality/yoga. However, if primary purpose is accommodation/food OR fitness camps (aerobics, dance, music classes) = TAXABLE under Entry 1!
  • Entry 80 Nuance: Arts/culture coaching is exempt ONLY if given by an individual (any individual; need not be registered charity — covers dance, music, painting, sculpture, literature, theatre). Sports coaching is exempt ONLY if given by a 12AA/12AB charitable entity (Example 13: Manavtaa Sansthaan Basketball camp = Exempt).
  • Services TO Charitable Trusts: NOT exempt! Suppliers must charge GST on supplies made to trusts unless separately exempt.
  • Religious Ceremonies (Entry 13(a)): Priest charging ₹12,000 for birthday pooja (Example 14) = EXEMPT.
  • Donations (Example 15): If donor requires ad promoting his business → TAXABLE (quid pro quo). If displayed merely as gratitude/philanthropy without advertising publicity → No supply, NO GST!
  • Boundary of Entry 13(b) Thresholds: Exemption requires charges to be STRICTLY LESS THAN the threshold ("₹1,000 or more" / "₹10,000 or more" are taxable!).
    • Example 16 (Durgadevi Trust): Shop rent = exactly ₹10,000/month → TAXABLE (exempt only if < ₹10,000/month).
    • Example 17 (Sarvodaya Trust): Community hall = ₹9,000/day → EXEMPT (< ₹10,000/day).

5. Category 2: Agriculture Related Services Entries 24, 24A, 24B, 54, 55, 55A

🌾 Core Agriculture Exemption Scope (Entry 54 & Allied Entries)
  • Entry 54: Services relating to cultivation of plants and rearing of all life forms of animals (EXCEPT horses), for food, fibre, fuel, raw material or similar products, OR agricultural produce, by way of:
    1. (a) Direct Agricultural Operations: Cultivation, harvesting, threshing, plant protection, testing (includes pisciculture, sericulture, floriculture, horticulture, forestry).
    2. (b) Supply of Farm Labour.
    3. (c) Farm-Level Primary Market Processes: Tending, pruning, cutting, drying, cleaning, trimming, sun drying, fumigating, curing, sorting, grading, cooling, bulk packaging — that do NOT alter essential characteristics and make it marketable for the primary market.
    4. (d) Renting/Leasing of Agro Machinery or Vacant Land (with/without structure incidental to use — Example 18: Moolchand leases land with greenhouse & storage shed to farmer Tulsidas = Exempt).
    5. (e) Loading, unloading, packing, storage or warehousing of Agricultural Produce.
    6. (f) Agricultural Extension Services (AES): Scientific research/knowledge transfer to farmers (kisan call centres, farm visits, kisan melas, advisory bulletins).
    7. (g) Services by APMC / Board or Commission Agent for sale/purchase of agricultural produce (Note: renting shops by APMC for commercial purposes = Taxable!).
  • Entry 55 (Intermediate Job Work): Carrying out intermediate production process as job work in relation to cultivation/rearing (except horses).
  • Entry 55A: Artificial insemination of livestock (other than horses).
  • Separate Warehousing Exemptions: Entry 24 (Loading, unloading, packing, storage/warehousing of Rice); Entry 24A (Warehousing of Minor Forest Produce); Entry 24B (Storage/warehousing of Cereals, Pulses, Fruits and Vegetables — note: Entry 24B covers even processed/split pulses & shelled fruits/vegetables in storage/warehousing!).

6. Category 3: Educational Services Entries 66 & 66A

Step 1: What is an "Educational Institution" (EI)?

Entry 66 only protects institutions falling in one of 3 specific categories:

  1. Category (i) — Schools: Institution providing pre-school education (includes Anganwadis!) and education up to higher secondary school or equivalent (Example 20: Littleways Public School).
  2. Category (ii) — Recognized Degree/Curriculum: Institution providing education as part of a curriculum for obtaining a qualification recognised by any Indian law for the time being in force (Example 19: Dharam Institute of Technology AICTE-recognized PG engineering incl. distance learning = EI).
    Exclusions: Private coaching institutes for IBPS/JEE (Example 22: Super Minds → NOT EI); Foreign university degrees not recognized by Indian law → NOT EI!
  3. Category (iii) — Approved Vocational Education Course:
    • Course run by an ITI/ITC affiliated to NCVET or SCVT offering courses in designated trades notified under Apprentices Act, 1961 (Example 21: Kaladrishti ITI running non-designated trades → NOT EI); OR
    • Modular Employable Skill (MES) Course approved by NCVET, run by a person registered with Directorate General of Training (DGT).

Crucial Clarifications & Latest 2024 Amendments in Education

  • Government vs. Private ITIs (Circular 55/29/2018): Private ITIs enjoy Entry 66 exemption ONLY for designated trades (non-designated trades taxable for output, entrance, and admission/exam inputs). Government ITIs providing vocational training & exams to individual students are exempt under Entry 6 (Govt services to non-business individuals) regardless of trade!
  • Hostels & Boarding Schools: Hostel lodging/boarding provided by the educational institution itself to its students is Exempt under Entry 66(a). Boarding schools charging inseparable consolidated fees qualify as Composite Supply (Principal supply = Education → Entire fee Exempt).
  • Dual Qualifications & Auxiliary Hobby Courses: Separate curriculum & separate fees = assessed separately. Artificial clubbing for single fee where only one is recognized = Mixed Supply u/s 2(74) (highest GST rate applies!).
  • IIMs Long vs. Short Duration Programmes (Circular 82/01/2019): Under IIM Act 2017, IIMs grant recognized degrees/diplomas. Long duration courses (≥ 1 year) to students = EXEMPT. Short-term executive programmes (< 1 year) awarding only "participation certificate" to executives/participants = TAXABLE!
  • Campus Placement Fee: Fees charged by IITs/IIMs from corporate employers/MNCs for campus recruitment = TAXABLE (provided to companies, not to students!).
  • Central/State Boards & NTA (Incl. NBE): Deemed EI for the limited purpose of conducting examinations (incl. entrance exams) for students under Entry 66(aa) & availing exam inputs under Entry 66(b)(iv). HOWEVER, accreditation fees / registration fees like FMGE screening test charged from institutions/professionals = TAXABLE!
  • Mess/Canteen in Colleges: Run by college itself → Exempt u/s 66(a). Outsourced to third-party caterer → TAXABLE for colleges (only exempt if provided to pre-school/HSC u/s 66(b)(ii)!).
  • Anganwadis (Circular 149/05/2021): Pre-school non-formal education = EI. Serving food/mid-day meals to Anganwadi is Exempt u/s 66(b)(ii) whether funded by Govt grant or CSR/corporate donation.
  • Application, Eligibility & Migration Fees (Circular 177/09/2022): Application fee, eligibility certificate fee for prospective students, and migration certificate fee from ex-students = EXEMPT u/s 66(a)/(aa).
  • Maritime Training Institutes (Circular 117/36/2019): Approved by DG Shipping under Merchant Shipping Act = EI → Exempt!
  • DGCA Approved Flying Training Organizations — FTOs (Circular 234/28/2024 dated 11.10.2024): Approved flying courses mandating completion certificates under Aircraft Act, 1934 = EI → EXEMPT!
  • University & School Board Affiliation Services (Circular 234/28/2024 & Entry 66A):
    • Affiliation by Universities to constituent colleges = TAXABLE (monitoring infrastructure, not admission/exam!).
    • Affiliation by Boards/Councils to private schools = TAXABLE!
    • NEW Entry 66A: Affiliation by Central/State Educational Boards/Councils to Government Schools (established, owned or controlled by CG/SG/UT/LA/GA/GE) = EXEMPT!

7. Category 4: Health Care & Veterinary Services Entries 46 & 74

🏥 Entries 46 & 74 of Notification No. 12/2017-CT(R)
  • Entry 46: Services by a veterinary clinic in relation to health care of animals or birds = EXEMPT.
  • Entry 74(a): Health care services by a clinical establishment, an authorised medical practitioner or para-medics.
    ROOM RENT PROVISO: Does NOT apply to services provided by a clinical establishment by way of providing a room [OTHER THAN ICU / CCU / ICCU / NICU] having room charges exceeding ₹5,000 per day to a person receiving health care services!
  • Entry 74(b): Transportation of a patient in an ambulance by any person other than those in (a) above = EXEMPT (even private ambulance operators!).

What Qualifies as "Health Care Services"?

  • Includes: Diagnosis, treatment or care for illness, injury, deformity, abnormality or pregnancy in any recognised system of medicines in India + transportation of patient to and from a clinical establishment.
  • 7 Recognized Systems (Clinical Establishments Act, 2010): Allopathy, Yoga, Naturopathy, Ayurveda, Homeopathy, Siddha, Unani (plus any other CG-recognized system). Example 29: Reiki healing is NOT recognized → Taxable!
  • Cosmetic / Plastic Surgery Exclusion: Hair transplant or cosmetic/plastic surgery is TAXABLE, EXCEPT when undertaken to restore/reconstruct anatomy or body functions affected due to congenital defects, developmental abnormalities, injury or trauma (Example 27: Cleft lip repair on newborn = Exempt healthcare).
  • Place Irrelevant (Example 28): Palliative care for terminally ill patients provided at patient's home = EXEMPT.
  • Clinical Establishment: Includes hospitals, nursing homes, clinics, sanatoriums AND independent pathological labs / diagnostic centres (so blood tests/scans are Exempt!).
  • Paramedics: Nursing staff, physiotherapists, technicians, lab assistants — exempt both as employees and in independent capacity.
📜 Crucial Healthcare Clarifications (Circulars 32/06/2018, 51/25/2018 & 177/09/2022)
  • Senior Doctors / Consultants (Circular 32/06/2018): Even when hospitals hire senior doctors/consultants/technicians independently without employer-employee relationship, services provided by them to hospitals = EXEMPT as healthcare services!
  • Retention Money by Hospitals (Circular 32/06/2018): If hospital bills patient ₹10,000, pays doctor ₹7,500, and retains ₹2,500 for nursing/infrastructure/paramedic care → ENTIRE ₹10,000 is EXEMPT (incl. retention money).
  • Food Supplied by Hospital (Circular 32/06/2018):
    • Food to in-patients as advised by doctor/nutritionist = Composite supply of healthcare → EXEMPT.
    • Food to out-patients (not admitted), attendants or visitors = TAXABLE!
    • Note: If hospital outsources food preparation to an outdoor caterer, the caterer charges GST to hospital (hospital gets NO ITC).
  • NHM Dial 102/108 Ambulance Services (Circular 51/25/2018): Transportation in ambulance to patients = Exempt u/s Entry 74(b). Fee charged by Private Service Provider (PSP) from State Govt for operating free ambulance under NHM = Exempt under Entry 3 (pure service) or Entry 3A (composite supply with goods ≤ 25%) because public health/sanitation is an Art 243G/243W function!
  • IVF / Assisted Reproductive Technology (Circular 177/09/2022): Infertility is an abnormality/disease; ART/IVF procedures are covered under "health care services" = EXEMPT!
  • Commercial Premises inside Hospitals: Renting shops, auditoriums, display of ads inside clinical establishment premises = TAXABLE.

8. Category 5: Services Provided BY Government Entries 4 to 9G, 24C, 34A, 47, 61–65B, 74A

🏛️ Key Definitions: Government, Local Authority, GA & GE
  • Government Sec 2(53): Central Government / State Government / UT Administrator. Regulatory bodies do NOT qualify as "Government" (e.g., CCI, Press Council, DGCA, SEBI, Central Pollution Control Board, IWAI).
  • Local Authority Sec 2(69): Strictly covers Panchayat, Municipality, Municipal Committee/Zilla Parishad/District Board managing municipal/local fund, Cantonment Board, 6th Schedule / Art 371 / 371A / 371J Councils/Boards. State Developmental Authorities (DDA, ADA, BDA) are NOT local authorities!
  • Governmental Authority (GA): Body set up by Act of Parliament/State Legislature OR established by Govt with ≥ 90% equity/control, to carry out Article 243G (Panchayat — 11th Sch) or Article 243W (Municipality — 12th Sch) functions.
  • Government Entity (GE): Same ≥ 90% Govt equity/control structure (incl. society, trust, corporation) to carry out ANY function entrusted by CG/SG/UT/LA.

Entry 6 Architecture: The 4 Excluded "Taxable Buckets" + Complete Govt Entry Matrix

Under Entry 6, services provided BY CG/SG/UT/LA are generally EXEMPT EXCEPT four buckets — items (a), (b), (c) [called "Specified Services"] and (d):

  1. (a) Specified Services — Dept. of Posts & Ministry of Railways (subject to specific carved-out exemptions in Entries 24C & 9E/9F/9G below!).
  2. (b) Specified Services — Aircraft or Vessel inside or outside precincts of a port or airport.
  3. (c) Specified Services — Transport of Goods or Passengers (check Category 7/8 exemptions).
  4. (d) Any OTHER service provided to a Business Entity (unless small business entity covered by Entry 7; if above registration threshold, business entity pays tax under RCM!).

9. Category 6: Construction Services Entries 10, 10A, 11, 41A, 41B

🤝 Clarification on Scope of "Intermediary" (Circular No. 159/15/2021-GST • Examples 80–84)
  • Prerequisites of Intermediary u/s 2(13): (1) Minimum 3 parties; (2) Two separate supplies — Main Supply between two principals + Ancillary Supply of arranging/facilitating; (3) Role is merely supportive (excludes anyone supplying main service on own account / principal-to-principal); (4) Sub-contracting is NOT intermediary!
  • Ex 80 (Sub-contracted AMC): A contracts with B for machinery AMC & subcontracts to C. C provides main AMC service to A → NOT an intermediary!
  • Ex 81 (Machine Broker): C locates buyer B for manufacturer A, helps finalize deal & charges commission from A → IS an intermediary!
  • Ex 82 (Sub-contracted Software): A outsources software module development to C who interacts with B for specs → C supplies main software service to A → NOT an intermediary!
  • Ex 83 (Claims Broker): Foreign insurer P hires Q in India to find claims-processor R; R processes claims for P; Q charges 1% commission → Q merely arranges between P & R → IS an intermediary!
  • Ex 84 (Outsourced Call Centre / BPO): US manufacturer A outsources customer care BPO to B on P2P basis → B delivers main customer care service → NOT an intermediary!

20. Power Not to Recover GST Short-Levied Due to General Practice Sec 11A CGST / Sec 6A IGST

📜 Regularization of Bona Fide Industry Practice (Section 11A CGST / Section 6A IGST)

Notwithstanding anything in the Act, if the Government is satisfied (on GST Council recommendation) that:

  1. a practice was, or is, generally prevalent regarding levy (or non-levy) of GST on any supply of goods/services; AND
  2. such supplies were legally liable to tax (or a higher rate) than what was paid under that general practice,

the Government may, by Notification in the Official Gazette, direct that the whole of the tax (or excess differential tax) shall NOT be required to be paid for supplies affected by that general practice! This protects taxpayers acting in good faith.

🎯 TYK Question 1 • Legal Services Turnover Threshold & Bank Minimum Balance Fees

Facts: Examine whether these independent intra-State services are exempt:
(a) Legal services by BMC & Partners, Delhi (partnership firm of advocates) to Vastukaar Enterprises, Delhi (architects, preceding FY aggregate turnover = ₹21 lakh).
(b) Minimum balance charges collected by Dhanvarsha Bank from current and savings account holders.

🔑 Answer:

  • (a) Taxable (under RCM): Under Entry 45(b), legal services by a partnership firm of advocates to a business entity are exempt ONLY if the recipient's preceding FY aggregate turnover makes it eligible for registration exemption (₹20 lakh for architect service provider in Delhi). Since Vastukaar Enterprises' preceding FY turnover (₹21 lakh) exceeds ₹20 lakh, the services are NOT exempt (taxable by recipient Vastukaar under Reverse Charge).
  • (b) Taxable: Under Entry 27, extending deposits/loans/advances is exempt only insofar as consideration is represented by interest or discount. Service charges, administrative fees, or minimum balance charges collected over and above interest are NOT exempt and are liable to GST.
🎯 TYK Question 2 • Shiva Medical Centre — Consultant Doctors & Hospital Retention Money

Facts: Shiva Medical Centre (multi-speciality hospital, Mumbai) hires senior doctors/consultants independently (no employer-employee contract) to treat in-patients, pays them consultancy fees, and charges a higher total fee from in-patients. Department contends GST applies on (i) doctors' consultancy and (ii) hospital retention money. Test Department's stand.

🔑 Answer: Department's stand is NOT correct.
Under Entry 74 & Circular No. 32/06/2018-GST: (1) Services by senior doctors/consultants/technicians hired by hospitals (whether employees or independent consultants) are health care services exempt from GST. (2) The entire amount charged by the hospital from patients — including retention money towards nursing care, infrastructure, paramedic care, emergency services, and doctor fees — is towards health care services supplied by the hospital to patients and is wholly EXEMPT.

🎯 TYK Question 3 • Vedanta Hospital (Annapurna Bhawan Canteen) — In-Patients vs Visitors

Facts: Vedanta Hospital's in-house basement restaurant Annapurna Bhawan supplies food to (i) in-patients as advised by doctor/nutritionist, and (ii) out-patients, attendants, and visitors. Hospital claims all its services are exempt.

🔑 Answer: Hospital's view is partially INCORRECT.
Per Circular No. 32/06/2018-GST: Food supplied by hospital canteen to in-patients as advised by doctor/nutritionist forms part of a composite supply where healthcare is the principal supply u/s 2(90) → EXEMPT. However, other supplies of food by hospital canteen to non-admitted patients, attendants, or visitors are NOT healthcare services → TAXABLE.

🎯 TYK Question 4 • IIM Indore — Placement Registration Fee from MNCs

Facts: IIM Indore organises a campus placement drive and collects ₹1,00,000 registration fee from participating multinational companies. IIM Indore claims exemption.

🔑 Answer: View taken by IIM Indore is INCORRECT (Taxable).
While IIMs qualify as an 'educational institution' under the IIM Act, 2017, Entry 66(a) exempts services provided by an educational institution ONLY "to its students, faculty and staff" (plus entrance exams). Placement participation services supplied to corporate entities/MNCs are NOT services to students/faculty/staff → TAXABLE.

🎯 TYK Question 5 • Central Government Guaranteeing Loan of PSU (with Default)

Facts: India Corporations Ltd. (a PSU) took a loan from Wellness Bank Ltd. guaranteed by Central Government, and defaulted in repayment. Examine GST liability on the guarantee service.

🔑 Answer: EXEMPT from GST.
Under Entry 34A, services supplied by CG/SG/UT to their undertakings or PSUs by way of guaranteeing loans taken from banking companies/financial institutions are EXEMPT. Subsequent default by the borrower PSU does NOT affect eligibility for the exemption.

🎯 TYK Question 6 • British High Commission Seminar Advisory Fee

Facts: British High Commission (chief UK diplomatic mission in India) organises a study-abroad seminar and collects registration fee of ₹5,000 per student for advisory services. Is it liable to GST?

Under Entry 59 of Notification No. 12/2017-CT(R), "Services by a foreign diplomatic mission located in India" are unconditionally exempt. Hence, advisory seminar services provided by the British High Commission to students are EXEMPT. (Exam contrast: Services provided TO a foreign diplomatic mission are taxable — see TYK Q10(iv)!)
