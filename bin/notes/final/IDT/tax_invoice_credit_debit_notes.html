CGST ACT, 2017 • SECTIONS 31 TO 34 • RULES 46 TO 55A

Chapter 9: Tax Invoice, Credit & Debit Notes

Exhaustive textbook revision notes on statutory documentation under GST. Covers tax invoice due dates for goods and services, mandatory contents under Rule 46, E-invoicing applicability (₹5 Cr threshold) and exempt sectors, Dynamic QR code (₹500 Cr), Revised Tax Invoices, Bill of Supply, Receipt & Payment Vouchers, Delivery Challans, and Credit & Debit Notes.

9.1 Significance of Tax Invoices under GST

Under GST, a Tax Invoice is not just a commercial document; it is the primary statutory document evidencing tax payment and the prerequisite for availing Input Tax Credit (ITC) under Section 16(2)(a).

📌 Core Invoicing Principles:
• Goods vs Services Due Dates: Goods invoice due date is governed by Section 31(1) (removal/delivery); services invoice due date is governed by Section 31(2) read with Rule 47 (30 days/45 days).
• B2C Invoice Exemption (< ₹200): No tax invoice need be issued if value of supply < ₹200, recipient is unregistered, and recipient does not require an invoice [Section 31(3)(b)]. A consolidated invoice is prepared at the end of each day.
• Invoice-cum-Bill of Supply [Rule 46A]: A registered person supplying both taxable and exempt goods/services to an unregistered recipient may issue a single document called "Invoice-cum-Bill of Supply".

Section 31 Due Dates for Issuance of Tax Invoices

Category of Supply Statutory Due Date of Tax Invoice
Supply of Goods [Section 31(1)] • Movement of goods involved: Before or at the time of removal of goods for delivery to recipient.
• Other cases: Before or at the time of delivery or making available thereof.
Continuous Supply of Goods [Section 31(4)] Before or at the time each successive statement of accounts is issued or each successive payment is received.
Goods Sent on Approval [Section 31(7)] Before or at the time of supply/acceptance, OR 6 months from the date of removal, whichever is earlier.
Supply of Services [Section 31(2) & Rule 47] Within 30 days from the date of the supply of service.
Banking / Insurance / NBFC / Telecom: Within 45 days from the date of supply of service.
Continuous Supply of Services [Section 31(5)] • Due date ascertainable from contract → On or before the due date of payment.
• Due date not ascertainable → Before or at the time supplier receives payment.
• Payment linked to completion of milestone → On or before the date of completion of that milestone.
Cessation of Service [Section 31(6)] At the time when supply ceases, to the extent of service supplied before cessation.

📑 Manner of Issuing Invoice [Rule 48]:

• Goods (Triplicate): (1) Original for Recipient; (2) Duplicate for Transporter; (3) Triplicate for Supplier.
• Services (Duplicate): (1) Original for Recipient; (2) Duplicate for Supplier.

Rule 48(4) Electronic Invoicing (E-Invoicing) & Dynamic QR Code

E-INVOICING REGIME

Applicability: ₹5 Crore Threshold

• Applies to registered persons whose Aggregate Annual Turnover in ANY preceding FY from 2017-18 onwards exceeds ₹5 Crores!
• Supplies Covered: B2B supplies, exports, and SEZ supplies. (B2C supplies are EXCLUDED from e-invoicing!).
• Involves reporting schema on Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) to generate a unique 64-character Invoice Reference Number (IRN) and QR code.
• Rule 48(5): An invoice without an IRN is completely INVALID under GST!

B2C DYNAMIC QR CODE

Applicability: ₹500 Crore Threshold

• Applies to registered persons whose AATO exceeds ₹500 Crores in any preceding FY.
• Mandatory on B2C tax invoices to enable digital payments by consumers via UPI/QR scan.
• Contains merchant GSTIN, UPI ID, invoice number, date, and invoice value.

🚫 Entities EXEMPT from E-Invoicing (Regardless of Turnover):

  • Special Economic Zone (SEZ) Units (Note: SEZ Developers are NOT exempt!);
  • An insurer, banking company, financial institution, or NBFC;
  • A Goods Transport Agency (GTA) supplying goods transport services;
  • Suppliers of passenger transportation services;
  • Persons supplying services by way of admission to exhibition of cinematograph films in multiplex screens;
  • A Government Department and a local authority.

Section 31(3) Revised Tax Invoices, Bills of Supply & Statutory Vouchers

Document Name When Issued / Governing Rule Key Statutory Features
Revised Tax Invoice [Sec 31(3)(a)] Issued against invoices already issued between the effective date of registration and the date of grant of registration certificate. Must be issued within 1 month from the date of issuance of certificate of registration. Allows recipient to claim ITC for the interim period.
Bill of Supply [Sec 31(3)(c)] Issued by: (1) Composition suppliers u/s 10; OR (2) Suppliers of wholly exempt goods/services. Cannot charge or collect any GST from the customer. Contains words: "Composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies".
Receipt Voucher [Sec 31(3)(d)] Issued on receipt of advance payment with respect to supply of goods or services. If rate of tax is not determinable → Tax @ 18%. If nature of supply not determinable → Treated as Inter-State Supply!
Refund Voucher [Sec 31(3)(e)] Issued when advance payment received (receipt voucher issued) but subsequently no supply is made and no tax invoice is issued. Refunds the advance amount and reverses the tax liability.
Payment Voucher [Sec 31(3)(g)] Issued at the time of making payment to supplier where tax is payable under Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM). Mandatory compliance for recipient paying tax under Section 9(3) or 9(4).
Delivery Challan [Rule 55] Transportation of goods without invoice (Job work, removal of liquid gas, goods on approval, reasons other than supply). Issued in triplicate. Accompanies goods in conveyance. Must contain serial number, date, HSN, description, quantity, and taxable value.

Section 34 Credit Notes & Debit Notes — Legal Framework

SECTION 34(1)

Credit Notes (Reduces Tax Liability)

Issued by supplier when:
1. Taxable value or tax charged in invoice exceeds the actual taxable value/tax payable; OR
2. Goods supplied are returned by the recipient; OR
3. Goods or services supplied are found to be deficient.

⏳ Statutory Time Limit to Declare in Return [Section 34(2)]:
Earlier of:
• 30th November following the end of the financial year in which supply was made; OR
• The date of furnishing of the relevant Annual Return (Form GSTR-9).
Restriction: No reduction in output tax liability if the incidence of tax has been passed on to any other person (unjust enrichment!).

SECTION 34(3)

Debit Notes (Increases Tax Liability)

Issued by supplier when:
• Taxable value or tax charged in original invoice is LESS THAN the actual taxable value/tax payable (supplementary invoice).

Statutory Effects:
• Debit note creates an additional output tax liability for the supplier in the month of issue.
• Recipient can claim Input Tax Credit on the basis of the debit note, subject to the time limits under Section 16(4) linked to the financial year of the debit note.

Sections 32 & 33 Prohibition of Unauthorised Tax Collection & Tax Indication

🚫 Section 32 — Prohibition of Unauthorised Collection of Tax:
• A person who is not a registered person shall not collect in respect of any supply of goods or services or both any amount by way of tax.
• No registered person shall collect tax except in accordance with the provisions of this Act or the rules made thereunder.

🏷️ Section 33 — Amount of Tax to be Indicated in Tax Invoice:
Where any supply is made for a consideration, every person who is liable to pay tax for such supply shall prominently indicate in all documents, invoices and other like documents the amount of tax which shall form part of the price at which such supply is made.

TYK Cases ICAI Test Your Knowledge — Solved Practical Problems (1 to 4)

🎯 TYK Question 1 • Jai General Store (Invoicing Rules & Tax Indication)

Facts: Jai, a registered supplier in Ludhiana (Punjab), operates a general store selling both taxable and exempt goods. Advise him on:
(i) Whether he must issue a tax invoice in all cases, even to end consumers?
(ii) He sells exempt and taxable goods worth ₹5,000 to a school student. Must he issue two separate GST documents?
(iii) Must the tax amount be shown separately in invoices issued to customers?

Statutory Analysis & Solution:

  • (i) Tax Invoice in all cases: No. Under Section 31(3)(b) read with Rule 46, a registered person is not required to issue a tax invoice if: (a) value of goods < ₹200; (b) recipient is unregistered; and (c) recipient does not require an invoice. A consolidated tax invoice is prepared at the close of day. For exempt goods, a Bill of Supply is issued.
  • (ii) Single vs Separate Document: Under Rule 46A, where a supplier makes taxable as well as exempt supplies to an unregistered person, he may issue a single "Invoice-cum-Bill of Supply" instead of two separate documents.
  • (iii) Prominent Tax Mention: Yes. Under Section 33, every person liable to pay tax shall prominently indicate in all tax invoices the amount of tax which forms part of the price.
🎯 TYK Question 2 • Avtaar Enterprises (Revised Tax Invoices Timeline)

Facts: Avtaar Enterprises, Kanpur, exclusively traded in ayurvedic medicines from 1st July. Turnover exceeded ₹40 Lakhs on 3rd October. The firm applied for registration on 31st October and was issued registration certificate on 5th November. Examine whether revised invoices can be issued, the period covered, and the last date to issue them.

Statutory Analysis & Solution:

  • Eligibility to Issue Revised Invoices: Under Section 25(1), application was made within 30 days of becoming liable (applied on 31st October ≤ 30 days from 3rd October). Therefore, registration is effective from 3rd October. Under Section 31(3)(a), a registered person can issue Revised Tax Invoices for supplies made during the intervening period.
  • Intervening Period Covered: From 3rd October (effective date) to 5th November (date of grant of registration certificate).
  • Last Date to Issue Revised Invoices: Under Section 31(3)(a) read with Rule 53(2), revised tax invoices must be issued within 1 month from the date of issuance of certificate of registration → Last date is 5th December!
🎯 TYK Question 3 • Special Invoicing Documents

Facts: Discuss the provisions relating to issuance of invoice/document in the following cases:
(i) Advance payment received, but subsequently no supplies are made;
(ii) Goods sent on approval for sale or return removed before supply takes place;
(iii) Continuous supply of services where payment due date is not ascertainable and no advance received.

Statutory Solutions:

  • (i) Advance with No Supply: Under Section 31(3)(e), where a receipt voucher was issued upon advance receipt, and subsequently no supply is made and no tax invoice is issued, the supplier shall issue a Refund Voucher against such payment.
  • (ii) Goods on Approval: Under Rule 55, goods removed on approval must be accompanied by a Delivery Challan. Under Section 31(7), the tax invoice shall be issued at the time of supply/acceptance OR 6 months from the date of removal, whichever is earlier.
  • (iii) Continuous Supply of Services: Under Section 31(5)(b), where the due date of payment is not ascertainable from the contract, the tax invoice shall be issued before or at the time when the supplier of service receives the payment.
🎯 TYK Question 4 • Pari & Sons (Effective Date & Revised Invoice Period)

Facts: Pari & Sons is an unregistered dealer of taxable supplies in Kerala. On 10th August, its aggregate turnover exceeded ₹20,00,000. The firm applied for registration on 27th August and was granted registration on 1st September. Advise Pari & Sons regarding: (1) Effective date of registration, and (2) Period for issuance of revised tax invoices.

Solution:

  • 1. Effective Date: Under Rule 10(1), since the application was submitted on 27th August (within 30 days from 10th August when liable), the effective date of registration is 10th August.
  • 2. Revised Invoices Period: Under Section 31(3)(a), Pari & Sons can issue revised tax invoices for supplies made between 10th August and 1st September. The revised invoices must be issued within 1 month from grant of certificate (i.e. on or before 1st October).
