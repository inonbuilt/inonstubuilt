CGST ACT, 2017 • SECTIONS 12, 13 & 14

Chapter 5: Time of Supply

Exhaustive textbook revision notes on the statutory point in time when the liability to pay GST crystallizes. Covers forward charge, reverse charge mechanism, Notification No. 66/2017-CT exemption for advances on goods, 30/45 days invoice rules for services, continuous supplies, goods sent on approval, and the foolproof 2-out-of-3 Golden Rule under Section 14.

1.0 Concept & Significance of Time of Supply

While Section 7 determines whether an activity is a "supply" and Section 9 creates the charge of tax, the Time of Supply fixes the exact point in time when the tax liability arises. This dictates in which month's return (Form GSTR-3B) the tax must be deposited and whether interest under Section 50 triggers for delayed payment.

📌 Core Legal Proposition:
• Goods vs Services: The rules for determining Time of Supply for Goods [Section 12] and Services [Section 13] are fundamentally different—most notably regarding the treatment of advances.
• Advances on Goods: 100% EXEMPT from GST at the advance stage vide Notification No. 66/2017-Central Tax.
• Advances on Services: 100% TAXABLE at the time of receipt of advance!

Section 12(2) Time of Supply of Goods — Forward Charge

Under Section 12(2) of the CGST Act, the time of supply of goods under forward charge is the earliest of:

  1. Date of issue of invoice by the supplier; OR
  2. Last date on which the invoice is required to be issued under Section 31(1); OR
  3. Date on which supplier receives payment (entered in books OR credited in bank, whichever is earlier).

⚡ THE GAME CHANGER: Notification No. 66/2017-Central Tax (Dated 15.11.2017)

The Central Government has exempted all registered persons (other than composition dealers u/s 10) from paying GST on advances received for supply of goods.

👉 Practical Exam Rule for Goods (Forward Charge):
GST is payable ONLY AT THE TIME OF INVOICE!
Hence, Time of Supply of Goods is Earliest of:
1. Actual date of invoice; OR
2. Last date on which invoice ought to have been issued under Section 31(1).
(Payment date / advance date is COMPLETELY IRRELEVANT for goods under forward charge!).

Due Date of Invoice under Section 31(1) for Goods:
Nature of Supply of Goods Statutory Due Date of Tax Invoice [Section 31(1)]
Where supply involves movement of goods (whether by supplier, recipient or any other person) Before or at the time of removal of goods for delivery to the recipient.
Where supply does not involve movement of goods Before or at the time of delivery of goods or making available thereof to the recipient.
💡 Proviso to Section 12(2) (Excess Payment up to ₹1,000):
Where a supplier receives an amount up to ₹1,000 in excess of the invoice amount, the supplier has the option to take the time of supply in respect of such excess as the date of invoice issued for such excess amount (instead of adjusting it immediately).

Section 12(3) Time of Supply of Goods — Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM)

Where tax is payable under reverse charge on goods (e.g. Cashew nuts, bidi wrapper leaves, tobacco leaves, raw cotton, supply of lottery, metal scrap under Section 9(3)), the Time of Supply is the EARLIEST of the following 3 dates:

CRITERIA 1

Date of Receipt of Goods

The physical date on which the recipient actually receives the goods in his premises.

CRITERIA 2

Date of Payment

Earlier of: Date of entry in recipient's books of account OR Date debited in bank account.

CRITERIA 3

31st Day from Invoice

Date immediately following 30 days from the date of issue of invoice by the supplier.

⚠️ Residual Rule: If it is impossible to determine Time of Supply under (1), (2), or (3), the Time of Supply shall be the date of entry of goods in the books of account of the recipient!

Special Rules Continuous Supply, Vouchers & Goods Sent on Approval

Special Category Statutory Time of Supply / Invoice Due Date
Continuous Supply of Goods [Section 31(4)]
Successive statements or successive payments involved		 Invoice must be issued before or at the time each statement is issued or each payment is received.
Time of Supply: Earliest of statement date / payment date.
Goods Sent on Approval for Sale or Return [Section 31(7)] Invoice must be issued earlier of:
1. Before or at the time of supply / acceptance by recipient; OR
2. 6 months from the date of removal of goods!
Exam Rule: If 6 months pass without acceptance, Time of Supply triggers on the last day of the 6-month period!
Supply of Vouchers for Goods [Section 12(4)] • If the supply is identifiable at the point of voucher issuance → Date of issue of voucher.
• In all other cases (multi-purpose vouchers) → Date of redemption of voucher.
Interest, Late Fee or Penalty for Delayed Payment [Section 12(6)] Date on which the supplier actually receives such interest, late fee or penalty amount.

Section 13(2) Time of Supply of Services — Forward Charge (The 30/45 Days Rule)

Unlike goods, Notification No. 66/2017 does NOT apply to services. Tax on services must be paid on advances! Time of Supply depends on whether the tax invoice is issued within the statutory period under Section 31(2) read with Rule 47:

CASE A

Invoice Issued Within 30 / 45 Days

If invoice is issued within 30 days from the date of completion of service (or 45 days for Banking/Insurance/NBFCs):

Time of Supply is EARLIER of:
1. Date of issue of invoice; OR
2. Date of receipt of payment (Book entry or Bank credit, whichever earlier).

CASE B

Invoice NOT Issued Within 30 / 45 Days

If supplier fails to issue invoice within 30 days (or 45 days for banks):

Time of Supply is EARLIER of:
1. Date of completion of provision of service; OR
2. Date of receipt of payment (Book entry or Bank credit, whichever earlier).

⚠️ Residual Provision: Where neither Case A nor Case B applies, Time of Supply is the date on which the recipient shows the receipt of services in his books of account.

Section 13(3) Time of Supply of Services — Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM)

For services taxable under RCM (Notification 13/2017: Director sitting fees, GTA, Advocates, Sponsorship, etc.), Time of Supply is the EARLIEST of:

CRITERIA 1

Date of Payment

Earlier of: Date of entry of payment in recipient's books OR Date of debit in his bank account.

CRITERIA 2

61st Day from Supplier's Invoice

Date immediately following 60 days from the date of issue of invoice by the supplier.
(Notice contrast: 31st day for Goods vs 61st day for Services!).

🏢 Proviso: Associated Enterprises Outside India:
Where supply is received from an Associated Enterprise located outside India (Import of Services between group entities), Time of Supply is EARLIER OF:
1. Date of entry in the books of account of the recipient; OR
2. Date of payment.
(The 60-day window does NOT apply to associated cross-border enterprises!).

Special Rules Continuous Supply of Services & Cessation of Supply

Category Statutory Due Date of Invoice / Time of Supply
Continuous Supply of Services [Section 31(5)]
Service provided under contract > 3 months with periodic payments		 (a) Due date of payment ascertainable from contract: On or before the due date of payment.
(b) Due date NOT ascertainable: On or before the date of receipt of payment.
(c) Payment linked to milestone / event: On or before the date of completion of that milestone/event.

• General / multi-purpose → Date of redemption of voucher.
Interest / Late Fee on Services [Section 13(6)] Date of actual receipt of such interest, late fee or penalty by the supplier.

Section 14 Change in Rate of Tax — The "2 Out of 3" Golden Rule

When the rate of tax changes during the course of a transaction, Section 14 overrides Sections 12 and 13. To determine the applicable tax rate and Time of Supply, evaluate the 3 key milestones:
1. Supply of Goods/Services (S)
2. Issue of Invoice (I)
3. Receipt of Payment (P)

👑 The "2 Out of 3" Master Rule:
Whichever side (BEFORE or AFTER the date of rate change) has at least 2 out of the 3 milestones dictates the APPLICABLE RATE OF TAX!
Between the two remaining events on the winning side, the EARLIER ONE is the Time of Supply!
Supply (S) Invoice (I) Payment (P) Winning Side (2 of 3) Applicable Rate Statutory Time of Supply [Section 14]
Before After After After (I & P) NEW RATE Earlier of Invoice or Payment date
Before Before After Before (S & I) OLD RATE Date of issue of Invoice
Before After Before Before (S & P) OLD RATE Date of receipt of Payment
After Before Before Before (I & P) OLD RATE Earlier of Invoice or Payment date
After Before After After (S & P) NEW RATE Date of receipt of Payment
After After Before After (S & I) NEW RATE Date of issue of Invoice
⚠️ Proviso to Section 14 (Anti-Dated Cheque Rule):
Normally, date of receipt of payment is earlier of book entry or bank credit.
Exception: Where payment is entered in books BEFORE the rate change, but the bank credit occurs more than 4 working days AFTER the rate change, the date of receipt of payment is DEEMED to be the date of credit in the bank account! This prevents backdating payment entries to evade higher tax rates.

TYK Cases ICAI Test Your Knowledge — Solved Practical Problems (1 to 10)

🎯 TYK Case 1 • Forward Charge on Goods with Advance Receipt

Facts: Modern Furnishers received advance of ₹2,00,000 on 10th October for supply of custom furniture. Goods were manufactured and removed from factory on 28th October along with tax invoice dated 28th October. Balance payment of ₹3,00,000 received on 15th November. Determine Time of Supply for (i) ₹2,00,000 advance and (ii) ₹3,00,000 balance.

Statutory Analysis & Solution:

  • Under Section 12(2) read with Notification No. 66/2017-CT, a registered person is exempt from paying GST on advances received for supply of goods. Tax is payable only at the time of invoice.
  • Due date of invoice under Section 31(1)(a) is on or before removal of goods (i.e. 28th October). Actual invoice was also issued on 28th October.
  • Conclusion:
    • For Advance ₹2,00,000 → Time of Supply is 28th October (Invoice date).
    • For Balance ₹3,00,000 → Time of Supply is 28th October (Invoice date).
    Entire tax on ₹5,00,000 is payable in the October GSTR-3B return (due 20th November).
🎯 TYK Case 2 • Reverse Charge on Goods (30-Day Rule)

Facts: Agro Mills purchased raw tobacco leaves from an agriculturist (taxable under RCM u/s 9(3)). Agriculturist issued invoice on 1st September. Agro Mills received goods in godown on 5th September. Payment made in books on 20th September and debited in bank on 22nd September. Determine Time of Supply.

Statutory Analysis: Under Section 12(3), Time of Supply under RCM for goods is earlier of:

  1. Date of receipt of goods = 5th September
  2. Date of payment = Earlier of 20th Sept (books) or 22nd Sept (bank) = 20th September
  3. 31st day from supplier's invoice (1st Sept + 30 days) = 2nd October

Conclusion: Earliest of above is 5th September. Agro Mills must pay tax under RCM in September return.

🎯 TYK Case 3 • Goods Sent on Approval for Sale or Return [Sec 31(7)]

Facts: Radiant Jewellers sent diamond jewellery worth ₹15,00,000 on "approval for sale or return" to a client on 1st December. Client approved and purchased jewellery worth ₹10,00,000 on 25th May (following year). Remaining jewellery worth ₹5,00,000 was neither approved nor returned till 15th June. Determine Time of Supply for both portions.

Statutory Analysis & Solution: Under Section 31(7), where goods are sent on approval, invoice must be issued earlier of: (a) at the time of supply/acceptance, OR (b) 6 months from the date of removal (i.e. 1st June, since removal was 1st December).

  • For ₹10,00,000 accepted on 25th May: Accepted before 6 months. Time of Supply is 25th May.
  • For ₹5,00,000 not accepted within 6 months: 6 months expired on 1st June. Time of Supply is 1st June (deemed supply), even though no approval was received!
🎯 TYK Case 4 • Forward Charge on Services — Invoice Delayed Beyond 30 Days

Facts: An event management company completed organizing an event on 12th July. Invoice was issued on 20th August. Advance of ₹1,00,000 received on 2nd July. Balance ₹4,00,000 received on 28th August. Determine Time of Supply.

Statutory Analysis & Solution:

  • Completion date: 12th July. 30 days under Rule 47 expired on 11th August. Since invoice was issued on 20th August, invoice is DELAYED beyond 30 days!
  • Consequently, Section 13(2)(b) applies: Time of Supply is earlier of Date of provision of service (12th July) OR Date of payment.
  • Conclusion:
    • For Advance ₹1,00,000: Paid on 2nd July (before completion) → Time of Supply is 2nd July.
    • For Balance ₹4,00,000: Completion (12th July) is earlier than payment (28th August) → Time of Supply is 12th July!
    Exam Alert: The supplier cannot postpone tax liability to August by delaying the invoice!
🎯 TYK Case 5 • Reverse Charge on Services — 61st Day Rule vs Payment

Facts: Supreme Power Ltd. engaged Senior Advocate Sharma who issued fee invoice on 7th July. Supreme Power Ltd. made payment to the advocate on 20th September (entered in books & bank debited on same day). Determine Time of Supply under RCM.

Statutory Analysis: Under Section 13(3), Time of Supply under RCM for services is earlier of:

  1. Date of payment = 20th September
  2. 61st day from date of invoice: 7th July + 60 days = 24 days in July + 31 days in Aug + 5 days in Sept = 60 days end on 5th September. Hence 61st day is 6th September!

Conclusion: Earliest is 6th September. Liability arises in September return, not when payment is delayed.

🎯 TYK Case 6 • RCM on Import of Services from Associated Enterprise

Facts: Alpha Inc. (USA) provides technical consultancy to its subsidiary Beta Ltd. (India). Alpha Inc. issued invoice on 10th January. Beta Ltd. recorded the transaction in its books of account on 15th January. Payment was remitted to Alpha Inc. on 10th May. Determine Time of Supply.

Statutory Solution: Under the second proviso to Section 13(3), in case of supply by an Associated Enterprise located outside India, Time of Supply is earlier of: (a) Date of entry in recipient's books (15th January), OR (b) Date of payment (10th May).
Conclusion: Time of Supply is 15th January! The general 60-day rule does NOT apply to cross-border associated entities.

🎯 TYK Case 7 • Change in Rate of Tax [Section 14] — S, I, P Analysis

Facts: GST rate on a service was increased from 12% to 18% w.e.f. 1st October. Service completed on 20th September. Invoice issued on 5th October. Payment received on 8th October. Determine applicable tax rate and Time of Supply.

Application of 2-out-of-3 Rule:

  • Supply (S) = 20th Sept [BEFORE]
  • Invoice (I) = 5th Oct [AFTER]
  • Payment (P) = 8th Oct [AFTER]
  • Winning side: AFTER (2 out of 3: I & P) → Applicable Rate is NEW RATE (18%).
  • Time of Supply: Earlier of Invoice (5th Oct) or Payment (8th Oct) = 5th October.
🎯 TYK Case 8 • Section 14 Anti-Dated Cheque Proviso

Facts: Rate on certain machinery reduced from 18% to 12% w.e.f. 15th November. Goods supplied on 18th November. Invoice issued on 18th November. Buyer delivered cheque on 10th November (entered in supplier's books on 10th November), but cheque was credited in supplier's bank account on 22nd November. Determine applicable rate.

Statutory Analysis & Solution:

  • Under the proviso to Section 14, bank credit occurred on 22nd November—which is more than 4 working days after the rate change on 15th November.
  • Therefore, the date of receipt of payment is deemed to be 22nd November [AFTER], ignoring the book entry date of 10th November!
  • Milestones: Supply (S) = AFTER (18th Nov); Invoice (I) = AFTER (18th Nov); Payment (P) = AFTER (22nd Nov).
  • All 3 events are AFTER → NEW REDUCED RATE (12%) applies! Time of Supply is 18th November (Invoice date).
🎯 TYK Case 9 • Continuous Supply of Services Linked to Milestones

Facts: Architect agreement dated 1st April provides payment milestones: (1) 20% on completion of foundation plan; (2) 30% on completion of structure; (3) 50% on project handover. Foundation plan completed on 15th May, but invoice issued on 25th June. Payment received on 30th June. Determine Time of Supply.

Statutory Solution: Under Section 31(5)(c), where payment is linked to completion of an event, invoice must be issued on or before the date of completion of that event (15th May). Since invoice was delayed to 25th June, Section 13(2)(b) governs: Time of Supply is earlier of event completion (15th May) or payment (30th June).
Conclusion: Time of Supply is 15th May!

🎯 TYK Case 10 • Recovery of Delayed Payment Interest [Sec 12(6) / 13(6)]

Facts: Supplier sold goods on 1st January for ₹1,00,000 + 18% GST with 30 days credit. Invoice issued on 1st January. Buyer delayed payment and paid principal of ₹1,18,000 on 15th April along with ₹5,000 late payment interest. When does tax liability arise on ₹5,00, and at what rate?

Statutory Solution:

  • Under Section 12(6), Time of Supply for interest/late fee is the date on which supplier receives such addition in value (15th April).
  • Under Section 15(2)(d), interest forms part of the value of the main supply and carries the same rate (18%).
  • Back-calculation: ₹5,000 is inclusive of GST → Value = ₹5,000 × 100 / 118 = ₹4,237; GST = ₹763 payable in April return.
