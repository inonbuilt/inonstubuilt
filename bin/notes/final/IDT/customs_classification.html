Classification of Imported and Export Goods

Exhaustive analysis of the 8-digit HSN structure under the Customs Tariff Act, 1975, the 6 General Interpretative Rules (GIR Rules 1 to 6) applied in seriatim, Section and Chapter Notes jurisprudence, Project Imports under Heading 9801, and solved ICAI case scenarios.

1. Tariff Architecture & Harmonized System of Nomenclature (HSN)

Correct classification of goods is the fundamental prerequisite for determining the rate of customs duty, eligibility for exemptions, and applicability of trade defense measures.

The First Schedule to the Customs Tariff Act, 1975 is aligned with the international Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HSN) developed by the World Customs Organization (WCO). While the WCO operates at a 6-digit level, India adopts an 8-digit classification system:

Hierarchical Level Digit Count Statutory Nomenclature Illustrative Example (Chapter 08: Edible Fruit & Nuts)
Chapter First 2 Digits Broad commodity group 08 (Edible fruit and nuts; peel of citrus fruit or melons)
Heading First 4 Digits Specific commodity category 0801 (Coconuts, Brazil nuts and cashew nuts, fresh or dried)
Sub-Heading First 6 Digits International WCO sub-classification 0801 31 (Cashew nuts: in shell)
Tariff Item All 8 Digits National Indian detailed tariff line 0801 32 10 (Cashew kernel, broken)

Structure of the First Schedule (Import Tariff)

  • Comprises 21 Sections and 98 Chapters (Chapters 1 to 97 reflect WCO HSN; Chapter 98 is India's national chapter for Project Imports, Baggage, and Postal goods).
  • Contains 6 General Rules for the Interpretation (GIR) and 3 General Explanatory Notes which form an integral, statutory part of the Act.

2. General Explanatory Notes & Additional Notes

A. Dash Nomenclature in Commodity Descriptions

Single Dash ("-")

Denotes that the article or group of articles represents a primary sub-classification under the main 4-digit Heading.

Double Dash ("--")

Denotes a sub-classification of the immediately preceding single-dash ("-") article or group of articles.

Triple / Quad Dash ("---")

Denotes a detailed sub-sub classification under the preceding double-dash ("--") category, leading directly to the 8-digit Tariff Item.

B. Meaning of "%" & Standard Rate Default

  • The abbreviation "%": Specifies that customs duty is ad valorem, calculated at the specified percentage on the Assessable Value determined under Section 14 of the Customs Act.
  • Absence of Preferential Rate: Where Column (5) does not specify any preferential rate of duty, the Standard Rate specified in Column (4) automatically applies.
  • Standard Unit of Quantity: Prescribed in Column (3) (e.g., kg, mt, u, cc) to ensure uniformity in international trade statistical compilation and analysis.

C. Overriding Legal Status of Section Notes & Chapter Notes

Landmark Ruling: Saurashtra Chemicals v. CC (1986) 23 ELT 283 (Tri-LB) [Approved by SC]

The Supreme Court affirmed that Section Notes and Chapter Notes are an integral part of the statute. They possess statutory force to restrict or expand the scope of headings and have an overriding effect over the general wording of headings.

3. GIR Rule 1: The General Rule of Classification

Statutory Text of Rule 1

"The titles of Sections, Chapters and Sub-Chapters are provided for ease of reference only; for legal purposes, classification shall be determined according to the terms of the headings and any relative Section or Chapter Notes and, provided such headings or Notes do not otherwise require, according to the subsequent rules."

Core Legal Principles Emerging from Rule 1:

  1. Titles have zero legal force: Section, Chapter, and Sub-Chapter titles serve purely as directional indexes and cannot be cited to restrict or expand legal liability.
  2. Primacy of Headings & Statutory Notes: Classification is governed primarily by the exact wording of the 4-digit Headings read with relative Section and Chapter Notes.
  3. Self-Sustaining Rule: Where classification can be definitively determined under Rule 1, recourse to Rules 2 to 6 is prohibited.
  4. Non-Transferability of Notes: Notes of one chapter or section cannot be imported to interpret entries in other chapters.

Practical Example: Letter Closing & Sealing Machine

Conflict: Heading 8422 covers container filling/sealing machinery; Heading 8472 covers office machines including mail closing/sealing machines.
Resolution under Rule 1: Chapter Note 2 to Chapter 84 expressly provides that Heading 8422 does not cover office machinery of Heading 8472. Thus, by statutory note command, the machine is classified under Heading 8472 under Rule 1 itself!

4. GIR Rule 2: Incomplete Articles, CKD/SKD & Mixtures

A. Rule 2(a): Incomplete or Unfinished Articles & Unassembled Goods

Rule 2(a) expands the scope of a finished product heading in two directions:

1. Incomplete / Unfinished Articles

Classified under the finished goods heading provided that, as presented, the article has the essential character of the complete or finished article (e.g. railway coaches or passenger cars presented without seats).

2. Unassembled / Disassembled (CKD/SKD)

Articles presented completely knocked down (CKD) or semi-knocked down (SKD) for assembly convenience during transport are classified as finished articles.

Essential Character Threshold: Minor vs Major Processing

Goods requiring only minor assembly or adjustments (e.g. bolting, riveting, welding pre-fabricated parts) are treated as having the essential character of the finished article. However, blanks or castings requiring major machining processes (such as turning, grinding, heat treatment, deep grooving) CANNOT be classified as finished goods under Rule 2(a).

B. Rule 2(b): Mixtures and Combinations of Substances

Any reference in a heading to a material or substance includes mixtures or combinations of that material with other substances (e.g. coffee mixed with chicory; natural rubber mixed with synthetic rubber). If a mixture consists of multiple substances classifiable under different headings, Rule 2(b) refers classification directly to Rule 3.

6. GIR Rule 4: The Akin Rule (Rule of Last Resort)

Statutory Text of Rule 4

"Goods which cannot be classified in accordance with the above rules shall be classified under the heading appropriate to the goods to which they are most akin."

Application of the Akin Rule: Rule 4 is a residual rule invoked only when a novel or unlisted product defies classification under Rules 1, 2, and 3. The product is assimilated with goods that share the closest similarity in composition, physical properties, intended use, and functional character.

Practical Example: Sun Glare Anti-Radiation Window Film

Plastic films pasted on car glass or building windows to filter solar glare do not have an express tariff item. Even though solar film is not builder's ware, its anti-glare function is most akin to plastic Venetian blinds. Hence, classified under Heading 3925 under Rule 4!

7. GIR Rule 5: Packaging Cases, Containers & Packing Materials

A. Rule 5(a): Cases and Containers Specially Shaped for Specific Articles

Camera cases, musical instrument cases, gun cases, necklace cases, and drawing instrument cases are classified with the article they contain, provided:

  1. They are specially shaped or fitted to contain the specific article or set;
  2. They are suitable for long-term use (durability equivalent to the product);
  3. They are presented along with the article for which they are intended; and
  4. They are of a kind normally sold with such goods.

Exception: Rule 5(a) does NOT apply to containers that give the whole consignment its essential character (e.g. an ornate solid silver tea caddy containing ordinary tea).

B. Rule 5(b): Packing Materials and Normal Containers

Packing materials and packing containers presented with goods therein are classified with the goods, if they are of a kind normally used for packing such goods (e.g., cardboard boxes for shoes, bottles for perfume).

Critical Statutory Exception: Containers Suitable for Repetitive Use

Rule 5(b) does NOT apply when packing containers are clearly suitable for repetitive use (durable containers like compressed gas cylinders, cryogenic tanks, returnable ISO metal containers). Such durable containers must be classified separately under their respective headings.

8. GIR Rule 6: Sub-Heading Comparison Principle

Statutory Rule: "Only Sub-Headings at the Same Level are Comparable"

For legal purposes, the classification of goods in the sub-headings of a heading shall be determined according to the terms of those sub-headings, related sub-heading notes, and Rules 1 to 5 mutatis mutandis, on the strict understanding that:

  • A one-dash ("-") sub-heading can ONLY be compared with another one-dash ("-") sub-heading within the same 4-digit heading.
  • A two-dash ("--") sub-heading can ONLY be compared with another two-dash ("--") sub-heading under the same one-dash branch.
  • Cross-level comparison (e.g. comparing a two-dash sub-heading with a one-dash sub-heading) is statutorily impermissible.

9. Classification of Machinery & Equipment Parts

The classification of parts across Section XVI (Machinery & Electrical equipment) and Section XVII (Vehicles & Transport equipment) follows strict statutory Section Notes:

  • Parts of General Use: Bolts, nuts, screws, washers, springs, and tubes (classified under Chapter 73 or base metals) are NEVER classified as machinery parts; they must be classified under their specific headings as parts of general use.
  • Parts with Specific Headings: If an independent tariff heading exists for a specific part (e.g., internal combustion engine parts under Heading 8409), it must be classified under that specific heading.
  • Sole / Principal Use Parts: Parts suitable solely or principally for use with a particular machine are classified under the same heading as that machine (or under the residual parts heading for that machine group).

10. Project Imports under Heading 9801

A. Purpose & Economic Rationale of Project Imports

When setting up a new mega-project or undertaking substantial expansion of an existing industrial unit, thousands of diverse machines, instruments, electrical gear, and components are imported. Assessing each item separately under its individual tariff heading would result in massive valuation delays, classification disputes, and prohibitive demurrage.

To resolve this, India created Heading 9801 in Chapter 98, establishing a unified assessment mechanism:

Salient Features of Project Imports (Heading 9801)

  • Single Consolidated Rate: All eligible capital equipment, instruments, apparatus, and appliances imported for the project are charged to duty at one single consolidated project customs duty rate, irrespective of individual tariff lines.
  • Eligible Projects: Industrial plants, irrigation projects, power projects (excluding solar power plants/projects), mining projects, oil/mineral exploration, and other notified infrastructure projects.
  • Permissible Spares, Raw Materials & Consumables: Spare parts, raw materials, and consumable stores up to 10% of the value of specified capital equipment are permitted to be imported at the concessional project rate.
  • Applicability of Individual Exemptions: The Supreme Court has ruled that if any specific machine imported within the project is eligible for a more beneficial individual exemption notification, the benefit of such exemption remains available even when declared under Project Import Heading 9801.

12. Solved ICAI Test Your Knowledge Problems (5 Problems)

ICAI Question 1

Classification of Incomplete/Unfinished Articles [Rule 2(a)]

Briefly explain the provisions of Rule 2(a) of the Rules of Interpretation of the First Schedule to the Customs Tariff Act, 1975 on the classification of incomplete or unfinished articles.
Statutory Analysis & Solution:
  1. Incomplete / Unfinished Articles: Any reference in a heading to an article includes that article in its incomplete or unfinished form, provided that, as presented, the incomplete article has the essential character of the complete or finished article (e.g., railway coach or motor car imported without seats).
  2. Unassembled / Disassembled Articles (CKD/SKD): A heading also includes finished articles presented in an unassembled or disassembled form (for packaging, transport, or logistics handling).
  3. Limitation: Applies only where goods require minor adjustments/assembly. Items requiring major manufacturing or cutting operations cannot be classified under Rule 2(a).
ICAI Question 2

Purpose of General Rules of Interpretation & The Akin Rule

What is the purpose of including General Rules of Interpretation in the Customs Tariff? Do they form part of the Tariff Schedule? Explain the Akin Rule of interpretation.
Statutory Analysis & Solution:
  1. Purpose & Statutory Status: The First Schedule contains 6 General Rules for the Interpretation (GIR) and 3 General Explanatory Notes. They are an integral part of the statutory tariff schedule. Their purpose is to provide uniform, legally enforceable principles for classifying traded goods, thereby minimizing administrative discretion and classification disputes.
  2. The Akin Rule [Rule 4]: Goods which cannot be classified by applying Rules 1, 2, and 3 shall be classified under the heading appropriate to the goods to which they are most akin. It is a residual rule of last resort, comparing physical properties, function, composition, and commercial utility.
ICAI Question 3

Project Imports under Heading 9801

Write a detailed note on "Project Imports" under the Customs Tariff Act, 1975.
Statutory Analysis & Solution:
  1. Concept & Need: Industrial projects require importing hundreds of diverse machinery, instruments, and auxiliary equipment. Individual classification would cause massive administrative delays and dispute. Heading 9801 enables assessing the entire project consignment at a single unified concessional rate.
  2. Coverage: Machinery, instruments, apparatus, appliances, components, and raw materials required for initial setup or substantial expansion of industrial plants, irrigation, mining, power (except solar), and oil/gas exploration.
  3. Auxiliary Allowance: Spare parts, raw materials, and consumable stores up to 10% of the value of specified capital goods can be imported at the project rate.
  4. Individual Exemption Overriding: Importers retain the option to claim individual specific exemption notifications on any item covered under the project import if more beneficial.
ICAI Question 4

Detailed Analysis of GIR Rule 3 (Specific, Essential Character, Latter)

Explain Rule 3 of the Rules for Interpretation of the Customs Tariff.
Statutory Analysis & Solution:

When goods consist of multiple materials or are prima facie classifiable under two or more headings, classification proceeds sequentially under Rule 3:

  1. Rule 3(a) [Specific over General]: The heading providing the most specific description is preferred over headings providing a general description (e.g. electric shavers u/h 8510 over general electro-mechanical appliances u/h 8509).
  2. Rule 3(b) [Essential Character Principle]: Composite goods, mixtures, and retail sets are classified according to the material or component that gives them their essential character (e.g. pencil with eraser stub classified as pencil).
  3. Rule 3(c) [Latter the Better]: Where goods cannot be classified under 3(a) or 3(b), they are classified under the heading that occurs last in numerical order among those equally meriting consideration (e.g. 8704 over 8703 in Mahindra & Mahindra).
ICAI Question 5

Meaning of Abbreviation "%" in Customs Tariff

Briefly explain the meaning of the abbreviation "%" in relation to the rate of duty in the Customs Tariff.
Statutory Analysis & Solution:

As per General Explanatory Note (b) to the First Schedule, the abbreviation "%" in any column in relation to the rate of duty means that the duty is an ad valorem duty, calculated at the specified percentage on the Assessable Value of the goods determined in accordance with the provisions of Section 14 of the Customs Act, 1962.
