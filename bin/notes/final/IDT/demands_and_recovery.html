1. Statutory Architecture & Landmark Legislative Shift

While GST operates primarily on voluntary self-assessment, tax administrations require robust statutory machinery to determine and recover taxes short-paid, unpaid, or erroneously refunded, and input tax credit wrongly availed or utilized. Under Chapter XV of the CGST Act [Sections 73 to 84], a historic legislative restructuring was enacted by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024:

Historic Paradigm (Up to FY 2023-24)

Bifurcated Regime: Sections 73 & 74

Separate statutory timelines and proceedings existed for Non-Fraud Cases (Section 73) (SCN within 2 years 9 months; order within 3 years) and Fraud Cases (Section 74) (SCN within 4 years 6 months; order within 5 years).

New Paradigm (FY 2024-25 Onward)

Unified Regime: New Section 74A

A single, standardized procedural window applies to BOTH Fraud and Non-Fraud cases. SCN must be issued within 42 months (3.5 years), and final adjudication order must be passed within 12 months (1 year) from SCN date!

2. The New Unified Adjudication Regime [Section 74A]

Section 74A governs determination of tax not paid, short paid, erroneously refunded, or ITC wrongly availed or utilized for Financial Year 2024-25 and subsequent financial years:

Core Timelines under Section 74A (Applicable to Both Fraud & Non-Fraud):
$$\text{Show Cause Notice (SCN) Due Date} = \mathbf{42 \text{ Months (3.5 Years)}} \text{ from Annual Return Due Date or Date of Erroneous Refund [Sec 74A(2)]}$$ $$\text{Final Adjudication Order Due Date} = \mathbf{12 \text{ Months (1 Year)}} \text{ from the Date of Issuance of SCN [Sec 74A(7)]}$$

• Extension of Order: The Commissioner (or an officer authorized by him not below Joint Commissioner) can extend the 12-month order timeline by a further period not exceeding 6 months on sufficient cause shown in writing.
• Deemed Dropping: If the order is not passed within 12 months (or extended 18 months), proceedings shall be deemed to be concluded under Section 75(10)!

5. General Adjudication Safeguards [Section 75]

Section 75 establishes non-negotiable statutory rules and natural justice guarantees governing all adjudication proceedings:

Section 75(4) & 75(5)

Personal Hearing & 3 Adjournments

An opportunity of personal hearing MUST BE GRANTED where a written request is received or where an adverse decision is contemplated against the taxpayer. The Proper Officer can grant adjournment on sufficient cause, but NOT MORE THAN 3 ADJOURNMENTS shall be permitted to a person!

Section 75(7)

SCN Sets Absolute Boundary

The amount of tax, interest, and penalty confirmed in the final adjudication order CANNOT EXCEED the amount specified in the Show Cause Notice. Furthermore, the order cannot confirm demand on any grounds other than those specifically stated in the SCN.

Direct Recovery of Unpaid Self-Assessed Tax [Section 75(12)]: Where any tax is self-assessed by a taxable person in Form GSTR-3B, or the tax liability declared in Form GSTR-1 exceeds the tax paid in GSTR-3B, such unpaid self-assessed tax along with Section 50 interest SHALL BE RECOVERED DIRECTLY UNDER SECTION 79 without the necessity of issuing any Show Cause Notice under Section 73/74/74A!

Other Crucial Section 75 Rules:

  • Section 75(1): Period during which proceedings are stayed by an order of a Court or Tribunal shall be excluded in computing the period of limitation.
  • Section 75(2): Where an appellate forum holds that charges of fraud/suppression are not sustainable, the Proper Officer shall determine tax as if notice was issued for bonafide error.
  • Section 75(6): The order must be a speaking order setting forth the facts and the basis of the decision.
  • Section 75(13): Where any penalty is imposed under Section 73, 74, or 74A, no separate penalty for the same act/omission shall be imposed under any other provision of the Act.

6. Tax Collected but not Paid to Government [Section 76]

Section 76 prevents unjust enrichment at the cost of the sovereign treasury. It contains strict strictures against retaining tax collections:

Mandatory Immediate Remittance [Section 76(1)]: Every person who has collected from any other person any amount representing tax under GST SHALL FORTHWITH DEPOSIT the said amount with the Government, irrespective of whether the supplies in respect of which such amount was collected are taxable or exempt!
No Limitation Period

SCN Without Time Bar [Sec 76(2)]

The Proper Officer can issue an SCN for recovery of tax collected but not deposited AT ANY TIME WITHOUT ANY LIMITATION OF 3 OR 5 YEARS! Interest under Section 50 applies from the date of collection till deposit.

Order & Penalty

Order in 1 Year & 100% Penalty

Adjudication order must be passed within 1 YEAR from the date of SCN [Sec 76(6)]. Penalty equal to 100% of the amount collected is mandatorily leviable upon adjudication [Sec 76(11)].

7. Tax Wrongfully Collected & Paid [Section 77 CGST & Sec 19 IGST]

Where a registered person has paid CGST and SGST/UTGST on a transaction considering it to be an intra-State supply, but which is subsequently held to be an inter-State supply:

  • The taxpayer shall pay the correct Integrated Tax (IGST). NO INTEREST under Section 50 shall be payable on the payment of IGST [Section 19(2) IGST].
  • The taxpayer is entitled to a full refund of CGST and SGST wrongly paid under Section 77(1).
  • Identical reciprocal rule applies if IGST was wrongly paid instead of CGST/SGST.

8. Statutory Modes of Recovery [Sections 78 & 79]

Under Section 78, any amount payable pursuant to an adjudication order must be paid within 3 MONTHS from the date of service of the order, failing which recovery proceedings shall be initiated. (The Proper Officer may shorten the 3-month window if expedient in the interest of revenue).

The 7 Statutory Modes of Recovery under Section 79:

Clause Recovery Mechanism Operational Modality
79(1)(a) Deduction from Government Dues Deducting the amount from any money owing to the defaulter by the GST Department or Customs.
79(1)(b) Detention & Sale of Goods Detaining and selling any goods belonging to the defaulter which are under the control of the Proper Officer.
79(1)(c) Garnishee Proceedings (Third-Party Notice) Serving notice in Form GST DRC-13 to any third party (bankers, debtors, employers holding salary, post offices) who holds money on behalf of the defaulter to pay directly to the Government.
79(1)(d) Distraint & Sale of Movable / Immovable Property Distraining any movable or immovable property belonging to the defaulter and auctioning it after 30 days in Form GST DRC-16/17.
79(1)(e) Arrears of Land Revenue via District Collector Issuing a certificate in Form GST DRC-18 to the District Collector to recover the amount as if it were arrears of land revenue.
79(1)(f) Recovery through Magistrate as Fine Applying to the appropriate Magistrate in Form GST DRC-19 to recover the amount as if it were a fine imposed under CrPC/BNSS.
79(2) Recovery from Surety Enforcing execution of bond against the surety as if the surety were the principal defaulter.

9. Payment in Installments [Section 80 & Rule 158]

Commissioner's Discretion

Maximum 24 Monthly Installments

On an application filed in Form GST DRC-20, the Commissioner may, for reasons recorded in writing, extend the time for payment or allow payment of any tax demand in monthly installments not exceeding 24 installments, subject to payment of Section 50 interest.

Strict Disqualifications

Self-Assessed Tax CANNOT be Deferred

Absolute Bar: The facility of paying in installments SHALL NOT BE ALLOWED in respect of any amount self-assessed in any return!
Default Consequence: Default in paying any single installment makes the entire remaining balance due immediately without notice.

10. Provisional Attachment to Protect Revenue [Section 83]

Section 83 empowers the Commissioner to provisionally attach property, including bank accounts, to protect the interest of Government revenue during the pendency of proceedings under Section 62, 63, 64, 67, 73, 74, or 74A.

Parameter Statutory Mandate under Section 83 & Rule 159
Competent Authority Commissioner ONLY (Opinion must be formed on credible material).
Properties Covered Any property belonging to the taxable person, including bank accounts, immovable property, and shares.
Statutory Order Form Order issued in Form GST DRC-22.
Statutory Lifespan Maximum 1 YEAR
Every provisional attachment automatically ceases to have effect after the expiry of 1 year from the date of the order.
Taxpayer's Remedy Taxpayer can file objections in Form GST DRC-22A within 7 days. Commissioner must grant personal hearing and decide within 5 days.
Tax as First Charge [Section 82]: Notwithstanding anything contained in any contract, any amount payable on account of tax, interest, or penalty shall be a FIRST CHARGE on the property of the taxable person, subject only to the provisions of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC).

11. ICAI Test Your Knowledge (TYK) - Complete Solved Problems

ICAI TYK 1 (Tax Collected but Unpaid u/s 76)

Question:

Mohan Enterprises is entitled to exemption from tax under GST law. However, it collected tax from its buyers worth â‚¹50,000 in August and has not deposited it with the Government. Brief Mohan Enterprises on the consequences of collecting tax but not depositing the same under Section 76.

Answer:

As per Section 76 of the CGST Act, 2017:

  1. Mandatory Immediate Remittance [Section 76(1)]: Every person who has collected from any other person any amount representing tax under GST shall forthwith pay the said amount to the Government, irrespective of whether the supplies in respect of which such amount was collected are taxable or exempt. Hence, Mohan Enterprises is statutorily obligated to deposit the â‚¹50,000 immediately.
  2. Issuance of Notice [Section 76(2)]: The proper officer may serve an SCN requiring Mohan Enterprises to show cause why the amount of â‚¹50,000 along with Section 50 interest and penalty should not be recovered. Crucially, there is NO statutory limitation period for issuing notice under Section 76!
  3. Interest Liability [Section 76(4)]: Interest under Section 50 shall be payable from the date the amount was collected till the date of deposit.
  4. Mandatory Penalty [Section 76(11)]: Upon passing of the adjudication order (within 1 year of SCN), a penalty equivalent to the amount specified in the notice (i.e., â‚¹50,000 - 100% penalty) shall be imposed on Mohan Enterprises.
ICAI TYK 2 (Time Limit for SCN under Sec 74A)

Question:

Discuss briefly the time limit for issue of show cause notice as contained under Section 74A in case where (i) the short payment is on account of fraud, and (ii) the short payment is on account of a bonafide error.

Answer:

Under the unified regime of Section 74A of the CGST Act (applicable for Financial Year 2024-25 onward):

  • Section 74A provides a standardized and identical time limit for the issuance of Show Cause Notice (SCN) for BOTH fraud and bonafide (non-fraud) cases.
  • Under Section 74A(2), the proper officer shall issue the SCN within 42 months (3.5 years) from:
    1. The due date for furnishing the annual return for the financial year to which the tax relates; OR
    2. The date of erroneous refund.

Conclusion: In both scenariosâ€”whether short payment is due to fraud or bonafide errorâ€”the time limit for issuing SCN is 42 months from the due date of the annual return or date of erroneous refund.

ICAI TYK 3 (Adjudication Time Limits under CGST Act)

Question:

Is there any time limit prescribed for adjudication of cases under the CGST Act, 2017? If yes, discuss the same.

Answer:

Yes, definite statutory limitation periods are prescribed for adjudication orders under the CGST Act:

  • Under Section 74A (For FY 2024-25 onward): The proper officer shall issue the adjudication order within 12 months (1 year) from the date of issuance of the SCN [Sec 74A(7)]. This period can be extended by the Commissioner by a further period of up to 6 months.
  • Under Section 73 (Non-Fraud up to FY 2023-24): Order within 3 years from the due date of furnishing the annual return or date of erroneous refund.
  • Under Section 74 (Fraud up to FY 2023-24): Order within 5 years from the due date of furnishing the annual return or date of erroneous refund.
  • Under Section 76 (Tax collected but unpaid): Order within 1 year from the date of service of notice.
ICAI TYK 4 (Reduced Penalty in Fraud Cases before SCN)

Question:

A person is chargeable with tax in case of fraud. He decides to pay the amount of demand along with interest and penalty before issuance of show cause notice. Is any option of reduced penalty available to such person?

Answer:

Yes. Under Section 74A(9)(i) of the CGST Act, 2017 (and Section 74(5) for earlier periods):

Where a person chargeable with tax in a case involving fraud, wilful-misstatement, or suppression of facts pays the tax along with Section 50 interest and a reduced penalty equivalent to 15% of such tax on the basis of his own ascertainment or as ascertained by the proper officer BEFORE service of the Show Cause Notice, and informs the proper officer in writing of such payment:

  • The proper officer shall not serve any Show Cause Notice in respect of the tax so paid or any penalty.
  • All proceedings in respect of the said tax shall be deemed to be fully concluded.
ICAI TYK 5 (Modes of Recovery)

Question:

Briefly discuss the modes of recovery of tax available to the proper officer under Section 79.

Answer:

Under Section 79(1) of the CGST Act, the proper officer can recover unpaid tax through seven distinct modes:

  1. Deduction from Government Money: Deducting amount from any money payable to the defaulter by the department;
  2. Detention & Sale of Goods: Detaining and selling goods belonging to the defaulter under department custody;
  3. Garnishee Proceedings [Sec 79(1)(c)]: Serving notice to third parties (bankers, debtors, employers) holding money for the defaulter to pay directly to the Government;
  4. Distraint & Sale of Property: Distraining and auctioning movable/immovable property;
  5. Arrears of Land Revenue: Certifying to the District Collector to recover as land revenue;
  6. Recovery via Magistrate: Applying to a Magistrate to recover as a criminal court fine;
  7. Recovery from Surety: Enforcing bond against the guarantor/surety.
ICAI TYK 6 (Circumstances & Timelines under Sec 74A)

Question:

Enlist the circumstances under which a show cause notice can be issued by the proper officer under Section 74A. Specify the time limit for issuance of such SCN as also the time period for issuance of order under Section 74A.

Answer:

1. Circumstances for Issuance of SCN [Section 74A(1)]:

An SCN can be issued under Section 74A where it appears to the proper officer that:

  • Tax has not been paid; or
  • Tax has been short paid; or
  • Tax has been erroneously refunded; or
  • Input tax credit has been wrongly availed or utilised;

whether by reason of fraud, or any wilful-misstatement or suppression of facts, or otherwise (bonafide error).

2. Timelines:

  • Issuance of SCN [Section 74A(2)]: Within 42 months (3.5 years) from the due date of furnishing the annual return for the financial year or date of erroneous refund.
  • Issuance of Order [Section 74A(7)]: Within 12 months (1 year) from the date of issuance of the SCN (extendable by up to 6 months by Commissioner).
ICAI TYK 7 (Mistaken Tax Collection on Exempt Goods)

Question:

Subharti Enterprises collected GST on goods supplied by it on the belief that the supply was taxable. Later, it discovered that the goods were exempt. The accountant advised that mistakenly collected tax was not required to be deposited. Seek your advice on the same.

Answer:

The advice given by the accountant is completely INCORRECT.

Under Section 76(1) of the CGST Act, 2017, every person who has collected from any other person any amount representing tax under GST shall forthwith pay the said amount to the Government, irrespective of whether the supplies in respect of which such amount was collected are taxable or not.

Once an amount has been collected as representing GST, the supplier has no legal right to retain it in its commercial coffers. If Subharti Enterprises fails to deposit the amount, the proper officer will issue an SCN under Section 76(2) without any period of limitation, recover the amount with interest under Section 50, and levy a mandatory 100% penalty under Section 76(11).

Remedy: Subharti Enterprises must deposit the amount with the Government, or refund it to the buyers through a credit note/refund voucher if returns permit, and claim refund under Section 54.

ICAI TYK 8 (Self-Assessed Tax Penalty Challenge)

Problem Statement:

Anant & Co. self-assessed its CGST liability as â‚¹90,000 for April, but failed to pay. Department initiated penal proceedings under Section 74A and issued SCN on 10th August. Anant & Co. deposited tax + interest on 25th August (within 30 days of SCN). Department contends penalty of â‚¹45,000 (50% of â‚¹90,000). Examine the correctness of Department's stand.

Detailed Answer & Legal Position:

The stand taken by the Department is INCORRECT.

  1. Unpaid Self-Assessed Tax Rule: Under the Explanation to Section 74A(8) and Section 75(12), in cases of self-assessed tax or tax collected and not paid, if such tax remains unpaid beyond a period of 30 days from the due date of payment, a penalty equal to 10% of the tax or â‚¹10,000, whichever is higher, is payable.
  2. Inapplicability of 50% Penalty: A 50% penalty applies under Section 74A(9)(iii) only in cases of fraud/suppression where payment is made within 60 days of communication of an adjudication order. It does not apply to simple non-payment of self-assessed tax before order!

Correct Penalty Calculation:

  • 10% of â‚¹90,000 = â‚¹9,000.
  • Statutory minimum = â‚¹10,000.
  • Correct Penalty Payable = â‚¹10,000 (not â‚¹45,000!).
