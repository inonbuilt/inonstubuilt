1. Statutory Architecture & Constitutional Foundations

Tax adjudication must balance the sovereign's power to collect legitimate revenue with the taxpayer's constitutional right to fair trial and protection against arbitrary exactions. Chapter XVIII of the CGST Act [Sections 107 to 121] provides a comprehensive, multi-tiered appellate framework providing judicial remedies against erroneous administrative orders.

Natural Justice Guarantee

Speaking Order & De Novo Powers

Appellate authorities must follow principles of natural justice, granting mandatory personal hearings (up to 3 adjournments) and issuing speaking orders. Crucially, the Appellate Authority does NOT have the power to remand cases back to the adjudicating authority; it must decide the matter conclusively!

Automatic Stay on Recovery

Pre-Deposit Consequence

Under Section 107(7) and Section 112(9), once the mandatory pre-deposit is paid, the recovery proceedings for the remaining balance tax demand are AUTOMATICALLY STAYED until the disposal of the appeal!

3. First Appeal to Appellate Authority [Section 107]

Any person aggrieved by an order passed by an Adjudicating Authority under the CGST Act or SGST/UTGST Act may appeal to the Appellate Authority in Form GST APL-01:

Appellant Statutory Filing Window Condonable Delay Period Governing Section
Taxpayer (Aggrieved Person) Within 3 MONTHS from the date of communication of order Further period up to 1 MONTH if sufficient cause is shown Section 107(1) & (4)
Department (Review Application) Within 6 MONTHS from the date of communication of order Further period up to 1 MONTH if sufficient cause is shown Section 107(2) & (4)
Procedural Mandates

Maximum 3 Adjournments

Under Section 107(9), the Appellate Authority may grant adjournments on sufficient cause, but NO MORE THAN 3 ADJOURNMENTS shall be allowed to a party during the hearing of the appeal.

No Remand Power

Finality of Order (No Remand)

Under Section 107(11), the Appellate Authority shall pass an order confirming, modifying, or annulling the decision, but CANNOT REMAND the case back to the original officer!

5. Revisional Authority (Suo-Motu Revision) [Section 108]

Section 108 empowers the Revisional Authority (Principal Commissioner or Commissioner) to examine records of any proceeding and revise any order passed by a subordinate officer if he considers that such order is erroneous in so far as it is prejudicial to the interest of revenue, or is illegal or improper.

When Revisional Authority CANNOT Revise an Order [Section 108(2)]: The Revisional Authority is strictly prohibited from revising an order if:
  1. The order has been subject to an appeal under Section 107 (AA), Section 112 (GSTAT), Section 117 (High Court), or Section 118 (Supreme Court);
  2. More than 3 YEARS have expired from the date of passing of the decision/order;
  3. The order was passed less than 6 MONTHS ago (cooling-off period to allow appellate options); or
  4. The order has already been revised under Section 108 earlier.

6. GSTAT Architecture: Principal vs State Benches [Section 109]

The Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT) is the dedicated second appellate forum constituted under Section 109:

Principal Bench (New Delhi)

Exclusive Place of Supply Jurisdiction

Consists of the President, a Judicial Member, a Technical Member (Centre), and a Technical Member (State).
Exclusive Domain: Hears all appeals where one of the issues involved relates to the PLACE OF SUPPLY.
Appeal Route: Appeals against Principal Bench orders lie DIRECTLY TO THE SUPREME COURT [Section 118]!

State Benches

Operational Across States

Constituted in various States/cities consisting of Judicial and Technical members.
Domain: Hears all GST disputes other than Place of Supply (valuation, classification, ITC eligibility, penalties).
Appeal Route: Appeals lie to the HIGH COURT on a substantial question of law [Section 117].

8. Appeals to High Court & Supreme Court [Sections 117 & 118]

Section 117 (High Court)

Substantial Question of Law

  • Appeals lie against orders of the State Benches of the Tribunal.
  • Time Limit: Within 180 DAYS from date of communication of order (condonable on sufficient cause).
  • Prerequisite: The High Court must be satisfied that the case involves a SUBSTANTIAL QUESTION OF LAW. No appeal lies on pure questions of fact!
  • Heard by a bench of not less than two Judges.
Section 118 (Supreme Court)

Apex Judicial Forum

  • Direct Appeal from GSTAT: From any order passed by the Principal Bench of the Tribunal (involving Place of Supply).
  • From High Court: From any judgment or order passed by the High Court in an appeal under Section 117 which the High Court certifies to be a fit one for appeal to the Supreme Court.

9. Orders Against Which NO Appeal Lies [Section 121]

Section 121 enlists four specific administrative orders against which NO APPEAL SHALL LIE to any appellate authority or Tribunal:

Clause Non-Appealable Order under Section 121 Statutory Rationale
(a) An order of the Commissioner or other authority empowered to direct transfer of proceedings from one officer to another officer. Purely an internal administrative reallocation of files; does not adjudicate civil rights.
(b) An order pertaining to the seizure or retention of books of account, register and other documents. Interlocutory investigative measure under Section 67; challenged via writ or trial.
(c) An order sanctioning prosecution under the Act. Executive sanction for initiating criminal complaint; merits evaluated by Criminal Court.
(d) An order passed under Section 80 (allowing or refusing payment of tax in installments). Discretionary administrative grace; taxpayer cannot claim installments as an appealable right.

10. Departmental Monetary Limits for Appeals [Section 120]

To reduce frivolous litigation and backlog in appellate forums, Section 120 empowers the CBIC to issue orders fixing monetary limits below which departmental officers are instructed not to file appeals:

  • No Acquiescence Rule [Sec 120(3)]: Non-filing of an appeal by a CGST officer due to monetary limits does NOT mean the department has accepted (acquiesced in) the legal stand. The officer can contest the exact same question of law in another case involving higher tax amounts!
  • No taxpayer can contend in court that the department has conceded the issue by not appealing in an earlier smaller case.

11. ICAI Test Your Knowledge (TYK) - Complete Solved Problems

ICAI TYK 1 (Right of Appeal under Sec 107)

Question:

Does CGST law provide for any appeal to a person aggrieved by any order or decision passed against him by an adjudicating authority under the CGST Act? Explain the related provisions under the CGST Act.

Answer:

Yes. Under Section 107(1) of the CGST Act, 2017, any person aggrieved by any decision or order passed against him by an Adjudicating Authority has the statutory right to appeal to the Appellate Authority:

  • Time Limit: The appeal must be filed within 3 months from the date on which the order is communicated to such person. The Appellate Authority may condone delay of up to 1 month if satisfied there was sufficient cause.
  • Mandatory Pre-Deposit [Section 107(6)]: The appeal can be admitted only when the appellant pays:
    1. 100% of the admitted tax, interest, fine, fee, and penalty; PLUS
    2. 10% of the disputed tax amount, subject to a maximum cap of â‚¹20 Crore CGST (â‚¹40 Crore in case of IGST).
  • Transit Orders: For orders under Section 129(3), pre-deposit is 25% of the penalty.
  • Non-Appealable Orders [Section 121]: No appeal lies against transfer of proceedings, seizure of books, sanction of prosecution, or Section 80 installment orders.
ICAI TYK 2 (Departmental Appeal under Sec 107(2))

Question:

Describe the provisions relating to Departmental appeal to the Appellate Authority under Section 107.

Answer:

Under Section 107(2) of the CGST Act, 2017:

  • The Commissioner may, on his own motion or upon request from the State/UT Commissioner, review any order passed by an adjudicating authority subordinate to him to examine its legality or propriety.
  • The Commissioner may direct any officer subordinate to him to apply to the Appellate Authority within 6 months from the date of communication of the impugned order in Form GST APL-03.
  • The Appellate Authority can condone delay of up to 1 month on sufficient cause [Section 107(4)].
  • Such application by the department shall be treated as an appeal and all provisions relating to appeals shall apply.
ICAI TYK 3 (Pre-Deposit Amount & Stay of Recovery)

Question:

With reference to Sections 107(6) and 112(8), specify the amount of mandatory pre-deposit which should be made along with every appeal made before the Appellate Authority and the Appellate Tribunal. Does making the pre-deposit have any impact on recovery proceedings?

Answer:

1. Mandatory Pre-Deposit Amounts:

  • Before Appellate Authority [Section 107(6)]: Full admitted tax, interest, fine, fee and penalty; PLUS 10% of the remaining tax in dispute, subject to a maximum of â‚¹20 Crore CGST (â‚¹40 Crore IGST).
  • Before Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT) [Section 112(8)]: Full admitted dues; PLUS 10% of the remaining tax in dispute (in addition to the 10% paid before AA), subject to a maximum of â‚¹20 Crore CGST (â‚¹40 Crore IGST).

2. Impact on Recovery Proceedings [Sections 107(7) & 112(9)]:

Where the appellant has paid the mandatory pre-deposit amounts as specified above, the recovery proceedings for the balance unpaid demand shall automatically be DEEMED TO BE STAYED till the disposal of the appeal. The department cannot initiate recovery actions under Section 79 during this period.

ICAI TYK 4 (Suo-Motu Revisional Powers under Sec 108)

Question:

With reference to Section 108, elaborate whether a CGST/SGST authority can revise an order passed by his subordinates.

Answer:

Yes. Under Section 108 of the CGST Act, 2017, the Revisional Authority may call for and examine the records of any proceeding and pass a revisional order if he considers that the subordinate officer's order is erroneous in so far as it is prejudicial to the interest of revenue, or is illegal or improper.

Statutory Bars on Revision [Section 108(2)]: The Revisional Authority CANNOT revise an order where:

  • The order is subject to an appeal before the Appellate Authority, Tribunal, High Court, or Supreme Court;
  • The order was passed more than 3 years ago or less than 6 months ago;
  • The order has already been revised under Section 108.
ICAI TYK 5 (Tribunal Discretion to Refuse Appeal)

Question:

The Appellate Tribunal has the discretion to refuse to admit any appeal. Examine the correctness of the statement.

Answer:

The statement is CORRECT.

Under Section 112(2) of the CGST Act, 2017, the Appellate Tribunal has statutory discretion to refuse to admit an appeal where:

  • The tax or input tax credit involved; or
  • The difference in tax or input tax credit involved; or
  • The amount of fine, fee or penalty determined by such order,

DOES NOT EXCEED â‚¹50,000.

In all other cases exceeding â‚¹50,000, if the appeal is filed within limitation and mandatory pre-deposit is paid, the Tribunal must admit and adjudicate the appeal.

Problem Statement:

In an order dated 20th August, Joint Commissioner confirmed an IGST demand of â‚¹280 Crore against GH (P) Ltd. The company disputes the entire demand.

Detailed Solution & Statutory Computations:

(I) Pre-Deposit for Filing Appeal before Appellate Authority [Section 107(6)]:

Under Section 107(6) of the CGST Act read with Section 20 of the IGST Act, the pre-deposit for disputed tax is 10% of the disputed tax amount, subject to a maximum cap of â‚¹40 Crore for IGST.

  • 10% of disputed IGST of â‚¹280 Crore = â‚¹28 Crore.
  • Statutory maximum cap for IGST = â‚¹40 Crore.
  • Pre-Deposit for Appellate Authority = Lower of â‚¹28 Crore or â‚¹40 Crore = â‚¹28 Crore.
(II) Pre-Deposit for Filing Appeal before Appellate Tribunal [Section 112(8)]:

Under Section 112(8) of the CGST Act read with Section 20 of the IGST Act, the pre-deposit for filing appeal before GSTAT is 10% of the remaining tax in dispute, subject to a maximum cap of â‚¹40 Crore for IGST (in addition to the pre-deposit already paid at First Appeal stage).

  • 10% of disputed IGST of â‚¹280 Crore = â‚¹28 Crore.
  • Statutory maximum cap for IGST = â‚¹40 Crore.
  • Pre-Deposit for Appellate Tribunal = Lower of â‚¹28 Crore or â‚¹40 Crore = â‚¹28 Crore.
ICAI TYK 7 (Non-Appealable Orders under Sec 121)

Question:

With reference to the provisions of Section 121, specify the orders against which no appeals can be filed.

Answer:

Under Section 121 of the CGST Act, 2017, NO APPEAL shall lie against any decision or order relating to any of the following four matters:

  1. An order of the Commissioner or other authority empowered to direct transfer of proceedings from one officer to another officer;
  2. An order pertaining to the seizure or retention of books of account, register and other documents;
  3. An order sanctioning prosecution under the CGST Act; or
  4. An order passed under Section 80 (allowing or refusing payment of tax in installments).
ICAI TYK 8 (Place of Supply Dispute Appeal Route)

Problem Statement:

Mr. A had filed an appeal before the Appellate Tribunal against an order of the Appellate Authority where the issue involved relates to Place of Supply. The Tribunal ruled in favor of the Department. Mr. A wants to file an appeal before a higher forum. Advise him suitably.

Detailed Answer & Legal Advice:

Mr. A must file an appeal before the SUPREME COURT, NOT the High Court.

  1. Jurisdiction of Principal Bench [Section 109(5)]: Under Section 109(5) of the CGST Act, 2017, only the Principal Bench of the Appellate Tribunal (New Delhi) has the statutory jurisdiction to hear and decide appeals where one of the issues involved relates to the Place of Supply. Therefore, Mr. A's appeal was decided by the Principal Bench.
  2. Appellate Route to Supreme Court [Section 118(1)(a)]: Under Section 118(1)(a), an appeal lies directly to the Supreme Court from any order passed by the Principal Bench of the Appellate Tribunal.
  3. Exclusion of High Court: Under Section 117(1), appeals lie to the High Court only from orders passed by the State Benches of the Tribunal. Since State Benches cannot hear place of supply issues, the High Court has no appellate jurisdiction in this matter.

Conclusion: Mr. A is advised to file an appeal directly before the Supreme Court of India under Section 118.

ICAI TYK 9 (Departmental Monetary Limits under Sec 120)

Question:

With reference to the provisions of Section 120, list the cases in which appeal is not to be filed and specify other relevant provisions.

Answer:

Under Section 120 of the CGST Act, 2017:

  • Fixing Monetary Limits [Sec 120(1)]: The Board (CBIC) may, on the recommendations of the GST Council, issue orders or directions fixing monetary limits for regulating the filing of appeal or application by CGST officers. Appeals involving amounts below such limits are not to be filed by officers.
  • No Preclusion [Sec 120(2)]: Non-filing of an appeal by an officer on account of monetary limits shall not preclude such officer from filing an appeal in any other case involving the same or similar issues or questions of law.
  • No Acquiescence [Sec 120(3)]: No person who is a party in an appeal can contend that the CGST officer has agreed with (acquiesced in) the decision on the disputed issue by reason of not filing an appeal in another case on account of monetary limits.
  • Tribunal/Court Regard [Sec 120(4)]: The Tribunal or Court hearing an appeal shall have regard to the circumstances for non-filing of appeal on account of monetary limits.
