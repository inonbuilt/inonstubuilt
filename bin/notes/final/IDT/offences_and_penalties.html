Offences and Penalties

Comprehensive Master Revision Notes: CGST Sections 122 to 138, The 21 Specific Tax Breaches under Section 122(1), 100% Retained-Benefit Mastermind Penalties under Section 122(1A), Natural Justice Disciplines under Section 126, Interception in Transit under Section 129 (200% Penalty Standard), Confiscation & Redemption Fines under Section 130, Three-Tier Criminal Prosecution Framework under Section 132, and Revised Compounding Ranges (25% to 100%) under Section 138.

1. Penal Architecture & Statutory Objectives

Tax statutes require a graduated enforcement matrix to penalize non-compliance, recover lost revenue, and deter willful tax fraud. Under Chapter XIX of the CGST Act [Sections 122 to 138], the GST law establishes three distinct levels of penal consequences:

Level 1: Civil Penalties

Sections 122 to 128

Monetary penalties adjudicated directly by tax officers for transactional contraventions, bogus invoicing, failure to deduct/collect tax, and non-compliance with summons.

Level 2: In Rem Actions

Sections 129 & 130

Real actions against physical contraband: interception, detention, and seizure of conveyances and goods in transit, leading to confiscation and redemption fines.

Level 3: Criminal Prosecution

Sections 132 to 138

Criminal trials before Judicial Magistrates with mandatory imprisonment up to 5 years, arrest powers under Section 69, and compounding settlements.

2. The 21 Specified Offences & Penalties [Section 122(1)]

Section 122(1) enumerates 21 specific contraventions attracting the core statutory penalty. Any taxable person committing any of these breaches is liable to a penalty which shall be the HIGHER OF:

$$\mathbf{\text{Penalty under Section 122(1)}} = \text{HIGHER OF: } \begin{cases} \mathbf{â‚¹10,000}; \text{ OR} \\ \mathbf{100\%} \text{ of Tax Evaded / Unpaid / Wrongly Availed / Falsely Claimed} \end{cases}$$

(iii)Collects tax but fails to pay to Government beyond 3 months from due date.

3. Masterminds, Beneficiaries & ECO Penalties [Sec 122(1A) & (1B)]

Section 122(1A)

The Mastermind / Beneficiary Penalty

Any person who retains the benefit of transactions involving fake invoicing [clauses (i), (ii), (vii), or (ix) of Sec 122(1)] and at whose instance such transaction is conducted:
$$\mathbf{\text{Penalty}} = \mathbf{100\% \text{ of Tax Evaded or ITC Availed/Passed On!}}$$ Impact: Punishes the actual beneficial owner / ringleader operating behind dummy/proxy firm fronts!

Section 122(1B)

E-Commerce Operators Penalties

An ECO is penalized if it allows unregistered persons to supply goods through its platform without enrollment ID, or allows inter-State supply by unregistered/composition dealers:
$$\mathbf{\text{Penalty}} = \mathbf{â‚¹10,000 \text{ OR Tax Involved}} \text{ (whichever higher).}$$

Section 122(2): Taxable Person Short-Paying / Not Paying Tax:

  • Non-Fraud Cases [Sec 122(2)(a)]: Higher of â‚¹10,000 OR 10% of tax unpaid/short-paid.
  • Fraud Cases [Sec 122(2)(b)]: Higher of â‚¹10,000 OR 100% of tax unpaid/short-paid.

Section 122(3): Aiding, Abetting & Ancillary Contraventions (Up to â‚¹25,000 Penalty):

A penalty extending up to â‚¹25,000 applies to ANY person who:

  1. Aids or abets any of the 21 specified offences in Section 122(1);
  2. Acquires, transports, keeps, conceals, supplies, or purchases goods liable to confiscation;
  3. Receives or deals with supply of services which he knows is in contravention of the Act;
  4. Fails to appear before a tax officer when issued with a SUMMONS under Section 70; or
  5. Fails to issue an invoice in accordance with provisions or account for an invoice in books.

4. General Disciplines & Natural Justice Code [Section 126]

Section 126 provides a statutory charter of rights protecting taxpayers against arbitrary, mechanical, or disproportionate penalties:

Sub-Section Statutory Discipline Guarantee [Section 126]
126(1) No Penalty for Minor Breaches: No penalty shall be imposed for "minor breaches" where the tax involved is less than â‚¹5,000, or for procedural/documentation errors easily rectifiable without fraudulent intent.
126(2) Proportionality Principle: The penalty imposed must be commensurate with the degree and severity of the breach.
126(3) Opportunity of Hearing: No penalty shall be imposed without giving the person a reasonable opportunity of being heard.
126(4) Speaking Order Requirement: The order imposing penalty must explicitly specify the nature of the breach and the exact legal provision under which penalty is imposed. An adhoc penalty order without stating contraventions is illegal and challengeable!
126(5) Mitigating Effect of Voluntary Disclosure: When a person voluntarily discloses the breach to an officer prior to discovery, this factor shall be considered in mitigating the penalty.
Section 125

General / Residual Penalty

Where any person contravenes any provision of the Act/rules for which no specific penalty is provided, penalty extends up to â‚¹25,000 CGST + â‚¹25,000 SGST (Total â‚¹50,000).

Section 128

Power to Waive Penalty

The Government may, on Council recommendations, by notification waive off penalty partially or fully for any class of persons.

6. Confiscation of Goods & Redemption Fine [Section 130]

Confiscation is the ultimate in rem remedy where title of goods or conveyances vests entirely in the Government. Triggered where goods are supplied/received in contravention with intent to evade tax, failure to account for goods, or transporting goods without documents.

Redemption Fine on Goods

Section 130(2) Goods Fine

The owner is given an option to pay a fine in lieu of confiscation:
• Maximum Fine: Market Value of goods − Tax chargeable thereon.
• Minimum Fine: Penalty leviable under Section 129(1) (i.e. 200% of tax).

Redemption Fine on Conveyance

Section 130(2) Vehicle Fine

Where the conveyance was used without knowledge or connivance of the owner, the owner can redeem the conveyance by paying a fine equal to the tax payable on the goods being transported.

8. Compounding of Offences [Section 138 & Rule 162]

Compounding is a legal mechanism whereby an accused settles criminal charges by paying a compounding fee to the Government, thereby preventing or abating criminal trial and conviction:

Amended Statutory Compounding Fee Limits [Section 138(2)]:
$$\text{Minimum Compounding Amount} = \mathbf{25\% \text{ of Tax Involved}} \quad \text{(Amended from 50\%) }$$ $$\text{Maximum Compounding Amount} = \mathbf{100\% \text{ of Tax Involved}} \quad \text{(Amended from 150\%) }$$

Non-Compoundable Circumstances [Provisos to Section 138(1)]:

  • A person who has been allowed to compound once in respect of offences involving fake invoices [Sec 132(1)(a)-(f)] cannot compound again;
  • Offences under other criminal statutes (IPC/BNS/Narcotics) cannot be compounded under GST;
  • A person accused of committing offences under treaties/customs/PMLA.

ICAI TYK 2 (Adhoc Penalty Challenge under Section 126)

Problem Statement:

Mr. X (unregistered person) purchases goods supplied by Mr. Y (registered person) without receiving a tax invoice and thus helps in tax evasion. An adhoc penalty of â‚¹20,000 is imposed on Mr. X by passing an order without describing the contravention and without mentioning the statutory provisions. Should Mr. X pay the penalty or challenge the order?

Detailed Answer & Legal Evaluation:

Mr. X should CHALLENGE the order passed by the Department.

Section 126 of the CGST Act, 2017 prescribes the General Disciplines related to Penalty, which are binding statutory natural justice mandates:

  • Section 126(2): The penalty imposed must be commensurate with the degree and severity of the breach.
  • Section 126(4): "The officer imposing penalty shall specify the nature of the breach and the applicable law, regulation or procedure under which the amount of penalty for the breach has been specified."

In the present case, the order passed by the department is an adhoc order which fails to describe the contravention committed by Mr. X and fails to cite the specific section/rule under which the penalty of â‚¹20,000 is levied. This is a fatal jurisdictional defect violating Section 126(4) and principles of natural justice. Therefore, the order is legally unsustainable and should be challenged in appeal before the Appellate Authority under Section 107.

ICAI TYK 3 (Comprehensive Prosecution & Bailability Analysis)

Problem Statement:

Examine the implications as regards bailability and quantum of punishment on prosecution under the CGST Act, 2017 in the following cases for December:

  • (i) 'X' collects â‚¹245 Lakh as tax from clients and deposits â‚¹241 Lakh with the Government. He has falsified financial records and not maintained proper accounts.
  • (ii) 'Y' collects â‚¹550 Lakh as tax from clients but deposits only â‚¹30 Lakh with the Government.

What will be the implications on prosecution? What if X and Y repeat the offences?

Detailed Statutory Analysis & Solutions:

Case (i): Assessment of 'X':
  • Failure to Deposit Collected Tax [Sec 132(1)(d)]: Tax collected but unpaid beyond 3 months = â‚¹245 Lakh − â‚¹241 Lakh = â‚¹4 Lakhs. Since â‚¹4 Lakh does not exceed â‚¹200 Lakhs (â‚¹2 Crore), failure to deposit this amount does NOT attract imprisonment under Section 132(1).
  • Falsification of Financial Records [Sec 132(1)(f)]: Falsifying records with intent to evade tax is punishable under Section 132(1)(iv) with imprisonment up to 6 MONTHS or with fine or both.
  • Bailability: Under Section 132(4), this offence is Non-Cognizable and BAILABLE.
Case (ii): Assessment of 'Y':
  • Tax Evaded / Unpaid [Sec 132(1)(d)]: Tax collected but unpaid beyond 3 months = â‚¹550 Lakh − â‚¹30 Lakh = â‚¹520 Lakhs (â‚¹5.2 Crore).
  • Quantum of Punishment [Sec 132(1)(i)]: Since the tax evaded exceeds â‚¹500 Lakhs (â‚¹5 Crore), 'Y' is punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to 5 YEARS and with Fine.
  • Bailability [Sec 132(5)]: Since the amount exceeds â‚¹5 Crore and involves Section 132(1)(d), the offence is COGNIZABLE and NON-BAILABLE! Bail cannot be granted by the tax department.
Consequences of Repeat Offences [Section 132(2)]:

If 'X' and 'Y' repeat the specified offences after being convicted, they shall be punishable under Section 132(2) for the second and every subsequent offence with imprisonment up to 5 YEARS and with Fine. Furthermore, such imprisonment shall NOT be less than 6 months in the absence of special and adequate reasons recorded in the judgment.

ICAI TYK 4 (Cognizable vs Non-Cognizable Offences)

Question:

Discuss the cognizable and non-cognizable offences under Section 132.

Answer:

Under Section 132(5) of the CGST Act, 2017, the following offences are COGNIZABLE and NON-BAILABLE, provided the amount of tax evaded or ITC wrongly availed/utilized or refund wrongly taken EXCEEDS â‚¹5 CRORE:

  1. Supplying goods or services without invoice with intention to evade tax [Sec 132(1)(a)];
  2. Issuing invoice or bill without supply of goods/services leading to wrongful ITC or refund [Sec 132(1)(b)];
  3. Availing ITC using invoices against which no supply is received, or fraudulently availing ITC without invoice [Sec 132(1)(c)];
  4. Failing to pay the amount collected as tax to the Government beyond a period of 3 months from due date [Sec 132(1)(d)].

Non-Cognizable & Bailable: Under Section 132(4), ALL OTHER OFFENCES specified under Section 132 (including offences involving amounts up to â‚¹5 Crore) are NON-COGNIZABLE and BAILABLE.

ICAI TYK 5 (Failure to Appear for Summons Penalty)

Question:

Bindusar, CEO of Ashoka Solutions Ltd., is issued a summons to appear before the central tax officer to produce books of accounts in an enquiry conducted on the company. Determine the amount of penalty that may be imposed on Bindusar under the CGST Act, 2017 if he fails to appear.

Answer:

Under Section 122(3)(d) of the CGST Act, 2017:

"Any person who fails to appear before the officer of central tax, when issued with a summon for appearance to give evidence or produce a document in an inquiry, shall be liable to a penalty which may extend to â‚¹25,000."

Conclusion: If Bindusar fails to appear before the central tax officer without reasonable justification, a monetary penalty up to â‚¹25,000 can be imposed on him personally under Section 122(3)(d) of the CGST Act, 2017, in addition to potential criminal prosecution for non-attendance under Section 229 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023.
