Types of Customs Duties & Trade Remedies

Exhaustive analysis of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975: Basic Customs Duty (BCD), Integrated Tax u/s 3(7), Warehoused In-Bond Sale Valuation u/s 3(8A), Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS 10%), Safeguard Duty u/s 8B, Subsidized Articles Countervailing Duty u/s 9, Anti-Dumping Duty u/s 9A, and CESTAT Appeals u/s 9C.

1. Tariff Architecture & Customs Duties Taxonomy

Customs duties in India are levied under Section 12 of the Customs Act, 1962 at the rates specified in the Customs Tariff Act, 1975 (CTA). The tariff comprises two primary schedules:

  • First Schedule (Import Tariff): Formatted at an 8-digit Harmonized System of Nomenclature (HSN) level. Contains standard and preferential rates of Basic Customs Duty.
  • Second Schedule (Export Tariff): Prescribes export duties on a restricted list of commodities to protect domestic supply.
Class of Duty / Measure Statutory Provision Nature & Primary Objective Basis / Capping of Levy
Basic Customs Duty (BCD) Sec 12 Customs Act & Sec 2 CTA Fiscal revenue duty on imports; standard or preferential rate. Ad-valorem, specific, or compound rate on Assessable Value (AV).
Integrated Tax (IGST) Sec 3(7) of CTA, 1975 Countervailing tax equal to IGST on like domestic supply. Up to 40% (generally 18%) on [AV + BCD + SWS + Trade Duties].
Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) Finance Act, 2018 Finances education, health, and social welfare infrastructure. 10% of aggregate BCD (Excludes Safeguard, CVD, ADD & IGST).
Safeguard Measures Sec 8B of CTA, 1975 Emergency protection against sudden surge in import volume. Safeguard duty / Tariff-rate quota; 4 yrs (extendable to 10 yrs).
Countervailing Duty (CVD) Sec 9 of CTA, 1975 Neutralizes foreign government export subsidies. Capped at the exact amount of foreign subsidy; 5 yrs (+ 5 yrs).
Anti-Dumping Duty (ADD) Sec 9A of CTA, 1975 Counteracts predatory dumping of foreign goods below Normal Value. Lower of Margin of Dumping or Injury Margin; 5 yrs (+ 5 yrs).
Emergency Import/Export Duties Sec 8 & 8A of CTA, 1975 Immediate executive action to increase duties during crises. By Official Gazette notification; subject to parliamentary approval.

2. Basic Customs Duty (BCD) & Preferential Tariff Rates

A. Standard Rate vs Preferential Rate of BCD

The First Schedule to the Customs Tariff Act, 1975 classifies goods into 5 columns: HSN Code, Description, Unit, Standard Rate (Column 4), and Preferential Rate (Column 5).

  • Standard Rate: Applicable by default to all non-preferential imports. The peak rate for non-agricultural industrial goods is generally 10%.
  • Preferential Rate: Concessional rates granted under trade agreements (Free Trade Agreements - FTAs / Preferential Trade Agreements - PTAs).

Four Cumulative Conditions for Claiming Preferential Rate of Duty

To avail the preferential rate under Section 25 read with Section 4(3) of CTA, the importer must satisfy:

  1. Specific Claim: The importer must make a specific claim for the preferential rate at the time of presenting the Bill of Entry.
  2. Country of Manufacture: The importer must establish that the goods were produced or manufactured in such preferential area.
  3. Notified Preferential Area: The exporting country/territory must be notified by the Central Government under Section 4(3) of CTA.
  4. Rules of Origin Compliance: The origin of the goods must be determined and verified in accordance with the Customs Tariff (Determination of Origin) Rules made under Section 4(2).

Default Consequence: If any condition fails, the standard rate of BCD applies automatically.

3. Integrated Tax (IGST) & Valuation Mechanism (Section 3(7) & 3(8))

A. Levy of Integrated Tax [Section 3(7) of CTA]

Any article imported into India is liable to integrated tax at a rate not exceeding 40% (governed by general GST notifications like Notfn 01/2017-IT(R) and Notfn 02/2017-IT(R)).

  • SEZ Exemption: Goods imported by a unit or developer in a Special Economic Zone (SEZ) for authorized operations are 100% exempt from IGST (Notfn 64/2017-Cus).
  • EOU Exemption: 100% Export Oriented Undertakings (EOUs) enjoy duty-free imports under Notification 52/2003-Cus subject to export obligations.

B. Computation of Assessable Value for Levying IGST [Section 3(8) of CTA]

Statutory Formula for IGST Assessable Base

$$\text{Value for IGST} = \text{Assessable Value u/s 14} + \text{BCD} + \text{SWS} + \text{Other Customs Duties (Safeguard / ADD / AIDC)}$$

Exclusions: Section 3(8) expressly provides that the value shall EXCLUDE the Integrated Tax u/s 3(7) and GST Compensation Cess u/s 3(9).

5. Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) & Agriculture Infrastructure Cess (AIDC)

A. Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS)

Levied under the Finance Act, 2018 at the rate of 10% on the aggregate of customs duties. In practice, SWS is charged at 10% of the Basic Customs Duty (BCD).

Express Statutory Exclusions from SWS Computation

The following duties are strictly excluded while computing SWS:

  • Safeguard measures under Section 8B.
  • Countervailing duty under Section 9.
  • Anti-dumping duty under Section 9A.
  • Social Welfare Surcharge itself.
  • Integrated Tax (IGST) and GST Compensation Cess (exempted vide Notification 13/2018-Cus).

B. Agriculture Infrastructure and Development Cess (AIDC)

Imposed on specified imports (apples, coal, urea, gold, silver) on the assessable value determined under Section 14 to finance agricultural infrastructure. AIDC forms part of the customs duty pool.

6. Emergency Powers of Central Government (Sections 8 & 8A CTA)

Export Duties [Section 8]

Central Government may impose or enhance export duties by amending the Second Schedule via Gazette notification if circumstances render it immediately necessary (e.g. banning/taxing milk powder exports during dry summer shortages).

Import Duties [Section 8A]

Central Government may increase import duties on items specified in the First Schedule to protect domestic markets during sudden price collapses. The notification must be laid before Parliament for approval.

7. Safeguard Measures (Section 8B of CTA, 1975)

A. Grounds and Forms of Safeguard Measures

Safeguard measures are emergency trade defense mechanisms imposed when an article is imported into India in increased quantities (surge in imports), causing or threatening to cause serious injury to the domestic industry producing like or directly competitive articles.

  • Modes: (a) Safeguard duty; (b) Tariff-rate quota (TRQ); or (c) Quantitative restrictions.
  • Duration: Valid for 4 years from initial imposition; extendable up to a maximum total of 10 years if domestic industry demonstrates adjustment.
  • Provisional Safeguard Duty: Maximum period of 200 days pending final determination (refunded if no injury found).

B. Statutory Exemptions from Safeguard Duty

Developing Country Exemption Thresholds [Section 8B Proviso]

  1. Single Developing Country: No safeguard duty if imports from that developing country do not exceed 3% of total imports of that article into India.
  2. Group of Developing Countries: No safeguard duty if aggregate imports from developing countries (each with < 3% share) does not exceed 9% of total imports into India.
  3. 100% EOU & SEZ Units: Safeguard duty does NOT apply to imports by EOUs or SEZ units unless specifically notified OR when goods/finished products are cleared into the Domestic Tariff Area (DTA).

8. Countervailing Duty on Subsidized Articles (Section 9 CTA)

A. Statutory Criteria for CVD

Imposed when any foreign country or territory directly or indirectly pays or bestows a subsidy upon the manufacture, production, export, or transportation of an article imported into India.

  • Definition of Subsidy: Direct transfer of funds (grants/loans), revenue forgone/tax holidays, or income/price support.
  • Ceiling Limit: Countervailing duty shall not exceed the actual amount of subsidy bestowed.
  • Duration: Valid for 5 years; extendable upon sunset review for up to 5 additional years (interim continuation up to 1 year pending review).
  • Retrospective Imposition: Can be levied retrospectively up to 90 days prior to notification date if massive imports cause acute injury.

B. Anti-Circumvention & Absorption

CVD can be extended to altered articles, unassembled/disassembled kits, or rerouted countries of origin if exporters attempt to bypass duty (anti-circumvention) or lower export prices without changing Indian resale prices (absorption).

9. Anti-Dumping Duty (Section 9A of CTA, 1975)

A. The Concept of Dumping

Dumping occurs when an exporter sells a product in India at an Export Price lower than its Normal Value in its domestic market, causing material injury to the Indian domestic industry producing like articles.

Quantitative Determination: The Lesser Duty Rule

$$\text{Anti-Dumping Duty} = \min(\text{Margin of Dumping}, \text{Injury Margin})$$

B. Operational Guidelines & Duration

  • Duration: 5 years from date of imposition; extendable for up to 5 additional years upon review.
  • Provisional Anti-Dumping Duty: Imposed during investigation; if final margin is lower, the excess is refunded u/s 9A(2).
  • Retrospective Imposition: Up to 90 days prior to provisional duty date if history of dumping and massive imports exist.
  • Refund of Excess Duty [Section 9AA]: Importer can claim refund of definitive ADD paid in excess of actual dumping margin upon establishing proof before the Central Government.

10. Statutory Prohibitions on Trade Remedy Levies (Section 9B CTA)

Four Statutory Bars under Section 9B

  1. No Double Levy: No article shall be subjected to both countervailing and anti-dumping duties to compensate for the same situation of dumping or export subsidization.
  2. Domestic Exemption in Origin Country: CVD/ADD cannot be imposed merely because the goods are exempt from duties/taxes in their country of origin or export, or because such domestic taxes were refunded upon export.
  3. WTO / MFN Injury Determination: Definitive CVD/ADD cannot be imposed on imports from WTO members or Most Favored Nation (MFN) partners unless material injury (or material retardation of domestic industry establishment) is affirmatively determined.
  4. Provisional Duties Threshold: Provisional CVD/ADD cannot be imposed on specified countries without preliminary findings of subsidy/dumping and injury.

11. Appeals to CESTAT Special Bench (Section 9C CTA)

Section 9C provides a statutory appellate remedy directly before the Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT) against any determination or review regarding safeguard duty, subsidy/CVD, or dumping/ADD:

13. Solved ICAI Test Your Knowledge Problems (10 Problems)

ICAI Question 1

Refund of Anti-Dumping Duty under Section 9AA

With reference to Section 9AA of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975, state briefly the provisions of refund of anti-dumping duty.
Statutory Analysis & Solution:
  1. Entitlement to Refund: Where an importer proves to the satisfaction of the Central Government that he has paid anti-dumping duty imposed under Section 9A(1) in excess of the actual margin of dumping in relation to such article, the Central Government shall reduce such duty and refund the excess duty collected.
  2. Definitive vs Provisional Duty: Section 9AA applies strictly to definitive anti-dumping duty. Provisional anti-dumping duty is separately refundable under Section 9A(2) if the provisional duty exceeds the final determined dumping margin.
ICAI Question 2

Circumvention of Anti-Dumping Duty [Section 9A(1A)]

With reference to Section 9A(1A) of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975, mention the ways that constitute circumvention of anti-dumping duty warranting action by the Central Government.
Statutory Analysis & Solution:

Circumvention refers to avoiding duty through artificial arrangements. Section 9A(1A) covers:

  1. Altering the description, trade name, or composition of the article subject to duty;
  2. Importing the article in an unassembled or disassembled form (SKD/CKD kits) for assembly in India;
  3. Changing the country of origin or country of export (transshipment through third countries);
  4. Any other manipulative manner whereby the anti-dumping duty is rendered ineffective.

Consequence: Central Government may extend anti-dumping duty to such altered or rerouted articles from a date not earlier than the date of initiation of the inquiry.

ICAI Question 3

When Shall Safeguard Measures under Section 8B NOT be Imposed?

When shall the safeguard measures under Section 8B of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975 be not imposed? Discuss briefly.
Statutory Analysis & Solution:

Safeguard measures under Section 8B shall not be imposed on:

  1. Single Developing Country: Where imports originating from a developing country do not exceed 3% of total imports of that article into India;
  2. Group of Developing Countries: Where aggregate imports from developing countries (each having an individual share < 3%) taken together do not exceed 9% of total imports into India;
  3. 100% EOUs / SEZ Units: Unless specifically notified, or where the imported goods or manufactured products are cleared into the Domestic Tariff Area (DTA).
ICAI Question 4

Conditions for Claiming Preferential Rate of Duty

What conditions are required to be fulfilled by an importer to make imported goods eligible for the preferential rate of duty?
Statutory Analysis & Solution:
  1. Make a specific claim for the preferential rate at the time of importation in the Bill of Entry;
  2. Establish that the goods were produced or manufactured in the notified preferential area;
  3. The area must be notified under Section 4(3) of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975;
  4. Comply with Rules of Origin framed under Section 4(2) of CTA.
ICAI Question 5

Emergency Power to Impose/Enhance Import Duties [Section 8A]

Write a note on "Emergency power to impose or enhance import duties under Section 8A of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975".
Statutory Analysis & Solution:
  1. Where the Central Government is satisfied that circumstances exist which render it necessary to take immediate action to increase the import duty on any article specified in the First Schedule, it may amend the First Schedule by Official Gazette notification.
  2. Such notification has the immediate effect of enhancing the BCD rate.
  3. Every notification issued must be placed before each House of Parliament for approval while in session.

ICAI Questions 7 & 8

Retrospective Anti-Dumping Duty (Chaintop Industries)

Chaintop Industries challenged retrospective imposition of anti-dumping duty on grounds of unconstitutionality. Will it succeed?
Statutory Analysis & Solution:

Chaintop Industries will NOT succeed if statutory conditions under Section 9A(3) are met:

  1. Constitutional Validity: Section 9A(3) explicitly authorizes the Central Government to levy anti-dumping duty retrospectively from a date prior to provisional duty imposition.
  2. Conditions: (a) A history of dumping causing injury exists, or importer was/should have been aware of dumping; and (b) Massive dumping occurred in a short time undermining the remedial effect of the duty.
  3. 90-Day Ceiling: The retrospective period cannot extend beyond 90 days from the notification date. If these conditions are fulfilled, retrospective levy is entirely constitutional and valid.
