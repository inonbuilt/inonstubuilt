Miscellaneous Provisions under GST & Anti-Profiteering

Exhaustive analysis of CGST Chapter XXI (Sections 144 to 174), Administration (Sections 3 to 6), IGST Apportionment of Tax (Section 17), Consent-Based Data Sharing (Section 158A), Service of Notice (Section 169), and the GSTAT Anti-Profiteering Sunset Framework (Section 171).

1. Statutory Framework & Chapter Architecture

Chapter XXI of the CGST Act, 2017 (Sections 143 to 174) and Chapters VIII & IX of the IGST Act, 2017 form the closing statutory framework governing indirect tax administration in India. These miscellaneous provisions address critical evidentiary presumptions, confidentiality, inter-agency information sharing, administrative cross-empowerment, delegated rule-making, Force Majeure relief, and transition savings.

2. Documents & Evidentiary Presumptions (Sections 144 & 145)

A. Presumption as to Documents in Certain Cases [Section 144]

Under ordinary rules of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, the contents of a document must be proved by primary evidence, signatures and handwriting must be affirmatively proven, and attested documents require attesting witnesses. Furthermore, under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899, unstamped or deficiently stamped documents are strictly inadmissible.

Section 144 enacts a statutory departure in favor of the prosecution in respect of documents:

1. Produced by Any Person

Documents produced by any person under the CGST Act or any other law in force.

2. Seized Documents

Documents seized from the custody or control of any person under the CGST Act or any other law.

3. Received from Abroad

Documents received from any place outside India in the course of any statutory proceedings.

Three Statutory Presumptions of Law under Section 144

  • Truth of Contents: The Court shall presume the truth of the contents of such document.
  • Signature & Handwriting: The Court shall presume that the signature and every part of the document purporting to be in the handwriting of any particular person is in that person's handwriting.
  • Due Execution & Attestation: The Court shall presume that the document was duly executed or attested by the person by whom it purports to have been executed or attested.

Shifting of Burden of Proof: The burden of rebutting these statutory presumptions shifts entirely onto the taxable person / accused.

Inapplicability of Stamp Act: Section 144 expressly provides that such documents shall be admissible in evidence even if they are not duly stamped under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899.

B. Admissibility of Electronic Records, Microfilms & Computer Printouts [Section 145]

As per Section 2(41), "document" includes written/printed records of any sort and electronic records under Section 2(1)(t) of the Information Technology Act, 2000.

Section 145(1) treats the following as deemed documents admissible in any GST proceeding without production of the original:

Class of Deemed Document Statutory Scope under Section 145(1) Evidentiary Status
Microfilm A microfilm of a document or reproduction of image(s) embodied therein, whether enlarged or not. Admissible as evidence of any contents of the original without producing original negatives.
Facsimile Copy An exact physical or digital reproduction/copy of a document true to original. Direct evidence admissible without establishing custody of the physical original.
Computer Printout A statement contained in a document included in printed material produced by a computer. Admissible as primary evidence provided certification criteria u/s 145(2) are satisfied.
Electronic Data Storage Information stored electronically in any device, server, ERP database, or cloud media, including hard copies thereof. Complete evidentiary equivalence to direct oral or documentary evidence.

Certification Safeguards under Section 145(2)

A certificate identifying the document containing the statement, describing the manner of production, and giving device particulars to establish computer production shall constitute conclusive evidence of the facts stated therein. It is statutorily sufficient for the certificate to state matters to the best of the knowledge and belief of the certifying person.

3. Information Governance, Confidentiality & Consent Sharing (Sections 150 to 159)

A. Power to Call for Information [Section 151] & Bar on Disclosure [Section 152]

Under Section 151, the Commissioner or authorized officer may, by order, direct any person to furnish information relating to any matter connected with the Act within specified time, form, and manner.

Section 152 imposes a strict statutory bar on disclosure:

  • No information collected under Section 150 (Information Return) or Section 151 shall, without previous consent in writing of the concerned person/representative, be published so as to enable individual identification.
  • No such information shall be used in any adverse proceedings against the person without affording a reasonable opportunity of being heard.
  • Exception: Publication of generic data relating to a class of taxable persons or class of transactions is permitted if the Commissioner deems it desirable in the public interest.

B. Disclosure of Information by Public Servants [Section 158]

Under Section 158(1), all particulars contained in statements made, returns filed, accounts/documents produced, and records of evidence/proceedings under the Act are strictly confidential.

11 Permitted Statutory Exceptions to Non-Disclosure [Section 158(3)]

Notwithstanding Section 158(1), disclosure of confidential GST information is legally permissible in the following cases:

  1. Criminal Prosecution: For prosecution under Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (or erstwhile IPC), Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, or GST laws.
  2. Implementation of the Act: To Central/State Government officials executing the objectives of the Act.
  3. Notice Service & Recovery: When occasioned by the lawful exercise of any process for service of notice or recovery of tax demand.
  4. Civil Court in Govt Proceedings: To a Civil Court in suits/proceedings where the Government or GST Authority is a party.
  5. Audit of Tax Receipts: To C&AG or officers appointed for audit of tax receipts or refunds.
  6. Inquiry into GST Officer Conduct: To inquiry officers probing official misconduct of any tax officer.
  7. Levy/Realization of Other Taxes: To officers of Central/State Governments enabling them to levy or realize any customs, direct, or excise duties.
  8. Lawful Exercise of Powers: Occasioned by lawful exercise of statutory powers by any public servant or authority.
  9. Disciplinary Action Against Professionals: To Disciplinary Committees of ICAI (Chartered Accountants), ICMAI (Cost Accountants), ICSI (Company Secretaries), or Bar Councils for professional misconduct inquiries.
  10. IT & Automated Systems: To IT agencies appointed for data entry, cloud hosting, or operating automated GST systems under non-disclosure contracts.
  11. Public Interest & Other Laws: To Government officers for enforcing any other law in force in India, or published generic class data in public interest.

C. Consent-Based Sharing of Taxpayer Information [Section 158A & Rule 163]

Section 158A (enacted to integrate GST data with financial architecture) enables the common portal to share specified registration, invoice, and return particulars with external systems subject to statutory consent.

Data Class Shared Source Statutory Form Mandatory Consent Required
Registration Particulars Form GST REG-01 (as amended) Consent of the Applicant / Taxable Person
Return Summary & Cash/ITC Data Form GSTR-3B Consent of the Taxable Person
Outward Invoices, Debit & Credit Notes Form GSTR-1 / GSTR-1A Dual Consent: Supplier gives consent only after obtaining consent of all recipients; deemed recipient consent upon supplier intimation.
Movement of Goods & E-Way Bills Section 68 documents / EWB Portal Consent of Supplier & Recipient (where identity is disclosed)

Notified Systems for Consent-Based Sharing

  • Account Aggregators (AAs): NBFC-Account Aggregators licensed under Section 45JA of the RBI Act, 1934 to facilitate real-time credit underwriting and working capital financing for MSMEs.
  • Public Tech Platform for Frictionless Credit (PTPFC): RBI Innovation Hub open architecture enterprise platform conceptualized in August 2023 for seamless digital access across financial service providers and data repositories.

Legal Immunity: Section 158A(3) explicitly declares that no legal action or liability shall lie against the Government or Common Portal consequent to information shared under this section.

D. Publication of Information in Respect of Persons in Certain Cases [Section 159]

Section 159 empowers the Commissioner (or authorized officer) to publish names and particulars relating to any proceedings or prosecutions under the Act if deemed necessary or expedient in the public interest.

Mandatory Statutory Bar Relating to Penalties [Section 159(2)]

No publication shall be made in relation to any penalty imposed under the Act:

  • Until the limitation period for presenting an appeal under Section 107 has expired (3 months + 1 month condonable period) without an appeal having been filed; OR
  • Where an appeal has been presented, until such appeal has been finally heard and disposed of by the Appellate Authority.

4. Procedural Safeguards, Rectifications & Rounding Off (Sections 153, 160, 161 & 170)

A. Taking Assistance from an Expert [Section 153]

Any officer not below the rank of Assistant Commissioner may take the assistance of any expert (e.g., Chartered Accountants, Cost Accountants, IT forensic experts, valuation engineers) at any stage of scrutiny, inquiry, investigation, or adjudication, having regard to the nature, technical complexity, and revenue involved.

B. Assessment Proceedings Not Invalid on Certain Grounds [Section 160]

Section 160 cures procedural defects, ensuring that assessments, adjudications, reviews, summons, and notices are not rendered invalid or void merely by reason of:

  • A mistake, defect, or omission;
  • Provided the proceeding is in substance and effect in conformity with or according to the intent, purpose, and requirements of the Act.

Estoppel on Challenging Service of Notice [Section 160(2)]

The service of any notice, order, or communication cannot be called in question if:

  1. The notice, order, or communication has already been acted upon by the person to whom it was issued; OR
  2. The validity of service was not raised at the earliest proceedings commenced, continued, or finalized pursuant to such notice.

C. Rectification of Errors Apparent on the Face of Record [Section 161]

Section 161 overrides all provisions of the Act (except Section 160). Any authority who passed any decision, order, notice, or certificate may rectify any error apparent on the face of the record:

Initiation Channel Limitation for Application Outer Limitation for Order Exceptions & Natural Justice
Suo Motu by Authority At any time within 6 months 6 Months from date of issue of decision/order/notice No 6-month bar for purely clerical or arithmetical errors arising from accidental slips/omissions.

Natural Justice: Reasonable opportunity of hearing is mandatory if rectification adversely affects any person.
Brought by GST Officer At any time within 6 months 6 Months from date of issue
Application by Affected Person Within 3 Months from date of issue 6 Months from date of issue

Judicial Principle: "Error Apparent on the Face of Record"

An error apparent on the face of the record must be a glaring, obvious, and patent mistake (e.g., miscalculation of tax rate, arithmetical addition errors, omitting an express statutory provision). A debatable point of law or a matter requiring elaborate arguments and conflicting judicial interpretations can NEVER be rectified under Section 161 (the proper remedy is an appeal under Section 107).

D. Rounding Off of Tax, Interest, Penalty, Fine & Refund [Section 170]

Under Section 170, the final amount of tax, interest, penalty, fine, refund, or any other sum payable or due under the Act must be rounded off to the nearest rupee:

  • If the fraction contains fifty paise or more: increased to next whole rupee (+â‚¹1).
  • If the fraction is less than fifty paise: fraction is completely ignored.

Critical Distinction: Rounding Off Does NOT Apply to Tax Invoices

Section 170 applies strictly to aggregate statutory payments and refunds to/from the Government (e.g., final liability in GSTR-3B or refund payment in RFD-06). A supplier is NOT required to round off individual line items or invoices issued to customers.

5. Delegated Legislation, CBIC Circulars & Force Majeure (Sections 164 to 168A)

A. Rule-Making & Regulation Powers [Sections 164, 165 & 166]

  • Rules [Section 164]: Framed by the Central Government on GST Council recommendations. May carry retrospective effect (not prior to 01.07.2017) and may prescribe a general penalty up to â‚¹10,000 for rule breaches.
  • Regulations [Section 165]: Framed directly by CBIC (the Board) consistent with the Act and rules to implement administrative mechanisms.
  • 30-Day Parliamentary Laying [Section 166]: Every rule, regulation, and notification must be laid before each House of Parliament for a total period of 30 days while in session. Any modification or annulment agreed by both Houses takes effect without prejudicing past actions.

B. Delegation of Powers [Section 167]

The Commissioner may, by notification, direct that powers exercisable by any authority/officer may be exercised by another officer subordinate or designated, subject to specified statutory conditions.

C. Instructions and Directions: Legal Binding Nature [Section 168]

Section 168 empowers the Board (CBIC) to issue orders, instructions, or directions to ensure uniformity in GST implementation across all Commissionerates.

Settled Jurisprudence on CBIC Circulars

  • Binding on Department: Circulars are strictly binding on GST tax officers, adjudicating authorities, and departmental representatives.
  • Not Binding on Assessees: Taxpayers are free to challenge circulars before High Courts or Appellate Authorities.
  • Not Binding on Courts/Tribunals: The Appellate Tribunal and Courts are not bound by CBIC circulars and interpret the statutory text independently.
  • Conflict with Statutory Law: If a circular contradicts the express language of the CGST Act, the Act prevails over the circular unconditionally.

D. Power to Extend Time Limits in Special Circumstances [Section 168A]

Section 168A enables the Government (on GST Council recommendation) to extend any limitation period under the Act which cannot be completed due to Force Majeure.

Definition of "Force Majeure" under Section 168A

"Force majeure" means war, epidemic, flood, drought, fire, cyclone, earthquake, or any other natural calamity affecting the implementation of provisions of the Act. Notifications under Section 168A can be issued with retrospective effect.

6. Omission, Repeal and Saving (Sections 173, 174 & Section 6 General Clauses Act)

A. Omission of Service Tax [Section 173]

Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994 (which governed Service Tax) stands omitted with effect from 01.07.2017 upon GST rollout.

B. Repeal and Saving Provisions [Section 174]

Section 174 repeals the Central Excise Act, 1944 (except for petroleum crude, HSD, motor spirit, ATF, natural gas, and tobacco under Entry 84 of the Union List), Medicinal & Toilet Preparations Act, 1955, and Central Excise Tariff Act, 1985.

Scope of the Saving Clause [Section 174(2)]

The repeal shall NOT:

  • Revive anything not in force or existing at the time of repeal.
  • Affect the previous operation of repealed enactments or anything duly done thereunder.
  • Affect any right, privilege, obligation, or liability acquired, accrued, or incurred under previous laws.
  • Affect any duty, tax, surcharge, fine, penalty, or interest due, or forfeiture/punishment incurred.
  • Affect any ongoing investigation, inquiry, verification, assessment, or recovery proceedings.
  • Incentive Notifications: Any investment-linked tax exemption granted under earlier notifications does NOT continue as a vested privilege if the notification was rescinded on or after the appointed day.

The principles of Section 6 of the General Clauses Act, 1897 fully govern the repeal and saving operations under Section 174.

7. Other Statutory Provisions (Sections 146, 148, 149, 154-157, 162-163 & 169)

Common Portal [Sec 146]

Notified portal for registration, payments, returns, and settlement: www.gst.gov.in. E-way bill portal: www.ewaybillgst.gov.in. E-invoicing portal: Invoice Registration Portals (IRP).

Special Procedures [Sec 148]

Enables notified procedures for builders (TDR), exemption from tax on advances for goods (Notfn 66/2017-CT), pan masala tracking, and CIRP corporate debtors.

Compliance Rating [Sec 149]

Assigns a dynamic compliance rating score based on filing and payment parameters; score is intimated and placed in public domain to build transparency.

Power to Take Samples [Sec 154]

Proper officer may draw samples of goods from taxable persons whenever necessary; officer must provide a written acknowledgment/receipt.

Burden of Proof for ITC [Sec 155]

The burden of proving eligibility and entitlement to Input Tax Credit lies squarely on the taxable person claiming the credit.

Deemed Public Servants [Sec 156]

All officers and persons discharging GST statutory functions are deemed public servants under Section 21 of the Indian Penal Code, 1860 (Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita).

Good Faith Immunity [Sec 157]

Statutory protection against civil/criminal suits for actions done or intended to be done in good faith by GSTAT President/Members, GST officers, and employees.

Bar on Civil Courts [Sec 162]

Civil courts are strictly barred from entertaining suits or deciding questions arising under GST. Direct appeal lies only to High Court & Supreme Court u/s 117-118.

Service of Notice in Certain Circumstances [Section 169]

Section 169(1) prescribes six legal modes for serving any notice, decision, order, summons, or communication:

Clause Mode of Service Statutory Addressee / Place Deemed Date of Service / Receipt
(a) Personal Delivery / Messenger / Courier Directly tendered to taxpayer, manager, authorized rep, advocate, STP, employee, or adult resident family member. Deemed served on the date tendered.
(b) Registered Post / Speed Post / Courier AD Sent to last known place of business or residence with acknowledgment due. Deemed received at expiry of normal postal transit period.
(c) Electronic Mail (Email) Sent to the registered email address provided in registration profile or updated from time to time. Deemed served on the date of transmission.
(d) Common Portal Dashboard Making the notice/order available on the taxpayer's dashboard at www.gst.gov.in. Deemed served on the date uploaded to portal.
(e) Newspaper Publication Published in a newspaper circulating in the locality of last known residence or place of business. Deemed served on the date of newspaper publication.
(f) Affixture (Substituted Service) Affixing on conspicuous place at last known business/residence, or on the notice board of the jurisdictional tax office. Deemed served on the date copy is affixed.

8. IGST Cross-Application & Apportionment of Funds (Sections 17 to 20 IGST Act)

A. Application of CGST Provisions to IGST [Section 20 of IGST Act]

Section 20 makes the entirety of CGST machinery provisions applicable to the IGST Act (Scope of supply, Valuation, ITC, Registration, Returns, Assessments, Demands, Job work, E-Commerce, etc.). Notable rate variations:

  • IGST TDS Rate: 2% (equivalent to 1% CGST + 1% SGST).
  • IGST TCS Rate: Capped at 2%; currently notified at 0.5% (Notfn 15/2024-CT).
  • Penalties: The penalty leviable under IGST shall be the sum total of penalties leviable under CGST and SGST/UTGST.

B. Apportionment of Tax and Settlement of Funds [Section 17 of IGST Act]

Because Integrated Tax (IGST) is collected centrally on inter-State transactions and imports, Section 17 establishes the clearing-house mechanism to apportion revenues between Centre and States:

Apportionment Hierarchy under Section 17

  1. Unutilized / Ineligible IGST Pool [Sec 17(1)]:
    • Inter-State supplies to B2C unregistered persons or composition dealers.
    • Supplies where recipient is legally ineligible for ITC (blocked credits u/s 17(5)).
    • Supplies where ITC was not claimed within the annual return due date limitation.
    • Import of goods/services by unregistered persons, composition dealers, or for blocked purposes.
  2. Apportionment Method [Sec 17(2)]:
    • To Central Government: Amount equal to CGST rate on a similar intra-State supply.
    • To Destination State: The remaining balance of IGST is transferred to the SGST account of the consumption State (or to UTGST/Centre for Union Territories).
    • Unidentifiable State: Apportioned among States in proportion to total supplies made to each State by such taxpayer, or in proportion to SGST collections of preceding financial year.
  3. Ad-Hoc 50:50 Apportionment of B2B ITC Pool [Sec 17(2A)]: IGST collected on B2B supplies where credit is actively utilized across States may be provisionally apportioned 50% to Centre and 50% to States on an ad-hoc basis, adjusted in subsequent periodic settlements.

9. Administration & Cross-Empowerment (Sections 3 to 6 CGST Act)

A. Officer Hierarchy [Section 3]

The Principal Chief Commissioners, Chief Commissioners, Principal Commissioners, Commissioners, Additional Commissioners, Joint Commissioners, Deputy Commissioners, Assistant Commissioners, and Superintendents/Inspectors constitute the administrative classes of Central Tax officers.

B. Statutory Cross-Empowerment [Section 6 of CGST Act]

To eliminate dual administrative control and duplicate litigation, Section 6 provides seamless cross-empowerment between Central and State officers:

Cross-Empowerment Principle Statutory Mandate under Section 6 Legal Effect
Single Order Rule [Sec 6(2)(a)] Where a proper officer issues an order under the CGST Act, he must concurrently issue an order under the SGST/UTGST Act. Taxpayer receives one consolidated adjudication order covering CGST, SGST, and IGST components.
Bar on Parallel Proceedings [Sec 6(2)(b)] Where a State proper officer has initiated proceedings on a subject matter, no proceedings can be initiated by the Central officer on the same subject matter (and vice-versa). Eliminates double jeopardy and jurisdictional harassment across administrative wings.
Appeals & Rectifications [Sec 6(3)] Appeals and rectifications against orders passed by a CGST officer lie exclusively before Central appellate authorities. State appellate forums cannot entertain appeals against Central tax orders.

10. Special Procedure for Corporate Debtors under CIRP (Section 148)

Under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016, once Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) begins, the management and assets of the Corporate Debtor vest in the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP) or Resolution Professional (RP).

Compliance Framework under Notification 11/2020-CT & Circular 138/08/2020-GST

  • Distinct Person Treatment: The corporate debtor undergoing CIRP is treated as a distinct person from the erstwhile company.
  • New Registration within 30 Days: The IRP/RP must obtain a fresh GST registration in each State where the debtor was previously registered within 30 days of appointment.
    Exception: If the debtor has complied with all past GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B filings up to date, separate registration is not mandatory.
  • Single Registration for CIRP: A change in the IRP/RP after initial appointment does NOT require a new registration; it is treated merely as a change of authorized signatory.
  • Tax Liabilities during CIRP: The IRP/RP is responsible for filing returns and paying GST liabilities accrued during the CIRP period from the date of appointment. Pre-CIRP dues must be claimed by the Revenue before the NCLT.

11. General Clauses Act, 1897 & Computation of Time

A. Exclusion of First Day and Inclusion of Last Day [Section 9]

Under Section 9 of the General Clauses Act, 1897, in computing any limitation period in a Central statute:

  • The word "from" excludes the first day in a series of days.
  • The word "to" includes the last day in the period.

Landmark Precedent: Econ Antri Ltd v. Rom Industries Ltd. (Supreme Court)

The Supreme Court affirmed that while computing the statutory limitation period, the day on which the cause of action arises or the offence is committed must be strictly excluded from computation.

B. Meaning of "Month" [Section 3(35)]

Under Section 3(35) of the General Clauses Act, 1897, a "month" means a month reckoned according to the British calendar, and NOT a period of 30 days. In CCE v. Ashok Kumar Tiwari (All. HC), it was held that statutory limitation stipulated in terms of months must be computed by calendar month rather than counting arbitrary blocks of 30 days.

12. Anti-Profiteering Measure & Sunset Framework [Section 171]

A. Statutory Mandate of Section 171

Section 171 mandates that any reduction in the rate of tax on any supply of goods/services or the benefit of input tax credit must be passed on to the recipient by way of a commensurate reduction in prices.

Critical Regulatory Timeline & Sunset Clause w.e.f. 01.04.2025

Period Adjudicating Authority Status & Regulatory Changes
01.07.2017 to 30.11.2022 National Anti-Profiteering Authority (NAA) Handled initial post-GST transition profiteering investigations.
01.12.2022 to 30.09.2024 Competition Commission of India (CCI) NAA dissolved; jurisdiction transferred to CCI.
01.10.2024 to 31.03.2025 Principal Bench of GSTAT (New Delhi) Empowered vide Notfn 18/2024-CT to adjudicate anti-profiteering matters.
With effect from 01.04.2025 (Sunset Clause) GSTAT Principal Bench Sunset Clause Triggered [Notfn 19/2024-CT]: GSTAT shall NOT accept any fresh requests for examination after 01.04.2025. It solely adjudicates legacy applications filed prior to 01.04.2025.

B. Investigation & Order of the Authority

  • Director General of Anti-Profiteering (DGAP): Conducts detailed investigations with powers of a Civil Court; completes report within 6 months (+ 3 months extension).
  • Orders of GSTAT: Issued within 6 months of receiving the DGAP report:
    1. Order commensurate price reduction.
    2. Order refund of excess amount along with 18% interest from date of collection till repayment.
    3. Deposit unclaimed amounts in the Consumer Welfare Fund (50% Central CWF, 50% State CWF).
    4. Impose statutory penalty and/or cancel GST registration in severe cases.
  • 10% Penalty: 10% of the profiteered amount if the sum is not deposited within 30 days of the order date.

13. Solved ICAI Test Your Knowledge Problems

ICAI Question 1

Determination of GST Compliance Rating Score

Briefly explain how the GST compliance rating score is determined and its significance under GST laws.
Statutory Analysis & Solution:
  1. Legal Provision: Section 149(1) of the CGST Act provides that every registered person may be assigned a goods and services tax compliance rating score based on his record of compliance with the provisions of the Act.
  2. Parameters: As per Section 149(2), the GST compliance rating score is determined on the basis of specified parameters such as timely filing of returns (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B), prompt tax payment, static reconciliation matching, and absence of demands/penalties.
  3. Public Domain: As per Section 149(3), the rating score is updated periodically, intimated to the taxable person, and placed in the public domain on the Common Portal.
  4. Commercial Significance: Enables prospective buyers to evaluate vendor reliability, prevent loss of ITC due to supplier default, and foster voluntary compliance.
ICAI Question 2

Publication of Information Relating to Proceedings & Penalties

When shall the particulars relating to any proceedings or prosecution be published under GST laws? What is the statutory limitation on publishing information relating to penalties?
Statutory Analysis & Solution:
  1. Power to Publish [Section 159(1)]: The Commissioner or authorized officer may publish the name of any person and particulars relating to any proceedings or prosecution under the Act if deemed necessary or expedient in the public interest. For companies or firms, names of partners, directors, or managers may also be published.
  2. Statutory Bar on Penalties [Section 159(2)]: No publication under Section 159 shall be made in relation to any penalty imposed under the Act:
    • Until the time limit for filing an appeal before the Appellate Authority under Section 107 (3 months + 1 month condonable period) has expired without an appeal having been filed; OR
    • Where an appeal has been presented, until such appeal has been finally heard and disposed of by the Appellate Authority.
ICAI Question 3

Rectification of Errors Apparent on Face of Record

Explain the provisions relating to rectification of errors apparent on the face of the record under Section 161.
Statutory Analysis & Solution:
  1. Scope of Rectification: Any authority who issued a decision, order, notice, certificate, or document may rectify an error apparent on the face of the record either suo motu, or when brought to notice by a GST officer, or upon an application by the affected person.
  2. Limitation Period:
    • Application by Affected Person: Must be filed within 3 months from the date of issue of the document.
    • Outer Limit for Order: No rectification shall be made after 6 months from the date of issue of the decision/order/document.
  3. Exception to 6-Month Limitation: The 6-month limit does NOT apply where rectification is purely in the nature of correction of a clerical or arithmetical error arising from an accidental slip or omission.
  4. Principles of Natural Justice: An opportunity of being heard must be granted if the proposed rectification adversely affects any person.
ICAI Question 4

Modes of Service of Notice under Section 169

State the various modes of service of a notice, decision, order, summons, or communication under the CGST Act.
Statutory Analysis & Solution:

Under Section 169(1), service can be effected through any of the following 6 statutory modes:

  1. Direct Tender: Personal delivery directly or by messenger/courier to addressee, manager, authorized representative, advocate, employee, or adult resident family member.
  2. Registered / Speed Post: With acknowledgment due to the last known place of business or residence.
  3. Email: To the registered e-mail address provided in the GST registration profile.
  4. Common Portal: Making the document available directly on the dashboard at www.gst.gov.in.
  5. Newspaper Publication: Circulating in the locality of last known residence or business.
  6. Affixture (Substituted Service): Affixing at a conspicuous place at last known business/residence, or on the notice board of the jurisdictional GST office.
ICAI Question 5

Exceptions to Confidentiality of Public Servants

Section 158(1) lays down that information obtained by a public servant from GST records is confidential. Are there any exceptions to this rule?
Statutory Analysis & Solution:

Yes. Section 158(3) contains specific statutory exceptions where disclosure is permitted:

  • Prosecution under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, or GST laws.
  • Carrying out the objectives of the Act to Central/State Government officials.
  • Lawful exercise of process for service of notice or recovery of tax demand.
  • Civil Court proceedings where the Government or GST Authority is a party.
  • Audit of tax receipts or refunds by C&AG or authorized audit officers.
  • Inquiry into official misconduct of GST officers.
  • Enabling Central/State Governments to levy or realize other taxes and duties.
  • Inquiry into charges of professional misconduct against Chartered Accountants (ICAI), Cost Accountants, or Advocates.
  • Appointed IT agencies for automated systems under non-disclosure obligations.
  • In the public interest as notified by the Commissioner.
ICAI Question 6

Scope and Legal Binding Nature of CBIC Circulars

Explain the scope and binding nature of circulars and instructions issued by the Board under Section 168.
Statutory Analysis & Solution:
  1. Statutory Source: Section 168 empowers the Board (CBIC) to issue orders, instructions, or directions to Central tax officers for ensuring uniformity in the implementation of the Act.
  2. Binding on Officers: All officers and persons employed in the execution of the Act are legally bound to follow CBIC circulars.
  3. Not Binding on Assessees: Circulars are not binding on taxpayers; assessees can contest circular interpretations before appellate forums.
  4. Not Binding on Courts: Tribunals and Courts interpret provisions independently of executive circulars.
  5. Statutory Supremacy: Where a circular conflicts with express statutory provisions, the statute prevails over the circular.
ICAI Question 7

Extension of Statutory Time Limits under Section 168A

"The time limits provided under the CGST Act cannot be extended." Do you agree? Give your views with reference to Section 168A.
Statutory Analysis & Solution:
  1. Inaccuracy of Statement: The statement is incorrect. The Government has statutory power to extend limitation periods under Section 168A.
  2. Force Majeure Limitation: Section 168A empowers the Government, on GST Council recommendations, to extend any time limit specified in the Act which cannot be completed or complied with due to force majeure.
  3. Meaning of Force Majeure: Means war, epidemic, flood, drought, fire, cyclone, earthquake, or any natural calamity affecting the implementation of the Act.
  4. Retrospective Power: Such extensions can also be notified with retrospective effect. Outside force majeure conditions, extensions require specific statutory provisions (e.g., Section 168 for returns or Section 143 for job work).
