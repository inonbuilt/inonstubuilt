1. Statutory Valuation Framework (Section 14)

Where customs duties are levied on an ad valorem basis (as a percentage of value), duty is computed on the Assessable Value determined under Section 14 of the Customs Act, 1962. Section 14 provides two alternative statutory benchmarks:

1. Transaction Value [Section 14(1)]

The primary statutory basis: the price actually paid or payable for goods when sold for export to India (or for export from India), for delivery at the time and place of importation/exportation, where buyer and seller are not related and price is the sole consideration.

2. Tariff Value [Section 14(2)]

CBIC is empowered to fix static "tariff values" for specified sensitive commodities (e.g. crude palm oil, brass scrap, poppy seeds, gold, silver) by Official Gazette notification based on international price trends. Tariff values override Section 14(1) transaction value unconditionally.

Five Cumulative Conditions for Acceptance of Transaction Value [Section 14(1)]

  1. Sale for Export: Goods must be sold for export to India.
  2. Time and Place of Delivery: Delivery at the time and place of importation (Indian port/airport).
  3. Unrelated Parties: Buyer and seller must not be related persons under Rule 2(2) (or if related, relationship must not have influenced the price).
  4. Price as Sole Consideration: There must be no other consideration, financial flow, or condition attached.
  5. Rule 10 Additions: Mandatory costs and services (commissions, packing, engineering, royalties, freight, insurance) must be added to the extent not already included in invoice price.

4. Customs Valuation Rules, 2007: Sequential Hierarchy (Rules 3 to 9)

Where the transaction value cannot be determined under Rule 3 (e.g. price influenced by relationship, restrictions on use, indeterminate consideration), the proper officer must proceed sequentially through Rules 4 to 9 in seriatim:

Rule Valuation Method Key Statutory Mechanism & Standards
Rule 3 Transaction Value Price paid/payable adjusted for Rule 10 costs and services. Acceptable between related persons if relationship did not influence price or value approximates test values.
Rule 4 Identical Goods Transaction value of goods same in all respects (quality, physical traits, reputation, same country & producer), imported at or about the same time. If multiple values found, lowest value is adopted.
Rule 5 Similar Goods Transaction value of goods having like characteristics and components enabling them to perform same functions and commercially interchangeable. Lowest value used.
Rule 6 Reversal of Order At the request of the importer and with approval of the proper officer, the order of application of Rule 7 and Rule 8 may be reversed.
Rule 7 Deductive Value Unit price at which imported, identical, or similar goods are sold in India in the greatest aggregate quantity to unrelated buyers, minus: (i) commissions/profits; (ii) transport & insurance within India; (iii) Indian customs duties/taxes.
Rule 8 Computed Value Cost of foreign production/fabrication + usual profit and general expenses for sales to India + Rule 10(2) freight and insurance.
Rule 9 Residual Method Reasonable means consistent with WTO principles on data available in India. Cannot be based on Indian selling price, highest of two values, export price to third countries, or arbitrary values.

Related Persons Criteria [Rule 2(2)]

Persons are deemed related ONLY if:

  • They are officers or directors of one another's businesses; legally recognized partners; employer and employee.
  • Any person directly or indirectly owns, controls, or holds 5% or more of outstanding voting stock of both.
  • One directly or indirectly controls the other (exercising restraint or direction); or both controlled by a third party.
  • They are members of the same family. Sole agents/distributors are deemed related only if falling within these criteria.

6. Rule 10(2): Transportation & Insurance Computation Rules

A. Cost of Transport (Freight) to Place of Importation [Rule 10(2)(a)]

Importation by Sea

Actual freight incurred. If freight is not ascertainable, it shall be taken as 20% of FOB value. (If FOB is unknown, 20% of FOB + Insurance).

Importation by Air

Even if actual air freight is known, it is capped at 20% of FOB value! If actual air freight is less than 20% of FOB, the actual lower amount is taken.

Ship Demurrage & Lighterage Charges Included

As per Explanation to Rule 10(2), the cost of transport includes ship demurrage charges on chartered vessels and lighterage/barge charges incurred at the port of importation to bring cargo from ship to shore.

B. Cost of Insurance to Place of Importation [Rule 10(2)(b)]

  • Actual Insurance: If ascertainable, actual insurance premium paid is added.
  • Not Ascertainable: If insurance cost is unknown, it shall be taken as 1.125% of FOB value. (If FOB is unknown, 1.125% of FOB + Freight).

Landing Charges (1%) OMITTED Completely!

Pursuant to amendments aligned with WTO Valuation Agreements, landing charges (1% loading, unloading, and handling charges at Indian port) have been OMITTED from the Customs Valuation Rules. Under no circumstances should 1% landing charges be added to Assessable Value!

7. Non-Includible Costs (Post-Importation Exclusions)

The following costs must be strictly EXCLUDED from Assessable Value provided they are distinguished from the price paid/payable:

Expense Category Statutory Justification for Exclusion
Buying Commission Specifically exempted under Rule 10(1)(a)(i); paid to buyer's own agent abroad.
Engineering & Design Undertaken in India Excluded under Rule 10(1)(b)(iv); only foreign engineering assists are includible.
Post-Importation Erection & Assembly Charges for construction, erection, assembly, or maintenance undertaken after importation.
Inland Freight within India Freight and insurance incurred from Indian port of entry to Inland Container Depot (ICD) or factory.
Port Demurrage / Airport Storage Demurrage or ground rent paid to Port Trust / Airport authorities for delayed clearance is a post-importation storage cost.
Domestic Taxes & Duties Customs duties, IGST, and local taxes payable in India by reason of import or sale.
Transshipment Costs Transport and insurance for transshipment between two Indian customs stations (e.g. Mumbai to Cochin).

8. Rejection of Declared Value (Rule 12)

When the proper officer has "reason to doubt" the truth or accuracy of the declared value, he may call for further evidence. If doubts persist, transaction value is deemed rejected and re-determined under Rules 4 to 9.

Legitimate Statutory Grounds for Raising Doubts under Rule 12

  • Significantly higher value assessed for identical/similar goods imported at or about the same time.
  • Sale involves abnormal discounts or special discounts limited to exclusive agents.
  • Misdeclaration of quantity, quality, description, country of origin, or year of manufacture.
  • Non-declaration of critical pricing parameters like brand, grade, or technical specifications.
  • Fraudulent or manipulated invoices and documentation.

9. Customs Valuation (Determination of Value of Export Goods) Rules, 2007

  • Rule 3 (Transaction Value): Price actually paid/payable for delivery at time and place of exportation (FOB port of export), non-related, price sole consideration.
  • Rule 4 (Comparison): Transaction value of identical or similar export goods.
  • Rule 5 (Computed Value): Cost of production + general expenses and profit in India.
  • Rule 6 (Residual Method): Reasonable means consistent with general principles.
  • Relevant Exchange Rate: CBIC rate on the date of filing the Shipping Bill under Section 50.

11. Solved ICAI Test Your Knowledge Problems (17 Cases)

ICAI Question 1

Goods of Same Class or Kind & Computed Value

Briefly explain: (i) Goods of the same class or kind; (ii) Computed value, under the Customs Valuation Rules, 2007.
Statutory Definitions:
  • (i) Goods of Same Class or Kind [Rule 2(1)(c)]: Imported goods that fall within a group or range of imported goods produced by a particular industry or industrial sector, and includes identical goods or similar goods.
  • (ii) Computed Value [Rule 8]: Consists of: (a) cost or value of materials and fabrication/processing employed in foreign production; (b) an amount for profit and general expenses usually reflected in sales to India; and (c) Rule 10(2) freight and insurance.
ICAI Questions 2 & 3

Canalizing Agent Charges & Vendor Inspection

Examine validity: (i) Service charges paid to canalizing agent are not includible in assessable value. (ii) Vendor inspection charges on second-hand goods carried out by foreign supplier on his own are not includible.
Statutory Analysis:
  • (i) Statement is INVALID: In high sea sales through a canalizing agent, service charges paid to the agent form part of the total consideration paid for import delivery and are strictly includible in assessable value (*Hyderabad Industries v. UOI*).
  • (ii) Statement is VALID: Vendor inspection charges voluntarily incurred by the foreign supplier for his own satisfaction (not stipulated as a condition of sale by buyer and not required to make goods ready for shipment) are not includible in assessable value.

ICAI Question 10

Warehoused Goods Ex-Bond Clearance (M/s Impex)

Consignment warehoused on 5th June (duty @ 50%). Exemption notification on 1st July reduced duty to 30%. Bill of entry for home consumption filed on 1st August. Department charged 50%.
(i) What is the applicable rate of duty?
(ii) Can the exchange rate on 1st August be adopted?
Statutory Resolution:
  1. (i) Applicable Rate of Duty: Under Section 15(1)(b), the relevant rate of duty for goods cleared from a warehouse is the rate in force on the date on which a Bill of Entry for Home Consumption (Ex-Bond BE) is presented. Therefore, the rate in force on 1st August (30% ad valorem) applies. The Department's demand @ 50% is contrary to law.
  2. (ii) Rate of Exchange: Under Section 14, the exchange rate is determined on the date of presentation of the original Bill of Entry for warehousing under Section 46 (5th June). The exchange rate on 1st August CANNOT be adopted.
