1. Statutory Framework & Business Architecture

Electronic Commerce (E-Commerce) is the digital backbone of contemporary trade. Under GST, two parallel legal mechanisms govern e-commerce transactions to eliminate tax avoidance, guarantee point-of-sale visibility, and capture the digital economic surplus:

Mechanism 1

Section 9(5) CGST / Sec 5(5) IGST: Full Deemed Supplier

The Electronic Commerce Operator (ECO) steps directly into the shoes of the supplier. The ECO is liable to pay the entire GST on notified services supplied through its portal as if the ECO were the actual supplier. The actual supplier does not charge tax.

Mechanism 2

Section 52 CGST: Tax Collection at Source (TCS)

The actual supplier remains liable to pay GST on outward supplies made through the portal. The ECO acts as a tax collector, collecting TCS @ 0.5% (0.25% CGST + 0.25% SGST / 0.5% IGST) on the net value of taxable supplies where consideration is collected by the ECO.

Critical Distinction to Memorize for Exams: Section 9(5) and Section 52 are mutually exclusive for any specific transaction. If a service is notified under Section 9(5) (e.g., Zomato restaurant delivery or Uber ride), the ECO pays 100% tax under Section 9(5) and NO TCS is collected under Section 52. TCS under Section 52 only applies to supplies where the underlying supplier pays the tax.

2. Statutory Definitions (CGST Act, 2017)

Term & Section Statutory Legal Meaning & Scope
Electronic Commerce [Section 2(44)] Means the supply of goods or services or both, including digital products over digital or electronic network (Internet, mobile apps, EDI, web portals).
Electronic Commerce Operator (ECO) [Section 2(45)] Means any person who owns, operates or manages digital or electronic facility or platform for electronic commerce. Includes both resident and foreign operators having digital platforms accessible in India.
Agent [Section 2(5)] Means a person, including a factor, broker, commission agent, arhatia, del credere agent, an auctioneer or any other mercantile agent, who carries on the business of supply or receipt of goods/services on behalf of another. Crucial Note: An ECO acting strictly in an agency capacity where invoices are issued in the agent's name falls under Schedule I para 3 and is treated as actual supplier, outside Section 52.
Aggregate Turnover [Section 2(6)] Aggregates all taxable supplies (excluding RCM inward supplies), exempt supplies, exports, and inter-State supplies of persons having the same PAN on all-India basis, excluding GST and cess. Crucial Exam Rule: Restaurant services supplied through ECOs form part of the restaurant's aggregate turnover, not the ECO's!
Outward Supply [Section 2(83)] Supply of goods/services made or agreed to be made by a person in the course or furtherance of business by sale, transfer, barter, exchange, licence, rental, lease or disposal.
E-Commerce Business Models:
  • Marketplace Model (Amazon, Flipkart, Swiggy): Platform facilitates transactions between independent third-party sellers and buyers. Attracts Section 52 TCS or Section 9(5) Deemed Supplier.
  • Inventory-Based Model (Shoppers Stop own website, Zara.com): The website owner owns the inventory and bills directly to the consumer. This is a direct outward supply under Section 9(1). No TCS arises because goods are sold on own account.
  • Hybrid Model (Flipkart selling third-party goods AND its own private labels): TCS applies exclusively to sales of third-party sellers; own sales attract normal GST with zero TCS.

3. Section 9(5) / 5(5): Tax Payable by ECO on Notified Services

Under Section 9(5) of the CGST Act and Section 5(5) of the IGST Act, the Government has notified 6 specific categories of services where the ECO is statutorily fictioned as the supplier liable to pay tax. All provisions of the GST law apply to such ECO as if he were the supplier liable for paying tax in relation to the supply of such services.

Notified Service Category Scope & Conditions Liability of ECO vs Actual Supplier
1. Passenger Transportation
(Notfn 17/2017-CT(R) item (i))		 Transportation of passengers by a radio-taxi, motorcab, maxicab, motorcycle, or any other motor vehicle EXCEPT omnibus. (e.g., Uber, Ola, Rapido). ECO Liable in ALL Cases
Regardless of whether the actual driver/cab owner is registered or unregistered.
2. Hotel & Lodging Accommodation
(Notfn 17/2017-CT(R) item (ii))		 Providing accommodation in hotels, inns, guest houses, clubs, campsites or other commercial places meant for residential/lodging purposes. Conditional
ECO liable ONLY IF the actual hotelier is NOT liable for registration under Section 22(1). If hotel is registered, hotel itself charges GST; ECO collects TCS u/s 52.
3. Housekeeping Services
(Notfn 17/2017-CT(R) item (iii))		 Plumbing, carpentering, cleaning, painting, appliance repair, etc. (e.g., Urban Company). Conditional
ECO liable ONLY IF the service provider is NOT liable for registration under Section 22(1). If service provider is registered, provider pays GST; ECO collects TCS u/s 52.
4. Restaurant Services
(Notfn 17/2017-CT(R) item (iv))		 Supply of restaurant service OTHER THAN services supplied by restaurants, eating joints etc. located at "specified premises". (e.g., Swiggy, Zomato). ECO Liable in ALL Cases

6. Local Delivery Services
(Notfn 17/2017-CT(R) item (vi) - Recent)		 Services by way of local intra-city pickup and delivery. Conditional
ECO is liable EXCEPT where the person supplying such service through ECO is liable for registration under Section 22(1).
What is "Specified Premises"? (Crucial for Restaurant Taxation): "Specified premises" means premises from where the supplier provides hotel accommodation service having declared tariff of any unit of accommodation above â‚¹7,500 per unit per day (or equivalent) in the current or preceding financial year (e.g., 5-star luxury hotels like Taj, Marriott).
Rule: If a restaurant inside Taj Hotel sells food on Zomato, Taj Hotel pays GST @ 18%. Zomato does NOT pay tax under Section 9(5); Zomato collects TCS under Section 52! For all standalone restaurants, cloud kitchens, and budget hotels (≤ â‚¹7,500), Zomato pays GST @ 5% under Section 9(5).

Person Liable to Pay Tax under Section 9(5) - Hierarchy:

  • Case A: If the ECO has a physical presence in the taxable territory → The ECO itself.
  • Case B: If the ECO does NOT have a physical presence in the taxable territory → Any person representing such ECO for any purpose in the taxable territory.
  • Case C: If the ECO has neither physical presence nor a representative in the taxable territory → The ECO shall appoint a person in the taxable territory for paying tax and such person shall be liable.

4. Restaurant Services through ECO (CBIC Circular No. 167/23/2021-GST)

The CBIC issued comprehensive clarifications regarding the operational and legal modalities when restaurant services are supplied through e-commerce operators (e.g., Swiggy, Zomato):

Reporting & Mode of Payment

Mandatory Cash Payment - NO ITC!

Liability under Section 9(5) cannot be discharged by utilizing Input Tax Credit (ITC). Tax on notified restaurant services MUST be paid in 100% CASH through Electronic Cash Ledger. The ECO is not the recipient of restaurant service; hence, no ITC can be claimed or used.

Reporting in GSTR-3B

Separate Reporting Tables

ECO reports notified restaurant supplies in Table 3.1.1(i) of Form GSTR-3B as outward taxable supplies and pays tax. The actual restaurant reports such supplies in Table 3.1.1(ii) (supplies made through ECO u/s 9(5)) and pays NIL tax on them.

Aspect Clarified Legal Position under Circular No. 167/23/2021-GST
TCS Applicability ECOs are NOT required to collect TCS under Section 52 and will not furnish Form GSTR-8 for restaurant services.
Separate Registration ECOs already registered under GST do NOT require separate registration for paying tax on restaurant services under Section 9(5).
Unregistered Restaurants ECO is liable to pay GST even if the restaurant is completely unregistered under GST.
Aggregate Turnover Computation The value of restaurant services supplied through ECO SHALL be included in the Aggregate Turnover of the restaurant [under Section 2(6)] for determining threshold limit (& â‚¹20L/â‚¹40L) and composition limit.
Invoicing for Mixed Orders Where a customer places an order containing both restaurant services (pizza) and goods/services other than restaurant services (packed soft drinks/ice cream billed on MRP by manufacturer):
• ECO issues invoice for the restaurant service.
• Restaurant/seller issues separate invoice for the other goods. ECO collects TCS u/s 52 on the other goods.
E-Invoicing Applicability E-invoicing is not applicable to ECOs for restaurant services since the ECO is not an invoice issuer in normal terms, but the restaurant (if turnover > â‚¹5 Cr) must comply for supplies where it issues invoices.

5. Section 52: Tax Collection at Source (TCS) Mechanism

Section 52 mandates every Electronic Commerce Operator (not being an agent) to collect tax at source from the supplier on taxable supplies made through its digital platform where consideration is collected by the operator.

Statutory Formula for Net Value of Taxable Supplies [Section 52(1)]:
$$\text{Net Value of Taxable Supplies} = \text{Aggregate Value of Taxable Supplies of Goods/Services (other than Sec 9(5) notified services)} - \text{Aggregate Value of Taxable Supplies Returned during the month}$$

• Calculated on a monthly basis per registered supplier (GSTIN-wise).
• Excludes central tax, State tax, UT tax, integrated tax, and cess.
• Excludes supplies on which recipient pays tax on reverse charge basis.
• Excludes completely exempt supplies.

Updated Rate (Notfn 15/2024-CT)

Current TCS Rates

Intra-State Supplies: 0.25% CGST + 0.25% SGST/UTGST = 0.50% Total
Inter-State Supplies: 0.50% IGST
(Reduced from the earlier 1% rate to lower compliance burden and enhance working capital liquidity for MSMEs).

Remittance & Credit

10th Day Deposit & Cash Ledger Credit

TCS collected during a month must be paid to the Government by the 10th of the succeeding month. Deposited amounts reflect in the Electronic Cash Ledger of the respective suppliers upon acceptance of GSTR-2X, which can be used to settle tax liabilities or claimed as refund u/s 54(1).

Non-Applicability of Section 52 TCS (Exclusions to Remember):
  1. Sale of Own Goods/Services: Selling products on own website (e.g., Nike selling shoes on nike.com; Sitcom Technologies buying and reselling in own name).
  2. Notified Services under Section 9(5): Passenger transport, hotels, housekeeping, restaurants through ECO.
  3. Exempt Supplies: Supply of alcoholic liquor, unprocessed fresh agricultural produce, etc.
  4. Reverse Charge Supplies: Supplies where recipient is liable under Section 9(3) (e.g., GTA services).
  5. Direct Collection of Consideration: Where payment is made directly by buyer to seller (e.g., cash on delivery directly handed to seller's own courier without ECO handling funds, or hotel paid at check-in).
  6. Agent under Section 2(5): Where ECO acts as a commission agent issuing invoices in its own name under Schedule I.

6. ONDC & Multiple ECO Transactions (CBIC Circular No. 194/06/2023-GST)

With the advent of the Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC), e-commerce architectures often involve multiple operators in a single supply chain: a Buyer-side ECO (providing interface to the consumer) and a Seller-side ECO (aggregating and listing the seller). CBIC issued Circular No. 194/06/2023-GST to definitively settle who collects TCS:

Transaction Scenario Operational Flow Who Must Collect TCS under Section 52?
Scenario 1: Seller-side ECO is NOT the Supplier
(Buyer → Buyer ECO → Seller ECO → Supplier)		 Buyer pays Buyer-side ECO. Buyer-side ECO deducts commission and remits balance to Seller-side ECO. Seller-side ECO deducts commission and pays actual supplier. Seller-side ECO
The ECO who finally releases payment to the supplier is liable to collect TCS and furnish Form GSTR-8. Buyer-side ECO has NO TCS obligation.
Scenario 2: Seller-side ECO IS the Supplier
(Buyer → Buyer ECO → Supplier who is itself an ECO)		 Buyer pays Buyer-side ECO. Buyer-side ECO deducts commission and directly remits balance to the supplier (who is an ECO selling own products). Buyer-side ECO
Since the supplier is receiving payment directly from the Buyer-side ECO, the Buyer-side ECO must collect TCS while releasing payment to the supplier.
Visualising ONDC TCS Responsibility:
$$\text{Rule of Thumb: } \mathbf{\text{Whichever entity cuts the final cheque to the underlying seller collects TCS!}}$$

7. Procedural Relaxations for Small & Unregistered Sellers

Previously, Section 24(ix) required compulsory registration for ANY person selling through an ECO liable for TCS, creating an unfair barrier for micro-entrepreneurs. The GST Council introduced special procedures under Section 148:

Notification 34/2023 & 37/2023-CT

Unregistered Persons Supplying Goods

Persons supplying goods through an ECO having aggregate turnover within Section 22(1) threshold (≤ â‚¹20L or â‚¹40L) are exempted from mandatory registration subject to conditions:

  • Intra-State supply only (No inter-State supplies allowed).
  • Supplier must obtain a PAN-based Enrolment Number on the GST portal.
  • ECO shall NOT collect TCS on supplies by such persons.
  • ECO must report details in Table 3.1 & Table 4.1 of Form GSTR-8.
Notification 36/2023-CT

Composition Dealers Supplying Goods

Composition dealers under Section 10 are allowed to supply goods through an ECO (Notification 36/2023-CT) subject to safeguards:

  • ECO must ensure no inter-State supply is made by composition dealers.
  • ECO MUST collect TCS @ 0.5% under Section 52(1).
  • ECO reports such supplies in Form GSTR-8.
  • Note: Composition dealers CANNOT supply services through ECO [Section 10(2)(d)].
Service Providers Exemption (Notification No. 65/2017-CT): Persons supplying services (other than Section 9(5) notified services) through an ECO are exempt from compulsory registration under Section 24(ix) if their aggregate turnover does not exceed â‚¹20 Lakhs (â‚¹10 Lakhs in Special Category States). Because such small service providers are not liable to register, ECOs are NOT required to collect TCS on their supplies!

10. Master Comparative Summary: Section 9(5) vs Section 52

Parameter Section 9(5) - Deemed Supplier Section 52 - Tax Collection at Source
Legal Status of ECO Treated as the deemed supplier liable for 100% GST. Acts merely as a tax collector at source.
Scope of Supplies Only 6 specifically notified services (passenger transport, accommodation, housekeeping, restaurant, omnibus, delivery). All taxable goods and non-notified services where consideration is collected by ECO.
Rate of Tax Collected/Paid Full applicable GST rate (e.g., 5%, 12%, 18%). 0.5% Total (0.25% CGST + 0.25% SGST or 0.5% IGST) on net value.
Use of ITC to Pay Liability PROHIBITED. Must be paid 100% in CASH. TCS must be deposited in cash; actual supplier can use ITC for its own liability.
Reporting Return Reported in Form GSTR-3B (Table 3.1.1(i)). Reported in Form GSTR-8 (Monthly) & GSTR-9B (Annual).
Credit to Actual Supplier Not applicable (Supplier does not pay tax). Credited to supplier's Electronic Cash Ledger.

ICAI TYK 2 (TCS Rate Composition)

Question:

Whether the rate of tax of 0.5% notified under Section 52 is CGST or SGST or a combination of both CGST and SGST?

Answer:

As per Notification No. 15/2024-Central Tax and Notification No. 01/2024-Integrated Tax, the rate of TCS notified under Section 52(1) is:

  • Intra-State Supplies: 0.25% under the CGST Act, with an equal 0.25% under the respective SGST/UTGST Act, aggregating to an effective total rate of 0.50%.
  • Inter-State Supplies: 0.50% under the IGST Act.
ICAI TYK 3 (Universal Collection Duty)

Question:

Is every e-commerce operator required to collect tax on behalf of the actual supplier?

Answer:

Yes, every e-commerce operator (not being an agent) is required to collect TCS under Section 52 where consideration in respect of the supply made through it is collected by the operator.

However, NO TCS is required to be collected in the following specific circumstances:

  1. On supply of services notified under Section 9(5) of the CGST Act (since the ECO is itself the deemed supplier paying 100% tax).
  2. On wholly exempt supplies.
  3. On supplies where the recipient is liable to pay tax under reverse charge basis (RCM) under Section 9(3).
  4. Where the ECO does not collect the consideration for the supply.
  5. Where suppliers sell their own goods/services through their own website.
  6. On supplies made by unregistered persons exempted under Notification No. 34/2023-CT / Notification No. 65/2017-CT.
ICAI TYK 4 (Own Website vs Marketplace)

Question:

State whether the provisions pertaining to tax collected at source under Section 52 will be applicable in the below-mentioned scenarios:

(a) Fitan sells watches on its own through its own website.
(b) ABC Limited, who is a dealer of Fitan brand, sells watches through Slipkart, an electronic commerce operator.

Answer:

  • Scenario (a): As per Section 52(1), TCS is collected on supplies made through an ECO by other suppliers. Where a person sells its own products through its own website, it is a supply on own account under Section 9(1). Therefore, TCS provisions are NOT applicable. Fitan will charge normal GST on outward supply.
  • Scenario (b): Here, ABC Limited is an independent third-party supplier making supplies of watches through the electronic platform of Slipkart (an ECO), and consideration is collected by Slipkart. Therefore, Section 52 TCS provisions ARE applicable. Slipkart is statutorily required to collect TCS on the net value of taxable supplies made by ABC Limited.
ICAI TYK 5 (ECO Acting as Agent)

Question:

A is an electronic commerce operator who supplies goods through its electronic portal in the capacity of an agent of the supplier. Whether TCS provisions under Section 52 shall apply?

Answer:

Section 52(1) explicitly states: "every electronic commerce operator, not being an agent, shall collect an amount calculated at such rate...".

Where the ECO acts as an agent of the supplier in terms of Section 2(5) (where invoices are issued by the agent in its own name under Schedule I, Para 3), the agent is deemed to be the supplier of the goods. Consequently, the liability to collect TCS under Section 52 shall NOT arise in this case; instead, normal GST provisions apply to the agent as supplier.

ICAI TYK 6 (Direct Payment to Hotel)

Question:

X books a hotel room in Hotel Grand through a web portal 'Yatra.com'. The entire room tariff amount was payable at the hotel at the time of check-in. Whether TCS provisions shall apply in the present case?

Answer:

Under Section 52(1), TCS is required to be collected by an ECO on supplies made through it "where the consideration with respect to such supplies is to be collected by the operator".

In the present case, Yatra.com does not collect any consideration from X. The entire consideration is paid directly by customer X to Hotel Grand at the time of check-in. Therefore, the essential statutory condition of collecting consideration by the ECO is not satisfied, and TCS provisions under Section 52 shall NOT apply.

ICAI TYK 7 (Multi-ECO / ONDC Network)

Question:

Sumitra Nandan books a Hotel â€“ Hillpoint Residency (registered under GST) via Zitcom Technologies Ltd. (an ECO) who in turn is integrated with another ECO â€“ Techsuper Ltd. who has an agreement with Hillpoint Residency. You are required to determine who is required to collect TCS in the given case.

Answer:

This transaction represents a multiple e-commerce operator model (such as ONDC), where Buyer-side ECO (Zitcom Technologies Ltd.) interfaces with the customer and Seller-side ECO (Techsuper Ltd.) interfaces with the actual supplier (Hillpoint Residency).

As clarified by CBIC Circular No. 194/06/2023-GST dated 17.07.2023:

  • In a multiple ECO setup where the supplier-side ECO is not the supplier itself, the compliance under Section 52 including collection of TCS shall be done by the supplier-side ECO who finally releases the payment to the supplier for the supply made through it.
  • Buyer-side ECO does not have the GST details of the supplier and will not collect TCS.

Conclusion: In the given case, Techsuper Ltd. (Seller-side ECO) is releasing the payment to Hillpoint Residency; hence, Techsuper Ltd. is required to collect TCS under Section 52 and file Form GSTR-8.

ICAI TYK 8 (Housekeeping via ECO & Registration)

Question:

AB Pvt. Ltd., Pune, Maharashtra, provides housekeeping services. The company supplies its services exclusively through an e-commerce website owned and managed by Hi-Tech Indya Pvt. Ltd., Pune. The turnover of AB Pvt. Ltd. in the current financial year is â‚¹18 Lakhs. Advise AB Pvt. Ltd. as to whether it is required to obtain GST registration.

Answer:

1. Statutory Legal Provisions:

  • Under Section 22(1), a supplier of services is liable for registration in Maharashtra if its aggregate turnover exceeds â‚¹20 Lakhs in a financial year.
  • Section 24(ix) mandates compulsory registration for persons supplying goods or services through an ECO who is required to collect TCS u/s 52. However, this is subject to an express carve-out: "other than supplies specified under sub-section (5) of section 9".
  • Under Notification No. 17/2017-Central Tax (Rate) issued under Section 9(5), services by way of housekeeping (such as plumbing, carpentering, cleaning) supplied through an ECO are notified services on which the ECO is liable to pay tax, EXCEPT where the person supplying such service through ECO is liable for registration under Section 22(1).

2. Analysis of the Present Case:

  • The aggregate turnover of AB Pvt. Ltd. is â‚¹18 Lakhs, which is below the statutory threshold limit of â‚¹20 Lakhs under Section 22(1).
  • Since AB Pvt. Ltd. is not liable for registration under Section 22(1), its housekeeping service falls squarely under the Section 9(5) notification.
  • Consequently, the ECO (Hi-Tech Indya Pvt. Ltd.) is statutorily liable to pay GST on such services under Section 9(5).
  • Since tax is payable by the ECO under Section 9(5), the mandatory registration requirement under Section 24(ix) does not apply to AB Pvt. Ltd.

Conclusion: AB Pvt. Ltd. is NOT required to obtain GST registration. Hi-Tech Indya Pvt. Ltd. will pay the tax directly in cash under Section 9(5).
