Liability to Pay Tax in Certain Cases

Comprehensive Master Revision Notes: CGST Chapter XVI (Sections 85 to 94), Joint and Several Liabilities in Business Transfers, Retrospective Distinction in Corporate Amalgamations, Liquidator 30-Day Notification Modality, Piercing the Corporate Veil for Private Company Directors, Partnership Firm Retirements & The 1-Month Rule, Deceased Persons & Limited Estate Liabilities, HUF Partition, and All Solved ICAI Test Your Knowledge Problems.

1. Statutory Architecture & Scope

In the normal course of commerce, tax liability attaches directly to the registered taxable person who effects taxable supplies. However, complex commercial eventsâ€”such as transfers of going concerns, corporate mergers, liquidations, deaths, partnership retirements, and trust dissolutionsâ€”threaten to frustrate tax recovery. Chapter XVI of the CGST Act [Sections 85 to 94] specifies statutory representative and vicarious liabilities to guarantee that government tax dues cannot be evaded through restructuring or dissolution.

Relationship with Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC): Sections 88, 93, and 94 are explicitly enacted with the opening phrase: "Save as otherwise provided in the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016". Where resolution or liquidation proceedings are initiated under the IBC, the statutory waterfall under Section 53 of the IBC overrides GST recovery claims.

2. Liability in Case of Transfer of Business [Section 85]

Where a taxable person liable to pay tax transfers his business in whole or in partâ€”by sale, gift, lease, leave and license, hire, or in any other manner (including death of sole proprietor):

Extent of Transfer Period of Liability Allocation of Tax, Interest & Penalty
Transfer as a Whole (100% Concern) Taxes due up to the date of transfer (whether determined before or after). Transferor and Transferee are JOINTLY AND SEVERALLY LIABLE.
Transfer as a Whole (Post-Transfer Period) Taxes due after the date of transfer. Transferee is SOLELY LIABLE. (Must obtain new GST registration u/s 22(3)).
Transfer in Part Taxes due up to the date of transfer. Transferor and Transferee are JOINTLY AND SEVERALLY LIABLE only to the extent of tax/dues attributable to the transferred portion.
Rule 41 Transfer of ITC

Transfer of Unutilized ITC

Upon transfer of business with specific provision for transfer of liabilities, the unutilized ITC in Electronic Credit Ledger can be transferred to the transferee via Form GST ITC-02, duly certified by a practicing CA/CMA.

Exemption on Going Concern

Slump Sale GST Exemption

Services by way of transfer of a going concern, as a whole or an independent part thereof, is wholly exempt from GST under Notification No. 12/2017-CT(Rate).

3. Liability of Agent and Principal [Section 86]

Where an agent supplies or receives any taxable goods on behalf of his principal:

$$\mathbf{\text{Principal and Agent are JOINTLY AND SEVERALLY LIABLE}} \text{ for the payment of tax on such goods!}$$
  • The Department can proceed against either the principal or the agent, or both concurrently, for recovering tax dues on supplies handled through the agency arrangement.
  • Applies to mercantile agents, commission agents, del credere agents, and consignment agents acting in terms of Section 2(5) and Schedule I Para 3.

4. Amalgamation or Merger of Companies [Section 87]

Under corporate law, an amalgamation or merger sanctioned by an order of the High Court, National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), or Central Government takes effect retrospectively from an "Appointed Date". Section 87 contains a critical tax carve-out overriding this retrospective fiction:

Retrospective Separation Rule [Section 87(1)]: Notwithstanding that the court/NCLT order takes effect from an earlier appointed date, the amalgamating companies SHALL BE TREATED AS TWO DISTINCT COMPANIES up to the date of the order of the Court / NCLT.
• All supplies of goods or services between the two companies made between the appointed date and the order date SHALL BE INCLUDED IN OUTWARD SUPPLIES AND TAXED TO GST!
• The separate GST registrations of the amalgamating companies shall be cancelled only with effect from the date of the order [Section 87(2)].

5. Company in Liquidation [Section 88]

When any company is being wound up under court/Tribunal orders or otherwise, Section 88 lays down strict procedural timelines for the liquidator and Commissioner:

Step / Actor Statutory Mandate Strict Legal Timeline
1. Liquidator / Receiver Must intimate his appointment to the Commissioner of Central Tax in writing. Within 30 DAYS of appointment [Sec 88(1)].
2. Commissioner of Central Tax Must notify the liquidator of the amount sufficient to provide for any tax, interest, or penalty payable or likely to become payable by the company. Within 3 MONTHS from the date of receipt of intimation [Sec 88(2)].
3. Directors of Private Company Where tax dues cannot be recovered from the wound-up private company, every director during the relevant period is jointly and severally liable. Unless director proves absence of gross neglect, misfeasance, or breach of duty [Sec 88(3)].

6. Liability of Directors of Private Company [Section 89]

Section 89 pierces the corporate veil to hold directors of private companies personally liable for unrecovered GST debts:

General Rule [Sec 89(1)]

Joint & Several Liability

Where any tax, interest, or penalty due from a private company cannot be recovered, every person who was a director of the company during the period for which the tax was due shall be jointly and severally liable.

Safe Harbour Defense

Absence of Gross Neglect

A director can escape personal liability ONLY IF he proves to the satisfaction of the Commissioner that the non-recovery cannot be attributed to any gross neglect, misfeasance, or breach of duty on his part.

Conversion into Public Company [Section 89(2)]: Where a private company gets converted into a public company, directors are IMMUNE from personal liability for the pre-conversion period taxes of the company!
Exception: Any personal penalty imposed on the director individually under the Act continues to be enforceable.

7. Partners of Firm & Retiring Partner [Section 90]

Section 90 regulates partnership firms (including Limited Liability Partnerships (LLPs)):

Partnership Firm

Joint & Several Liability

Where any firm is liable to pay any tax, interest, or penalty, the firm and EACH of the partners shall be jointly and severally liable for such payment, notwithstanding any contract to the contrary.

Retiring Partner

The Mandatory 1-Month Rule

A retiring partner must intimate the date of his retirement to the Commissioner in writing. He is liable for taxes due up to his retirement date.

Crucial 1-Month Intimation Rule for Retiring Partners [Proviso to Section 90]: If the retiring partner (or the firm) FAILS to intimate the retirement to the Commissioner within 1 MONTH from the date of retirement:
$$\mathbf{\text{The liability of the retiring partner SHALL CONTINUE until the date on which such intimation is received!}}$$ Even if the tax liability arose months after his physical retirement, he remains personally liable until formal intimation is served!

8. Representative Assessees: Guardians, Trustees & Court of Wards

Governing Section Class of Representative Scope of Tax Liability
Section 91 Guardian, Trustee, or Agent of Minor or Incapacitated Person Tax, interest, or penalty shall be levied upon and recoverable from such guardian, trustee, or agent to the same extent as would be recoverable from the minor or incapacitated person if of full age and capacity.
Section 92 Court of Wards, Administrator-General, Official Trustee, Receiver/Manager Where business is conducted under court custody/receivership, tax shall be levied upon and recoverable from such receiver to the same extent as if the business were conducted by the owner.

9. Special Provisions: Death, HUF Partition & Dissolution [Section 93]

Death: Continued

Business Continued [Sec 93(1)(a)]

If the business is continued after the person's death by his legal representative or any other person, such legal representative or successor is liable for all tax, interest, or penalty.

Death: Discontinued

Business Closed [Sec 93(1)(b)]

If business is discontinued, the legal representative is liable to pay out of the estate of the deceased, ONLY to the extent to which the estate is capable of meeting the charge.

HUF Partition

Partition of HUF [Sec 93(2)]

Karta and every adult member of the HUF are jointly and severally liable for tax due up to the partition date, whether determined before or after partition.

Other Section 93 Cases:

  • Dissolution of Firm [Sec 93(3)]: Every partner at the time of dissolution is jointly and severally liable for dues up to dissolution date.
  • Termination of Trust / Guardianship [Sec 93(4)]: The ward or beneficiary becomes directly liable for all tax, interest, or penalty due up to termination date.

10. Discontinuation of Business by Firm, AOP or HUF [Section 94]

Under Section 94, where a firm, Association of Persons (AOP), or HUF discontinues business:

  • Tax, interest, or penalty up to the date of discontinuance shall be determined as if no such discontinuance had taken place [Section 94(1)].
  • Every person who was a partner/member at the time of discontinuance is jointly and severally liable.
  • Reconstitution [Section 94(2)]: In case of change in constitution, partners/members as existing before and after reconstitution are jointly and severally liable for pre-reconstitution dues.

11. ICAI Test Your Knowledge (TYK) - Complete Solved Problems

ICAI TYK 1 (Transfer of Business Joint Liability)

Question:

Explain the liability to pay tax in case of transfer of business under Section 85 of the CGST Act.

Answer:

Under Section 85(1) of the CGST Act, 2017, where a taxable person liable to pay tax transfers his business in whole or in part by sale, gift, lease, leave and license, hire or in any other manner:

  • Pre-Transfer Liability: The transferor and the transferee shall jointly and severally be liable to pay the tax, interest, or penalty determined under the Act on such business up to the time of such transfer, whether such tax, interest, or penalty has been determined before such transfer or is determined thereafter.
  • Post-Transfer Liability: Where the transferee carries on such business, he shall be solely liable to pay tax on supplies effected by him from the date of transfer and must obtain a fresh registration under Section 22(3).
  • Part Transfer: In case of transfer in part, the transferee is jointly and severally liable only to the extent of tax/dues attributable to the transferred portion.
ICAI TYK 2 (Principal and Agent Tax Liability)

Question:

Determine the liability of an agent and a principal to pay tax under Section 86.

Answer:

As per Section 86 of the CGST Act, 2017:

Where an agent supplies or receives any taxable goods on behalf of his principal, such agent and his principal shall be jointly and severally liable for the payment of tax on such goods or services or both under this Act.

The tax department is legally entitled to proceed against the agent or the principal, or both simultaneously, to recover the full tax liability arising out of goods traded through the agent.

ICAI TYK 3 (Corporate Amalgamation Order Date Rule)

Question:

Explain the liability in case of amalgamation or merger of companies under Section 87.

Answer:

Under Section 87 of the CGST Act, 2017:

  1. Distinct Entities Rule [Section 87(1)]: When two or more companies are amalgamated or merged in pursuance of an order of court or Tribunal or otherwise, and the order is to take effect from an earlier date (appointed date), then the transactions of supply of goods or services between the said companies between the appointed date and the date of the order shall be treated as supplies between two distinct companies.
  2. Taxability: The companies shall be liable to charge and pay GST on supplies between each other as distinct entities up to the date of the final order.
  3. Cancellation of Registration [Section 87(2)]: The registration certificates of the amalgamating companies shall be cancelled only with effect from the date of the order of the court or Tribunal.
ICAI TYK 4 (Company in Liquidation Procedure)

Question:

Describe the statutory provisions relating to a company in liquidation under Section 88.

Answer:

Under Section 88 of the CGST Act, 2017:

  • Intimation by Liquidator [Sec 88(1)]: Every person appointed as liquidator/receiver of assets of a company being wound up shall, within 30 days after his appointment, give written intimation of his appointment to the Commissioner.
  • Determination by Commissioner [Sec 88(2)]: The Commissioner shall, after inquiry, notify the liquidator within 3 months from receipt of intimation, the amount sufficient to provide for any tax, interest, or penalty payable or likely to become payable.
  • Directors' Joint and Several Liability [Sec 88(3)]: When a private company is wound up and tax dues cannot be recovered, every person who was a director at any time during the period for which tax was due shall be jointly and severally liable, unless he proves to the satisfaction of the Commissioner that non-recovery was not due to gross neglect, misfeasance, or breach of duty on his part.
ICAI TYK 5 (Private Company Directors Personal Liability)

Question:

Explain the liability of directors of a private company under Section 89.

Answer:

As per Section 89 of the CGST Act, 2017:

  • Joint and Several Liability [Section 89(1)]: Notwithstanding anything contained in the Companies Act, 2013, where any tax, interest, or penalty due from a private company in respect of any supply for any period cannot be recovered, every person who was a director during that period shall, jointly and severally, be liable for payment of such dues.
  • Defense: A director is not liable if he proves to the satisfaction of the Commissioner that non-recovery cannot be attributed to any gross neglect, misfeasance, or breach of duty on his part.
  • Conversion to Public Company [Section 89(2)]: Where a private company is converted into a public company, directors are not liable for pre-conversion unrecovered corporate dues. However, personal penalties on such directors remain enforceable.
ICAI TYK 6 (Meaning of Firm & Partner)

Question:

Whether a Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) is treated as a firm for the purposes of Chapter XVI?

Answer:

Yes. As per the Explanation to Chapter XVI of the CGST Act, 2017:

"A Limited Liability Partnership formed and registered under the provisions of the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008 shall also be considered as a firm."

Consequently, all provisions governing partnership firms and partners under Section 90 and Section 94 apply with equal force to an LLP and its partners.

ICAI TYK 7 (Tax Liability on Death of Taxable Person)

Question:

Discuss the liability to pay tax in case of the death of a taxable person under Section 93(1).

Answer:

Under Section 93(1) of the CGST Act, save as otherwise provided in the IBC 2016, where a person liable to pay tax dies:

  1. Where Business is Continued [Sec 93(1)(a)]: If the business is continued after his death by his legal representative or any other person, such legal representative or other person shall be liable to pay the tax, interest, or penalty due from such deceased person under the Act.
  2. Where Business is Discontinued [Sec 93(1)(b)]: If the business is discontinued (whether before or after death), his legal representative shall be liable to pay out of the estate of the deceased, ONLY to the extent to which the estate is capable of meeting the charge, the tax, interest, or penalty due, whether determined before or after death.
ICAI TYK 8 (Partners of Firm & Retiring Partner Liability)

Question:

Section 90 explains the liability of partners of a firm to pay tax. Detail the provisions relating to a retiring partner.

Answer:

Under Section 90 of the CGST Act, 2017:

  • Firm & Partners: The firm and each partner shall, jointly and severally, be liable to pay any tax, interest, or penalty of the firm.
  • Retiring Partner: Where any partner retires from the firm, he or the firm shall intimate the date of retirement to the Commissioner by a written notice. Such partner shall be liable to pay tax, interest, or penalty due up to the date of his retirement, whether determined or not on that date.
  • The 1-Month Rule: If NO such intimation is given within 1 month from the date of retirement, the liability of such partner shall continue until the date on which such intimation is actually received by the Commissioner.
ICAI TYK 9 (Summarize Section 88 Liquidation Provisions)

Question:

Summarize the core duties of a liquidator and the powers of the Commissioner under Section 88.

Answer:

  1. Liquidator must give written notice of appointment to Commissioner within 30 days.
  2. Commissioner ascertains and notifies tax/penalty amount within 3 months of receiving intimation.
  3. Liquidator must set aside assets/funds to satisfy the notified amount.
  4. Directors of wound-up private companies are jointly and severally liable unless gross neglect is disproved.
ICAI TYK 10 (Failure of Retiring Partner to Intimate)

Question:

What are the consequences if a retiring partner fails to intimate his retirement to the Commissioner within 1 month?

Answer:

Under the proviso to Section 90 of the CGST Act, 2017:

If neither the retiring partner nor the firm intimates the retirement to the Commissioner within 1 month from the date of retirement, the statutory joint and several liability of the retiring partner for any tax, interest, or penalty of the firm shall continue to run until the date on which the intimation is actually received by the Commissioner.

Therefore, the partner continues to remain legally liable for debts incurred by the firm even after his physical departure until formal intimation is served on the tax department.
