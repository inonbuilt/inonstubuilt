Job Work under GST

Comprehensive Master Revision Notes: CGST Sections 143 & 19, Meaning & Scope of Job Work as Supply of Services (Schedule II Para 3), Delivery Challan Rule 55 & Form GST ITC-04 Compliance, Statutory 1-Year / 3-Year Return Windows & Extensions, Retrospective Deemed Supply Consequence, Perpetual Exemption for Moulds & Dies, Direct Clearance from Job Worker's Premises, and Step-by-Step Solved ICAI Test Your Knowledge Problems.

1. Statutory Architecture & Commercial Role

Job work is an essential feature of modern manufacturing and industrial subcontracting. Under GST, special procedural concessions and facilities are provided under Sections 143 and 19 of the CGST Act to allow seamless movement of intermediate goods without tax cascading while placing compliance responsibility on the principal manufacturer.

Concept

Tax-Free Goods Movement

A registered person (Principal) can dispatch inputs or capital goods to a job worker without payment of GST under a Delivery Challan. The principal continues to enjoy full Input Tax Credit (ITC) without any reversal.

Accountability

Principal Holds Core Tax Liability

The principal remains accountable for bringing back the processed goods within statutory timelines or clearing them directly from the job worker's premises on payment of tax or for export.

2. Statutory Meaning of Job Work [Section 2(68)]

Section 2(68) Definition:
"Job work means any treatment or process undertaken by a person on goods belonging to another registered person."
1. Nature of Supply

Supply of Services

Under Schedule II, Para 3 of the CGST Act, "any treatment or process which is applied to another person's goods is a supply of services." Hence, job work is legally a service, taxed at 12% or 18%.

2. Ownership of Goods

Remains with Principal

Ownership and title of goods never pass to the job worker. The job worker merely holds temporary custody for carrying out specific industrial operations (machining, dyeing, coating, assembling).

3. Use of Own Material

Circular 38/12/2018-GST

A job worker can use its own goods/materials (e.g., thread, paint, metal frames, buttons) in addition to goods sent by the principal. The transaction remains a job work service!

Crucial Precondition: Principal MUST be Registered! If goods belong to an unregistered person, any process undertaken by a contractor is NOT job work under Section 2(68); it is treated as a normal supply of goods/services between two independent parties.

3. Statutory Job Work Procedure [Section 143(1)]

Section 143(1) allows a registered principal to send inputs or capital goods, without payment of tax, to a job worker and from there to another job worker:

Permissible Goods Flow Governing Condition & Document Tax Consequence
Principal → Job Worker (JW 1) Covered by Delivery Challan issued by Principal [Rule 55]. No GST Payable
Supplier → Directly to Job Worker (JW 1) Supplier issues invoice mentioning Principal as buyer and JW as consignee. Principal issues Delivery Challan. No GST Payable (Principal claims full ITC).
Job Worker 1 → Job Worker 2 Endorsed Delivery Challan or fresh challan issued by JW 1. No GST Payable
Job Worker → Back to Principal Delivery Challan issued by Job Worker. No GST on Goods; JW charges GST on job charges invoice.
Job Worker → Direct Supply to Final Buyer Principal issues Tax Invoice; goods cleared from JW premises. Principal pays GST (or zero-rated if exported).

4. Input Tax Credit on Job Work Goods [Section 19]

Section 19 contains specialized rules governing ITC availment by the principal on inputs and capital goods dispatched for job work:

Section 19(1) & 19(2)

ITC on Inputs Sent for Job Work

The principal is entitled to take ITC on inputs sent to a job worker. The principal can take credit even if the inputs are directly sent to a job worker from the supplier's premises without being first brought to the principal's registered place of business.

Section 19(4) & 19(5)

ITC on Capital Goods Sent for Job Work

The principal is entitled to take ITC on capital goods sent to a job worker. Credit is admissible even if the capital goods are directly sent to a job worker without first being received at the principal's premises.

5. Statutory Time Limits & Deemed Supply [Sec 143(3) & 143(4)]

Goods sent for job work must either be brought back to the principal or supplied directly to buyers within strict statutory timeframes:

Category of Goods Initial Statutory Period Permissible Extension by Commissioner Maximum Extended Period
Inputs [Sec 143(1)(a) & Sec 19(3)] 1 YEAR from being sent out (or received by JW if sent direct) Further period up to 1 YEAR on sufficient cause shown Total 2 Years
Capital Goods [Sec 143(1)(b) & Sec 19(6)] 3 YEARS from being sent out (or received by JW if sent direct) Further period up to 2 YEARS on sufficient cause shown Total 5 Years
Legal Consequence of Non-Return: Retrospective Deemed Supply! If inputs or capital goods are NOT returned or supplied from job worker's premises within the statutory 1-year / 3-year period (or extended period):
  • It shall be DEEMED TO BE A SUPPLY of such goods by the principal to the job worker.
  • Effective Date of Supply: Deemed to have been supplied on the DATE ON WHICH THE GOODS WERE SENT OUT (or received by job worker if sent directly).
  • Tax & Interest Liability: The principal must issue a tax invoice, declare the supply in Form GSTR-1 & GSTR-3B, and pay GST along with mandatory interest under Section 50 computed from the original date of dispatch!

6. Perpetual Exemption: Moulds, Dies, Jigs & Fixtures [Sec 143(5) & Sec 19(7)]

Zero Time Limit

Sections 143(5) & 19(7)

The time limits of 1 year and 3 years DO NOT APPLY to moulds and dies, jigs and fixtures, or tools sent out to a job worker for job work.

Permanent Retention

ITC Fully Protected

Moulds, dies, and tools can remain with the job worker indefinitely until worn out. They are NEVER deemed to be a supply, and the principal is never required to reverse ITC!

Valuation of Moulds & Dies (Circular No. 38/12/2018-GST): Where the principal supplies moulds and dies free of cost to the job worker, the value of such moulds/dies is NOT added to the value of job work services under Section 15(2)(b), provided the job worker was not contractually obligated to supply the moulds/dies on its own account.

7. Direct Supply from Job Worker's Premises [Section 143(1) Provisos]

The principal can supply finished goods directly from the job worker's premises (within India on payment of tax, or for export with/without tax) without bringing them back to the principal's factory.

General Rule Statutory Exceptions (No APOB Declaration Needed)
The principal must declare the job worker's premises as an Additional Place of Business (APOB) in its GST registration. The principal can supply goods directly from the job worker's premises WITHOUT declaring it as APOB in either of two cases:
  1. Where the job worker is REGISTERED under Section 25; OR
  2. Where the principal is engaged in the supply of notified goods.
Aggregate Turnover Attribution [Explanation (ii) to Section 22]: Where goods are supplied directly from the premises of a job worker, the entire turnover of such goods SHALL BE INCLUDED IN THE AGGREGATE TURNOVER OF THE PRINCIPAL, not the job worker!

8. Supply of Waste & Scrap [Section 143(5)]

During machining, processing, and assembly operations, industrial waste and scrap are inevitably generated. Section 143(5) provides clear tax allocation rules:

Case 1: Registered Job Worker

Tax Paid by Job Worker

If the job worker is registered under GST, the waste and scrap generated during job work may be supplied directly from the job worker's place of business on payment of tax by the job worker. (Treated as supply of goods by the job worker).

Case 2: Unregistered Job Worker

Tax Paid by Principal

If the job worker is unregistered, the waste and scrap must be supplied by the principal on payment of tax (or returned to the principal's premises).

9. Documentation: Delivery Challan & Form GST ITC-04

Delivery Challan [Rule 45 & Rule 55]:

  • All goods dispatched to or received from a job worker must move under cover of a Delivery Challan containing serial number, date, GSTIN of principal & job worker, HSN code, description, quantity, taxable value, and tax rate.
  • E-Way Bill: Mandatory under Rule 138 for inter-State movement between principal and job worker irrespective of consignment value (Zero Threshold!).

10. ICAI Test Your Knowledge (TYK) - Complete Solved Problems

ICAI TYK 1 (Direct Supply without APOB)

Question:

Under what circumstances can the principal directly supply goods from the premises of the job worker without declaring the premises of the job worker as his additional place of business (APOB)?

Answer:

As per the first proviso to Section 143(1) of the CGST Act, 2017, the principal can supply goods directly from the place of business of a job worker without declaring such place as his additional place of business (APOB) in the following two circumstances:

  1. Where the job worker is registered under Section 25 of the CGST Act; OR
  2. Where the principal is engaged in the supply of such goods as may be notified by the Commissioner in this behalf.
ICAI TYK 2 (Consequences of Non-Return of Goods)

Question:

What happens when the inputs or capital goods are not received back or supplied from the place of business of the job worker within the prescribed time period?

Answer:

Under Section 143(3) and Section 143(4) read with Section 19(3) and Section 19(6) of the CGST Act, 2017:

  • Deemed Supply: If inputs (within 1 year or extended 1 year) or capital goods (within 3 years or extended 2 years) are not received back by the principal or not supplied directly from the job worker's premises, they shall be deemed to have been supplied by the principal to the job worker.
  • Point of Taxation: The supply is deemed to have taken place on the day when the goods were sent out (or on the date of receipt by the job worker if sent directly).
  • Tax & Interest: The principal is liable to issue a tax invoice, pay the output GST on such goods, and pay interest under Section 50(1) from the original date of dispatch till the date of payment of tax.

ICAI TYK 4 (Direct Delivery of Mould & 4-Year Audit Demand)

Problem Statement:

Genie Engineers had a mould delivered directly to a job worker from the supplier for making precision parts. As per agreement, the mould was to remain with the job worker as long as work was sent. After 4 years, a departmental audit team visited the job worker, noticed the mould, and demanded GST from Genie Engineers for: (i) taking ITC without receiving the mould; and (ii) not bringing the mould back after 3 years. How should Genie Engineers respond?

Detailed Answer & Legal Defense:

Genie Engineers should refute the departmental audit demand on the following statutory grounds:

  1. Validity of ITC on Direct Delivery [Section 19(5)]: Section 19(5) explicitly allows the principal to take input tax credit on capital goods even if the capital goods are directly sent to a job worker without being first brought to the principal's premises. Hence, the demand for taking ITC without receiving the mould at the factory is legally baseless.
  2. Perpetual Exemption for Moulds [Section 19(7) & Section 143(5)]: Section 19(7) and Section 143(5) specifically provide that the 3-year time limit for return of capital goods DOES NOT APPLY to moulds and dies, jigs and fixtures, or tools sent out to a job worker. Moulds and dies can remain with the job worker indefinitely without being deemed as a supply.

Conclusion: Genie Engineers is fully compliant with the GST law. No tax, reversal, or interest is payable.

ICAI TYK 5 (Job Work Service vs Own Manufacture)

Problem Statement:

Sudama Industries Ltd. (J&K) manufactures plastic pipes on job-work basis. On 10th Jan, Plasto Manufacturers (HP) sent plastic (â‚¹4 Lakh) and moulds (â‚¹50,000) FOC. Sudama Industries used its own lamination material worth â‚¹1 Lakh in manufacture, raised invoice of â‚¹2 Lakh as job charges, and returned pipes on 20th Oct. Same quantity/quality of pipes were manufactured by Sudama from its own material and sold to Solid Pipes for â‚¹7.5 Lakh.

Examine: (i) Difference between two manufactures; (ii) Can job worker use own material? (iii) Is sending plastic/moulds a supply? (iv) Should FOC plastic/moulds be included in job charges?

Detailed Answer:

  • (i) Difference in Classification: Pipes made for Plasto Manufacturers qualify as Job Work Service (Supply of Service) under Schedule II, Para 3, since processing is done on goods belonging to another registered person. Pipes made from own raw materials and sold to Solid Pipes constitute a Supply of Goods.
  • (ii) Use of Own Material: Yes. Circular No. 38/12/2018-GST clarifies that a job worker can use its own goods/material in addition to goods sent by the principal; the activity continues to be classified as a service.
  • (iii) Sending Plastic & Moulds: Under Section 143(1), sending goods for job work under a Delivery Challan is NOT a supply. No tax invoice is required; movement takes place under a Delivery Challan.
  • (iv) Non-Inclusion of FOC Material: Under Section 15(2)(b) read with Circular 38/12/2018-GST, the value of plastic and moulds supplied FOC by the principal is NOT to be included in the job charges of â‚¹2 Lakh, since supplying raw materials and moulds was the responsibility of the principal, not the job worker.

ICAI TYK 7 (Direct Sent Capital Goods & Jigs/Fixtures)

Problem Statement:

Bedi Manufacturers instructed supplier to send capital goods directly to Rajesh Enterprises (job worker). Supplier sent goods on 10th April; received by job worker on 15th April. Rajesh Enterprises did not return the capital goods. Discuss whether Bedi Manufacturers can retain ITC and what action is required. What if jigs and fixtures were supplied instead?

Detailed Answer:

  • 1. Retention of ITC [Section 19(5)]: Bedi Manufacturers is entitled to retain full ITC on capital goods sent directly to Rajesh Enterprises.
  • 2. 3-Year Time Limit [Section 19(6)]: The 3-year period is computed from the date of receipt by the job worker (15th April). If not returned within 3 years, it shall be deemed to be a supply of capital goods by Bedi Manufacturers to Rajesh Enterprises on 15th April. Bedi Manufacturers must issue a tax invoice, pay GST, and pay interest under Section 50.
  • 3. Position for Jigs and Fixtures [Section 19(7)]: Under Section 19(7), the 3-year time limit does NOT apply to jigs and fixtures. Even if Rajesh Enterprises never returns the jigs and fixtures, it will NOT be treated as a supply, and Bedi Manufacturers can retain full ITC without paying any tax or interest.
ICAI TYK 8 (Yarn Processing & Nature of Supply)

Question:

Nandeeshwar Manufacturers sends yarn for processing in January. The job worker uses its own material during processing. Processed yarn is sold directly from job worker's premises in March. Balance yarn and waste returned in April. The job worker's accountant opines that this is a composite supply of goods because job worker used its own materials. Examine whether the accountant's opinion is correct.

Answer:

The opinion of the job worker's accountant is INCORRECT.

Under Section 7(1A) read with Schedule II, Para 3 of the CGST Act, 2017, any treatment or process applied to another person's goods is classified as a Supply of Services.

CBIC Circular No. 38/12/2018-GST has clarified that a job worker can use its own goods/chemicals/materials in the processing activity in addition to goods sent by the principal. The goods used by the job worker do not constitute an independent supply of goods, but are consumed in providing the job work service.

Conclusion: The activity constitutes a Supply of Service by the job worker to Nandeeshwar Manufacturers, chargeable to GST under the service rate.
