Chapter 10: Accounts & Records; E-Way Bill

Exhaustive textbook revision notes on books of accounts, documentary compliance, and goods transit monitoring. Covers Section 35 mandatory registers, Section 36 statutory 72-month retention formula, E-Way Bill ₹50,000 threshold, zero-threshold job work/handicrafts rules, Part B 50-km exemption, normal cargo (200 km/day) vs ODC (20 km/day) validity, and Rule 138E portal blocking provisions.

Section 35 Accounts and Other Records to be Maintained

Under Section 35(1), every registered person shall keep and maintain at his Principal Place of Business (PPoB) a true and correct account of:

1. Production Accounts:
Production or manufacture of goods (raw materials, quantitative details).
2. Inward & Outward Supplies:
Inward and outward supply of goods or services or both.
3. Stock Register:
Stock of goods (opening, receipts, supplies, lost, stolen, destroyed, written off, balance).
4. ITC Register:
Input tax credit availed and utilized.
5. Output Tax Register:
Output tax payable and paid.
6. Goods in Storage:
Details of goods stored in godowns/warehouses (including during transit).
Specific Additional Records by Class of Taxable Persons [Rule 56]:
Class of Person Additional Records Mandated by Law
Manufacturer Monthly production accounts showing quantitative details of raw materials/services used in manufacture and quantitative details of goods manufactured (including waste and by-products).
Service Provider Quantitative details of goods used in the provision of services, details of input services consumed, and output services supplied.
Works Contractor Names and addresses of persons on whose behalf works contract executed; description, value and quantity of goods/services received and utilized; payments received.
Agent Authorization received from each principal; particulars of goods/services received and supplied on behalf of principal; accounts furnished to principal; tax paid.
Warehouse Keeper & Transporter [Sec 35(2)] Must maintain books of accounts in respect of goods stored/transported (consignor, consignee, receipt, dispatch). Applies even if unregistered! (Obtain unique enrolment ID under Rule 58).

Section 36 Period of Retention of Accounts (72 Months Rule)

GENERAL RULE

72 Months from Due Date of Annual Return

Every registered person must preserve books of accounts and records until the expiry of 72 months (6 years) from the due date of furnishing of annual return (31st December) for the year pertaining to such accounts and records.

Example (FY 2024-25):
• Due date of Annual Return: 31st December, 2025.
• 72 months expires on: 31st December, 2031!

SPECIAL EXCEPTION

Appeals, Revision or Investigation Cases

Where accounts/records are subject matter of an appeal, revision, or any other proceedings before Appellate Authority, Tribunal or Court, or investigation:

Must be preserved for:
• 1 year after final disposal of such appeal/revision/proceedings; OR
• The standard 72-month period,
whichever is LATER!

Rule 138 Electronic Way Bill (E-Way Bill) Framework

An E-Way Bill (Form GST EWB-01) is an electronic document generated on the common portal for the movement of goods. Under Section 68 read with Rule 138, generation of E-Way Bill is mandatory prior to commencement of movement where:

📌 General Threshold: Consignment Value of Goods exceeds ₹50,000:
• In relation to a supply; OR
• For reasons other than supply (e.g. branch transfer, job work, exhibition); OR
• Due to inward supply from an unregistered person.

🧮 What is "Consignment Value"?
Invoice value determined under Section 15 INCLUDING CGST, SGST, IGST, UTGST and Cess, but EXCLUDING the value of exempt supplies where a single invoice includes both taxable and exempt goods!

⚡ Mandatory E-Way Bill EVEN IF Consignment Value ≤ ₹50,000 (Zero Threshold):

  1. Inter-State Job Work: Goods sent by a principal located in one State/UT to a job worker located in any other State/UT (generated by principal or registered job worker).
  2. Inter-State Handicrafts: Inter-State movement of handicraft goods by a dealer who is exempted from obtaining registration under Section 24.

Parts of EWB Form GST EWB-01 Structure & The 50 Km Relaxation

Component Information Contained Furnished By
Part A GSTIN of supplier & recipient, Place of dispatch & delivery (PIN codes), Document No. & Date, Value of goods, HSN code, Reason for transportation. Registered person who is consignor/consignee, or transporter on authorization.
Part B Transport details: Vehicle number for road transport; Railway Receipt (RR) No., Airway Bill No. or Bill of Lading for Rail/Air/Vessel. Transporter or registered person transporting the goods.

🛵 The 50 Km Part-B Exemption [Third Proviso to Rule 138(3)]:

Where goods are transported for a distance of up to 50 km within the same State or Union territory:
1. From the place of business of the consignor to the place of business of the transporter for further transportation; OR
2. From the place of business of the transporter to the place of business of the consignee,
Part B (vehicle details) is NOT required to be updated! Updating Part A alone is legally sufficient.

Rule 138(10) Validity Period of E-Way Bill (Distance Matrix)

The validity period of an E-Way Bill begins from the time it is generated, and is calculated based on the distance between origin and destination:

Category of Cargo / Transport Mode Distance Threshold Statutory Validity Period
Normal Cargo (Other than ODC) Up to 200 km 1 Day
Normal Cargo (Other than ODC) For every additional 200 km or part thereof 1 Additional Day
Over Dimensional Cargo (ODC) OR Multimodal shipment involving a ship Up to 20 km 1 Day
Over Dimensional Cargo (ODC) OR Multimodal shipment involving a ship For every additional 20 km or part thereof 1 Additional Day
⏳ Extension of Validity [Second Proviso to Rule 138(10)]:
Validity can be extended by the transporter in case of transhipment delay, natural calamity or breakdown:
• Can be extended within 8 hours before or 8 hours after the expiry of the validity of the E-Way Bill!

Rule 138(14) Situations Where NO E-Way Bill is Required

No E-Way Bill is required to be generated in the following circumstances:

  • Where goods being transported are specified in Annexure to Rule 138(14) (fresh milk, fruits, vegetables, bread, salt, curd, meat, LPG for domestic use, postal baggage, currency, jewellery);
  • Where goods are being transported by a non-motorised conveyance (e.g. cycle, hand-cart);
  • Where goods are being transported from customs port, airport, air cargo complex and land customs station to an ICD or CFS for customs clearance;
  • Where goods are being transported under customs bond or customs supervision/escort;
  • Transit cargo transported to or from Nepal or Bhutan;
  • Empty cargo containers being transported;
  • Where goods are moved up to a distance of 20 km from consignor's place of business to a weighbridge for weighment, accompanied by a delivery challan.

Rule 138E Blocking & Unblocking of E-Way Bill Portal Facility

Under Rule 138E, generation of E-Way Bill (as consignor, consignee, or transporter) is automatically blocked on the common portal if a registered person:

  • Composition Dealer: Has not furnished Statement Form CMP-08 for 2 consecutive quarters;
  • Regular Monthly Filer: Has not furnished Form GSTR-3B for 2 consecutive months;
  • QRMP Quarterly Filer: Has not furnished Form GSTR-3B for 2 consecutive tax periods (quarters);
  • GSTR-1 Default: Has not furnished Form GSTR-1 / IFF for 2 consecutive months / quarters;
  • Suspended Registration: Whose registration has been suspended under Rule 21A.

Unblocking: The facility is automatically restored upon filing the pending returns, OR the Commissioner can unblock on an application in Form GST EWB-05 upon sufficient cause shown.

TYK Cases ICAI Test Your Knowledge — Solved Practical Problems (1 to 7)

🎯 TYK Question 1 • Mala Services Ltd. (Records for Service Providers)

Facts: Mala Services Ltd. provides management consultancy services. The company believes that since it provides services and not goods, it is not required to maintain any quantitative details or stock registers under Section 35. Examine the correctness of this view.

Solution: The view taken by Mala Services Ltd. is INCORRECT.
Under Rule 56(12) of the CGST Rules, every registered person providing services is statutorily required to maintain:
1. Quantitative details of goods used in the provision of each service;
2. Details of input services consumed;
3. Details of services supplied.

🎯 TYK Question 2 • Essel Groups (Period of Retention Computation)

Facts: Essel Groups started making taxable supplies in FY 2024-25. Annual return for FY 2024-25 was filed on 10th November 2025 (statutory due date: 31st December 2025). Determine the period up to which Essel Groups must maintain its accounts and records.

Statutory Analysis & Solution:
• Under Section 36, books of accounts must be retained until the expiry of 72 months from the due date of furnishing of annual return for the year.
• Due date of annual return for FY 2024-25 is 31st December, 2025. (Actual earlier filing date of 10th November is irrelevant!).
• 72 months from 31st December 2025 expires on: 31st December, 2031.
• Conclusion: Essel Groups must preserve all books and records until 31st December, 2031!

🎯 TYK Question 3 • Swad Restaurant (Composition Scheme Stock Records)

Facts: Swad Restaurant opted for composition scheme under Section 10. The proprietor contends that composition dealers pay tax on turnover and cannot claim ITC, so they need not maintain stock registers of goods under Section 35. Advise Swad Restaurant.

Solution: The contention is INCORRECT.
Section 35(1) applies to every registered person, which includes composition dealers. Under Rule 56(1), Swad Restaurant must maintain true accounts of: (a) stock of goods received and supplied; (b) manufacturing accounts; and (c) tax payable and paid.

🎯 TYK Question 4 • Raghav & Sons (Agent Accounts and Records)

Facts: ABC Manufacturers Ltd. engages Raghav & Sons as an agent to sell electrical equipment. What specific records must Raghav & Sons maintain under GST?

Solution: Under Rule 56(11), every agent shall maintain:
1. Particulars of authorisation received from each principal;
2. Particulars of goods/services received on behalf of each principal (description, value, quantity);
3. Particulars of goods/services supplied on behalf of each principal;
4. Details of accounts furnished to each principal;
5. Tax paid on receipt or supply of goods/services carried on behalf of each principal.

🎯 TYK Question 5 • Sindhi Toys (E-Way Bill Distance Validity Computation)

Facts: Sindhi Toys Manufacturers, registered in Punjab, dispatches a consignment of toys by road to a distributor in Maharashtra. Distance = 450 km. Consignment is normal cargo. E-Way Bill was generated on 10th April at 9:00 AM. Determine the validity period of the E-Way Bill.

Statutory Analysis & Computation:
• Under Rule 138(10), for normal cargo:
  • Up to 200 km = 1 Day;
  • For every additional 200 km or part thereof = 1 Additional Day.
• Distance = 450 km = 200 km (1 day) + 200 km (1 day) + 50 km (1 day) = 3 Days!
• Expiry Rule: Each day count ends at midnight of the following day. E-Way Bill generated at 9:00 AM on 10th April:
  • Day 1 expires at midnight of 11th April;
  • Day 2 expires at midnight of 12th April;
  • Day 3 expires at midnight of 13th April (12:00 midnight)!

🎯 TYK Question 6 • Power Electricals Ltd. (Bill-to Ship-to E-Way Bill)

Facts: Power Electricals Ltd. (Delhi) receives order from Super Traders (Mumbai) to supply transformers to Site of Project (Pune). Power Electricals dispatches directly to Pune from Delhi. Distance Delhi to Pune is 1,400 km. Power Electricals claims that two E-Way Bills are required because there are two taxable supplies (Delhi → Mumbai and Mumbai → Pune). Examine correctness.

Solution: The claim of Power Electricals Ltd. is INCORRECT.
CBIC Clarification / FAQ confirms that in a Bill-to Ship-to model, there are two transactions involved, but physically there is ONLY ONE movement of goods. Therefore:
• ONLY ONE E-Way Bill is required for the physical transit from Delhi to Pune!
• In E-Way Bill: Dispatch from = Delhi; Bill to = Super Traders (Mumbai); Ship to = Project Site (Pune).

🎯 TYK Question 7 • Beauty Cosmetics Ltd. (Multiple Consignments & Consolidated EWB)

Facts: Beauty Cosmetics Ltd. transports 10 individual consignments of cosmetics to different retailers in a single truck. Value of each individual consignment is ₹30,000 (below ₹50k threshold). However, total combined value in the vehicle is ₹3,00,000. Examine E-Way Bill requirements.

Solution:
• Under Rule 138(1), E-Way Bill is mandatory if the consignment value of an individual consignment exceeds ₹50,000. Since each consignment is ₹30,000, individual generation is not mandatory under Rule 138(1).
• However, under Rule 138(7), the transporter transporting multiple consignments in one conveyance where aggregate value exceeds ₹50,000 may generate individual E-Way Bills and combine them into a Consolidated E-Way Bill in Form GST EWB-02 on the portal before departure.
