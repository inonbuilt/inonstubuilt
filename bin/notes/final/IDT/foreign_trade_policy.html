LEGAL FOUNDATIONS

1. Statutory Architecture & WTO Principles of FTP 2023

Foreign Trade Policy is formulated and administered by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry (Department of Commerce) through the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) in exercise of the sovereign statutory powers conferred under Section 5 of the Foreign Trade (Development and Regulation) Act, 1992 [FT(D&R) Act].

The FT(D&R) Act, 1992 Framework

  • Section 3: Central Government may make provisions for facilitating and controlling foreign trade.
  • Section 5: Central Government formulates and amends the Foreign Trade Policy by Official Gazette notifications.
  • Section 6: Appointment of Director General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) to advise Central Govt and administer the policy.
  • Interpretation Finality: If any question arises regarding the interpretation of FTP, the decision of DGFT is final and binding (NOT CBIC!).

WTO Disciplines & Core Philosophy

Under WTO agreements (Agreement on Subsidies and Countervailing Measures - ASCM), direct subsidies on export products are strictly prohibited as trade-distorting.

Fundamental Canon of Export Taxation:
"Goods and services are to be exported, but NOT taxes."
Indirect taxes borne by export goods are lawfully remitted or exempted via Duty Drawback, Advance Authorisation, RoDTEP, and RoSCTL.

Categorization of Goods under FTP

Category Legal Treatment Regulatory Mechanism
Free Goods Freely importable or exportable without any licensing or permit. Default general regime unless restricted or prohibited.
Restricted Goods Allowed only against an Import/Export Authorisation or License issued by DGFT. Subject to strategic, defence, environmental, health, or safety quotas.
Prohibited Goods Strictly barred from entering or leaving India. Trophies, peacock feathers, tallow/fat, wild animals, dangerous narcotics.
State Trading Enterprises (STE) Can be imported/exported only through designated canalizing agencies (e.g., STC, MMTC, FCI). Food grains, fertilizers, high-speed diesel, and urea.
IEC & GENERAL PROVISIONS

2. Importer-Exporter Code (IEC) & General Import/Export Principles

Every commercial transaction crossing India's customs frontier is anchored on the mandatory registration of the trader under the electronic foreign trade database.

The Importer-Exporter Code (IEC) Architecture

10-Digit Alphanumeric Code:
IEC is a unique 10-digit PAN-based alphanumeric number issued electronically by DGFT (same as PAN of the entity).
Annual Electronic Update:
IEC holder must confirm/update his IEC details online on the DGFT portal every year between April to June.
Deactivation for Non-Update:
If not updated within the prescribed window, the IEC is deactivated until updated online.
Statutory Exemptions:
Exempt for personal imports, Central/State Govt ministries, and goods from/to Nepal/Myanmar up to prescribed limits.

Import of Samples under FTP

Import of trade samples of even 'restricted' items is freely permitted without an import authorization (except for defence/security items, seeds, bees, and new drugs). Duty-free import of commercial samples up to â‚¹ 3,00,000 per annum is allowed for all exporters subject to customs conditions.

DUTY EXEMPTION SCHEME

4. Advance Authorisation Scheme (15% Value Addition)

Advance Authorisation (AA) permits the duty-free import of inputs (including fuel, oil, and catalyst) physically incorporated into the export product, governed by strict Actual User conditions.

Operational Features of Advance Authorisation

  • Eligible Beneficiaries: Manufacturer exporters OR merchant exporters tied to supporting manufacturers.
  • Duties Exempted: Basic Customs Duty (BCD), Additional Customs Duty (IGST), Compensation Cess, Education Cess, ADD, CVD, and Safeguard duties.
  • Actual User Condition: Inputs imported are strictly non-transferable even after fulfillment of Export Obligation (EO).
  • Validity Windows: 12 months for import; 18 months from issue date for Export Obligation (EO).

Mandatory Minimum 15% Value Addition

Advance Authorisation mandates a minimum Value Addition (VA) of 15% (except tea, spices, and gems & jewellery).

VA = [ (A - B) / B ] × 100
A = FOB value of export realized / FOR value of deemed exports
B = CIF value of inputs covered by authorization

Advance Authorisation for Annual Requirement

Issued to exporters having past export performance in at least the preceding 2 financial years. Entitlement in terms of CIF value of imports is capped at 300% of FOB value of physical exports (and/or FOR value of deemed exports) in the preceding year or â‚¹ 1 Crore, whichever is higher. Issued only for items notified under SION.

TRANSFERABLE SCHEME

5. Duty Free Import Authorisation (DFIA) Scheme (20% VA)

DFIA is a post-export duty exemption instrument that blends input duty freedom with post-export commercial market transferability.

High-Yield Comparative Analysis: Advance Authorisation vs DFIA

Key Dimension Advance Authorisation (AA) Scheme Duty Free Import Authorisation (DFIA) Scheme
Transferability NON-TRANSFERABLE — Governed by Actual User condition. FREELY TRANSFERABLE after fulfillment of Export Obligation.
Minimum Value Addition Minimum 15% Value Addition. Minimum 20% Value Addition.
Norms Requirement Can be issued under SION, Self-Declaration, or Ad-hoc Norms. Issued ONLY where SION is pre-notified (no ad-hoc/self-declaration).
Duties Exempted Exempts BCD, IGST, Compensation Cess, ADD, CVD, and Safeguard duty. Exempts Basic Customs Duty (BCD) ONLY. (IGST/Cess payable).
Duty Drawback on Duty-Paid Inputs Available on duty-paid inputs used in export product alongside AA. Drawback available for duty-paid inputs used in export product.
CAPITAL GOODS SCHEME

6. Export Promotion Capital Goods (EPCG) Scheme (Zero Customs Duty)

The EPCG Scheme facilitates technological modernization of Indian industry by permitting the import of capital goods for pre-production, production, and post-production at ZERO customs duty (exempt from BCD, IGST, and Cess).

Specific Export Obligation (Specific EO)

Specific EO is equivalent to SIX (6) TIMES the amount of duties, taxes, and cess saved on capital goods imported under EPCG.
Time Window: Must be fulfilled within 6 YEARS reckoned from the date of issue of the authorization.

Average Export Obligation (Average EO)

In addition to Specific EO, the exporter must maintain the average level of exports achieved in the preceding 3 licensing years for same and similar products.
Specific EO is strictly OVER AND ABOVE the Average EO.

Concessions & Fast-Track Reliefs under EPCG

  • Indigenous Sourcing Incentive: If capital goods are procured from domestic manufacturers instead of imports, the Specific EO is reduced by 25% (i.e. only 4.5 times duty saved).
  • Fast-Track Early Redemption: If an authorization holder fulfills 75% or more of Specific EO and 100% of Average EO in half or less of the EO period (within 3 years), the balance export obligation is completely waived!
  • Ineligible Goods: Second-hand capital goods cannot be imported under EPCG.
REMISSION & EOU SCHEMES

7. RoDTEP Scheme & Export Oriented Units (EOU / EHTP / STP)

RoDTEP Scheme (Remission of Duties & Taxes)

RoDTEP refunds embedded uncredited duties/taxes borne on export products (e.g. VAT on transportation fuel, electricity duty, mandi tax, stamp duty).

  • Disbursed as transferable electronic duty credit scrips in ICEGATE ledger.
  • Support for DTA units available till 30.09.2026.
  • Ineligible: SEZ, EOU, job work, deemed exports.

EOU / EHTP / STP / BTP Schemes

Units undertaking to export their entire production of goods or services.

  • Duty-Free Procurement: Capital goods and raw materials imported/procured free of duty.
  • Net Foreign Exchange (NFE): Must achieve Positive Cumulative NFE over 5 years (NFE = A - B > 0).
  • Permissible DTA sales up to 50% of FOB value upon payment of applicable duties.
DEEMED EXPORTS

8. Deemed Exports Architecture & Fiscal Benefits

'Deemed Exports' refers to specified transactions where goods supplied do not leave India, but are manufactured domestically and substitute imports in priority national projects or export-oriented manufacturing.

ICAI TYK Question 1: Policy for Import of Samples Samples Policy

Mr. Ayush Bhandari wants to import samples from US. State in brief the policy for import of samples under FTP.

Detailed Statutory Solution:
Under the Foreign Trade Policy 2023, the policy for import of samples is as follows:
  • Import of samples of even 'restricted' items is allowed without an import authorization. (Exceptions: defence/security items, seeds, bees, and new drugs which require specific authorization).
  • Duty-Free Import Limit: Duty-free import of commercial samples up to â‚¹ 3,00,000 for all exporters is allowed subject to terms and conditions of customs notifications.
ICAI TYK Question 2: Advance Authorisation for Annual Requirements Annual Authorisation

State the salient aspects of Advance Authorisation for annual requirements to exporters.

Detailed Statutory Solution:
The salient aspects governing Advance Authorisation for annual requirements are:
  1. Eligibility: Issued to exporters having past export performance in at least the preceding 2 financial years to import inputs on an annual basis.
  2. Norms Restriction: Issued ONLY for items notified under SION (Standard Input Output Norms); cannot be issued on ad-hoc norms or self-declaration where SION does not exist.
  3. Entitlement Quantum: CIF value of imports allowed is up to 300% of FOB value of physical exports (and/or FOR value of deemed exports) in the preceding financial year or â‚¹ 1 Crore, whichever is higher.
ICAI TYK Question 3: Benefits of Deemed Exports under FTP Deemed Export Benefits

Mr. X is desirous to know the benefits of deemed exports under FTP. Discuss the same with reference to FTP.

Detailed Statutory Solution:
Supplies qualifying as deemed exports are eligible for any or all of the following fiscal benefits:
  • (a) Advance Authorisation / DFIA: Advance Authorisation or DFIA for duty-free procurement/import of inputs.
  • (b) Deemed Export Duty Drawback: Refund of drawback on inputs used in manufacture/supply under deemed exports on All Industry Rate (AIR) or Brand Rate basis.
  • (c) Refund of Terminal Excise Duty (TED): Refund of TED on specified excisable goods provided recipient does not avail CENVAT credit/rebate (or refund of GST on deemed exports).
ICAI TYK Question 4: Similarities & Differences: Advance Authorisation vs DFIA AA vs DFIA Comparison

Discuss the key similarities and differences between Advance Authorisation and DFIA (Duty Free Import Authorisation) schemes.

Detailed Statutory Solution:
Key Similarities:
  • Both permit duty-free import of inputs, oil, and catalyst consumed in production of export products.
  • Validity period for import under both schemes is 12 months from date of issue.
Key Differences:
  1. Transferability: Advance Authorisation is non-transferable (Actual User condition). DFIA is freely transferable after export obligation fulfillment.
  2. Value Addition: Advance Authorisation requires min 15% VA; DFIA requires min 20% VA.
  3. Norms: Advance Authorisation can be issued under SION, self-declaration, or ad-hoc norms. DFIA can only be issued if SION is pre-fixed.
  4. Duties Exempted: Advance Authorisation exempts BCD, IGST, Compensation Cess, ADD, CVD, Safeguard duty. DFIA exempts Basic Customs Duty (BCD) ONLY.
ICAI TYK Question 5: Privileges of Status Holders Status Privileges

Discuss the privileges granted under FTP to Status Holders.

Detailed Statutory Solution:
Status holders are entitled to the following major privileges:
  • Customs clearance of imports and exports on self-declaration basis.
  • Priority fixation of Standard Input Output Norms within 60 days by Norms Committee.
  • Exemption from compulsory negotiation of documents through banks.
  • 100% Exemption from furnishing Bank Guarantee (BG) under FTP schemes.
  • Two Star and above permitted to establish export warehouses.
  • Three Star, Four Star & Five Star manufacturers eligible to self-certify origin of goods ("Approved Exporters") under FTAs.
  • Entitled to export freely exportable items on free of cost basis up to prescribed annual limits for export promotion.
  • Preferential priority handling of consignments by port and customs agencies.
ICAI TYK Question 6: True/False Statutory Statements Statutory Verification

With reference to the provisions of FTP, discuss giving reasons whether the following statements are true or false:
(i) If any doubt arises in respect of interpretation of any provision of FTP, said doubt should be forwarded to CBIC, whose decision thereon would be final and binding.
(ii) IEC is a unique 12 digit PAN based alphanumeric code allotted to a person for undertaking export/import activities.

Detailed Statutory Solution:

(i) FALSE: Under Section 6 of the FT(D&R) Act, 1992 and paragraph 1.06 of FTP 2023, if any question or doubt arises in respect of the interpretation of any provision of the Foreign Trade Policy, the said doubt ought to be referred to the Director General of Foreign Trade (DGFT), whose decision thereon would be final and binding (and NOT the CBIC).

(ii) FALSE: An Importer-Exporter Code (IEC) is a unique 10-digit PAN-based alphanumeric code (and NOT a 12-digit code) allotted electronically by DGFT.

ICAI TYK Question 7: UN Aid Supplies & Advance Authorisation Eligibility Deemed Export Scope

Flintex Manufacturers manufactures goods by using imported inputs and supplies the same under Aid Programme of the United Nations. Payment is received in free foreign exchange. Can Flintex Manufacturers seek Advance Authorisation under FTP for these supplies?

Detailed Statutory Solution:
YES, Flintex Manufacturers can seek an Advance Authorisation.

Statutory Grounds: Under Chapter 7 of FTP 2023, supply of goods to the United Nations or international organisations for their official use or supplied to projects financed by them qualifies as 'Deemed Exports'. Advance Authorisation is lawfully issued for procurement/import of inputs required for supplies qualifying as deemed exports. Hence, Flintex Manufacturers is fully entitled to apply for and obtain an Advance Authorisation.

ICAI TYK Question 9: Part Duty-Free & Part Duty-Paid Inputs (AA + Drawback) Dual Benefit

'A' has used some duty-paid inputs in its export products. However, for the rest of the inputs, he wants to apply for Advance Authorisation. Can he do so? Explain with reference to FTP.

Detailed Statutory Solution:
YES, 'A' can do so.

Statutory Provisions: In case of part duty-free and part duty-paid inputs, both Advance Authorisation and Duty Drawback can be simultaneously availed. The exporter can obtain Duty Drawback for any duty-paid material (whether imported or indigenous) used in the export product as per the drawback rate fixed by the Directorate of Drawback, Ministry of Finance. For the balance inputs, Advance Authorisation can be utilized to import them duty-free. Full details of the duty-paid materials must be declared in the application for Advance Authorisation.
