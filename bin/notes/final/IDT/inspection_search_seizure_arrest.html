1. Enforcement Architecture & Guiding Philosophy

Enforcement provisions such as inspection, search, seizure, and arrest are extraordinary statutory powers designed to protect legitimate government revenues and create a level playing field for honest taxpayers by deterring deliberate tax evasion. Under GST, these coercive powers are exercised only in exceptional circumstances and as a measure of last resort, surrounded by stringent statutory safeguards and judicial checks.

Hierarchy of Authority

Statutory Sanctioning Authorities

  • Inspection [Sec 67(1)]: Authorized in writing by officer ≥ Joint Commissioner in Form GST INS-01.
  • Search & Seizure [Sec 67(2)]: Authorized by officer ≥ Joint Commissioner in Form GST INS-01.
  • Arrest [Sec 69(1)]: Authorized ONLY by the COMMISSIONER where tax evasion > â‚¹2 Crore.
Judicial Cornerstone

"Reason to Believe" Standard

The authorization cannot be issued on mere suspicion, gossip, or speculative whim. It demands an objective determination based on credible material and intelligent evaluation that would cause a reasonable, honest person to conclude that evasion has occurred.

2. Power of Inspection [Section 67(1)]

Inspection is a softer enforcement power enabling officers to access the business premises of a taxable person, transporter, or warehouse operator. It can be initiated only when an officer of the rank of Joint Commissioner or above has "reasons to believe" that:

Target Person Statutory Grounds for Authorizing Inspection [Section 67(1)]
Taxable Person The taxable person has done any of the following to evade tax:
  1. Suppressed any transaction relating to supply of goods or services or both;
  2. Suppressed the stock of goods in hand;
  3. Claimed input tax credit in excess of his entitlement; or
  4. Contravened any provision of the Act or rules to evade tax.
Transporter / Warehouse Operator Any person engaged in transporting goods or storing goods (warehouse/godown owner) has:
  1. Kept goods which have escaped payment of tax; or
  2. Kept accounts or goods in a manner that is likely to cause evasion of tax.
Power of Test Purchase [Section 67(12)]: An authorized officer can conduct a "test purchase" of any goods or services from a taxable person's business premises to verify whether tax invoices or bills of supply are regularly issued. Upon return of the goods purchased, the taxable person is bound to refund the amount paid and cancel the tax invoice/bill of supply issued earlier.

3. Power of Search and Seizure [Section 67(2)]

Where pursuant to inspection or otherwise, the Joint Commissioner has reason to believe that any goods liable to confiscation or any documents, books, or things useful for proceedings are secreted in any place, he may authorize an officer in writing (or search himself) to search and seize such items in Form GST INS-01.

Power 1: Ingress

Break Open Doors & Almirahs

If access is denied, the searching officer has statutory authority to break open the door of the premises, or break open any almirah, box, or electronic safe in which accounts or goods are suspected to be concealed.

Power 2: Seal Premises

Sealing of Premises

Where access cannot be obtained immediately or the key holder absconds, the officer may seal the premises to prevent destruction or tampering with evidence.

Power 3: Prohibition

Prohibition Order (INS-03)

Where it is not practicable to physically seize bulky goods, the officer issues a detention order directing the custodian not to remove or part with the goods without prior permission.

4. Search Safeguards, Panchnama & The Mandatory 6-Month Rule

Search Safeguards

CrPC / BNSS Protocol

  • Search warrant (Form GST INS-01) must be shown to the person in charge before entry.
  • Search must be conducted in the presence of at least two independent, respectable witnesses (Panchas) from the locality.
  • A detailed Panchnama recording proceedings and an Inventory of Seizure (Form GST INS-02) must be prepared on the spot and signed by witnesses and the occupant.
  • Searching officers must offer themselves for personal search before entering and after concluding search.
Seized Books & Documents

Right to Copies [Sec 67(5)]

The person from whose custody books/documents are seized is entitled to make copies or take extracts in the presence of an authorized officer (unless it prejudicially affects investigation). Seized documents can be retained only as long as necessary for examination and proceedings.

The Absolute 6-Month Rule for Seized Goods [Section 67(7)]: Where any goods are seized under Section 67(2) and NO SHOW CAUSE NOTICE is given within 6 MONTHS of the seizure of the goods:
$$\mathbf{\text{The seized goods MUST BE UNCONDITIONALLY RETURNED to the person from whose possession they were seized!}}$$ Extension: The Proper Officer can extend this 6-month period by a further period not exceeding 6 months on sufficient cause shown in writing.
Immediate Disposal of Perishable/Hazardous Goods [Section 67(8) & Notfn 27/2018-CT]: Goods of hazardous or perishable nature (e.g., fresh fruits, milk, meat, fish, petroleum, fireworks, chemicals, onions) or goods depreciating rapidly in value may be disposed of immediately after seizure, and the net sale proceeds deposited with the Government.

5. Inspection of Goods in Transit [Section 68]

Under Section 68, the person-in-charge of a conveyance carrying goods of value exceeding specified limits (â‚¹50,000) must carry prescribed documents (E-way bill and Tax Invoice/Delivery Challan) and produce them for physical verification upon interception.

Transit Interception Key Distinction: Unlike Section 67(1) premises inspection which mandates a prior written authorization from a Joint Commissioner, inspection of goods in movement under Section 68 can be carried out by field officers directly without requiring any specific Joint Commissioner authorization.

6. Power to Arrest [Section 69 & Section 132]

Arrest is the taking into custody of a person under lawful authority. Because arrest deprives a citizen of personal liberty (Article 21 of Constitution), the GST law has built extraordinary statutory thresholds:

Sole Authority

Commissioner Sanction Mandatory

Under Section 69(1), arrest can be authorized ONLY BY THE COMMISSIONER in writing. The Commissioner must have an objective "reason to believe" that the person has committed specified offences under Section 132.

Monetary Bar

Minimum â‚¹2 Crore Evasion Threshold

No arrest can be made under GST unless the amount of tax evaded, ITC wrongly availed/utilized, or refund wrongly taken EXCEEDS â‚¹2 CRORE (â‚¹200 Lakhs)!
Exception: Repeat offenders previously convicted under Section 132 can be arrested without any monetary limit.

The 4 Specified Offences Attracting Arrest [Section 132(1)(a) to (d)]:

  1. Supplying goods/services without issuing any invoice with intent to evade tax [Sec 132(1)(a)];
  2. Issuing invoice/bill without supply of goods/services leading to wrongful ITC or refund (Bogus Invoicing / Fake Firms) [Sec 132(1)(b)];
  3. Availing ITC using fake invoices or fraudulently availing ITC without any invoice [Sec 132(1)(c)];
  4. Collecting tax from customers but failing to deposit with the Government beyond 3 months from due date [Sec 132(1)(d)].

8. Power to Summon Persons [Section 70]

Under Section 70, the proper officer has the power to summon any person whose attendance he considers necessary either to give evidence or to produce a document or any other thing in any inquiry in the same manner as a Civil Court under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (CPC).

Nature of Proceedings

Deemed Judicial Proceedings

Proceedings before the summoning officer are deemed to be judicial proceedings within the meaning of Sections 229 & 267 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS) [erstwhile Sec 193 & 228 IPC]. Giving false evidence or fabricating records attracts criminal prosecution.

Legal Obligations

Duty to Attend & Speak Truth

All persons summoned are legally bound to attend (in person or through authorised representative as directed) and are bound to state the truth. Statements recorded during summons are admissible in prosecution under Section 136.

CBIC Guidelines on Summons (Instructions dated 17.08.2022):
  • Summons should NOT be issued routinely where documents are already available on the GST portal.
  • Senior management (CEOs, CFOs, Managing Directors) should NOT be summoned mechanically unless there is clear evidence of their direct personal involvement in the decision-making of the tax fraud.
  • Asking for evidence does NOT authorize calling for the "entire set of books of account"; summons must specify the exact documents required.
  • Non-attendance without reasonable justification attracts penalty under Section 122(3)(d) up to â‚¹25,000 and prosecution for absconding under BNS/IPC.

9. Access to Business Premises (Sec 71) & Assistance (Sec 72)

Section 71

Access to Business Premises

An officer authorized by a Joint Commissioner shall have access to any business premises of a registered person to inspect books of accounts, computers, software, networks, trial balances, and audited financial statements. The taxpayer must furnish records within 15 working days.

Section 72

Officers Bound to Assist

All officers of Police, Railways, Customs, State Revenue (VAT/Excise), Village Officers, Postal Authorities, and Banking Companies are statutorily bound to assist GST officers in the execution of their statutory duties upon requisition.

10. ICAI Test Your Knowledge (TYK) - Complete Solved Problems

ICAI TYK 1 (Reason to Believe Jurisprudence)

Question:

What is meant by 'reason to believe'?

Answer:

'Reason to believe' means having knowledge of facts which, although not amounting to direct mathematical proof, would cause a reasonable person, knowing the same facts, to reasonably conclude that an offence or evasion has taken place.

  • Under Section 26 of the Indian Penal Code / BNS, a person is said to have 'reason to believe' a thing, if he has sufficient cause to believe that thing, but not otherwise.
  • 'Reason to believe' contemplates an objective determination based on intelligent care and evaluation as distinguished from a purely subjective whim or personal suspicion.
  • It is more than mere suspicion, conjecture, or gossip, but less than conclusive evidence in possession about violation of the law.
  • It must be held in good faith by an honest and reasonable officer based on relevant, credible, and objective material.
ICAI TYK 2 (Search and Seizure Safeguards)

Question:

What are the safeguards provided in the GST law in respect of search and seizure?

Answer:

Under Section 67 of the CGST Act, 2017 read with the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (CrPC) / BNSS, the following safeguards are guaranteed:

  1. Written Authorization: Search can be carried out only under a valid written authorization (Search Warrant in Form GST INS-01) issued by an officer of the rank of Joint Commissioner or above who has "reason to believe".
  2. Presence of Independent Witnesses: The search must be conducted in the presence of at least two independent, respectable inhabitants (Panchas) of the locality.
  3. Panchnama & Seizure Order: A Panchnama describing all happenings and an inventory of all seized goods/documents must be drawn up on the spot and signed by witnesses and the occupant. A seizure order in Form GST INS-02 must be served.
  4. Personal Search of Officers: The searching officers must offer their own personal search to the occupant before commencing and after concluding the search.
  5. Copies of Seized Records: The taxpayer has a statutory right to make copies or take extracts of seized books/documents in terms of Section 67(5).
  6. Mandatory 6-Month Return Rule [Section 67(7)]: If no show-cause notice is issued within 6 months of seizure (extendable by 6 months), the seized goods must be returned to the person from whom seized!
ICAI TYK 3 (Circumstances for Inspection)

Question:

What are the circumstances under which inspection can be carried out?

Answer:

Under Section 67(1), an inspection can be carried out by a proper officer only upon written authorization by an officer of the rank of Joint Commissioner or above where he has reasons to believe that:

  • (A) A taxable person has:
    1. Suppressed any transaction of supply of goods or services or both;
    2. Suppressed the stock of goods in hand;
    3. Claimed input tax credit in excess of his entitlement; or
    4. Contravened any provision of the Act to evade tax.
  • (B) Any person engaged in transporting goods or operating a warehouse/godown has:
    1. Kept goods which have escaped payment of tax; or
    2. Kept accounts or goods in a manner likely to cause evasion of tax.
ICAI TYK 4 (Authorization of Search and Seizure)

Question:

Who can authorize search and seizure and under what circumstances?

Answer:

Under Section 67(2) of the CGST Act, 2017, an officer of the rank of Joint Commissioner or above can authorize in writing (Form GST INS-01) any proper officer subordinate to him to search any place and seize goods, documents, books, or things. The Joint Commissioner himself can also carry out search and seizure.

Circumstances: Such authorization can be given only when the Joint Commissioner has "reasons to believe" that:

  • Any goods liable to confiscation under the Act are secreted in any place; OR
  • Any documents, books, or things which in his opinion shall be useful for or relevant to any proceedings under the Act are secreted in any place.
ICAI TYK 5 (Powers of Officer during Search)

Question:

What powers can an officer exercise during search?

Answer:

An officer conducting search under Section 67(2) can exercise the following statutory powers:

  1. Search and Seize: Search for and seize goods liable to confiscation and documents, books, or things relevant for proceedings;
  2. Break Open: Break open the door of any premises, or break open any almirah, electronic safe, or box if access to the same is denied;
  3. Seal Premises: Seal any premises or storage unit where access is denied or custody cannot be taken immediately;
  4. Prohibition Order [Section 67(2) Proviso]: Where it is not practicable to seize goods, serve a prohibition order in Form GST INS-03 directing the custodian not to remove or deal with the goods;
  5. Immediate Disposal: Order immediate disposal of perishable or hazardous goods under Section 67(8).
ICAI TYK 6 (Responsibilities under Summons)

Question:

What are the responsibilities of a person who is issued summons?

Answer:

Under Section 70 of the CGST Act, 2017, a person who is issued summons has the following mandatory legal obligations:

  • Duty to Attend: The person is legally bound to attend before the summoning officer either in person or through an authorised representative as directed;
  • Duty to Speak the Truth: The person is bound to state the truth upon any subject matter respecting which he is examined or make statements;
  • Duty to Produce Documents: The person must produce such documents, electronic records, or things as required by the summons;
  • Consequence of Default: Since the proceeding is deemed to be a judicial proceeding, failure to appear without justifiable cause, giving false evidence, or absconding attracts monetary penalty up to â‚¹25,000 under Section 122(3)(d) and criminal prosecution under Sections 229 & 267 of BNS / Sections 193 & 228 of IPC.
ICAI TYK 7 (Meaning of Arrest)

Question:

What is the meaning of arrest?

Answer:

The term 'arrest' is not defined in the CGST Act, 2017. However, under judicial interpretation and common legal jurisprudence:

  • Arrest denotes "the taking into custody of a person under some lawful command or authority".
  • In other words, a person is said to be arrested when he is apprehended by an officer empowered by law and restrained of his personal liberty by power or colour of a lawful warrant.
  • Under GST, arrest is an exceptional administrative power exercised under Section 69 only upon written authorization from the Commissioner for specified grave tax offences exceeding â‚¹2 Crore.
ICAI TYK 8 (Circumstances of Arrest & Monetary Limits)

Question:

Under what circumstances can an officer arrest a person under GST law?

Answer:

Under Section 69(1) of the CGST Act, 2017, the Commissioner can authorize any officer to arrest a person if he has "reasons to believe" that the person has committed an offence punishable under Section 132(1)(a), (b), (c), (d) or Section 132(2):

  1. Specified Offences:
    • Supply of goods/services without invoice with intent to evade tax;
    • Issue of invoice without any supply (bogus billing);
    • Availing ITC using fake invoices or without receipt of goods/services;
    • Tax collected from customers but not deposited with Government beyond 3 months.
  2. Monetary Limit: The amount of tax evaded, ITC wrongly availed/utilized, or refund wrongly taken MUST EXCEED â‚¹2 CRORE (â‚¹200 Lakhs).
  3. Repeat Offenders: If a person is convicted of an offence again (even after prior conviction), he can be arrested irrespective of the monetary limit involved [Section 132(2)].
  4. Bail Rules:
    • If amount > â‚¹5 Crore: The offence is Cognizable and Non-Bailable; must be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours.
    • If amount is between â‚¹2 Crore and â‚¹5 Crore: The offence is Non-Cognizable and Bailable; Assistant/Deputy Commissioner can grant bail.
