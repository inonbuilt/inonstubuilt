Import and Export under GST

Comprehensive Master Notes: Cross-Border Taxability, Section 7(2) & 7(5) Deemed Inter-State Supplies, Customs Tariff Act Section 3(7) Interface, Five Mandatory Tests for Export of Services, Zero-Rating Architecture under Section 16 IGST, Foreign Exchange Realization Clawback (Rule 96B), Schedule III Non-Taxable High Sea Sales, Merchant Exports @ 0.1%, Deemed Exports Section 147, and All Solved ICAI Practical Case Studies.

1. Cross-Border Tax Architecture

GST is a destination-based consumption tax. In international trade, the GST framework is designed around two bedrock macroeconomic objectives:

Imports

Level Playing Field for Domestic Industry

All imports into India are treated as inter-State supplies [Section 7(2) & Section 7(5)(a) of IGST Act]. Imported goods and services attract Integrated GST (IGST) equivalent to the domestic GST rate, ensuring domestic producers are not disadvantaged against foreign goods.

Exports

Zero-Rating (Destination Principle)

India follows the internationally accepted tax principle of "exporting goods and services, not exporting taxes". Exports of goods and services are classified as Zero-Rated Supplies [Section 16(1) of IGST Act], allowing complete tax neutralization across the supply chain.

2. Import of Goods [Section 2(10) IGST & Valuation]

Under Section 2(10) of the IGST Act, "import of goods" means bringing goods into India from a place outside India.

The Dual Legal Mechanism for Taxing Imported Goods: While IGST on imported goods is charged under Section 5(1) proviso of the IGST Act, it is levied and collected at the customs port under the machinery of the Customs Act, 1962 read with Section 3(7) of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975 (CTA) at the time when duties of customs are levied on the said goods (i.e., at the time of filing the Bill of Entry for home consumption).

Valuation for IGST on Imported Goods:

Statutory Formula for Assessable Value for IGST [Sec 3(7) CTA]:
$$\text{Taxable Value for IGST} = \text{Assessable Value under Customs Act (CIF)} + \text{Basic Customs Duty (BCD)} + \text{Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS)} + \text{Any other applicable Customs Duty}$$

• Note: IGST and GST Compensation Cess are NOT added into the assessable base for computing IGST.
• Input Tax Credit (ITC): The importer can avail full ITC of the IGST paid on import of goods on the basis of the Bill of Entry via ICEGATE integration into Form GSTR-2B.

3. Import of Services [Section 2(11) IGST & RCM]

Under Section 2(11) of the IGST Act, a supply of service qualifies as an "import of services" only when ALL THREE cumulative conditions are met:

Condition 1

Supplier Location

The supplier of the service is located outside India.

Condition 2

Recipient Location

The recipient of the service is located in India.

Condition 3

Place of Supply

The place of supply of service is in India (determined u/s 13 IGST Act).

Nature of Import Transaction Governing Section Taxability & RCM Status
Import of Service WITH Consideration for Business Purpose Section 7(1)(b) CGST Taxable Supply. Recipient pays IGST under Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) [Section 5(3) IGST].
Import of Service WITH Consideration for PERSONAL Purpose Section 7(1)(b) CGST Taxable Supply. If received by unregistered individual (NTOR) for OIDAR / online gaming → Foreign supplier pays under Forward Charge. If non-OIDAR → Exempt under Notification 9/2017-IT(R).
Import of Service WITHOUT Consideration from Related/Distinct Person Section 7(1)(c) read with Schedule I, Para 4 Taxable Supply provided it is in the course or furtherance of business. Recipient pays IGST under RCM.
Import of Service WITHOUT Consideration from Unrelated Person Outside Scope NOT a supply under GST (No consideration and not covered in Schedule I).

4. Export of Goods [Section 2(5) IGST]

Under Section 2(5) of the IGST Act, "export of goods" means taking goods out of India to a place outside India.

Physical Movement is Indispensable: For a transaction to qualify as an export of goods, physical movement of the goods crossing the geographical boundary of India is mandatory.
• If an Indian seller sells goods to a foreign buyer (e.g., UAE company), but on instructions of the foreign buyer delivers the goods to a company in Karnataka → NOT an export of goods! (It is an intra-State or inter-State domestic supply).

5. Export of Services [Section 2(6) IGST - 5 Cumulative Conditions]

Unlike export of goods, services are intangible. Section 2(6) of the IGST Act prescribes FIVE CUMULATIVE CONDITIONS that must all be concurrently fulfilled for a supply of service to qualify as an "export of services":

No. Statutory Condition [Section 2(6)] Practical Exam Implication & Judicial Dicta
1 The supplier of service is located in India. Has a registered place of business or fixed establishment in India.
2 The recipient of service is located outside India. Foreign entity or foreign resident having business/residence outside India.
3 The place of supply of service is outside India. Determined strictly in accordance with Section 13 of the IGST Act. (Crucial: If POS falls in India under Section 13(3) [performance-based] or Section 13(8) [intermediary services/banking], it FAILS this test and is NOT an export of service!).
4 Payment received in convertible foreign exchange (or Indian Rupees where permitted by RBI). Realization in INR through Special Rupee Vostro Accounts (SRVA) or for exports to Nepal/Bhutan as permitted by RBI is valid.
5 Supplier and recipient are NOT merely establishments of a distinct person. Supply of service between an Indian head office and its foreign branch office (or vice versa) is treated as supply between distinct persons under Explanation 1 to Section 8; hence, it does NOT qualify as export of services.

6. Zero-Rated Supplies [Section 16 IGST Act]

Under Section 16(1) of the IGST Act, "zero rated supply" means any of the following supplies of goods or services or both:

  1. Export of goods or services or both; or
  2. Supply of goods or services or both for authorised operations to a Special Economic Zone (SEZ) developer or an SEZ unit.
Zero-Rating vs Exempt Supplies [Section 16(2)]: Section 16(2) provides that Input Tax Credit (ITC) may be availed for making zero-rated supplies, notwithstanding that such supply may be an exempt supply.
• Normal exempt supply → ITC blocked under Section 17(2).
• Zero-rated exempt supply → Full ITC available and refundable!
SEZ Supplies - Intra-State or Inter-State? [Section 7(5)(b)]: Under Section 7(5)(b) of the IGST Act, supply of goods or services to or by an SEZ developer or SEZ unit is mandatorily treated as an INTER-STATE SUPPLY, even if the supplier and the SEZ are located within the same State! (e.g., hotel conferencing services provided by a Mumbai hotel to a Mumbai SEZ unit is an inter-State supply).

7. Procedural Routes for Zero-Rating & Rule 96B Recovery

Section 16(3) & 16(4) of the IGST Act provide two distinct procedural routes for effecting zero-rated supplies:

Route 1 (Universal Default)

Supply under Bond / LUT without Tax

The exporter exports goods/services under a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) in Form GST RFD-11 without paying IGST, and claims a refund of unutilized Input Tax Credit under Section 54(3) of the CGST Act read with Rule 89(4).

Route 2 (Restricted Exception)

Supply with Payment of IGST

Permitted ONLY for notified class of persons or notified goods/services. Exporter pays IGST and claims refund of the IGST paid under Section 54 read with Rule 96. Prohibited for: Goods subjected to export duty and pan masala/tobacco products (Notfn 01/2023-IT).

Statutory Modalities of Letter of Undertaking (LUT) [Notification 37/2017-CT & Cir. 8/8/2017]:

  • Annual Validity: A single LUT submitted online in Form GST RFD-11 is valid for the entire financial year. A separate LUT is NOT required for every export shipment!
  • Eligibility: Extended to all registered exporters, except those prosecuted for an offence under GST/customs involving tax evasion > â‚¹2.5 Crore.
  • Timelines for Export under LUT:
    • Goods: Must be exported out of India within 3 months from the invoice date.
    • Services: Foreign exchange must be realized within 1 year from the invoice date.
    • Default: If not exported/realized within time, IGST along with Section 50 interest must be paid within 15 days!
Mandatory Foreign Exchange Recovery [Rule 96B of CGST Rules]: Where refund of unutilized ITC or IGST paid has been granted to an exporter on export of goods, and the sale proceeds in respect of such goods are NOT realized within the FEMA period (normally 9 months):
• The exporter shall deposit back the amount of refund along with interest under Section 50 within 30 days of the expiry of the period.
• Relief: If proceeds are realized later within extended RBI permission, the deposited amount is re-refunded to the exporter.

8. Special Cross-Border Transactions

Schedule III, Para 8(b)

High Sea Sales

Supply of goods by the consignee to any other person, by endorsement of documents of title to the goods, after the goods have been dispatched from the port of origin outside India but before clearance for home consumption is treated as NEITHER A SUPPLY OF GOODS NOR A SUPPLY OF SERVICES. No GST is payable at the time of sale; customs duty and IGST are paid once by the ultimate buyer upon filing the Bill of Entry.

Schedule III, Para 8(a)

Out-and-Out Supplies (Merchant Trading)

Supply of goods from a place in the non-taxable territory to another place in the non-taxable territory without such goods entering into India (e.g., goods bought in China and shipped directly to Germany). Explicitly excluded from GST under Schedule III; zero GST liability!

Merchant Exports @ Concessional Rate of 0.1% [Notification No. 40/2017-CT(R) & 41/2017-IT(R)]:

To support merchant exporters who procure goods domestically and export them, domestic manufacturers can supply goods to a merchant exporter at a nominal tax rate of 0.1% (0.05% CGST + 0.05% SGST or 0.1% IGST) subject to mandatory conditions:

  • The registered recipient (merchant exporter) must export the goods within 90 days from the date of the tax invoice.
  • The merchant exporter must mention the GSTIN and invoice number of the supplier on the Shipping Bill / Bill of Export.
  • The merchant exporter must be registered with an Export Promotion Council or Commodity Board.
  • Goods must be moved directly from the supplier to the port/ICD/warehouse.
  • The domestic manufacturer can claim refund of unutilized ITC accumulated due to inverted duty structure under Section 54(3).

9. Deemed Exports [Section 147 & Notification 48/2017-CT]

Under Section 147 of the CGST Act, the Government has notified certain supplies of manufactured goods where the goods do not physically leave India, but are treated as "deemed exports" because they substitute imports or support export manufacturing:

Category Description of Notified Deemed Export Supplies
1 Supply of goods by a registered person against Advance Authorisation (AA).
2 Supply of capital goods by a registered person against Export Promotion Capital Goods (EPCG) Authorisation.
3 Supply of goods by a registered person to an Export Oriented Unit (EOU) / EHTP / STP / BTP.
4 Supply of gold by a Bank / PSU against Advance Authorisation.
Crucial Distinction: Deemed Exports vs Zero-Rated Supplies: • Deemed exports are NOT zero-rated supplies. Full applicable GST must be charged on the tax invoice.
• However, to relieve the tax burden, either the recipient EOU/AA holder or the supplier (upon receiving an undertaking from recipient not to claim refund) can claim refund of the GST paid under Section 54.

10. ICAI Test Your Knowledge (TYK) - Complete Solved Problems

ICAI TYK 1 (Taxation of Imports)

Question:

Explain how imports are taxed under GST.

Answer:

Under the GST regime, all imports of goods and services are deemed as inter-State supplies in terms of Section 7(2) and Section 7(5)(a) of the IGST Act, 2017, and are subjected to Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST):

  • Import of Goods: Levied under Section 5(1) proviso of the IGST Act, but collected under the machinery of the Customs Act, 1962 read with Section 3(7) of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975 at the customs port upon filing of the Bill of Entry for home consumption. IGST is calculated on the customs assessable value plus BCD and applicable customs surcharges.
  • Import of Services: Taxed under Section 5(1) of the IGST Act under the Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) in terms of Section 5(3) read with Notification No. 10/2017-Integrated Tax (Rate), where the recipient located in India is liable to pay IGST. (Exception: OIDAR services to unregistered persons where the foreign supplier pays under forward charge).
  • Input Tax Credit: IGST paid on imports is eligible for full Input Tax Credit (ITC) to registered persons for set-off against output tax liabilities.
ICAI TYK 2 (Taxation of Exports)

Question:

Describe how exports are taxed under GST.

Answer:

Exports of goods and services are classified as Zero-Rated Supplies under Section 16(1) of the IGST Act. Under zero-rating, the entire supply chain is freed from taxes:

  • Route 1 (Default): The exporter can export goods or services under a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) / Bond without payment of IGST and claim a refund of unutilized Input Tax Credit (ITC) under Section 54(3) of the CGST Act read with Rule 89(4).
  • Route 2 (Restricted): In case of notified classes of persons or notified goods/services, the exporter may export on payment of IGST and claim refund of the IGST paid under Section 54 read with Rule 96.
ICAI TYK 3 (Bond vs LUT Requirement)

Question:

Is it necessary to execute a bond for effecting zero-rated supplies? Elucidate.

Answer:

No, it is not mandatory to execute a bond with a bank guarantee.

Vide Notification No. 37/2017-Central Tax dated 04.10.2017 and Circular No. 8/8/2017-GST, the facility of furnishing a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) in place of a bond has been extended to all registered taxpayers who intend to supply goods or services for export or to an SEZ developer/unit without payment of integrated tax.

The only exception where an exporter cannot execute an LUT (and must execute a bond with bank guarantee) is where the exporter has been prosecuted for any offence under the CGST Act, IGST Act, or any existing law where the amount of tax evaded exceeds â‚¹250 Lakhs (â‚¹2.5 Crore).

ICAI TYK 4 (Third-Party Delivery within India)

Question:

A Ltd. enters into an agreement for sale of goods with B Ltd., a company based in UAE. B Ltd. requires the goods to be delivered by A Ltd. to C Ltd., a company based in Karnataka. Whether the transaction will qualify as an export of goods under GST? Analyze the scenario and offer your comments.

Answer:

Under Section 2(5) of the IGST Act, 2017, "export of goods" means taking goods out of India to a place outside India.

In the present case, although the contract of sale is with a foreign entity (B Ltd. in UAE) and payment may be received in foreign exchange, the goods do not physically leave the territory of India. The goods are delivered locally to C Ltd. in Karnataka.

Conclusion: Since the goods remain within India, the statutory condition of taking goods out of India to a place outside India is not satisfied. Consequently, the transaction does NOT qualify as an export of goods under GST. It will be treated as a normal domestic supply (intra-State or inter-State depending on the location of A Ltd. and Karnataka).

ICAI TYK 5 (ITC on Zero-Rated Exempt Supplies)

Question:

A Ltd. is making zero-rated supplies which are also specifically exempt from GST. The company has paid input tax of â‚¹2,00,000 on inputs and input services which have been used exclusively in effecting such zero-rated supplies. Examine if A Ltd. can avail ITC of input tax of â‚¹2,00,000.

Answer:

Under Section 16(2) of the IGST Act, 2017, credit of input tax may be availed for making zero-rated supplies, notwithstanding that such supply may be an exempt supply.

While Section 17(2) of the CGST Act normally blocks ITC on inputs and input services used for making exempt supplies, Section 16(2) of the IGST Act explicitly overrides Section 17(2) in respect of zero-rated supplies.

Conclusion: A Ltd. is eligible to avail the full ITC of â‚¹2,00,000 paid on inputs and input services used exclusively in effecting the zero-rated exempt supplies. Further, A Ltd. can claim a refund of this unutilized ITC under Section 54(3) of the CGST Act read with Rule 89(4).

ICAI TYK 6 (Same-State Supplies to SEZ)

Question:

Whether services of short-term accommodation, conferencing, banqueting etc. provided to an SEZ unit/developer by a supplier located in the same State as that of the SEZ unit/developer should be treated as an inter-State supply under Section 7(5)(b) or an intra-State supply in terms of Section 8(2) read with Section 12(3)(c)? Examine.

Answer:

Section 7(5)(b) of the IGST Act, 2017 provides that supply of goods or services or both to or by a Special Economic Zone developer or a Special Economic Zone unit shall be treated to be a supply of goods or services or both in the course of inter-State trade or commerce.

Section 7(5)(b) begins with a non-obstante effect over Section 8 and general place of supply provisions. Circular No. 48/22/2018-GST has clarified that services provided to an SEZ developer or SEZ unit are zero-rated supplies and shall always be treated as inter-State supplies under Section 7(5)(b), irrespective of the physical location of the supplier and place of supply falling in the same State under Section 12(3)(c).

Conclusion: Such accommodation and banqueting services are inter-State supplies subject to IGST (or zero-rated under LUT/bond if procured for authorized operations).

ICAI TYK 7 (Personal Import of Online Gaming)

Question:

Mr. Amar Kant, a Chartered Accountant (partner in a GST registered firm), purchases online gaming software electronically for his son from a company located in USA. He makes the payment from his personal bank account. Examine whether the transaction will be liable to GST. If yes, in whose hands will the tax liability arise?

Answer:

1. Taxability under GST:

  • Under Section 7(1)(b) of the CGST Act, import of services for a consideration is treated as a supply whether or not in the course or furtherance of business. Hence, the transaction is a taxable supply.
  • Online gaming software downloaded electronically from outside India qualifies as Online Information and Database Access or Retrieval (OIDAR) / Online Money Gaming services under Section 2(17) of the IGST Act.

2. Person Liable to Pay Tax:

  • Mr. Amar Kant received the service for personal use of his son and paid from his personal bank account; hence, he is a Non-Taxable Online Recipient (NTOR) under Section 2(16) of the IGST Act.
  • Under the proviso to Section 5(1) and Section 14 of the IGST Act, in case of OIDAR / online money gaming services supplied by a person located in non-taxable territory to an NTOR, the supplier located in the non-taxable territory (the USA company) is liable to pay IGST under forward charge.

Conclusion: The transaction is liable to GST. The tax liability will arise in the hands of the USA-based supplier company, which must obtain a simplified single registration in India under Rule 14 and discharge the IGST liability.

ICAI TYK 8 (Validity of LUT)

Question:

â€˜Separate LUT is to be furnished for every export supply.â€™ With reference to the provisions of the GST law, examine the veracity or otherwise of the statement.

Answer:

The statement is FALSE (Incorrect).

As per Circular No. 8/8/2017-GST dated 04.10.2017 and Rule 96A of the CGST Rules, 2017, the Letter of Undertaking (LUT) in Form GST RFD-11 is furnished on the common portal and is valid for the whole financial year in which it is tendered.

An exporter is NOT required to furnish an LUT for each individual export consignment or supply. Once an LUT is accepted for a financial year, all zero-rated supplies of goods or services during that entire financial year can be made under the cover of that single LUT.

ICAI TYK 9 (High Sea Sales Taxation)

Question:

AXT Ltd. entered into a high sea sale transaction with BYU Ltd. for certain goods. AXT Ltd. is of the view that GST on such sale transaction is payable at the time of such sale and basic customs duty is payable at the time of filing the bill of entry for import of goods. Examine whether the view taken by AXT Ltd. is correct.

Answer:

The view taken by AXT Ltd. is INCORRECT.

1. Statutory Legal Provision [Schedule III, Para 8(b)]:

Paragraph 8(b) of Schedule III to the CGST Act, 2017 provides that supply of goods by the consignee to any other person, by endorsement of documents of title to the goods, after the goods have been dispatched from the port of origin located outside India but before clearance for home consumption (popularly known as High Sea Sales) shall be treated as NEITHER A SUPPLY OF GOODS NOR A SUPPLY OF SERVICES.

2. Point of Taxation:

  • No GST (CGST, SGST, or IGST) is leviable on the high sea sale transaction between AXT Ltd. and BYU Ltd. at the time of the sale/transfer of documents.
  • All duties of customsâ€”including Basic Customs Duty (BCD) and Integrated GST (IGST) under Section 3(7) of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975â€”shall be collected at the time of clearance of goods for home consumption when the final buyer (BYU Ltd.) files the Bill of Entry with Customs Authorities.
  • Valuation: The customs assessable value for charging BCD and IGST is the high sea sales price paid by the final importer (BYU Ltd.).
