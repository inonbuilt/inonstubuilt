CONCEPTUAL OVERVIEW

1. Statutory Architecture & Objective of Warehousing

Warehousing is a specialized trade-facilitation mechanism codified under Chapter IX (Sections 57 to 73A) of the Customs Act, 1962. It allows an importer to defer the payment of customs duty by depositing imported dutiable goods into a licensed customs bonded warehouse without paying duty at the port of import.

Strategic & Commercial Utility of Warehousing

  • Working Capital Preservation: Avoids immediate cash outflow of customs duty upon cargo arrival, permitting duty payment only when goods are actually cleared for home consumption in staggered batches.
  • Re-Export & Duty-Free Outflow: Goods meant for re-export, supplies to foreign-going vessels/aircraft (ship stores), or duty-free shops (DFS) can be deposited and subsequently exported under Section 69 without ever paying import duty.
  • Supply Chain Buffer: Accommodates bulk shipments where minimum order quantities or freight economies exceed immediate factory absorption capacity.
  • In-Bond Manufacturing (MOOWR): Enables duty-free import of raw materials and capital goods to manufacture finished products in bond under Section 65.

STATUTORY DEFINITIONS [SECTION 2]

• Warehouse [Section 2(43)]: A public warehouse licensed u/s 57, a private warehouse licensed u/s 58, or a special warehouse licensed u/s 58A.
• Warehoused Goods [Section 2(44)]: Goods deposited in a warehouse pursuant to an order passed under Section 60.

SECTIONS 57, 58, 58A, 58B

2. Tripartite Classification of Warehouses & License Cancellation

Following the modernization of customs warehousing provisions, warehouses are bifurcated into record-based control warehouses (Public & Private) and physical customs control warehouses (Special Warehouses).

Licensing Process & Record Controls

Under Public and Private Warehouse Licensing Regulations, the applicant must establish solvency, secure premises, fire safety compliance, and provide computerized record-keeping software accessible to customs authorities. Licensees must appoint a Warehouse Keeper with suitable customs clearance experience.

Cancellation of License [Section 58B]

Where a licensee breaches any condition of the license or contravenes the Act, the PC/Commr may cancel the license after issuing a Show Cause Notice (SCN) giving reasonable opportunity of being heard. The license may be suspended during the pendency of inquiry. Upon cancellation, warehoused goods must be removed for home consumption, exported, or transferred within 7 days.

SECTIONS 59 & 60

3. Warehousing Bond & Permission for Deposit

Before any imported dutiable goods can be removed from the port of import to a warehouse, the importer must satisfy two mandatory statutory conditions under Section 59 and Section 60.

Warehousing Bond [Section 59]

Bond Quantum: The importer shall execute a bond binding himself in a sum equal to THRICE (3x) the amount of total duty assessed on such goods.

Conditions of the Bond:

  • Observe all provisions of the Customs Act, rules, and regulations.
  • Pay on or before the date specified in notice of demand all duties and interest payable under Section 61(2).
  • Pay all penalties and fines incurred for contravention of provisions.
General (Continuity) Bond: Assistant / Deputy Commissioner (AC/DC) may permit an importer to execute a general bond covering multiple consignments warehoused over a specified period.

Permission for Deposit [Section 60]

Upon execution of the bond under Section 59, the proper officer makes an order permitting the removal of goods from the customs station for deposit in a warehouse.

Crucial Date for Interest Computation:
The date on which the Section 60 order is passed by the proper officer marks the statutory baseline. The 90-day interest-free period under Section 61(2) begins from this date (and NOT from the date of filing the into-bond Bill of Entry).

Into-Bond Bill of Entry Procedural Flow

Step 1: Into-Bond BE

Importer presents Bill of Entry for Warehousing (Yellow BE) u/s 46.

Step 2: Assessment

Duty is assessed u/s 17/18. Exchange rate fixed as on date of Into-Bond BE [Sec 14].

Step 3: Triple Duty Bond

Importer executes Section 59 bond for 3x assessed duty with security.

Step 4: Section 60 Order

Proper officer issues order permitting removal from port to warehouse under escort/seal.

Mechanics of Interest Computation under Section 61(2)

Interest = [Customs Duty Payable at Ex-Bond Clearance] × [15 / 100] × [No. of Default Days / 365]

Where Default Days: Number of days from the expiry of 90 days (starting from day 91) up to the date of actual payment of customs duty on ex-bonding.
Benchmark Date: The 90-day period begins from the date of Section 60 order, NOT the into-bond filing date!

Landmark Judicial Precedents on Warehousing Interest

1. Kesoram Rayon v. Collector of Customs [1996 (86) ELT 464 (SC)] SUPREME COURT BENCHMARK

Legal Principle: Goods which are not removed from the warehouse within the permissible statutory period under Section 61 are deemed to be improperly removed under Section 72 on the very day the permissible period expires.
Applicable Rate of Duty: The rate of basic customs duty applicable is the rate prevalent on the date of expiry of the permissible warehousing period (the date of deemed improper removal), NOT the subsequent date of actual physical clearance! However, interest continues to accrue until the actual date of duty payment.

2. Pratibha Processors v. Union of India [1996 (88) ELT 12 (SC)] ACCESSORY PRINCIPLE

Legal Principle: Interest under Section 61(2) is merely an accessory to the principal duty. If at the time of clearance the imported goods are totally exempt from customs duty or are re-exported without payment of duty under Section 69, no interest can be demanded, because if principal duty is Nil, accessory interest is also Nil.

3. CCus v. Biecco Lawrie Ltd. [2008 (223) ELT 3 (SC)] SECTION 49 STORAGE

Legal Principle: Where ex-bond Bill of Entry is presented, duty is paid, and an out-of-charge order is issued under Section 68, but goods are retained in the warehouse on applicant's request under Section 49 pending delivery, they cease to be 'warehoused goods'. Any subsequent increase in customs duty rate prior to physical exit cannot be demanded by the Revenue.

SECTION 64

5. Owner's Statutory Rights to Deal with Warehoused Goods

Although the warehouse keeper holds physical custody, the legal title and ownership of the goods remain vested in the importer. Section 64 codifies the specific rights exercisable by the owner during the warehousing tenure:

1. Inspect the Goods

Examine condition, check packaging integrity, and verify contents.

2. Deal with Containers

Open packages, sort contents, and repack into new packages to prevent damage or loss.

3. Display for Sale

Show the warehoused goods to prospective prospective domestic or international purchasers.

4. Take Trade Samples

Take reasonable trade samples; if removed outside the warehouse, applicable duty must be paid.

SECTIONS 65, 65A & 66

6. Manufacture and Other Operations in Warehouse (MOOWR)

Section 65 provides a landmark flagship manufacturing scheme in India: Manufacture and Other Operations in Warehouse Regulations (MOOWR), 2019/2020. It permits carrying out manufacturing processes, assembly, or value addition on imported dutiable goods inside a private bonded warehouse.

Core Benefits of MOOWR Scheme

Duty Deferment:
Full deferment of BCD + IGST on imported capital goods and raw materials.
Zero Interest Burden:
Section 61(2) explicitly provides NIL interest for goods deposited in Sec 65 warehouse.
Indefinite Warehousing:
Capital goods remain warehoused till clearance; inputs till consumption/clearance.
Single Digital Approval:
Common application for warehouse license (Sec 58) and MOOWR permission (Sec 65).
Outflow Destination Duty Consequences on Capital Goods Duty Consequences on Raw Materials / Resulting Goods
Export of Resulting Finished Goods No duty payable during usage; upon export of capital goods, zero duty. 100% Remission of Customs Duty (BCD + IGST) on raw materials incorporated in exported goods.
Domestic Clearance (DTA Ex-Bonding) Duty payable on capital goods at depreciated value if cleared to DTA. Duty payable on imported raw materials contained in finished goods cleared to DTA (or finished goods duty if opted), PLUS domestic GST (CGST+SGST or IGST).
Waste and Refuse Generated in Process N/A • If Destroyed: Duty on raw materials in waste is remitted.
• If Cleared to DTA: Duty charged on the quantity of imported goods contained in such waste/refuse.

Status of Section 65A (Inserted vide Finance Act, 2023)

The Finance Act, 2023 inserted prospective Section 65A requiring upfront payment of IGST and GST Compensation Cess on goods brought for operations in warehouse (while basic customs duty remains deferred). However, as per ICAI Study Material, Section 65A has NOT been notified till 30.04.2026. Therefore, for May 2027 / Nov 2026-2027 examinations, the existing scheme (full deferment of BCD + IGST without upfront payment) continues to govern.

SECTIONS 67, 68 & 69

7. Warehouse Removals: Transfers, Home Consumption & Exports

Goods once deposited in a warehouse can only be removed through three lawful statutory channels sanctioned by the Act:

SECTION 67

Warehouse to Warehouse Transfer

Owner may remove goods from one warehouse to another warehouse (public/private/special) in the same port or another customs station:

  • Filing transfer application.
  • Execution of transport bond / transit security.
  • Transfer under one-time lock (OTL) or customs escort.
  • Period of warehousing counted from original Sec 60 order date.
SECTION 68

Clearance for Home Consumption

Removal for domestic consumption upon fulfilling four statutory conditions:

  • Filing of Ex-Bond Bill of Entry (Green BE).
  • Rate of duty & tariff valuation applicable as on date of presentation of ex-bond BE [Sec 15(1)(b)].
  • Payment of import duty, penalties, and Section 61(2) interest.
  • Out-of-charge order passed by Proper Officer.
SECTION 69

Clearance for Direct Export

Warehoused goods may be exported without payment of import duty:

  • Presentation of Shipping Bill / Bill of Export.
  • Payment of export duty and warehouse rent/charges (if any).
  • Proper officer passes order for clearance for export.
  • Zero import duty and zero interest payable u/s 61(2) (*Pratibha Processors*).

Rate of Exchange vs Rate of Duty in Warehousing Operations

• Rate of Exchange [Section 14(1)]: Determined with reference to the CBIC notified rate in force on the date of presentation of Into-Bond Bill of Entry under Section 46.
• Rate of Duty [Section 15(1)(b)]: Determined with reference to the rate of customs duty in force on the date of presentation of Ex-Bond Bill of Entry under Section 68.

SECTIONS 70, 71, 72, 73, 73A

8. Volatile Goods Allowance, Improper Removal & Warehouse Custodian Liabilities

Allowance in Volatile Goods [Section 70]

When any warehoused goods are at the time of delivery found to be deficient in quantity on account of natural evaporation or leakage, the Assistant/Deputy Commissioner of Customs may remit the duty on such deficiency.

Notified Goods under Section 70: Aviation gasoline, motor spirit, kerosene, mineral turpentine, crude petroleum, aviation turbine fuel, liquid paraffin, and industrial benzene stored in bonded installations.

Improper Removal from Warehouse [Section 72]

In any of the following 3 circumstances, goods are treated as improperly removed:

  • Removed in contravention of Section 71 (without order/permission).
  • Not removed upon expiry of the permissible period under Section 61.
  • Goods under Section 59 bond not accounted for to officer's satisfaction.

Consequence: Proper officer issues notice demanding immediate payment of full duty, interest @ 15%, penalties, and rent/charges.

Section Statutory Mandate Legal & Financial Ramifications
Section 71 Restriction on Removal No goods shall be removed from a warehouse except on clearance for home consumption, re-export, or transfer, and only under lawful orders of the proper officer.
Section 73 Cancellation & Return of Bond When all goods covered by the Section 59 bond are fully cleared, exported, or transferred, and all duties/interest paid, the proper officer cancels the bond and delivers it to the licensee.
Section 73A Custody & Removal Responsibilities Warehouse Keeper is the Custodian: Goods remain in the custody of the licensee until clearance/transfer. If any goods are removed without permission or lost, the licensee is personally liable to pay full customs duty, interest, and penalties.

9. Solved ICAI Practical Case Studies & Questions

ICAI TYK Question 1: Commencement of 90-Day Interest-Free Window Statutory Validity

"Interest-free period of ninety (90) days under Section 61(2) in respect of warehoused goods commences from the date on which an into-bond bill of entry in respect of such goods is presented." Comment on the validity of the statement.

Detailed Statutory Answer:
The statement is INVALID.

Statutory Position: In accordance with Section 61(2) of the Customs Act, 1962, where any warehoused goods remain in a warehouse beyond a period of 90 days from the date on which the order permitting the deposit of goods in a warehouse under Section 60(1) is made, interest shall be payable at 15% p.a. on the amount of duty payable at the time of clearance of goods.

Conclusion: The relevant starting date for computing the 90-day interest-free period is the date of the order passed by the proper officer under Section 60(1) permitting removal of goods from the customs station to the warehouse, and NOT the date on which the into-bond Bill of Entry is presented by the importer.

ICAI TYK Question 2: Duty on Waste Arising in Bonded Manufacturing Section 65 MOOWR

"If manufacturing operations are carried out on warehoused goods and finished products are cleared for home consumption, then appropriate duty of customs should be levied on the quantity of the warehoused goods contained in the waste or refuse arising out of such manufacturing process." Examine the correctness of the statement.

Detailed Statutory Answer:
The statement is VALID.

Statutory Provisions under Section 65: Section 65(2) of the Customs Act, 1962 explicitly stipulates that where manufacturing or other operations are carried out in a bonded warehouse and the finished products are cleared for home consumption, customs duty shall be charged on the quantity of warehoused goods contained in the waste or refuse arising from such manufacturing operations.

Alternative if Destroyed: Only if such waste or refuse is destroyed in the presence of the proper officer or exported out of India can the customs duty leviable on the warehoused goods contained therein be remitted. Since here the finished goods are cleared for domestic consumption and waste is not destroyed, duty is fully leviable on the input quantity contained in the waste.

ICAI TYK Question 3: Enumeration of Circumstances of Improper Removal Section 72

Explain the circumstances under Section 72 of the Customs Act, 1962 under which warehoused goods are treated as improperly removed from a warehouse.

Detailed Statutory Answer:
In terms of Section 72(1) of the Customs Act, 1962, warehoused goods are deemed to be improperly removed from a warehouse under any of the following three specific statutory circumstances:
  1. Contravention of Section 71: Where any warehoused goods are removed from a warehouse without an order of clearance passed by the proper officer for home consumption, export, or transfer.
  2. Expiration of Permitted Period: Where any warehoused goods have not been removed from a warehouse at the expiration of the permissible warehousing period specified under Section 61 (e.g., beyond 1 year or extended period).
  3. Failure to Account for Goods under Bond: Where any goods in respect of which a bond has been executed under Section 59, and which have not been cleared for home consumption or export, are not duly accounted for to the satisfaction of the proper officer.

ICAI TYK Question 5: Section 49 Storage vs Warehousing & Duty Hike Biecco Lawrie Doctrine

BL Ltd. imported Super Kerosene Oil (SKO) and stored it in a warehouse. An ex-bond bill of entry for home consumption was filed, duty was paid as per the rate prevalent on the date of presentation, and the order for clearance for home consumption was passed. On account of the highly combustible nature of SKO, the importer applied for permission to store the kerosene in the same warehouse until actual clearance for sale/use. The application was allowed. However, the rate of customs duty increased when the goods were actually removed. The Department demanded differential duty. Will the company succeed in challenging the demand?

Detailed Statutory Answer:
Yes, the company will succeed.

Legal Analysis & Precedent: The facts of the case are squarely identical to the landmark Supreme Court decision in CCus v. Biecco Lawrie Ltd. [2008 (223) ELT 3 (SC)].

The Supreme Court held that once duty on warehoused goods has been duly paid upon presentation of an Ex-Bond Bill of Entry and an out-of-charge order for home consumption is made under Section 68, the goods cease to be 'warehoused goods'.

Furthermore, Section 49 of the Customs Act, 1962 explicitly provides that where imported goods entered for home consumption cannot be cleared within a reasonable time and are stored in a warehouse upon the importer's application, they shall not be deemed to be warehoused goods for the purposes of this Act, and Chapter IX provisions cease to apply. Therefore, the subsequent increase in duty before physical delivery cannot be demanded from BL Ltd.
