Chapter 11: Payment of Tax

Exhaustive textbook revision notes on the settlement and tax payment mechanism under GST. Covers the 3 electronic registers, Section 49(8) hierarchy of discharging debts, Rule 86B mandatory 1% cash payment restriction, Form GST PMT-09 intra/inter-entity cash ledger transfers, Section 50 net cash interest formulas, and Section 51 Tax Deduction at Source (TDS).

Section 49 The Three Statutory Electronic Registers & Ledgers

REGISTER 1

Electronic Tax Liability Register [PMT-01]

• Debited with: All amounts payable by the person (self-assessed tax, interest, late fees, penalties, and demand orders u/s 73/74/74A).
• Credited with: Payments discharged via Electronic Credit Ledger or Electronic Cash Ledger.

LEDGER 2

Electronic Credit Ledger [PMT-02]

• Credited with: Admissible Input Tax Credit claimed in GSTR-3B.
• Debited ONLY with: Payment of Output Tax (IGST, CGST, SGST).
• STRICT BAR: Credit ledger CANNOT be used to pay RCM tax, interest, penalty, or late fees!

LEDGER 3

Electronic Cash Ledger [PMT-05]

• Credited with: Online payments (Net banking, UPI, NEFT, RTGS) & OTC deposits (max ₹10,000/challan).
• Debited with: Any payment towards tax, interest, penalty, fees or RCM liabilities.
• Organised into Major Heads (IGST, CGST, SGST, Cess) and Minor Heads (Tax, Interest, Penalty, Fee, Other).

⚖️ Mandatory Order of Discharging Dues [Section 49(8)]:

Every taxable person shall discharge his tax and other dues in the following mandatory priority:
  1. First: Self-assessed tax and other dues related to returns of previous tax periods;
  2. Second: Self-assessed tax and other dues related to the return of the current tax period;
  3. Third: Any other amount payable under this Act, including demands determined under Section 73 or 74 or 74A!

Rule 86B Restriction on Utilization of Credit (1% Cash Payment Rule)

Under Rule 86B of the CGST Rules, a registered person SHALL NOT utilize input tax credit in excess of 99% of output tax liability (i.e. at least 1% of output tax liability must be discharged in CASH through Electronic Cash Ledger) in any tax period where:

⚡ Trigger Condition: The value of taxable supply (other than exempt supply and zero-rated supply) in a month exceeds ₹50 Lakhs!

🛡️ Exceptions (Where Rule 86B Does NOT Apply):
1. If the registered person (or proprietor, Karta, MD, any two partners/directors) has paid more than ₹1 Lakh as Income Tax under the Income-tax Act, 1961 in each of the last 2 financial years;
2. If the registered person has received a refund of unutilized ITC > ₹1 Lakh on account of zero-rated supplies or inverted duty structure in the preceding FY;
3. If the registered person has discharged his cumulative output tax liability through cash ledger for an amount in excess of 1% of total output tax liability up to the said month in current FY;
4. Government Departments, Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs), Local Authorities or Statutory Bodies.

Section 49(10) & (11) Transfer of Amount in Cash Ledger via Form GST PMT-09

🔄 Intra-Entity Transfer [Sec 49(10)]

A registered person can transfer any amount of tax, interest, penalty, fee or other amount available in the Electronic Cash Ledger:
• From one Major Head to another Major Head (e.g. IGST to CGST); OR
• From one Minor Head to another Minor Head (e.g. Interest to Tax).
Mechanism: Executed instantly online via Form GST PMT-09!

🏢 Inter-Distinct Person Transfer [Sec 49(10) Proviso]

A registered person may transfer any amount from his Electronic Cash Ledger (CGST or IGST) to the Electronic Cash Ledger for CGST or IGST of a distinct person (registered under the same PAN in another State):
• Mandatory Condition: No unpaid liability exists in the Electronic Liability Register of the transferring entity!

Section 50 Interest on Delayed Payment of Tax & Net Tax Rule

Section Nature of Default Statutory Rate of Interest
Section 50(1) Failure to pay tax or delayed payment of tax within the prescribed period. 18% per annum
Section 50(3) Undue or excess claim of Input Tax Credit wrongly availed AND UTILIZED [Rule 88B]. 18% per annum
(Reduced retrospectively from 24% to 18%)

⚡ THE PROVISO TO SECTION 50(1) — NET CASH LIABILITY RULE

Where a return under Section 39 is furnished after the due date, interest on delayed payment of tax shall be payable ONLY on that portion of the tax which is paid by debiting the Electronic Cash Ledger!

Interest Payable = Net Tax Paid in Cash × 18% × (No. of Days of Delay / 365)

Crucial Exception: If proceedings have been initiated under Section 73, 74 or 74A in respect of the said period, interest applies on the GROSS tax liability without the benefit of the Net Tax proviso!

Section 51 Tax Deduction at Source (TDS) Framework under GST

Under Section 51, the Central Government has mandated specific deductors to deduct tax at source from payments made to suppliers of taxable goods or services:

🏛️ Mandatory Deductors:
1. Department or establishment of Central/State Government;
2. Local authorities;
3. Governmental agencies;
4. Notified entities: An authority/board set up by Parliament/State Legislature with 51%+ Govt equity, Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs), societies established by Govt.

💰 Threshold Limit:
Total value of supply under a single contract exceeds ₹2,50,000 (excluding CGST, SGST, IGST and Cess).

📈 Rate of TDS:
• Intra-State Supply: 2% (1% CGST + 1% SGST).
• Inter-State Supply: 2% IGST.

🚫 THE CRITICAL PROVISO TO SECTION 51(1) — WHEN TDS IS NOT DEDUCTIBLE:

No deduction of tax at source shall be made if:
Location of the Supplier (LoS) and the Place of Supply (PoS) are in a State/UT which is DIFFERENT from the State/UT of registration of the recipient (deductor)!

👉 Why? (The Mathematical Logic):
When LoS and PoS are in State A, the transaction is an Intra-State Supply attracting State A's CGST + SGST. If the Deductor is registered in State B, the deductor can only deduct State B's SGST. He has no authority or ledger to deduct State A's SGST. Hence, law exempts TDS in this exact combination!

TYK Cases ICAI Test Your Knowledge — Solved Practical Problems (1 to 8)

🎯 TYK Question 1 • Miss Nitya (Ledger Set-off & Rule 88A)

Facts: Miss Nitya has output liability of IGST ₹10,000, CGST ₹8,000, SGST ₹8,000. Balance in Electronic Credit Ledger: IGST ₹18,000, CGST ₹2,000, SGST ₹2,000. Compute net tax payable in cash and set-off sequence.

Statutory Solution under Rule 88A:
1. IGST credit of ₹18,000 must be used FIRST to pay IGST liability of ₹10,000. Balance IGST credit = ₹8,000.
2. Balance IGST credit of ₹8,000 can be used to set off CGST or SGST in any order/proportion. Let us set off ₹6,000 against CGST and ₹2,000 against SGST. (IGST credit is now 100% exhausted!).
3. CGST liability remaining = ₹8,000 − ₹6,000 = ₹2,000. Fully set off against CGST credit of ₹2,000. CGST cash payable = Nil.
4. SGST liability remaining = ₹8,000 − ₹2,000 = ₹6,000. Set off against SGST credit of ₹2,000. Balance SGST payable in cash = ₹4,000.

🎯 TYK Question 2 • ITC for Interest / Penalty? [Sec 49(4)]

Facts: Can a taxpayer utilize Input Tax Credit available in Electronic Credit Ledger for payment of interest, late fee, or reverse charge tax?

Solution: STRICTLY NO.
Under Section 49(4) of the CGST Act, the Electronic Credit Ledger can be utilized ONLY for making payment towards Output Tax.
Interest, late fees, penalties, and taxes payable under Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) do NOT constitute output tax of the payer. They must be discharged MANDATORILY IN CASH through the Electronic Cash Ledger!

🎯 TYK Question 3 • M/s PPC & Co. (Form GST PMT-09 Transfer)

Facts: M/s PPC & Co. deposited ₹50,000 under the Minor Head "Interest" of Major Head "CGST" in error, whereas tax was actually payable under Minor Head "Tax" of Major Head "IGST". Can this amount be adjusted, and what is the procedure?

Solution:
• Under Section 49(10), a registered person can transfer any amount available in the Electronic Cash Ledger from one major/minor head to another.
• M/s PPC & Co. can file Form GST PMT-09 on the common portal to transfer ₹50,000 from CGST (Interest) to IGST (Tax) seamlessly!

🎯 TYK Question 4 • ABC Ltd. (Net Cash Interest Computation u/s 50)

Facts: ABC Ltd. filed GSTR-3B for October on 20th December (statutory due date was 20th November; delay of 30 days). Total tax liability declared: ₹5,00,000. Discharged by: ITC ₹3,00,000 + Cash ₹2,00,000. Calculate interest payable under Section 50.

Statutory Analysis & Computation:
• Under the proviso to Section 50(1), when return is filed after due date, interest is payable ONLY on the net tax paid through Electronic Cash Ledger (₹2,00,000).
• Period of delay: 21st November to 20th December = 30 Days.
• Interest = ₹2,00,000 × 18% × (30 / 365) = ₹2,959 (rounded off).

🎯 TYK Question 5 • Manihar Enterprises (TDS Applicability across States)

Facts: Manihar Enterprises (Delhi supplier) enters into a contract worth ₹3,00,000 (excluding tax) with Ministry of Defence (Government deductor registered in Delhi) for supply of computers to be installed at defence base in Jaipur, Rajasthan. Examine whether TDS is deductible.

Statutory Analysis & Solution:
• Contract value = ₹3,00,000 > ₹2,50,000 threshold → Condition satisfied.
• Location of Supplier (LoS) = Delhi.
• Place of Supply (PoS) = Jaipur, Rajasthan [Sec 10(1)(d) installation].
• Registration State of Deductor = Delhi.
• Inter-State Supply: Since LoS (Delhi) ≠ PoS (Rajasthan), this is an Inter-State supply taxable under IGST. The deductor in Delhi is liable to deduct TDS @ 2% IGST (₹6,000). The proviso does NOT apply here because LoS and PoS are in different States!

🎯 TYK Question 6 • Yash Shoppe (Rule 86B 1% Cash Rule)

Facts: Yash Shoppe (Jaipur) has taxable turnover of ₹65 Lakhs in December. Output GST liability = ₹11,70,000. Balance in Electronic Credit Ledger = ₹15,00,000. None of the partners have paid ₹1L income tax. How much tax must be paid in cash?

Solution:
• Taxable turnover of ₹65 Lakhs exceeds the ₹50 Lakh threshold → Rule 86B triggers!
• Maximum ITC that can be utilized = 99% of ₹11,70,000 = ₹11,58,300.
• Minimum mandatory cash payment = 1% of ₹11,70,000 = ₹11,700!
• Yash Shoppe must pay at least ₹11,700 in CASH through Electronic Cash Ledger.
