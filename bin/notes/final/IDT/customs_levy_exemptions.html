Levy of and Exemptions from Customs Duty

Comprehensive study of Sections 12 to 25B of the Customs Act, 1962, Maritime Zones, Supreme Court Taxable Event Jurisprudence (Garden Silk Mills & Apar Ltd), Section 15 Relevant Date Matrix, Pilferage vs Loss/Destruction vs Abatement, IGCR Rules 2022, and Section 25 Statutory Exemptions.

1. Chapter Architecture & Statutory Scheme

Customs duty is an indirect tax levied on goods imported into or exported out of India. Under the Customs Act, 1962 read with the Customs Tariff Act, 1975, customs legislation operates as a complete self-contained code. The statutory framework of this chapter spans:

2. Constitutional Provisions Governing Customs Law

Article 265

Authority of Law: "No tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law." Requires an express Act of Parliament authorizing the levy.

Article 245

Territorial Competence: Parliament can enact laws for the whole or part of India, with express power of extra-territorial operation.

Article 246 & Entry 83

Union List Exclusive Competence: Entry 83 of List I (Union List) vests exclusive legislative authority in Parliament over "Duties of customs including export duties".

Article 286

State Bar on Cross-Border Trade: State legislatures are strictly prohibited from imposing tax on supplies in the course of import into or export out of India.

Three Stages of Imposition of Taxes

  1. Levy: The declaration of liability identifying taxable goods, point of taxation, and rate. Liability does not depend on assessment.
  2. Assessment: The quantification and computation of the exact duty payable (quantity, tariff classification, valuation, and applicable exemptions).
  3. Collection: The physical realization and recovery of duty into the Consolidated Fund of India (may be deferred, as in warehousing).

3. Statutory Definitions & Maritime Zones

A. Important Statutory Definitions

  • Beneficial Owner [Section 2(3A)]: Any person on whose behalf goods are being imported or exported or who exercises effective control over the goods.
  • Customs Area [Section 2(11)]: The area of a customs station or a warehouse and includes any area in which imported or export goods are ordinarily kept before clearance by Customs authorities.
  • Customs Station [Section 2(13)]: Any customs port, customs airport, international courier terminal, foreign post office, or land customs station.
  • Import [Section 2(23)]: Bringing into India from a place outside India.
  • Export [Section 2(18)]: Taking out of India to a place outside India.
  • India [Section 2(27)]: Includes the territorial waters of India (water column, seabed below, and airspace above).

B. Maritime Zones under Maritime Zones Act, 1976

Maritime Zone Statutory Breadth / Limit Sovereignty & Customs Jurisdiction
Baseline Low-water line along the coast Reference line from which maritime zones are measured.
Territorial Waters of India (TWI) 12 Nautical Miles (NM) from baseline (1 NM = 1.852 km) Complete sovereignty of India. Entry of vessel initiates import process; crossing TWI completes export.
Contiguous Zone / EEZ Beyond TWI up to 200 NM from baseline Sovereign rights for economic exploitation, exploration, and energy generation.
Indian Customs Waters [Sec 2(28)] Extends up to the limit of EEZ (200 NM) Enforcement jurisdiction: Officers have powers of arrest, search, stopping vessels, and seizing contraband up to 200 NM.
High Seas Waters beyond 200 NM (beyond EEZ) International waters; open to all nations.

4. Charging Section 12 & Judicial Taxable Event Jurisprudence

A. Charging Section [Section 12]

Duties of customs shall be levied at rates specified in the Customs Tariff Act, 1975 on goods imported into, or exported from India. Sub-section (2) explicitly provides that all goods belonging to Government are treated at par with goods belonging to private persons. (Specific exemptions exist for Indian Navy, Defence, Coast Guard via notifications).

B. Judicial Determination of Taxable Event

Landmark Supreme Court Jurisprudence on Taxable Event

  • Clearance for Home Consumption:
    In Garden Silk Mills v. UOI (1999) 113 ELT 358 (SC), the Supreme Court held:
    "Import of goods commences when they cross the territorial waters, but continues and is completed only when they become part of the mass of goods within the country; the taxable event is reached when goods reach the customs barriers and the Bill of Entry for home consumption is filed."
  • Clearance for Warehousing:
    In UOI v. Apar Ltd. (1999) 112 ELT 3 (SC) and Kiran Spinning Mills v. Collector (1999) 113 ELT 753 (SC), the Supreme Court ruled that in case of warehoused goods, duty collection is deferred under bond:
    "The taxable event occurs when goods are cleared from the warehouse for home consumption (crossing customs barriers), and NOT when goods landed in India or entered territorial waters."
  • Export Taxable Event:
    Export is complete when goods cross the outer limit of the territorial waters of India (12 NM).

B. Goods Derelict, Wreck, Jetsam & Flotsam [Section 21]

All goods derelict, jetsam, flotsam, and wreck brought or coming into India are treated as imported goods and subject to duty unless proven entitled to duty-free admission:

  • Derelict: Cargo or vessel abandoned at sea by master and crew without hope of recovery.
  • Jetsam: Goods thrown overboard (jettisoned) to lighten a vessel in distress to prevent sinking, which sink to sea bottom.
  • Flotsam: Goods jettisoned to save a ship which continue to float on the water surface.
  • Wreck: Remains of a ship or cargo cast ashore by tides after shipwreck.

8. Pilferage (Sec 13) vs Loss & Destruction (Sec 23) vs Relinquishment

Comparison Parameter Pilfered Goods [Section 13] Lost or Destroyed Goods [Section 23(1)] Relinquishment of Title [Section 23(2)]

10. Exemptions from Duty & Statutory Sunset Framework (Sections 25, 25A & 25B)

A. General vs Special Exemptions [Section 25]

  • General Exemption [Section 25(1)]: Granted in public interest by notification in the Official Gazette, either absolutely or subject to conditions, to all importers.
  • Special Ad-Hoc Exemption [Section 25(2)]: Granted in public interest by special order in each case under exceptional circumstances (not published in Gazette).
  • De-Minimis Limit: No duty shall be collected if the duty leviable is equal to or less than Ã¢â€šÂ¹100.

The 2-Year Statutory Sunset Clause under Section 25(1)

Where any conditional exemption is granted, such exemption shall, unless otherwise specified, varied, or rescinded, be valid up to the 31st day of March falling immediately after 2 years from the date of such grant.

Exclusions from the 2-Year Sunset Rule (Sunset does NOT apply to):

  1. Bilateral or multilateral trade agreements (FTAs / PTAs).
  2. International treaties, conventions, UN agencies, and diplomatic privileges.
  3. Privileges of constitutional authorities.
  4. Export promotion schemes under the Foreign Trade Policy (FTP).
  5. Central Government schemes having validity exceeding two years.
  6. Re-imports, temporary imports, gifts, and personal baggage.
  7. Duties under any other law (including IGST u/s 3(7) CTA and Compensation Cess u/s 3(9) CTA).

B. Inward Processing [Sec 25A] & Outward Processing [Sec 25B]

  • Inward Processing [Sec 25A]: Goods imported for repairs, processing, or manufacture exempt from duty provided they are re-exported within 1 year from clearance date and identifiable in export goods.
  • Outward Processing [Sec 25B]: Goods exported for repairs, processing, or manufacture exempt on re-importation provided they are re-imported within 1 year from export clearance date and identifiable.

11. Judicial Precedents Governing Customs Exemption Notifications

Legal Principle / Maxim Landmark Judicial Citation Ratio Decidendi
Strict Construction of Eligibility vs Liberal Construction of Condition CCE v. Hari Chand Shri Gopal (2010) 260 ELT 3 (SC) Eligibility criteria for entering an exemption notification must be construed strictly against the assessee; once eligibility is established, procedural and directory conditions receive liberal construction.
Strict vs Liberal Interpretation Stages UOI v. Wood Papers Ltd. (1990) 4 SCC 256 (SC) At entry stage, strict construction applies; once doubt about applicability is lifted, full play and liberal construction must be given to give effect to the statutory benefit.
No Estoppel Against Claiming Exemption Share Medical Care v. UOI (2007) 209 ELT 321 (SC) If an importer did not claim exemption at the initial stage, he is not debarred or estopped from claiming it subsequently at the assessment or appellate stage.
Option to Choose More Beneficial Notification CCE v. Indian Petro Chemicals (1997) 92 ELT 13 (SC) Where two exemption notifications are simultaneously applicable, the assessee has the absolute right to opt for the notification that is more beneficial.
Burden of Proof on Assessee Novapan India Ltd. v. CCE (1994) 73 ELT 769 (SC) The burden to prove that an imported product squarely falls within an exemption notification lies entirely upon the person claiming the exemption.
Government Power to Rescind / Modify in Public Interest Kasinka Trading v. UOI (1994) 74 ELT 782 (SC) Power to issue exemption notifications includes the power to withdraw or modify them prematurely in public interest; doctrine of promissory estoppel does not bar withdrawal.
Effective Date of Notification Pankaj Jain Agencies (1994) SC / Ruchi Soya (2021) Guj HC Notification becomes effective on the date of its issue for publication in the Official Gazette (electronic gazette publication date).

12. Solved ICAI Test Your Knowledge Problems (14 Cases)

ICAI Question 1

Effective Date of Notifications under Section 25

What are the provisions relating to the effective date of notifications issued under Section 25 of the Customs Act, 1962?
Statutory Analysis & Solution:
  1. General Rule: Every notification issued under Section 25(1) comes into operation on the date of its issue by the Central Government for publication in the Official Gazette, unless a different date is expressly stated in the notification.
  2. 2-Year Sunset Rule: Where an exemption is granted subject to any condition, it is valid up to 31st March falling immediately after 2 years from the date of grant/variation, unless specified otherwise.
  3. Non-Applicability to Specified Goods: The 2-year limit does not apply to trade agreements, diplomatic conventions, FTP schemes, gifts/baggage, and duties other than Section 12 (such as IGST u/s 3(7) CTA).
  4. Special Orders: Special orders u/s 25(2) take effect from the date of issue and are directly communicated to the beneficiary without gazette publication.
ICAI Question 2

100% EOU Surplus Power Sale Case Study

M/s XYZ, a 100% EOU, imported DG sets and furnace oil duty-free under an exemption notification to set up a captive power plant for export production. Power generated was used for exports, but surplus power (which cannot be stored) was sold in the DTA. The notification contained no restriction that imported goods must only be used for export goods. Customs demanded duty. Is the demand valid?
Statutory Analysis & Solution:

The demand of the Customs Department is NOT valid in law.

  1. Supreme Court Precedent: In Commissioner v. Hanil Era Textile Ltd. 2005 (180) ELT A44 (SC), the Supreme Court affirmed that in the absence of an express restrictive clause in the notification mandating that imported capital goods/inputs must be used exclusively for export goods, selling surplus electricity in the DTA does not violate exemption conditions.
  2. Factual Justification: Electricity is intangible and cannot be stored; the primary purpose of captive generation for export production was fulfilled, and sale of incidental surplus was an operational necessity.

ICAI Question 6

Re-Importation of Goods Manufactured in India

Goods manufactured or produced in India, which were earlier exported and thereafter imported into India will be treated at par with other goods imported into India. Is this proposition correct or are any concessions provided?
Statutory Analysis & Solution:

The proposition is correct in principle [Section 20], but subject to vital statutory concessions:

  1. Incentive Reversal: If exported under Drawback / refund / Advance Authorisation / RoDTEP, import duty is restricted to the incentive amount availed at export.
  2. Repairs Abroad: If exported for repairs abroad, duty is leviable only on the fair cost of repairs (including materials) + insurance and freight both ways.
  3. Conditions: Re-import within 5 years (extendable to 7 years); same goods; no change in ownership for repairs.
  4. Inward Repairs Exemption: Notification 158/95-Cus exempts goods re-imported for repairs if re-exported within 6 months (extendable to 1 year) under bond.

ICAI Question 13

Validity of Customs Definitions Statements

Examine the validity of the following statements:
(a) A beneficial owner is a person on whose behalf goods are imported.
(b) Customs area does not include a warehouse.
(c) Customs station includes international courier terminal.
Statutory Evaluation:
  • (a) Valid: Section 2(3A) expressly defines beneficial owner as any person on whose behalf goods are imported or who exercises effective control.
  • (b) Invalid: Section 2(11) explicitly defines "customs area" to include the area of a warehouse.
  • (c) Valid: Section 2(13) includes international courier terminals and foreign post offices within customs stations.
