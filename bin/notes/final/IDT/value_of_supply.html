1. Overview of Valuation Under GST Sec 15 CGST • Sec 20 IGST

Tax under GST is levied on an ad-valorem basis, i.e., as a statutory percentage of the taxable value of the supply of goods or services. Determining the correct taxable value is therefore the critical gateway for computing CGST, SGST/UTGST (intra-State supplies), and IGST (inter-State supplies).

⚖️ Statutory Architecture of Valuation: Section 15 & Chapter IV Rules
Statutory ProvisionGoverning ScopeWhen Applicable?
Section 15(1)
Transaction Value		 Price actually paid or payable for the supply Applied when: (1) Supplier & recipient are NOT related, AND (2) Price is the SOLE consideration. Covers >95% of normal commercial transactions.
Section 15(2)
Mandatory Inclusions		 5 compulsory statutory additions to price: Taxes (non-GST), Third-party payments, Incidentals, Delayed payment interest, Price-linked subsidies. Must be added if incurred/charged and not already included in the invoice price.
Section 15(3)
Permissible Exclusions		 Discounts: (a) Pre-supply (in invoice), and (b) Post-supply (under prior agreement + invoice linkage + ITC reversed by recipient). Allowed as deduction from value only upon strict fulfillment of statutory conditions.
Section 15(4)
Invocation of Rules		 Valuation when Section 15(1) fails (parties related, non-monetary consideration, etc.). Requires valuation strictly as per CGST Rules 27 to 31 sequentially.
Section 15(5)
Notified Supplies		 Valuation of specific notified businesses/supplies notwithstanding Sec 15(1) or 15(4). Governed by Rules 31A to 35 (Lottery, Online Money Gaming, Casino, RSP, Forex, Air Travel, Life Insurance, Margin Scheme, Pure Agent).

Note: Vide Section 20 of the IGST Act, 2017, all provisions relating to value of supply under the CGST Act apply equally to integrated tax on inter-State supplies.

2. Relevant Statutory Definitions Sections 2 & 15

Before computing taxable value, the following statutory definitions from the CGST Act must be mastered:

📖 Essential Definitions for Valuation
  • Consideration [Section 2(31)]: In relation to the supply of goods/services, consideration includes:
    1. any payment made or to be made, whether in money or otherwise, in respect of, in response to, or for the inducement of supply, by recipient or any other person (excluding Central/State Govt subsidy);
    2. the monetary value of any act or forbearance for the inducement of supply (excluding Central/State Govt subsidy).
    Deposit Rider: A deposit given in respect of supply shall NOT be considered as payment unless the supplier applies such deposit as consideration for the said supply.
  • Money [Section 2(75)]: Indian legal tender or foreign currency, cheque, promissory note, bill of exchange, letter of credit, draft, pay order, traveller cheque, money order, or RBI-recognised instruments when used as consideration to settle an obligation. Excludes currency held for its numismatic (collector) value!
  • Agent & Principal [Sections 2(5) & 2(88)]: An agent is a factor, broker, commission agent, arhatia, del credere agent, auctioneer, or mercantile agent who carries on business of supply or receipt of goods/services on behalf of another (the principal).
  • Recipient [Section 2(93)]: (a) where consideration is payable → the person liable to pay consideration; (b) where no consideration is payable for goods → person to whom goods are delivered/made available; (c) where no consideration is payable for service → person to whom service is rendered.
  • Supplier of Specified Actionable Claims [Section 2(105)]: Any person who organises or arranges, directly or indirectly, supply of specified actionable claims (online money gaming, betting, casino), including digital platform operators, whether supplied by him or through him, and whether consideration is in money, money's worth, or Virtual Digital Assets (VDAs).
👥 Deemed "Related Persons" [Explanation (a) to Section 15]

Persons shall be deemed to be related persons if and only if:

S.No.Relationship CategoryStatutory Test / Examples
1 Officers or Directors They are officers or directors of one another's businesses.
2 Partners They are legally recognised partners in business.
3 Employer & Employee Employer and employee relationship (Schedule I gifts > ₹50,000/yr taxable).
4 ≥ 25% Voting Stock / Shares Any person directly or indirectly owns, controls, or holds 25% or more of the outstanding voting stock or shares of both of them.
5 One Controls the Other One of them directly or indirectly controls the other (e.g. Q Ltd. decides corporate policy, operations, and quality control of R Ltd.).
6 Both Controlled by a Third Person Both of them are directly or indirectly controlled by a third person (e.g. Alpha Ltd. controls Board of Beta Ltd. and Gama Ltd.).
7 Together Control a Third Person Together they directly or indirectly control a third person (Brita Ltd. & Grita Ltd. together control Margarita Ltd.).
8 Members of the Same Family Family u/s 2(49): (i) spouse and children, and (ii) parents, grandparents, brothers & sisters if wholly or mainly dependent on the person.
Sole Agent Sole Agent / Sole Distributor Associated in business such that one is sole agent / sole distributor / sole concessionaire of the other → deemed related [Exp (c)].

3. Section 15(1): Transaction Value as Taxable Value

⚖️ Statutory Provision: Section 15(1)

"The value of a supply of goods or services or both shall be the transaction value, which is the price actually paid or payable for the said supply of goods or services or both where the supplier and the recipient of the supply are not related and the price is the sole consideration for the supply."

Transaction value is accepted as the taxable value under Section 15(1) if and only if BOTH conditions are satisfied simultaneously:

ConditionLegal SignificanceWhat Happens if Breached?
Condition 1: Supplier & Recipient are NOT Related Parties must deal at arm's length. If they fall within any of the 8 clauses of Explanation (a) to Section 15, transaction value is discarded. Valuation shifts mandatorily to Rule 28 (or Rule 28(2) for corporate guarantees).
Condition 2: Price is the SOLE Consideration Price in money must be the entire reward. There must be no additional non-monetary consideration, barter, exchange, or hidden obligation borne by buyer. Valuation shifts mandatorily to Rule 27 (Value of supply where consideration is not wholly in money).
⚠️ Exam Trap: Commercial Variation in Price vs. Related Party

Price may vary between customers due to commercial factors (e.g. quantity purchased, location, timing, market demand, customer loyalty). As long as parties are unrelated and price is the sole consideration, the actual invoice price charged to each customer is valid transaction value u/s 15(1). The Department cannot reject transaction value merely because another buyer was charged a different price!

4. Section 15(2): Five Mandatory Inclusions in Value of Supply

The following five items MUST be added to the invoice price to determine the taxable value, if charged separately or incurred by the recipient on the supplier's behalf:

SectionInclusion ElementDetailed Rule & Statutory ScopeExam Examples
Sec 15(2)(a) Non-GST Taxes, Duties, Cesses, Fees Any taxes, duties, cesses, fees and charges levied under any law for the time being in force OTHER THAN: CGST, SGST, UTGST, IGST, and GST Compensation Cess.
If charged separately by supplier → MUST be included!		 • Basic Excise Duty on tobacco/petroleum → INCLUDED.
• Municipal property tax / local body taxes → INCLUDED.
• Entertainment tax levied by local body → INCLUDED.
• Crucial Exception: Income-tax TCS is NOT included (Circular 76/50/2018).
Sec 15(2)(b) Third-Party Payments Incurred by Recipient on Supplier's Behalf Any amount that the supplier is legally or contractually liable to pay in relation to such supply, but which has been incurred by the recipient of the supply and not already included in the price. • Contract is F.O.R. destination (supplier liable for freight), but buyer pays freight to GTA → Freight INCLUDED in supplier's value.
• Landlord liable for society maintenance, tenant reimburses it → INCLUDED (TYK 7).
• Testing charges contractually supplier's liability, paid by buyer → INCLUDED.
• Contrast: Buyer hires transport under ex-factory contract → Not supplier's liability → NOT included.
Sec 15(2)(c) Incidental Expenses & Pre-Delivery Charges Incidental expenses, including commission, packing, inspection, handling, weighing, loading, charged by supplier to recipient, AND any amount charged for anything done by the supplier in respect of the supply at the time of, or before delivery of goods or supply of services. • Normal packing & special export packing → INCLUDED.
• Design, engineering, inspection charges done before delivery → INCLUDED.
• Associated handling & loading charges → INCLUDED.
• Erection, installation & commissioning of machinery at site → INCLUDED (part of composite supply).
Sec 15(2)(d) Interest, Late Fee or Penalty for Delayed Payment Interest or late fee or penalty charged for delayed payment of any consideration.
Crucial Rule: Treated as inclusive of GST (unless separately charged). Computed by back-calculation: Addition = Interest × 100 / (100 + Tax Rate).
Time of Supply: Falls on the date when such interest is actually received [Sec 12(6) / 13(6)]!		 • SA Ltd. receives ₹5,000 late payment interest (GST @ 18% not charged separately) → Value addition = ₹5,000 × 100 / 118 = ₹4,237 (Illustration 2).
• Late fee waived by supplier → No addition.
• Contrast: Penal interest charged by bank on delayed loan EMI → Exempt under Entry 27 (Circular 102/21/2019).
Sec 15(2)(e) Price-Linked Subsidies (Except Central/State Govt) Subsidies directly linked to the price of the supply.
• Subsidies provided by Central Government or State Government → EXCLUDED (Never added!).
• Subsidies provided by NGOs, corporate bodies (CSR), or private persons → MANDATORILY INCLUDED!		 • Selling price ₹25/pack + ₹5 price-linked subsidy from NM Ltd. (CSR) → Taxable value = ₹25 + ₹5 = ₹30.
• Fertilizer sold at ₹300 after ₹700 Central Govt subsidy → Taxable value = ₹300 only (Govt subsidy excluded).
• Lump-sum general grant for factory setup (not linked to per-unit price) → NOT included.

5. CBIC Clarifications on Valuation: TCS, Moulds & Dies, and Penalties

📜 Circular No. 76/50/2018-GST: Non-Inclusion of Income-Tax TCS

Under Section 206C of the Income-tax Act, 1961, sellers collect Tax Collected at Source (TCS) on high-value transactions (motor vehicles > ₹10L, scrap, minerals, etc.).

Clarification: For the purpose of determining the value of supply u/s 15(2)(a), taxable value shall NOT include TCS collected under the provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961. TCS is not a tax on the supply of goods; it is an interim collection of income-tax of the purchaser. (Corrigendum dated 07.03.2019 to Circular 76/50/2018).

⚙️ Circular No. 47/21/2018-GST: Moulds and Dies Supplied FOC by OEM

In automobile and engineering sectors, the Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM) frequently provides moulds and dies free of cost (FOC) to component manufacturers to produce components.

  • Case A — Contract specifies OEM will provide moulds/dies FOC: Providing moulds/dies was never the contractual liability of the component manufacturer. Hence, Section 15(2)(b) does NOT apply. The amortised cost of moulds/dies is NOT added to the value of components supplied by the component manufacturer.
  • Case B — Contract specifies component manufacturer is liable to make/procure moulds, but OEM provides them: The cost of moulds was the supplier's liability incurred by the recipient. Under Section 15(2)(b), the amortised cost of moulds/dies MUST be added to the value of the components! (Also OEM must reverse ITC on moulds if not included in component value).
⚖️ Circular No. 178/10/2022-GST & Circular No. 102/21/2019-GST
  • Liquidated Damages for breach of contract: Liquidated damages for delay in supply/construction are not consideration for an independent service of agreeing to tolerate an act; they are compensation for breach. They are not a separate supply, but if received by the supplier as additional consideration, governed by Section 15(2)(d).
  • Cheque Bounce Charges / Forfeiture: Liquidated damages/penal charges for cheque bounce or earnest money forfeiture in property sales follow the taxability of the underlying main supply.
  • Penal Interest on Loans vs Sales (Circular 102/21/2019):
    1. Case 1 — Seller charges delayed payment interest: Includible in taxable value u/s 15(2)(d) and taxed at the rate applicable to the underlying goods/services.
    2. Case 2 — Buyer enters separate loan agreement with finance company / NBFC, and NBFC charges penal interest: Extending loans is an independent service exempt under Entry 27; penal interest charged by the financier is EXEMPT as interest!

6. Section 15(3): Deductibility of Discounts & Post-Sale Schemes

The value of supply SHALL NOT INCLUDE any discount subject to strict statutory conditions:

CategoryStatutory TimingThree Cumulative Mandatory ConditionsMechanism
Pre-Supply Discount
[Sec 15(3)(a)]		 Given before or at the time of supply Must be duly recorded in the tax invoice issued in respect of such supply. Deducted straight from the invoice value. Tax is levied only on the net discounted amount.
Post-Supply Discount
[Sec 15(3)(b)]		 Given after the supply has been effected Condition 1: Established in terms of an agreement entered into before or at the time of supply and specifically linked to relevant invoices;
Condition 2: Input Tax Credit (ITC) as is attributable to the discount has been reversed by the recipient of the supply. 		Supplier issues a GST Credit Note u/s 34 along with GST. Supplier reduces output tax liability; recipient reverses proportionate ITC.
Secondary / Unplanned Discount
(Non-Deductible)		 Offered after supply without prior agreement Does NOT satisfy Section 15(3)(b) conditions. Discount cannot be deducted from GST taxable value. Supplier issues a Commercial / Financial Credit Note (without GST). No GST adjustment by supplier; no ITC reversal by buyer.
📢 Circular No. 251/08/2025-GST (12.09.2025): Post-Sale Discounts Clarification

Issue: Whether post-sale discounts offered by manufacturers to dealers to enable competitive end-pricing constitute "consideration" for an inducement to supply goods to end-customers u/s 2(31)?

  • Manufacturer to Dealer is Principal-to-Principal: Once goods are sold to the dealer, title passes completely. The dealer becomes the owner and sells to end-customers on his own account.
  • Not an Inducement Consideration: Such discounts are merely commercial price-push mechanisms for competitive pricing. They simply reduce the effective purchase cost of the dealer and are NOT consideration for any independent service rendered by the dealer to the manufacturer.
  • Exception: If the dealer is contractually required to perform special promotional campaigns, advertisements, or exhibitions for the manufacturer against a specific consideration/subsidy, that constitutes an independent supply of service by the dealer to the manufacturer liable to GST.

8. Rule 27: Value of Supply Where Consideration is Not Wholly in Money

🔄 Sequential Valuation Ladder Under Rule 27

Where the consideration for a supply is not wholly in money (e.g. barter, exchange, exchange offers, part-cash part-goods, provision of free facilities), value shall be determined sequentially as follows:

  1. Step 1 — Open Market Value (OMV): The full Open Market Value of such supply.
    Definition: The full value in money (excluding GST) that the supply would ordinarily fetch in the open market between unrelated parties at the same time and place.
  2. Step 2 — Money Consideration + Monetary Value of Non-Money Consideration: If OMV is not available, the sum total of consideration in money PLUS the monetary value of such non-monetary consideration if known at the time of supply.
  3. Step 3 — Value of Supply of Like Kind and Quality (LKQ): If value cannot be determined under Step 1 or Step 2, the value of goods or services of like kind and quality.
    Definition: Goods/services made under similar circumstances that closely resemble the characteristics, quality, quantity, functional components, materials, and reputation of the supply.
  4. Step 4 — Rule 30 or Rule 31: Cost-based valuation (110% of cost) or Best Judgment.
📱 Practical Examples Under Rule 27
  • Example 1 (Mobile Exchange Offer): A new mobile phone is supplied for ₹20,000 along with the exchange of an old mobile phone. In the absence of exchange, the open market price of the new phone is ₹24,000.
    → Taxable value under Rule 27(a) = ₹24,000 (OMV of new phone).
  • Example 2 (Laptop with Non-Money Consideration): A laptop is supplied for ₹40,000 cash plus a printer provided by the customer. The printer was purchased by the customer 2 days earlier for ₹4,000. Open market value of the laptop is unknown.
    → Taxable value under Rule 27(b) = ₹40,000 (money) + ₹4,000 (monetary value of printer) = ₹44,000.
  • Example 3 (Corporate Retainership with Car & Driver): A consultancy firm receives ₹15 lakh annual fee + free car with driver and fuel from client group (TYK 4). Price is not sole consideration. Taxable value must be determined under Rule 27 (OMV or Money + facility value).

9. Rule 28(1): Supplies Between Distinct Persons or Related Persons

Supplies between distinct persons (different GSTINs under same PAN u/s 25(4)/25(5)) or related persons [Explanation to Sec 15] other than through an agent are valued as follows:

🏢 Sequential Valuation Under Rule 28(1)
  1. (a) Open Market Value (OMV) of such supply;
  2. (b) Value of goods or services of like kind and quality (LKQ), if OMV is not available;
  3. (c) Value determined by applying Rule 30 (110% of Cost) or Rule 31 (Best Judgment) sequentially.
⭐ Two Powerful Provisos to Rule 28(1)
ProvisoStatutory BenefitScope & Practical Application
First Proviso
(90% Resale Price Option)		 Where goods are intended for further supply as such by the recipient, the supplier has the option to value the supply at 90% of the price charged by recipient to unrelated customer for goods of like kind and quality. Applies ONLY to goods (not services).
Ex: Factory in UP bills depot in Haryana at ₹90,000 (90% of ₹1,00,000 depot selling price).
Second Proviso
(Golden Rule for CA Final)		 Where the recipient is eligible for FULL INPUT TAX CREDIT (ITC), the value declared in the invoice shall be deemed to be the Open Market Value of the goods or services! Applies to BOTH goods and services.
Supplier can declare any commercial value (even nominal or cost of production) on the invoice, and it is legally deemed to be OMV because the transaction is tax-neutral for the revenue!
🏢 Cross Charge Mechanism & Circular No. 199/11/2023-GST

When a Head Office (HO) provides internally generated services (HR, IT, finance, legal, admin support) to its branch offices (BOs) in other States (distinct persons):

  • Where BO is eligible for Full ITC: As per the Second Proviso to Rule 28, the value declared in the tax invoice issued by HO to BO is deemed to be OMV. If HO has not issued an invoice, the value may be deemed to be NIL!
  • Employee Salary Costs: It is clarified that the salary cost of HO employees is NOT required to be included in the value of services provided by HO to BOs, even when invoice is issued!
  • Where BO has exempt supplies (Partial/No ITC): The value of internally generated services must be determined under Rule 28 read with Rule 30/31, and salary cost of employees involved in providing such services must be apportioned.

10. Corporate Guarantee & Director Guarantee Rule 28(2) • Circulars 204 & 218

🛡️ Rule 28(2): Valuation of Corporate Guarantee Between Related Entities

Notwithstanding anything contained in Rule 28(1), the value of supply of services by a supplier to a recipient who is a related person, by way of providing corporate guarantee to any banking company or financial institution on behalf of the said recipient, shall be deemed to be:

Value = HIGHER of:
(i) 1% per annum of the amount of such guarantee offered; OR
(ii) the actual consideration charged for the service.

Non-Obstante Override: Rule 28(2) overrides Rule 28(1). Hence, the Second Proviso (invoice value deemed OMV) does NOT apply to corporate guarantees! Valuation must strictly follow Rule 28(2).

📜 Circular No. 218/12/2024-GST & Circular No. 204/16/2023-GST Clarifications
IssueStatutory Clarification
Guarantee Offered vs. Loan Disbursed Valuation of 1% p.a. is calculated on the amount of guarantee offered / sanctioned, and NOT on the actual amount of loan disbursed or drawn down by the borrower.
Pro-Rata Valuation for Period < 1 Year or Multi-Year The 1% rate is strictly per annum (p.a.). If guarantee is issued for 6 months → Value = 0.5% (pro-rata). If issued for 3 years → 1% is charged for each year (or pro-rata per annum basis).
Multiple Related Guarantors (Co-Guarantee) Where two or more related entities jointly provide corporate guarantee (e.g. 50% each), the 1% p.a. value is apportioned proportionately between the guarantors according to their respective guarantee shares.
Takeover / Amalgamation / Pre-Closure If the credit facility is taken over by another bank or repaid early, valuation applies only for the actual operational tenure on a pro-rata basis.
Personal Guarantee by Directors Per RBI Guidelines, no consideration/commission can be paid to a director for giving personal guarantee for loans to the company.
• Therefore, the Open Market Value of director's personal guarantee is NIL! No GST is payable.
• Exceptional Case: If consideration/remuneration is actually paid to the director (e.g. director no longer in management), taxable value = actual consideration paid!

11. Rules 29, 30 & 31: Principal-Agent, Cost-Based & Best Judgment

👥 Rule 29: Valuation of Supply of Goods Between Principal & Agent

Supply of GOODS between principal and agent (u/s 2(5)/2(88) read with Schedule I) is valued by the following methods applied sequentially:

  1. Method (a) — Open Market Value OR 90% Option:
    • Open market value of the goods being supplied; OR
    • At the option of the supplier, 90% of the price charged for goods of like kind and quality by the recipient (agent) to his unrelated customer on the day of supply.
    Illustration: ABC Ltd. supplies goods to its agent who sells like goods to unrelated buyers at ₹1,000/unit. ABC Ltd. also sells to unrelated buyers at ₹950/unit. Value of supply to agent = ₹950 (OMV) OR ₹900 (90% of ₹1,000) at ABC Ltd.'s option!
  2. Method (b) — Rule 30 or Rule 31: If value cannot be determined under (a), sequential application of Cost-based (Rule 30) or Best Judgment (Rule 31).

Scope Note: Rule 29 applies ONLY to supply of goods between principal and agent. Supply of agency services (commission) is governed by Section 15(1) / Rule 28.

🏭 Rule 30: Cost-Based Valuation (110% of Cost)

Where the value of a supply cannot be determined by any of the preceding rules, the value shall be:

Taxable Value = 110% of the Cost of Production / Manufacture / Acquisition / Provision

Cost of production is computed in accordance with standard Cost Accounting Principles (CAS-4 / generally accepted cost accounting standards).

⚖️ Rule 31: Best Judgment Method (Residual Valuation)

Where value cannot be determined under Rule 30, it shall be determined using reasonable means consistent with the principles and general provisions of Section 15 and Chapter IV of the CGST Rules.

🚀 Crucial Proviso for Service Suppliers: Direct Jump to Rule 31!

Proviso to Rule 31: In the case of supply of services, the supplier MAY OPT FOR RULE 31 DIRECTLY, BYPASSING RULE 30!
Rationale: Computing the exact cost of production/provision of an intangible service under Rule 30 is commercially cumbersome. Hence, service providers are given the flexibility to invoke Rule 31 directly without exhausting Rule 30.

12. Rules 31A to 31D: Actionable Claims, Online Gaming & Retail Sale Price

Specific non-negotiable statutory valuation rules have been framed under Section 15(5) for actionable claims and high-revenue sensitive commodities:

🎰 Valuation Rules for Actionable Claims: Rules 31A, 31B & 31C
RuleSpecified ActivityStatutory Valuation FormulaExclusions / Critical Riders
Rule 31A(2) Lottery run by State Governments 100 / 128 of Face Value OR price notified in Official Gazette by organising State, whichever is HIGHER. Treated as inclusive of GST @ 28%. Back-calculated using 100/128 formula.
Rule 31A(3) Horse Racing in a race club 100% of the face value of the bet or the amount paid into the totalisator. GST is charged on total bet placed, not merely the race club commission.
Rule 31B Online Money Gaming (incl. virtual games, poker, rummy, fantasy sports) 100% of the total amount deposited with or paid to the supplier by or on behalf of the player in money or Virtual Digital Assets (VDAs). • No deduction for any refund or withdrawal.
• NO DOUBLE TAXATION: Any amount returned/refunded or winning amount credited to wallet and used to play further games shall NOT be taxed again!
Rule 31C Actionable Claims in Casino 100% of the face value of tokens, chips, coins or tickets purchased, or total amount paid for participating in casino games. No Deductions for Refunds: Money given back on return of unplayed chips/tokens is NOT deductible from value!
🏷️ Rule 31D: Supply of Goods on Basis of Retail Sale Price (RSP)

Notwithstanding anything contained in this Chapter, the value of supply of specified goods shall be determined on the basis of Retail Sale Price (RSP):

  • Specified Goods Covered: Pan Masala (2106 90 20), Unmanufactured tobacco (2401), Cigars, cigarillos & cigarettes (2402), Manufactured tobacco substitutes (2403), Tobacco products (2404).
  • RSP Defined: The maximum price declared on the package at which goods may be sold to the ultimate consumer, inclusive of all taxes.
  • Special RSP Determination Scenarios:
    1. More than one RSP declared: Taxable value shall be based on the MAXIMUM retail sale price.
    2. RSP altered to increase: The increased retail sale price shall be taken.
    3. Different RSPs declared for different markets: The RSP applicable to the particular market where the package is sold shall govern.

14. Rules 33, 34 & 35: Pure Agent, Rate of Exchange & Tax-Inclusive Value

🤝 Rule 33: Exclusion of Expenditure Incurred as a Pure Agent

Expenditure or costs incurred by a supplier as a pure agent of the recipient shall be excluded from the value of supply if and only if ALL FOUR cumulative conditions are satisfied:

ConditionStatutory RequirementPractical Test
1. Contractual Authorisation Supplier enters into a contract with recipient of supply to act as his pure agent to incur expenditure or costs in the course or furtherance of supply. Authorization must be in contract or engagement letter.
2. No Title to Goods/Services Supplier neither intends to hold nor holds any title to the goods or services so procured or provided as pure agent. Title passes directly from third party to client.
3. No Personal Use Supplier does not use for his own interest such goods or services so procured. Must be exclusively for recipient's benefit.
4. Actual Reimbursement Only Supplier receives only the actual amount incurred to procure such goods or services in addition to the amount received for supply on his own account. No markup, profit margin, or lump-sum padding allowed.

Classic Pure Agent vs. Includible Cost:
• Customs Broker (Rudra Logistics in TYK 11): Customs duty paid (₹5L), port charges (₹50k), dock dues (₹50k) paid on behalf of importer at actuals = EXCLUDED (Pure Agent).
• Own Office Expenses: Hotel expenses (₹45k), travel (₹50k), phone (₹2k) incurred by broker to execute the work = INCLUDED in value of service!

💱 Rule 34: Statutory Rate of Exchange for Foreign Currency Conversion
Supply Subject MatterStatutory Exchange Rate SourceCrucial Reference Date

🧮 Rule 35: Value Inclusive of Integrated Tax, Central Tax, State Tax

Where the value of supply is inclusive of GST, the tax amount is calculated by back-calculation:

Tax Amount = (Value Inclusive of GST × GST Rate in %) / (100 + GST Rate in %)

Example: Consideration inclusive of 18% GST = ₹118 → Tax amount = (118 × 18) / 118 = ₹18; Taxable Value = ₹100.

16. ICAI "Test Your Knowledge" — All 15 Solved Case Studies

Complete step-by-step examination solutions for all 15 Test Your Knowledge questions from ICAI CA Final IDT Module 1:

🎯 TYK Question 1 • TCS Under Income-Tax Act & Section 15(2)(a)

Question: "Income tax collected at source should be included in value of the supply in terms of section 15(2)(a)." Examine the correctness of the statement.

🔑 Answer: The statement is NOT correct.
Circular No. 76/50/2018-GST (as amended vide corrigendum dated 07.03.2019) clarified that for the purpose of determining the value of supply under Section 15(2)(a) of the CGST Act, taxable value shall NOT include Tax Collected at Source (TCS) collected under the provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961. TCS is not a tax on the supply of goods; it is an interim collection of income tax of the buyer. Therefore, TCS under the Income-tax Act is not includible in the value of supply for GST purposes.

🎯 TYK Question 2 • Moulds & Dies Owned by OEM Sent Free of Cost (FOC)

Question: How should the supply made by a component manufacturer be valued, when he uses moulds and dies owned by the original equipment manufacturer (OEM) sent free of cost to him? Explain.

🔑 Answer: Clarification per Circular No. 47/21/2018-GST:

  • Case 1 — Contract specifies OEM will provide moulds/dies FOC: If the contract between the OEM and component manufacturer specifies that moulds/dies will be supplied by the OEM FOC, the cost of moulds and dies was never the obligation of the component manufacturer. Hence, Section 15(2)(b) does not apply. The amortised cost of moulds and dies is NOT required to be added to the value of the components supplied by the component manufacturer.
  • Case 2 — Contract specifies component manufacturer must provide moulds/dies, but OEM provides them: If the original contract required the component manufacturer to fabricate/provide moulds and dies, but the OEM provides them FOC, an amount that the supplier was liable to incur has been incurred by the recipient. Under Section 15(2)(b), the amortised cost of the moulds and dies MUST be added to the value of components supplied by the component manufacturer.
🎯 TYK Question 3 • Deductibility of Discounts (Pre-Supply vs. Annual Turnover)

Question: Examine whether the following discounts ought to be excluded to determine the value of supply:
(i) Company offering 20% discount for single purchase above ₹10,000.
(ii) Company offering additional discount of 1% on purchase of 10,000 pieces in a year.

🔑 Answer:

  • (i) Excluded from Value u/s 15(3)(a): Under Section 15(3)(a), any discount given before or at the time of supply is deductible from value provided it is duly recorded in the invoice issued in respect of such supply. Since the 20% discount is offered at the time of sale for purchases above ₹10,000 and recorded in the invoice, it shall be excluded from taxable value.
  • (ii) Excluded from Value u/s 15(3)(b) Subject to Conditions: Under Section 15(3)(b), post-supply discounts are deductible only if:
    1. The discount is established in terms of an agreement entered into before or at the time of supply and specifically linked to relevant invoices; and
    2. The recipient has reversed proportionate Input Tax Credit (ITC) attributable to the discount on the basis of credit note issued u/s 34.
    If both conditions are satisfied, the 1% annual volume discount shall be excluded from the value of supply. If conditions are not fulfilled, it cannot be excluded, and supplier can only issue a financial credit note.
🎯 TYK Question 4 • Rajesh & Co. — Retainership Fee + Free Car, Driver & Fuel

Question: Rajesh & Co., a partnership firm, provides financial and management consultancy to a group of companies for an annual retainership fee of ₹15 lakh. Further, the firm is provided with a car (along with a driver) for its exclusive use throughout the year. The fuel cost is also borne by the Group. Rajesh & Co. pays GST on the amount of ₹15 lakh. Is the value correct?

🔑 Answer: No, the value of ₹15 lakh is NOT correct.
Under Section 15(1), transaction value is accepted only when price is the sole consideration. Per Section 2(31), consideration includes monetary value of any act or forbearance. In this case, provision of a dedicated car, driver, and fuel free of cost by the client represents non-monetary consideration for the consultancy services.
Therefore, Section 15(1) fails, and valuation must be determined under Rule 27 of the CGST Rules sequentially:

  1. Open Market Value (OMV) of such consultancy services;
  2. If OMV is not ascertainable, Money consideration (₹15 Lakh) PLUS the monetary value of the car facility, driver services, and fuel provided by the recipient;
  3. Value of services of like kind and quality;
  4. Rule 30 (110% of cost) or Rule 31 (Best Judgment).
🎯 TYK Question 5 • Gupta & Co. — Commodity 'y' Channelled Through State Marketing Corp

Question: The supplies of commodity 'y' to the market are channelled through a State Marketing Corporation which conducts an auction each day to arrive at the price. Gupta and Co. supplies commodity 'y' through the State Marketing Corporation. How will the supply of 'y' be valued for paying tax?

🔑 Answer: Governed by Rule 29 (Principal-Agent Valuation):
The State Marketing Corporation acts as an agent of Gupta and Co. in auctioning commodity 'y' on its behalf. Under Rule 29 of the CGST Rules, the value of supply of goods between a principal and an agent shall be determined as follows:

  1. Open Market Value (OMV) of the goods being supplied; OR
    At the option of the supplier (Gupta and Co.), 90% of the price charged by the State Marketing Corporation to its unrelated auction buyer for the supply of goods of like kind and quality on the day of supply.
  2. If value cannot be determined as above, value shall be determined by applying Rule 30 (110% of cost) or Rule 31 (Best Judgment) sequentially.
🎯 TYK Question 6 • Pharma Drug Intermediate Sent to Own Unit in Another State

Question: A pharma company supplies a drug intermediate to its own unit in another State for conversion into formulations. The drug intermediate is exclusive to this company with no market sale in India, and goods of like kind & quality are not available. How will value be determined?

🔑 Answer: Governed by Rule 28 (Distinct Persons):
Units of the same company registered in different States are distinct persons u/s 25(4). Valuation is governed by Rule 28:

  • Where Recipient Unit is Eligible for FULL ITC (Second Proviso to Rule 28): Under the Second Proviso to Rule 28, where the recipient is eligible for full input tax credit, the value declared in the invoice shall be deemed to be the open market value of the goods. Thus, the pharma company can adopt any invoice value (such as cost of production).
  • Where Recipient Unit is NOT Eligible for Full ITC: Since OMV and like kind & quality are not available, value must be determined under Rule 30, i.e., 110% of the cost of production / manufacture of the drug intermediate (computed under CAS-4). If not feasible, Rule 31 (Best Judgment).

🎯 TYK Question 9 • Fortune 365 (UAE) — Online Money Gaming Valuation [Rule 31B]

🎯 TYK Question 12 • Rustagi & Co. — Transfer to Unit Eligible for Full ITC [Rule 28]

🎯 TYK Question 13 • Dev Enterprises — Water Coolers with Non-Money Consideration [Rule 27]

Facts: Dev Enterprises supplies water coolers to Vimal Traders for ₹2,95,000 (inclusive of 18% GST) + materials worth ₹10,000 (exclusive of GST) given as additional consideration. Dev Enterprises also supplied the same goods to an unrelated person for ₹2,97,360 (inclusive of 18% GST). Questions: (1) Determine value of supply; (2) What if price of ₹2,97,360 is not available?

  1. Price of ₹2,97,360 is Available (Rule 27(a) — Open Market Value):
    Under Rule 27(a), where consideration is not wholly in money, the value shall be the Open Market Value (OMV) of such supply.
    • Dev Enterprises supplies the same goods to an unrelated person at ₹2,97,360 (inclusive of 18% GST).
    • OMV (excluding GST) = ₹2,97,360 / 1.18 = ₹2,52,000.
    • Value of Goods Supplied to Vimal Traders = ₹2,52,000.
  2. Price of ₹2,97,360 is NOT Available (Rule 27(b) — Money + Non-Money Value):
    Under Rule 27(b), if OMV is not ascertainable, value is the sum of consideration in money + monetary value of non-money consideration:
    • Consideration in money (excluding GST) = ₹2,95,000 / 1.18 = ₹2,50,000.
    • Value of materials given as additional consideration (excl. GST) = ₹10,000.
    • Taxable Value of Supply = ₹2,50,000 + ₹10,000 = ₹2,60,000.
