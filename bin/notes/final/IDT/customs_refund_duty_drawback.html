SECTION 27

2. Refund Application, 1-Year Limitation & Relevant Dates

Section 27(1) prescribes the substantive and procedural criteria governing the lodging of a customs duty or interest refund claim.

Eligible Claimants [Section 27(1)]

A refund claim can be filed by:

  • The person who actually paid the customs duty or interest; OR
  • The buyer/person who has borne the economic incidence of such duty or interest.
De Minimis Monetary Threshold: No refund shall be granted where the amount claimed is less than â‚¹ 100 [Third Proviso to Section 27(1)].

Statutory Presumption [Section 28D]

Every person who has paid duty on any goods under the Customs Act shall, unless the contrary is proved by him, be deemed to have passed on the full incidence of such duty to the buyer of such goods.

The applicant bears the affirmative burden of rebutting this presumption through audited financial statements, ledger entries, and CA certificates.

Computation of 1-Year Limitation Period [Section 27(1) & (1B)]

Claim by Buyer (who bore incidence) 1 Year Date of purchase of goods by such person [Explanation 1].

SECTION 27(2)

3. Doctrine of Unjust Enrichment & Consumer Welfare Fund

Because customs duty is an indirect tax intended to be passed down the supply chain to ultimate consumers, allowing an importer who has already recovered the duty in his sale price to pocket the refund would constitute unjust enrichment at the public's expense (*Mafatlal Industries Ltd. v. UOI* 9-Judge SC Bench).

General Rule of Credit to Consumer Welfare Fund

Where the proper officer is satisfied that the whole or any part of the duty and interest paid is refundable, he shall make an order accordingly, and the amount so determined shall be transferred to the Consumer Welfare Fund established under Section 57 of the CGST Act.

The 7 Statutory Exceptions (Where Refund is Paid to Claimant)

(a) Burden Borne by Importer/Exporter:

Where the claimant positively proves he has NOT passed on the incidence to any other person.

(b) Personal Imports:

Where imports were made by an individual for his own personal, non-commercial use.

(c) Buyer Not Passed On:

Where the buyer who bore the duty/interest proves he has not passed it on further.

(d) Export Duty on Returned Goods [Sec 26]:

Export duty paid on goods returned to exporter under specified terms of Section 26.

(e) Duty Drawback [Sec 74 & 75]:

Amounts claimed as duty drawback on re-export or export manufacturing.

(f) Notified Class of Applicants:

Class of applicants specifically notified in Official Gazette by Central Government.

(g) Excess Pre-Clearance Duty Evident from BE:

Duty paid in excess before clearance order where excess is evident from the self-assessed Bill of Entry or assessed Bill of Entry (e.g., paying duty for 400 units instead of 200 units as per typo).

SECTIONS 26 & 26A

5. Concessions on Returned Exports & Defective Imported Goods

Refund of Export Duty [Section 26]

Export duty paid on goods is refunded where:

  • Goods are returned to the exporter within 1 year from exportation.
  • Returned due to defect, rejection, or breach of contract.
  • Identified to satisfaction of AC/DC as same goods.
  • Refund claim filed within 6 months from out-of-charge order for re-import.
  • Goods are not re-sold in India before re-export.

Defective Imported Goods [Section 26A]

Duty paid on imported goods cleared for home consumption is refunded if goods are defective or not per specifications:

  • Goods identified to satisfaction of AC/DC.
  • Not worked/repaired/used (except indispensable use to detect defect).
  • No Duty Drawback claimed.
  • Re-exported, Relinquished, or Destroyed within 30 days (+3 months extension).
  • Application within 6 months from relevant date.

Relevant Dates under Explanation to Section 26A(2)

Mode of Disposal of Defective Goods Relevant Starting Date for 6-Month Refund Limitation
Goods Exported out of India Date on which the proper officer makes an order permitting clearance and loading for exportation (Let Export Order).
Relinquishment of Title to Customs Date of such relinquishment and surrender to Customs.
Destruction / Rendered Commercially Valueless Date of such destruction or rendering commercially valueless in presence of proper officer.
Ineligible Goods [Section 26A(3)]: Section 26A refund is strictly barred for perishable goods and goods which have exceeded their shelf life or recommended storage-before-use period.
LANDMARK PRECEDENTS

6. Refund Claim Cannot be a Substitute for Appeal

A foundational tenet of customs law reaffirmed by the Supreme Court is that an assessee cannot bypass the statutory appellate hierarchy by disguising an appeal as a refund claim.

1. ITC Ltd. v. Commissioner of Central Excise [2019 (368) ELT 216 (SC - 3 Judge Bench)] 3-JUDGE BENCH LANDMARK

Facts & Legal Question: Can an importer directly file a refund application under Section 27 on the ground of an incorrect classification or exemption claim without challenging the assessment / self-assessment order in appeal?
Supreme Court Ruling: The Supreme Court held that self-assessment under Section 17 is an order of assessment and is appealable under Section 128. An assessee who is aggrieved by a self-assessment or assessment order cannot file a refund claim under Section 27 without getting the assessment order modified or appealed against under Section 128. The refund authority cannot sit in review over an assessment order passed by an officer of equal rank.

2. Priya Blue Industries Ltd. v. CCus [2004 (172) ELT 145 (SC)] NO BACKDOOR RELIEF

Supreme Court Ruling: Once an assessment order has been passed (whether provisional finalized or original) and the limitation period for filing an appeal has expired, the assessee cannot circumvent limitation by filing a Section 27 refund claim on grounds alleging errors in the assessment report. A refund claim cannot be utilized as a backdoor appeal.

SECTIONS 74, 75 & 76

7. Duty Drawback Architecture: Re-Export vs Manufacturing

Duty Drawback is an export promotion device that refunds customs duties paid on imported materials when the goods are re-exported out of India.

Dimension Section 74: Drawback on Re-Export As Such Section 75: Drawback on Manufacturing Inputs
Nature of Export Goods Same imported goods re-exported as such (without manufacturing). Finished products manufactured in India using imported inputs.
Quantum of Drawback • Re-exported without use: 98% of import duty paid.
• Used before re-export: Sliding scale reduction based on length of use. 		Determined by Central Government rates:
• AIR (All Industry Rate)
• Brand Rate (where AIR is nil/inadequate by > 20%)
• Special Brand Rate.
Statutory Time Limit Goods must be entered for export within 2 years from date of payment of duty (extendable by CBIC on sufficient cause). No time cap on input import date, provided export obligation fulfilled per SION norms.
Identity of Goods Must be capable of easy physical identification by AC/DC. Inputs incorporated into export goods; verified via input-output ratios.
Prohibited Goods Zero drawback if used: wearing apparel, tea chests, cinematograph films, unexposed photographic films. Prohibited if export value < value of imported materials, or market price < drawback claimed [Sec 76].

Section 76 Statutory Prohibitions on Drawback

No duty drawback shall be allowed under Section 74 or 75 where:
1. The market price of export goods is less than the amount of drawback claimed.
2. Where value of export goods is less than the value of imported materials used therein (negative value addition).

8. Solved ICAI Practical Case Studies (Questions 1 to 10)

ICAI TYK Question 1: Limitation for Submission of Refund Application Section 27(1)

Explain the provisions of the Customs Act, 1962 relating to computation of limitation for submission of refund application.

Detailed Statutory Solution:
According to Section 27(1) of the Customs Act, 1962, an application for refund of duty or interest must be lodged before the expiry of one year from the relevant date:
  • Claim by Importer/Exporter: 1 year calculated from the date of payment of duty/interest.
  • Claim by Buyer: 1 year calculated from the date of purchase of goods.
  • Special Exemption Order [Sec 25(2)]: 1 year calculated from the date of issue of such exemption order.
  • Provisional Assessment [Sec 18]: 1 year calculated from the date of adjustment of duty after final assessment or reassessment.
  • Appellate Order/Court Judgment: 1 year calculated from the date of such judgment, decree, order, or direction.

Exception: The 1-year limitation does not apply where duty has been paid under protest. Customs authorities have no power to condone delay beyond the 1-year statutory limitation.

ICAI TYK Question 2: Provisional Assessment Finalization & Unjust Enrichment Section 18(5) & Sec 28D

M/s HIL imports copper concentrate provisionally assessed under Section 18 based on provisional invoice. Assessments are finalized based on London Metal Exchange prices. HIL filed a refund claim arising out of finalization. The Department ordered the refund credited to the Consumer Welfare Fund because HIL produced no evidence showing the burden of duty was not passed on. Discuss whether Department's action is correct.

Detailed Statutory Solution:
Yes, the Department's action is correct in law.

Statutory Provisions: Section 18(5) of the Customs Act, 1962 explicitly incorporates the doctrine of unjust enrichment into provisional assessments. It mandates that any refund arising upon finalization of provisional assessment shall be credited to the Consumer Welfare Fund unless the importer satisfies the proper officer that the incidence of such duty has not been passed on.

Furthermore, Section 28D creates a statutory presumption that the person who paid duty has passed on the entire incidence to the buyer. Since M/s HIL failed to produce documentary evidence rebutting this presumption, the Department acted in full conformity with law in crediting the refund to the Consumer Welfare Fund.

ICAI TYK Question 3: Conditions for Refund on Defective Goods [Section 26A] Section 26A

Discuss the conditions governing refund of import duty paid under Section 26A if goods are found defective or not as per specifications.

Detailed Statutory Solution:
Section 26A allows refund of import duty paid on home consumption clearance subject to the following statutory conditions:
  1. Goods are defective or not in conformity with specifications agreed between importer and supplier.
  2. Goods were not worked, repaired, or used after importation (except indispensable use to discover defect).
  3. Identified to AC/DC's satisfaction as the imported goods.
  4. Importer does not claim duty drawback under any other provision.
  5. Goods are re-exported OR relinquished/abandoned to Customs OR destroyed in presence of proper officer within 30 days from clearance order (extendable up to 3 months by Commissioner).
  6. Refund application filed within 6 months from relevant date.
  7. Goods are not perishable or shelf-life expired.
ICAI TYK Question 4: Minimum Monetary Threshold for Refund Threshold Rule

What is the minimum monetary limit prescribed in the Customs law below which no refund shall be granted?

Detailed Statutory Solution:
As per the third proviso to Section 27(1) of the Customs Act, 1962, where the amount of refund claimed is less than â‚¹ 100, no refund shall be granted. Refund is granted only when the duty/interest amount involved is â‚¹ 100 or more.
ICAI TYK Question 5: Doctrine of Unjust Enrichment Explained Legal Concept

Explain the doctrine of unjust enrichment with respect to refund of duty.

Detailed Statutory Solution:
The doctrine of unjust enrichment posits that no person should unjustly enrich himself at the expense of another or the public exchequer. Since customs duty is an indirect tax whose incidence is normally built into the pricing and passed on to buyers, granting a refund to an importer who has already recovered that duty would mean he collects the amount twiceâ€”once from the customer and once from the Government.

Consequently, under Section 27(2), all refundable amounts are credited to the Consumer Welfare Fund unless the claimant rebuts the Section 28D presumption and establishes that the burden was genuinely borne by him.

ICAI TYK Question 6: Refund as a Backdoor Appeal (Acme Sales Case) Priya Blue / ITC Precedent

Acme Salesâ€™ imports were provisionally assessed pending verification. Assessment finalized demanding â‚¹ 12 lakh, which Acme paid without appeal. After 6 months (when appeal was time-barred), Acme filed a Section 27 refund claim for â‚¹ 8 lakh alleging verification errors. Was this the correct mode of redressal? Discuss outcome based on case law.

Detailed Statutory Solution:
No, this was NOT the correct mode of redressal, and the refund claim will be REJECTED.

Judicial Ratio: In Priya Blue Industries Ltd. v. CCus [2004 (172) ELT 145 (SC)] and reaffirmed by the 3-Judge Bench in ITC Ltd. v. CCE [2019 (368) ELT 216 (SC)], the Supreme Court ruled that a refund claim under Section 27 cannot be used as a backdoor device to reopen or modify a final assessment order.

A person aggrieved by an assessment order must file a statutory appeal under Section 128 within the prescribed limitation period (60 days). An officer evaluating a Section 27 refund application has no jurisdiction to sit in review or set aside an assessment order. Since Acme Sales failed to file an appeal and allowed the order to become final, its refund claim is impermissible.

ICAI TYK Question 7: Pre-Clearance Excess Duty & Exception to Unjust Enrichment Section 27(2) Proviso (g)

Mr. N imported 1,000 units of article "ZEP" across 3 consignments (200, 400, 400). Duty was â‚¹ 250/unit. Profit was â‚¹ 350/unit; sale price â‚¹ 1,750/unit. One month after selling all 1,000 units, Mr. N found he paid duty for 400 units instead of 200 units on the first consignment, resulting in excess payment of â‚¹ 50,000 (200 × â‚¹ 250). He filed refund. Is the bar of unjust enrichment attracted?

Detailed Statutory Solution:
No, the bar of unjust enrichment is NOT attracted; Mr. N is entitled to direct refund.

Analysis under Clause (g) of First Proviso to Section 27(2): Under clause (g), the bar of unjust enrichment does not apply where excess duty was paid prior to clearance of goods and such excess is evident from the Bill of Entry (duty paid for 400 units while cargo landed was 200 units).

Analysis under Clause (a): Furthermore, Mr. N's sales invoices prove that he sold only 1,000 units and collected duty of â‚¹ 250 on 1,000 units only. He never collected duty on the phantom 200 units from any customer. Having demonstrably borne the burden himself, he escapes the bar under clause (a) as well.

ICAI TYK Question 8: Relevant Dates for Refund under Section 26A(2) Section 26A(2)

Explain the relevant dates as provided in Section 26A(2) of the Customs Act, 1962 for the purpose of refund of duty under: (i) goods exported out of India; (ii) relinquishment of title; (iii) goods destroyed or rendered valueless.

Detailed Statutory Solution:
Under Explanation to Section 26A(2), the 6-month limitation period commences from the following relevant dates:
  • (i) Goods Exported Out of India: The date on which the proper officer makes an order permitting clearance and loading of goods for exportation (Let Export Order).
  • (ii) Relinquishment of Title: The date on which title to goods is formally surrendered and relinquished to customs authorities.
  • (iii) Goods Destroyed or Rendered Valueless: The date on which the goods are destroyed or rendered commercially valueless in the presence of the proper officer.
ICAI TYK Question 9: Refund on Perishable Goods under Section 26A Section 26A(3)

Explain whether refund of import duty is allowed under Section 26A in case of perishable goods.

Detailed Statutory Solution:
NO. Refund of import duty is strictly NOT ALLOWED in case of perishable goods.

Statutory Bar: Section 26A(3) of the Customs Act, 1962 explicitly lays down that no refund under Section 26A shall be granted in respect of perishable goods and goods which have exceeded their shelf life or their recommended storage-before-use period.

ICAI TYK Question 10: Interest on Delayed Refund & Computation Period Section 27A Interest

Briefly explain whether interest is paid to the applicant in case of delayed refund by customs authorities. If yes, also explain the period for computation of interest.

Detailed Statutory Solution:
YES, interest is statutory and payable to the applicant under Section 27A.
  • Rate of Interest: Currently notified @ 6% per annum vide Notification No. 75/2003-Cus (NT).
  • Threshold Grace Period: Department has a grace window of 3 months from the date of receipt of a complete refund application.
  • Period of Computation: Interest is payable from the date immediately following the expiry of 3 months from the application receipt date, up to the date of actual refund.
  • Deemed Receipt Date: Date on which complete application acknowledged by Proper Officer is filed with all accompanying evidence.
