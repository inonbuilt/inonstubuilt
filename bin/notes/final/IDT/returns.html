Returns under GST

Comprehensive Master Notes: Sections 37 to 48, Outward Supplies (Form GSTR-1, IFF & Form GSTR-1A), Inward Credit Statements (Form GSTR-2B), Summary Returns (Form GSTR-3B), QRMP Fixed Sum & Self-Assessment Methods, Annual Return (Form GSTR-9) and Self-Certified Reconciliation (Form GSTR-9C), Information Returns (Sec 150), and Step-by-Step Solved ICAI Test Your Knowledge Problems.

1. Statutory Architecture & Filing Ecosystem

In any tax legislation, return filing forms the operational fulcrum of tax compliance and verification. Under GST, return filing operates on a self-assessment mechanism [Section 59] and acts as a bidirectional compliance validation between the supplier and recipient.

Core Objectives

Purpose of GST Returns

  • Transfer of Transactional Data: Electronic conveyance of sales, purchases, and tax data to tax administrations.
  • Compliance Verification & Cross-Matching: Automated matching of outward supply (GSTR-1) with input tax credit availed (GSTR-2B).
  • Self-Assessment & Finalization: Crystallizing tax liabilities, ITC claims, and net cash payouts within statutory limitation periods.
  • Audit & Risk Profiling: Triggers for automated risk assessment, scrutiny, and anti-evasion enforcement.
Statutory Rules

Core Legal Rules & Safeguards

  • No Revised Return: GST does not permit filing a "revised return". Corrections are made through amendment tables in subsequent returns or via Form GSTR-1A prior to GSTR-3B.
  • Sequential Filing Rule [Sec 37(4) & 39(10)]: Return for a current tax period cannot be furnished unless GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B for previous periods are furnished.
  • Valid Return Rule [Sec 39(7)]: A return is a "valid return" only when self-assessed tax is paid in full. An unpaid return is legally invalid.

2. Furnishing Details of Outward Supplies [Section 37 & Rule 59]

Section 37 mandates every registered person (other than ISD, NRTP, composition supplier, person deducting TDS u/s 51, or collecting TCS u/s 52) to furnish electronically the details of outward supplies of goods and/or services in Form GSTR-1.

  • Optional Facility: Available to taxpayers under the QRMP scheme for the 1st and 2nd month of the quarter.
  • Timeline: From 1st day till 13th day of the month succeeding the respective month (M1 & M2).

  • Intra-State B2C Supplies: Consolidated rate-wise summary for all supplies irrespective of value.

3. New Form GSTR-1A: Same-Month Correction Window [Proviso to Rule 59(1)]

One of the most consequential procedural amendments in GST return filing is the introduction of Form GSTR-1A. It provides an optional facility to amend or declare additional outward supplies for the current tax period after filing Form GSTR-1, but before filing Form GSTR-3B.

Same-Month Window

Why GSTR-1A is Unique

Historically, any mistake in GSTR-1 had to wait until the next month's return amendment tables. Form GSTR-1A allows a real-time correction window in the very same month, ensuring the current month's GSTR-3B automatically pulls the corrected liability.

Impact on Recipient

Reflection in Recipient's GSTR-2B

If GSTR-1A is filed before the GSTR-2B generation cut-off (13th/14th), it reflects in the recipient's GSTR-2B of the same month. If filed later, it reflects in the subsequent month's GSTR-2B.

Distinguishing Feature Form GSTR-1A Amendment Tables in Subsequent GSTR-1
Tax Period Covered Current tax period (the one whose GSTR-1 has just been filed). Past closed tax periods (previous months/quarters).
Filing Window After filing GSTR-1, but BEFORE filing GSTR-3B for the same period. While filing GSTR-1 of a subsequent tax period.
Impact on GSTR-3B Updates liability in the same month's GSTR-3B directly. Impacts tax liability in the subsequent month's GSTR-3B.

4. Debarment from Furnishing GSTR-1 / IFF [Rule 59(6)]

To enforce strict tax compliance and prevent circular trading, Rule 59(6) bars a registered person from furnishing Form GSTR-1 or utilizing IFF under the following circumstances:

Statutory Blocking Conditions under Rule 59(6):
  1. Default in Monthly GSTR-3B: A registered person who has not furnished Form GSTR-3B for the preceding month cannot file GSTR-1 for the current month.
  2. Default in Quarterly GSTR-3B (QRMP): A taxpayer who has not furnished Form GSTR-3B for the preceding tax period (quarter) cannot file GSTR-1 or use IFF for the current period.
  3. Rule 88C Non-Compliance: If a taxpayer has been issued an intimation in Form DRC-01B for tax liability declared in GSTR-1 exceeding GSTR-3B by specified limits, and has neither paid the difference nor replied within 7 days → GSTR-1 blocked!
  4. Rule 88D Non-Compliance: If a taxpayer has been issued an intimation in Form DRC-01C for excess ITC availed in GSTR-3B compared to GSTR-2B, and has neither reversed ITC nor replied within 7 days → GSTR-1 blocked!
  5. Bank Account Non-Furnishing [Rule 10A]: If a registered person fails to furnish valid bank account details within 30 days of registration grant or before GSTR-1 filing (whichever is earlier) → GSTR-1 blocked!

5. Inward Supplies & Form GSTR-2B [Section 38 & Rule 60]

Section 38 provides for an auto-generated statement communicating details of inward supplies and input tax credit available to the recipient. Under Rule 60(7), this statement is generated in Form GSTR-2B.

Generation Schedule

Static Nature of GSTR-2B

Form GSTR-2B is a static monthly statement generated automatically on the 14th day of the month succeeding the tax period. It captures all documents filed by suppliers in their GSTR-1, IFF, GSTR-5 (NRTP), and GSTR-6 (ISD) between the 12th/14th of the previous month and the 11th/13th of the current month, plus ICEGATE import data.

Two Core Segments

Eligible vs Ineligible Credit [Sec 38(2)]

Part A (ITC Available): Inward supplies where credit may be availed.
Part B (ITC Not Available): Inward supplies where credit CANNOT be availed because the supplier is in default (e.g., supplier newly registered within prescribed period, supplier defaulted in GSTR-3B, supplier's GSTR-1 exceeds GSTR-3B, or supplier paid less tax in cash u/r 86B).

Distinction Form GSTR-2A Form GSTR-2B
Nature Dynamic statement. Changes in real-time whenever a supplier uploads or amends an invoice. Static statement. Generated once a month on the 14th; values never change for that tax period.
Legal Basis for ITC Informational purpose only; cannot be used directly for GSTR-3B self-assessment. Statutory basis for claiming ITC in Form GSTR-3B in terms of Section 16(2)(aa) and Section 38.
Ineligible Flags Does not categorize into eligible and ineligible credit. Explicitly segregates credit into Eligible ITC and Ineligible ITC u/s 38(2).

7. Quarterly Return Monthly Payment (QRMP) Scheme

The QRMP scheme enables registered taxpayers with an aggregate turnover of up to â‚¹5 Crore in the preceding financial year to file Form GSTR-1 and Form GSTR-3B on a quarterly basis while depositing tax monthly.

Option Window

Exercising QRMP Option

A taxpayer can opt into QRMP from the 1st day of the 2nd month of the preceding quarter till the last day of the 1st month of the quarter for which the option is exercised (e.g., for Julyâ€“September, window is 1st May to 31st July). Once opted, it continues perpetually until aggregate turnover crosses â‚¹5 Cr or the taxpayer opts out.

Payment of Tax (M1 & M2)

Deposit via Challan GST PMT-06

Tax for the first two months of the quarter must be deposited in Form GST PMT-06 by the 25th of the succeeding month. The taxpayer can choose between two methods for each of the two months independently.

9. Annual Return & Self-Certified Reconciliation (Sec 44, GSTR-9 & 9C)

Section 44 mandates every registered person (other than ISD, casual taxable person, NRTP, and persons deducting/collecting tax u/s 51/52) to furnish an annual return electronically for every financial year.

Form GSTR-9

Annual Return (Due: 31st Dec)

Covers all 12 months' outward supplies, ITC availed/reversed, taxes paid, and demands/refunds. Exemption: Registered persons having aggregate turnover up to â‚¹2 Crore in a financial year are exempt from filing Form GSTR-9.

Form GSTR-9C

Reconciliation Statement (Threshold: > â‚¹5 Cr)

Mandatory only for taxpayers having aggregate turnover exceeding â‚¹5 Crore. It is a self-certified reconciliation reconciling audited financial statements with GSTR-9. CA/CMA certification is no longer required; it is self-certified by the taxpayer!

Important Clarification on Late Fees for GSTR-9C (CBIC Circular No. 246/03/2025-GST dated 30.01.2025):
  • Late fee is leviable for delay in furnishing the complete annual return (Form GSTR-9 + Form GSTR-9C).
  • If GSTR-9C is filed subsequent to GSTR-9, the late fee period runs from the due date up to the date of furnishing Form GSTR-9C.
  • Late fee is NOT separately leviable for GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C. A single consolidated late fee applies.
Maximum Time Limit for Furnishing Annual Return [Section 44(2)]: A registered person cannot furnish an annual return after the expiry of a period of 3 years from the statutory due date of furnishing the said annual return.

10. Defaults, Notices & Rationalized Late Fees [Sec 46 & 47]

When a registered person fails to furnish returns under Section 39, Section 44, or Section 45, a statutory notice in Form GSTR-3A is issued requiring the taxpayer to file the return within 15 days. 11. GST Practitioners (Sec 48) & Information Return (Sec 150)

Section 48 & Rules 83-84

Goods and Services Tax Practitioners (GSTP)

  • A registered person may authorise an approved GSTP in Form GST PCT-05 to furnish outward supplies (Sec 37), returns (Sec 39, 44, 45), apply for refund, or file ITC-04.
  • Client Confirmation: Before filing any statement/return, the GSTP must obtain confirmation from the registered person via SMS or email.
  • Ultimate Responsibility: The responsibility for correctness of any return prepared by a GSTP rests solely on the registered person, not the GSTP!
  • No return is required to be filed by a GSTP itself for activities carried out.
Section 150 & 123

Information Return (Big Data Harvesting)

  • Certain designated authorities (Income Tax, Banking companies, Electricity Boards, Registrar of Companies, Motor Vehicle Registering Authorities) must file Information Returns.
  • Defective Return: Proper officer intimates defect; must rectify within 30 days.
  • Notice for Non-Filing: Notice requires filing within up to 90 days.
  • Penalties u/s 123: â‚¹100/day up to â‚¹5,000 for initial delay; â‚¹50,000 for failure to furnish after 180 days.

12. ICAI Test Your Knowledge (TYK) - Complete Solved Problems

ICAI TYK 1 (Annual Return Applicability)

Question:

Which type of taxpayers need to file an annual return under Section 44? Enumerate.

Answer:

As per Section 44(1) of the CGST Act, 2017, every registered person is required to furnish an annual return for every financial year electronically in the prescribed form.

However, the following persons are specifically EXCLUDED from filing an annual return:

  1. An Input Service Distributor (ISD);
  2. A person paying tax under Section 51 (TDS deductor);
  3. A person paying tax under Section 52 (TCS collector - ECO);
  4. A casual taxable person; and
  5. A non-resident taxable person.

Statutory Exemption: Any department of the Central Government or a State Government or a local authority, whose books of account are subject to audit by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India (CAG) or an auditor appointed for auditing the accounts of local authorities, are exempt from furnishing the annual return.

Turnover Exemption: In terms of notification issued under Section 44, registered persons whose aggregate turnover in the relevant financial year is up to â‚¹2 Crore are also exempted from filing Form GSTR-9.

ICAI TYK 2 (Annual Return vs Final Return)

Question:

Is an annual return under Section 44 and a final return one and the same? Explain.

Answer:

No. An annual return and a final return are entirely distinct legal instruments under GST:

  • Annual Return [Section 44]: An annual statement of business activities, taxes paid, and ITC availed during the financial year. It must be filed by every regular registered taxpayer (in Form GSTR-9) and composition taxpayer (in Form GSTR-4) by 31st December following the end of the financial year.
  • Final Return [Section 45]: Required to be furnished only by those registered persons whose registration has been cancelled or surrendered. It is furnished in Form GSTR-10 within 3 months of the date of cancellation or the date of the cancellation order, whichever is later, to discharge closing stock liabilities under Section 29(5).
ICAI TYK 3 (ISD Outward Return Obligation)

Question:

Do Input Service Distributors (ISDs) need to file a separate statement of outward supplies (GSTR-1) with their return? Discuss.

Answer:

No. An Input Service Distributor does not make any outward taxable supplies of goods or services to external customers; it merely distributes input tax credit on common input services to its recipient branch units having the same PAN.

Under Section 39(4) read with Rule 65, an ISD is required to furnish an ISD return in Form GSTR-6 by the 13th day of the month succeeding the calendar month. Form GSTR-6 itself contains complete details of input tax credit received from service providers and credit distributed to recipient units. Hence, an ISD is statutorily excluded from filing Form GSTR-1 [Section 37(1)].

ICAI TYK 4 (Filing through GST Practitioner)

Question:

Is it compulsory for a taxpayer to file the return by himself? Explain.

Answer:

No. A registered taxpayer is not required to file returns personally. Under Section 48(2), a registered person may authorise an approved Goods and Services Tax Practitioner (GSTP) to furnish the statement of outward supplies under Section 37, return under Section 39, annual return under Section 44, or final return under Section 45.

However, before filing, confirmation of the registered person must be obtained over SMS or email. The legal responsibility for the correctness of any particulars furnished in the return continues to remain solely with the registered person.

ICAI TYK 5 (Rectification of GSTR-1 & Revised Return)

Question:

Mr. Anand Kumar, a regular taxpayer, filed GSTR-1 for the month of August before the due date. Later, in the month of February next year, he discovers an error in the GSTR-1 of August already filed and wants to revise it. Advise him on the future course of action.

Answer:

1. Non-Availability of Revised Return:

Under GST, there is no legal provision for filing a "revised return". Once a return or statement of outward supplies is filed, it cannot be recalled or re-submitted.

2. Rectification in Subsequent Return:

Under Section 37(3), any error or omission in Form GSTR-1 must be rectified in the statement of outward supplies to be furnished for the month/quarter in which such error or omission is noticed. Therefore, Mr. Anand Kumar can rectify the error discovered in February by making appropriate disclosure in the Amendment Tables of Form GSTR-1 for the month of February.

3. Statutory Time Limit:

Under Section 37(3), rectification of details in GSTR-1 is permitted up to earlier of:

  • 30th day of November following the end of the financial year to which such details pertain; OR
  • The actual date of furnishing of the relevant Annual Return.

Since February falls well before the cut-off date of 30th November of the succeeding FY, Mr. Anand Kumar is well within the statutory time limit. If short payment of tax is involved, he must pay the differential tax along with interest under Section 50 in Form GSTR-3B for February.

ICAI TYK 6 (Dual Late Fees CGST & SGST)

Question:

B Ltd. has filed the return for the month of October belatedly. At the time of computing the late fee to be paid for delay in filing return, B Ltd. has taken a view that if the late fee has been paid as per the provisions under the CGST Act, there is no requirement of paying the late fee under the SGST Act for the same default. Examine whether B Ltd. has taken a correct view.

Answer:

The view taken by B Ltd. is incorrect.

GST is a dual tax system where the Central Government and State Governments concurrently levy tax under the CGST Act and the respective SGST/UTGST Act. Section 47 of the CGST Act provides for late fees for delay in filing returns, and an identical mirror provision exists under Section 47 of the respective SGST/UTGST Act.

Although a common electronic portal and a common return (Form GSTR-3B) are used, late fees must be paid separately under both the CGST Act and the SGST/UTGST Act. For instance, if the rationalized late fee is â‚¹50 per day, â‚¹25 is payable under CGST and â‚¹25 under SGST.

ICAI TYK 7 (Rectification during Scrutiny)

Question:

Tax authorities have been scrutinizing the returns furnished by A Ltd. During the scrutiny process, A Ltd. has been made aware by the authorities about an incorrect disclosure in a return under Section 39 filed by it for a particular tax period. A Ltd. seeks your opinion to rectify the incorrect disclosure made in the return.

Answer:

Section 39(9) of the CGST Act permits a registered person to rectify any omission or incorrect particulars in a return filed under Section 39 in the return for the tax period during which such error is noticed.

Crucial Statutory Proviso: The proviso to Section 39(9) explicitly provides that no such rectification shall be allowed if the error or omission is discovered as a result of scrutiny, audit, inspection or enforcement activity by the tax authorities.

Conclusion: In this case, since the incorrect disclosure was pointed out to A Ltd. by the tax authorities during the statutory scrutiny process, A Ltd. CANNOT rectify the error on its own in subsequent returns under Section 39(9). The differential liability must be settled pursuant to the scrutiny proceedings (e.g., via Form DRC-03) along with applicable interest and penalties.

ICAI TYK 8 (Final Return without Cancellation Order)

Question:

ABC Ltd. applied for cancellation of GST registration in March. In September, the consultant of ABC Ltd. suggested furnishing the final return in September, advising that a final return must be furnished before the due date of September return of the subsequent FY or before furnishing annual return, whichever is earlier. However, the jurisdictional authorities have not yet passed an order of cancellation. Whether the consultant's advice is correct?

Answer:

No, the advice given by the consultant is incorrect on two counts:

  1. Wrong Trigger: Under Section 45 read with Rule 81, a Final Return (Form GSTR-10) is required to be filed only when the registration is cancelled. Filing an application for cancellation does not automatically cancel registration. Until the Proper Officer passes an effective cancellation order, the obligation to file a final return does not arise.
  2. Wrong Due Date Rule: The time limit for filing Form GSTR-10 is within 3 months of the date of cancellation OR date of order of cancellation, whichever is later. The 30th November / September due date rule pertains to rectification of returns under Section 39(9), not to the final return under Section 45.

Therefore, ABC Ltd. is not required to file Form GSTR-10 in September; it must continue to file regular returns until cancellation is approved, and then file GSTR-10 within 3 months of the order.

ICAI TYK 9 (TDS Credit Mechanism)

Question:

XYZ Ltd. has deducted TDS from the consideration payable to A Ltd. for supplies made by it. The deductee, i.e., A Ltd., seeks your advice on taking credit for the TDS deducted. Also, will the tax deducted be shown in the electronic credit ledger or electronic cash ledger of A Ltd.?

Answer:

1. Credit in Electronic Cash Ledger [Section 51(5) & Rule 87(9)]:

Under Section 51(5) read with Rule 66 and Rule 87(9), the amount of tax deducted by the deductor (XYZ Ltd.) and reflected in its Form GSTR-7 shall be made available electronically to the deductee (A Ltd.) on the common portal. Upon validation and acceptance, the TDS amount is credited directly to the Electronic Cash Ledger of A Ltd., NOT the electronic credit ledger.

2. Utilization of TDS Credit:

Since the TDS amount is credited to the Electronic Cash Ledger, it has the character of cash money deposited in the Government treasury. A Ltd. can use this balance for discharging any liability towards output tax, interest, penalty, fees, or can claim a refund under Section 54(1) if unutilized.

ICAI TYK 10 (GSTP Return Disclosure)

Question:

Whether GSTPs are required to furnish any return for disclosure of activities carried out by them for any registered person during a tax period? Elucidate.

Answer:

No. Under Section 48(2) read with Rule 83, an approved Goods and Services Tax Practitioner (GSTP) is authorized to perform specified functions on behalf of a registered person, such as filing statements of outward supplies (GSTR-1), returns (GSTR-3B, GSTR-4, GSTR-9, GSTR-10), refund applications, etc., upon being duly authorised.

However, the GST law does not prescribe any separate return or periodic disclosure to be furnished by the GST Practitioner detailing the activities or services rendered to various clients. The returns filed by the GSTP on behalf of taxpayers are treated as returns of the taxpayers themselves.
