1. Introduction & The Architecture of Place of Supply

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a destination-based consumption tax. This fundamental principle dictates that the revenue from the tax must accrue to the jurisdiction where the goods or services are actually consumed, rather than the jurisdiction where they are manufactured or from where they originate.

🏛️ The Fundamental Tax Dilemma: Tangibles vs. Intangibles

Goods (Tangibles): Because goods are physical, their movement, physical delivery, and location at the time of consumption can generally be ascertained with relative ease.
Services (Intangibles): Services pose substantial legal and operational hurdles in identifying the actual place of consumption because:

  • Manner of delivery alters dynamically: For example, a mobile connection may switch between pre-paid and post-paid, or the customer's billing address may change while traveling.
  • Absence of fixed boundaries: The service provider, receiver, and performance can be geographically dispersed (e.g. an IT consultant working from a hotel in Goa for a client in Bengaluru on servers situated in Hyderabad).
  • Flow across multiple jurisdictions: Construction of an expressway or bridge spanning across two or more States, or an all-India software licensing arrangement.
  • Rapid technological evolution: Cloud computing, OTT media streaming, online gaming, and digital database access allow borderless consumption without any face-to-face contact.
⚖️ Constitutional & Statutory Scheme of IGST Act

To resolve these issues without arbitrary disputes between the Union and States, Article 269A(1) and Article 269A(5) of the Constitution of India empower Parliament to formulate principles for determining the:

  1. Place of Supply (PoS); and
  2. When a supply of goods or services takes place in the course of inter-State trade or commerce.

Consequently, Chapter V [Sections 10 to 13] and Chapter IV [Sections 7 to 9] of the Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) Act, 2017 lay down comprehensive rules to determine the nature of supply and jurisdiction.

⚠️ Critical Exam Reality: Why Determining PoS is Paramount!

The character of a supply as Inter-State (liable to IGST) or Intra-State (liable to CGST + SGST/UTGST) depends entirely on two variables:

Location of Supplier (LoS)   ⟷   Place of Supply (PoS)

  • If LoS and PoS are in different States / different UTs / a State and a UT $ ightarrow$ Inter-State Supply (IGST) [Section 7].
  • If LoS and PoS are in the same State or same UT $ ightarrow$ Intra-State Supply (CGST + SGST/UTGST) [Section 8].

Wrong Classification Penalty & Remedy [Section 77 CGST Act & Section 19 IGST Act]: If a supplier incorrectly treats an inter-State supply as intra-State (paying CGST+SGST) or vice versa, they must pay the correct tax. No interest is payable on the delayed payment of the correct tax, and the wrongly paid tax will be refunded to the taxpayer. However, working capital is blocked in the process!

2. Guiding Principles & Statutory Proxies

Since the ultimate consumption point of an intangible service cannot always be tracked physically, the GST law utilizes internationally recognized legal fictions known as "Proxies". A proxy is an objective factual attribute used to approximate the destination of consumption:

Proxy Category Practical Illustration in Law Primary Justification & Application
1. Location of Service Receiver (LSR) B2B general supplies under Section 12(2)(a); cross-border supplies under Section 13(2). In B2B models, the registered recipient consumes the service for business purposes or passes it down the supply chain where ultimate consumption occurs.
2. Place of Performance (PoP) Restaurant & catering, beauty treatments, fitness centers, physical training [Sec 12(4), 12(5)(b), 13(3)]. These services inherently require the physical presence of the recipient or physical presence of goods for immediate on-site consumption.
3. Location of Immovable Property Architects, construction, interior decoration, hotel accommodation [Sec 12(3), 13(4)]. Immovable property is permanently affixed to the earth in a defined State/UT; economic enjoyment is tied directly to that real estate.
4. Place of Actual Event / Amusement Park Admission to concerts, sporting matches (IPL), theatrical shows, exhibitions [Sec 12(6), 13(5)]. The experience and entertainment are inseparable from the geographical venue where the event physically takes place.
5. Location of Service Provider (LSP) Banking services to non-account holders [Sec 12(12)]; Intermediary services [Sec 13(8)(b)]. Residual proxy used where the customer's identity is anonymous, untraceable, or where agency operations are anchored in the supplier's territory.

B2B vs. B2C Transactions: The Core Distinction

The IGST Act creates a conscious structural divide between Business-to-Business (B2B) and Business-to-Consumer (B2C) supplies:

  • B2B Supplies (Registered Recipient): The recipient uses the service as an input in business and claims Input Tax Credit (ITC). To ensure seamless, unbroken credit flow, the law anchors the Place of Supply to the location of the registered recipient. The tax paid flows straight to the destination State where the recipient's output liability arises.
  • B2C Supplies (Unregistered Recipient): The recipient is the end consumer. There is no subsequent ITC claim. If the recipient's address is on record, tax flows to their State; otherwise, it is captured at the location of the supplier to prevent untaxed leakages.

3. Relevant Statutory Definitions (IGST & CGST Acts)

Before mastering the operative rules in Sections 10 to 13, students must command the precise definitions that govern cross-border trade, establishments, and transportation:

📜 Core Cross-Border Definitions
  • Export of Goods [Section 2(5) IGST Act]: Taking goods out of India to a place outside India. (Physical departure beyond Indian customs territory).
  • Import of Goods [Section 2(10) IGST Act]: Bringing goods into India from a place outside India.
  • Export of Services [Section 2(6) IGST Act]: The supply of any service when all 5 cumulative conditions are satisfied:
    1. The supplier of service is located in India;
    2. The recipient of service is located outside India;
    3. The place of supply of service is outside India;
    4. The payment for such service has been received by the supplier in convertible foreign exchange (or Indian Rupees wherever permitted by RBI, e.g. Rupee-Vostro accounts); and
    5. The supplier and recipient are not merely establishments of a distinct person in terms of Explanation 1 to Section 8.
  • Import of Services [Section 2(11) IGST Act]: The supply of any service where:
    1. The supplier of service is located outside India;
    2. The recipient of service is located in India; and
    3. The place of supply of service is in India.
📍 Location of Supplier and Recipient of Services [Sections 2(14) & 2(15) IGST]

The law sets out an identical 4-tier hierarchical test to pinpoint the location of the supplier or recipient:

Tier Location of Recipient of Service [Sec 2(14)] Location of Supplier of Service [Sec 2(15)]
1. Registered Location Where supply is received at a place of business for which registration has been obtained $ ightarrow$ Location of such place of business. Where supply is made from a place of business for which registration has been obtained $ ightarrow$ Location of such place of business.
2. Fixed Establishment Where received at a place other than registered place (a fixed establishment) $ ightarrow$ Location of such fixed establishment. Where made from a place other than registered place (a fixed establishment) $ ightarrow$ Location of such fixed establishment.
3. Multiple Establishments Where received at more than one establishment $ ightarrow$ Establishment most directly concerned with receipt of supply. Where made from more than one establishment $ ightarrow$ Establishment most directly concerned with provision of supply.
4. Residual Default In absence of any such place $ ightarrow$ Usual place of residence of the recipient (incorporation address for body corporate). In absence of any such place $ ightarrow$ Usual place of residence of the supplier.
🌐 Digital & Intermediary Definitions
  • Fixed Establishment [Section 2(7) IGST]: A place (other than registered place of business) characterized by a sufficient degree of permanence and suitable structure in terms of human and technical resources to enable it to supply or receive services for its own needs.
  • Intermediary [Section 2(13) IGST]: A broker, an agent or any other person, by whatever name called, who arranges or facilitates the supply of goods or services or both, or securities, between two or more persons, but does not include a person who supplies such goods or services on his own account.
  • Non-Taxable Online Recipient (NTOR) [Section 2(16) IGST]: Any unregistered person receiving OIDAR services located in the taxable territory for non-business or personal purposes.
  • Continuous Journey [Section 2(3) IGST]: A journey for which a single or more than one ticket/invoice is issued at the same time, with no stopover between any of the legs for which one or more separate tickets are issued. (Stopover: A place where passenger can disembark or has a break other than technical halt).

4. Place of Supply of Goods (Domestic Supplies) [Section 10]

Section 10 of the IGST Act, 2017 governs the determination of the Place of Supply (PoS) for all domestic supplies of goods (i.e. supplies other than imports into or exports from India). It is partitioned into five distinct operational scenarios under sub-section (1), followed by a residual rule in sub-section (2):

Section 10(1)(a): Supply Involves Movement of Goods

📜 Statutory Rule: Section 10(1)(a)

Where the supply involves movement of goods, whether by the supplier, or the recipient, or by any other person (such as a common carrier/transporter):
$\longrightarrow$ The Place of Supply shall be the location of the goods at the time at which the movement of goods terminates for delivery to the recipient.

💡 ICAI Example 1 • Delivery by Supplier via Transporter

Mr. A (a manufacturer registered in Ahmedabad, Gujarat) sells 50 air conditioners to Mr. B (registered in Jaipur, Rajasthan). The contract stipulates that Mr. A will arrange transportation via a logistics agency to Mr. B's warehouse in Jaipur.
Analysis: The supply involves movement of goods. Movement terminates when the air conditioners are unloaded and delivered at Jaipur, Rajasthan.
Place of Supply: Jaipur (Rajasthan).
Nature of Supply: Supplier is in Gujarat; PoS is in Rajasthan $ ightarrow$ Inter-State Supply liable to IGST.

💡 ICAI Example 2 • Movement Initiated by Recipient (Ex-Factory / Self-Collection)

Ms. Neha (registered in Indore, Madhya Pradesh) places an order on Apex Furnishings (registered in Nagpur, Maharashtra) on an ex-factory basis. Ms. Neha sends her own private truck to Nagpur, takes delivery of furniture at Nagpur factory gates, and transports the furniture back to Indore.
Analysis: Even though the recipient took physical possession at factory gate, the movement of goods for delivery to the recipient terminates only when the goods reach Indore, MP.
Place of Supply: Indore (Madhya Pradesh).
Nature of Supply: LoS is Maharashtra; PoS is Madhya Pradesh $ ightarrow$ Inter-State Supply (IGST).

Section 10(1)(b): "Bill-to Ship-to" Model & Deemed Delivery

📜 Statutory Language: Section 10(1)(b)

Where the goods are delivered by the supplier to a recipient or any other person on the direction of a third person (whether acting as an agent or otherwise), before or during movement of goods, either by way of transfer of documents of title to the goods or otherwise:
$\longrightarrow$ It shall be deemed that the said third person has received the goods, and the Place of Supply of such goods shall be the principal place of business of such third person.

🔍 The Two-Tier Anatomy of a Bill-to Ship-to Transaction

In every "Bill-to Ship-to" model, the law recognizes two distinct, independent supplies occurring simultaneously, each requiring separate GST invoicing:

  1. Supply 1 (Supplier $ ightarrow$ Third Person [Buyer]):
    • Supplier bills the Third Person.
    • Deemed delivery occurs to the Third Person under Section 10(1)(b).
    • Place of Supply = Principal Place of Business of the Third Person.
  2. Supply 2 (Third Person $ ightarrow$ Ultimate Recipient [Consignee]):
    • Third Person bills the Ultimate Consignee.
    • Physical movement terminates at the Consignee's destination under Section 10(1)(a).
    • Place of Supply = Location where movement terminates for delivery to the recipient.
💡 ICAI Comprehensive Example • Bill-to Ship-to Mechanics

Facts: Priyank Sales (Pune, Maharashtra) enters into an agreement to sell 100 laptops to Bisht Enterprises (Bareilly, Uttar Pradesh). Bisht Enterprises immediately instructs Priyank Sales to deliver these laptops directly to Sahil Pvt. Ltd. (Shimoga, Karnataka), who is a client of Bisht Enterprises.

Leg Contracting Parties Applicable Legal Section Place of Supply Tax Treatment
Leg 1 (Deemed Supply) Priyank Sales (Pune, MH) $ ightarrow$ Bisht Enterprises (Bareilly, UP) Section 10(1)(b): Principal place of business of Third Person. Bareilly (UP) LoS (MH) $ eq$ PoS (UP) $ ightarrow$ Inter-State Supply (IGST). Priyank bills Bisht with IGST.
Leg 2 (Actual Movement) Bisht Enterprises (Bareilly, UP) $ ightarrow$ Sahil Pvt. Ltd. (Shimoga, KA) Section 10(1)(a): Where movement terminates for delivery. Shimoga (KA) LoS (UP) $ eq$ PoS (KA) $ ightarrow$ Inter-State Supply (IGST). Bisht bills Sahil with IGST.

Section 10(1)(c): Supply Does Not Involve Movement of Goods

📜 Statutory Rule: Section 10(1)(c)

Where the supply does not involve movement of goods, whether by the supplier or the recipient:
$\longrightarrow$ The Place of Supply shall be the location of such goods at the time of the delivery to the recipient.

💡 ICAI Example 6 • Sale of Leased Plant In-Situ

MA Pvt. Ltd. (New Delhi) has leased heavy printing machinery to KB Press in Ghaziabad (Uttar Pradesh). After two years, KB Press decides to purchase the machine permanently. The machine remains installed inside KB Press's premises in Ghaziabad, and title passes without any physical transit.
Analysis: No movement of goods occurs. The machinery is already situated at Ghaziabad, UP at the time of delivery (passing of title).
Place of Supply: Ghaziabad (Uttar Pradesh).
Tax: LoS is Delhi; PoS is UP $ ightarrow$ Inter-State Supply (IGST).

Section 10(1)(ca): Supply of Goods to an Unregistered Person (B2C & E-Commerce)

📜 Statutory Amendment [Inserted by Finance Act, 2023]

Where the supply of goods is made to an unregistered person:
$\longrightarrow$ The Place of Supply shall be the address of delivery of goods recorded in the invoice issued in respect of the said supply; and
$\longrightarrow$ Where the address of delivery is not recorded in the invoice, the Place of Supply shall be the location of the supplier.

📜 Landmark CBIC Clarification • Circular No. 209/3/2024-GST (dated 26.06.2024)

The Practical E-Commerce Issue: An unregistered consumer, Mr. A, residing in State X places an online order for a smartphone on Amazon/Flipkart. While ordering, Mr. A provides his permanent billing address in State X, but specifies that the phone must be delivered as a gift to his cousin residing in State Y.
The CBIC Clarification:

  • Under Section 10(1)(ca), the Place of Supply is strictly the address of delivery recorded on the tax invoice (State Y), NOT the billing address (State X).
  • The supplier/e-commerce platform is permitted to record the delivery address as the address of the recipient on the invoice to ensure appropriate SGST revenue flows to State Y where physical consumption takes place.

Section 10(1)(d): Goods Assembled or Installed at Site

📜 Statutory Rule: Section 10(1)(d)

Where the goods are assembled or installed at site:
$\longrightarrow$ The Place of Supply shall be the place of such installation or assembly.

💡 ICAI Example 7 • Assembly of Central Air Conditioning Plant

CoolTech Air Systems (registered in Kolkata, West Bengal) receives a contract to supply and install a complete centralized chilling plant at a hospital in Patna (Bihar). The various individual chillers, compressors, and ducts are dispatched from Kolkata and assembled at the hospital site in Patna.
Place of Supply: Patna (Bihar) — where the plant is assembled and tested.
Tax: LoS is West Bengal; PoS is Bihar $ ightarrow$ Inter-State Supply (IGST).

Section 10(1)(e): Goods Supplied on Board a Conveyance

📜 Statutory Rule: Section 10(1)(e)

Where the goods are supplied on board a conveyance, including a vessel, an aircraft, a train, or a motor vehicle:
$\longrightarrow$ The Place of Supply shall be the location at which such goods are taken on board the conveyance.

💡 ICAI Example 8 • Snacks Loaded on Train / Flight

Mr. X boards the New Delhi to Kota train at New Delhi. The IRCTC pantry contractor loaded meal packets and soft drinks onto the train coach at New Delhi railway station. Mr. X purchases a beverage packet while the train is passing through Mathura (Uttar Pradesh).
Analysis: Regardless of where the passenger consumes or purchases the beverage, the goods were taken on board at New Delhi.
Place of Supply: New Delhi.
Tax: If IRCTC's registration is in New Delhi $ ightarrow$ Intra-State Supply (CGST + UTGST/SGST).

⚖️ Section 10(2): Residual Category

Where the Place of Supply of goods cannot be determined under any of the clauses (a), (b), (c), (ca), (d), or (e) of Section 10(1), it shall be determined in such manner as may be prescribed by the Government on the recommendations of the GST Council.

Section 11 of the IGST Act establishes the international tax nexus for cross-border physical goods:

Import of Goods

6. Place of Supply of Services (Domestic: LoS & LoR in India) [Section 12]

Section 12 of the IGST Act establishes the comprehensive framework for determining the Place of Supply of services where both the supplier and the recipient are located in India. Sub-section (2) provides the default/general rule, while sub-sections (3) to (14) lay down specific rules for specialized categories of services:

Section 12(2): The General / Default Rule

📜 Statutory Rule: Section 12(2)

The Place of Supply of services, except the services specified in sub-sections (3) to (14), shall be:

  • (a) Made to a Registered Person (B2B): The location of such person (Location of Recipient).
  • (b) Made to Any Person other than a Registered Person (B2C):
    1. The location of the recipient where the address on record exists; and
    2. The location of the supplier of services in all other cases.
📜 Mandatory State Recording on Invoices • Circular No. 242/36/2024-GST (dated 31.12.2024)

To prevent unregistered B2C digital transactions from defaulting to the supplier's State under 12(2)(b)(ii), CBIC has clarified that under the proviso to Rule 46(f) of the CGST Rules, 2017:

  • Mandatory Rule: In respect of ALL online/digital supplies of services supplied to an unregistered recipient (such as OTT platforms, e-newspapers/magazines, online gaming, online telecom, and mobile software applications), suppliers are mandatorily required to record the name of the State of the recipient on the tax invoice, regardless of the value of supply.
  • Result: The Place of Supply shall always be declared as the location of the recipient (based on the name of the State) in GSTR-1/1A, ensuring destination tax credit flows to the consumer's home State.
💡 ICAI Example 11 & 12 • Chartered Accountant Consultancy

B2B Scenario: Mr. A (a CA registered in New Delhi) provides audit and management advisory services to MB Pvt. Ltd. (registered in Noida, Uttar Pradesh).
$ ightarrow$ Recipient is registered. Under Section 12(2)(a), PoS is Noida (UP). LoS (Delhi) $ eq$ PoS (UP) $ ightarrow$ Inter-State Supply (IGST).

B2C Scenario: Mr. A (CA in Gurugram, Haryana) provides personal income tax filing services to Mr. C (an unregistered individual residing in New Delhi).
$ ightarrow$ If Mr. C's Delhi address is on record in the engagement letter/invoice $ ightarrow$ PoS is New Delhi (IGST).
$ ightarrow$ If Mr. C is a walk-in client and no address is recorded $ ightarrow$ PoS is Gurugram (CGST + SGST).

Section 12(3): Immovable Property, Hotel Accommodation & Houseboats

📜 Scope of Section 12(3)(a), (b), (c) & (d)

The Place of Supply for services supplied:

  • (a) Directly in relation to an immovable property (including architects, interior decorators, surveyors, engineers, estate agents, construction management);
  • (b) By way of lodging accommodation by a hotel, inn, guest house, homestay, club, campsite, or houseboat/vessel;
  • (c) For organizing any marriage, reception, official, social, cultural, or business function in an immovable property (including banquet hall hire and event catering);
  • (d) Any service ancillary to the above services;

$\longrightarrow$ Shall be the location at which the immovable property, boat, or vessel is located or intended to be located.

⚠️ CRITICAL EXAM TRAP 1: Immovable Property Located OUTSIDE India [Proviso to Sec 12(3)]

Where the immovable property or boat or vessel is located or intended to be located OUTSIDE INDIA, and both supplier and recipient are in India:
$\longrightarrow$ The Place of Supply shall be the LOCATION OF THE RECIPIENT!

Illustration (ICAI TYK Question 8 Case I): Mr. Mahendra Goyal (an interior decorator in New Delhi) provides professional design services to Mr. Harish Jain (resident of Maharashtra) for his newly purchased penthouse located in New York, USA.
• Both supplier and recipient are in India.
• Property is located outside India (New York).
• Place of Supply = Location of Recipient = Maharashtra (NOT New York!).
• Tax: LoS (Delhi) $ eq$ PoS (Maharashtra) $ ightarrow$ Inter-State Supply liable to IGST.

📐 Multi-State Immovable Property Allocation [Rule 4 & Rule 5 CGST Rules]

Where the immovable property extends across more than one State or Union Territory (e.g. a national pipeline, railway line, or hotel chain booking):

  • The supply is treated as made in each of the respective States/UTs in proportion to the value specified in the contract.
  • In the absence of contract value, Rule 4 mandates allocation based on:
    • Lodging accommodation: Number of nights stayed in each property.
    • Banquet / Social events: Proportionate flat area situated in each State.
    • Other services: Area of property located in each State/UT.
  • Houseboats/Vessels [Rule 5]: Proportionate time spent by the boat/vessel in each State/UT as declared by the boat operator.

Section 12(4) & 12(5): Personal Care, Performance & Training

Sub-Section Service Description Recipient Category Place of Supply (PoS)
Section 12(4) Restaurant and catering services, personal grooming, fitness, beauty treatment, health services including cosmetic & plastic surgery. Registered (B2B) or Unregistered (B2C) Location where the services are ACTUALLY PERFORMED. (No B2B/B2C distinction!).
Section 12(5) Training and performance appraisal services (e.g. corporate leadership workshops, employee performance reviews). B2B: Registered Person Location of such registered person (LoR).
B2C: Unregistered Person Location where the services are ACTUALLY PERFORMED.
💡 ICAI Example 21 & 22 • Makeup Artist vs. Corporate Training

Section 12(4) Performance: Mr. Timmy Ferreira (a celebrity makeup artist in Kolkata) travels to Jaipur, Rajasthan to do bridal makeup for an industrialist family.
$ ightarrow$ PoS is Jaipur (Rajasthan) where makeup is physically performed. LoS (WB) $ eq$ PoS (RJ) $ ightarrow$ Inter-State Supply (IGST).

Section 12(5) B2B Training: DEO Consultants (Kolkata) is engaged by Tata Steel Ltd. (registered in Jamshedpur, Jharkhand) to conduct a 3-day executive training program for its senior managers at a retreat resort in Puri (Odisha).
$ ightarrow$ Service is provided to a registered company. Under Section 12(5)(a), PoS is Jamshedpur (Jharkhand), regardless of the training being physically held in Odisha!

Section 12(6) & 12(7): Admission to Events vs. Organizing Events

A vital distinction in GST law exists between selling entry tickets to attend an event [Sec 12(6)] versus event management/organizing services [Sec 12(7)]:

Feature Admission to Event [Section 12(6)] Organizing / Event Management [Section 12(7)]
Scope of Services Right to enter / tickets for cultural, artistic, sporting, scientific, educational, entertainment events or amusement parks. Planning, stage design, artist coordination, venue management, conferences, celebrations, and ancillary sponsorship.
B2B Supply Where the event is actually held (or where park is located). Location of the Registered Recipient.
B2C Supply Where the event is actually held. Where the event is actually held.
Event Held OUTSIDE India Where event is held (Outside India). Location of the Recipient (Proviso to Sec 12(7)!).
💡 ICAI Example 28 & 29 • Wedding Planning in India vs. Overseas

In India: Grand Wedding Planners (Chennai, TN) is hired by Mr. Ramesh (unregistered individual in Hyderabad, Telangana) to organize his daughter's wedding in Udaipur, Rajasthan.
$ ightarrow$ Recipient is unregistered. Under Section 12(7)(a)(ii), PoS is Udaipur (Rajasthan) where the wedding event is held.

Outside India (Proviso to Sec 12(7)): If Grand Wedding Planners (Chennai) is hired by Mr. Ramesh (unregistered in Hyderabad) to organize the destination wedding in Bali, Indonesia:
$ ightarrow$ Event is held outside India. Under the Proviso to Section 12(7), PoS is Location of Recipient = Hyderabad (Telangana). (Taxable in India as Inter-State supply, NOT exempt!).

Section 12(8): Transportation of Goods (Including Mail & Courier)

📜 Statutory Rule: Section 12(8)
  • (a) Made to a Registered Person (B2B): The location of such registered person.
  • (b) Made to an Unregistered Person (B2C): The location where goods are handed over for transportation.
⚠️ STATUTORY UPDATE • Omission of Proviso to Section 12(8) [Finance Act, 2023]

Prior Law: Formerly, a proviso to Section 12(8) specified that where goods were transported to a place outside India, the PoS was the destination of goods (outside India).
Current Amended Position: The Proviso has been omitted by the Finance Act, 2023. Therefore, domestic freight services for outbound goods are now strictly governed by clauses (a) and (b):
• If an Indian exporter (registered) hires an Indian shipping/courier company to transport goods to USA $ ightarrow$ PoS is the Location of the Registered Exporter in India! (Subject to GST; exporter claims ITC/refund).

Section 12(9): Passenger Transportation Services

📜 Statutory Rule: Section 12(9)
  • (a) To a Registered Person (B2B): Location of such registered person.
  • (b) To an Unregistered Person (B2C): Place where the passenger embarks on the conveyance for a continuous journey.

Explanation 1: The return journey shall be treated as a separate journey, even if the right to travel on both legs is issued at the same time.

• Leg 1 (Delhi $ ightarrow$ Mumbai): Passenger embarks at New Delhi $ ightarrow$ PoS is New Delhi.
• Leg 2 (Mumbai $ ightarrow$ Delhi): Treated as a separate journey. Passenger embarks at Mumbai $ ightarrow$ PoS is Mumbai (Maharashtra).

$\longrightarrow$ The Place of Supply shall be the location of the first scheduled point of departure of that conveyance for the journey.

7. Place of Supply of Services (Cross-Border: LoS or LoR Outside India) [Section 13]

Section 13 of the IGST Act applies where either the location of the supplier or the location of the recipient is outside India. This section is vital for determining whether an outbound transaction qualifies as a zero-rated Export of Services [Section 2(6)] or whether an inbound transaction attracts GST under the Reverse Charge Mechanism (Import of Services) [Section 2(11)]:

Section 13(2): The General / Default Cross-Border Rule

📜 Statutory Rule: Section 13(2)

The Place of Supply of services, except the services specified in sub-sections (3) to (13), shall be:

Place of Supply = Location of the Recipient of Services (LoR)

Residual Exception: Where the location of the recipient of services is not available in the ordinary course of business, the Place of Supply shall be the location of the supplier of services (LoS).

💡 ICAI Example • Software / Business Advisory to Foreign Client

Infosys (Bengaluru, India) provides customized enterprise software coding and architectural consulting to a client based in Germany. The service does not fall under any of the specific clauses (3) to (13).
$ ightarrow$ Governed by Section 13(2) $ ightarrow$ PoS is Germany (Location of Recipient).
$ ightarrow$ All 5 conditions of Section 2(6) satisfied $ ightarrow$ Export of Services (Zero-Rated).

Section 13(3): Performance-Based Services on Goods & Individuals

📜 Section 13(3)(a): Services Supplied in Respect of Goods

Services supplied in respect of goods which are required to be made physically available by the recipient to the supplier (or to a person acting on behalf of the supplier) in order to provide the service:
$\longrightarrow$ Place of Supply = Location where the services are ACTUALLY PERFORMED.

First Proviso (Remote Electronic Means): Where such services are provided from a remote location by electronic means without physical shifting of the goods:
$\longrightarrow$ Place of Supply = Location where the goods are SITUATED at the time of supply of service.

Second Proviso (Temporary Import for Repairs/Job Work - VITAL EXCEPTION!): This clause shall NOT apply in respect of goods that are temporarily imported into India for repair or for any other treatment or process and are exported back after such repair/process without being put to any use in India (other than use necessary for repair):
$\longrightarrow$ Falls outside 13(3)(a) and reverts to Section 13(2) (Location of Foreign Recipient) $ ightarrow$ Qualifies as an Export of Services!

💎 Cutting & Polishing of Diamonds • Circular No. 103/22/2019-GST

Unpolished raw diamonds imported temporarily into India for cutting, polishing, laser engraving, and finishing, and subsequently exported back to overseas diamond dealers without being put to any domestic use:
$ ightarrow$ Fully protected under the Second Proviso to Section 13(3)(a).
$ ightarrow$ Place of Supply is determined under Section 13(2) (Location of Overseas Recipient).
$ ightarrow$ Indian job-workers / diamond processors pay 0% GST (Zero-rated export) and can claim refund of accumulated unutilized ITC on capital goods and inputs!

📜 Section 13(3)(b): Services Supplied to an Individual

Services supplied to an individual which require the physical presence of the recipient or the person acting on his behalf:
$\longrightarrow$ Place of Supply = Location where the services are ACTUALLY PERFORMED. (e.g. cosmetic surgery at a Delhi hospital for an American tourist $ ightarrow$ PoS is New Delhi).

Section 13(4), 13(5), 13(6) & 13(7): Immovable Property, Events & Multi-State Rules

Sub-Section Category of Cross-Border Service Statutory Place of Supply Illustrative Application

Place where the immovable property is located or intended to be located. London architect designs a commercial complex in Mumbai for an Indian builder $ ightarrow$ PoS is Mumbai (India) $ ightarrow$ Import of service under RCM.
Section 13(5) Admission to or organization of cultural, artistic, sporting, scientific, educational, entertainment events, or celebrations/conferences. Place where the event is ACTUALLY HELD. Indian event organizer manages an international medical summit in Dubai for an Indian hospital $ ightarrow$ PoS is Dubai (Section 13(5) applies because event is outside India!).
Section 13(6) Where services under 13(3), 13(4), or 13(5) are supplied at more than one location, INCLUDING a location in the taxable territory. Location in the TAXABLE TERRITORY! ICAI Exam Trap (TYK 11(iii)): Interior decorator in Dehradun provides renovation services for villas in Dehradun (India) and Venice (Italy). Because part of supply is in India $ ightarrow$ Entire PoS is Dehradun (Taxable Territory)!
Section 13(7) Services under 13(3), 13(4), or 13(5) supplied in more than one State or Union Territory of India. Treated as supplied in each of the respective States/UTs in proportion to contract value. Allocation based on contractually agreed consideration or reasonable accounting principles.

Section 13(8): Specified Services Taxed at Location of Supplier (LSS)

⚡ The Section 13(8) Trap: PoS = Location of Supplier of Services (LSS)

Under Section 13(8), Parliament has carved out three specific industries where the Place of Supply is NOT the foreign recipient's location, but the LOCATION OF THE SUPPLIER OF SERVICES (LSS). As a result, these services CAN NEVER QUALIFY AS EXPORT OF SERVICES [because condition (c) of Section 2(6) fails!]:

  1. (a) Banking Company / Financial Institution / NBFC to Account Holders: Services supplied to account holders (e.g. SBI London branch serving Indian resident account holder).
  2. (b) Intermediary Services [Section 2(13)]: Commission agents, buying/selling brokers, and representatives who merely arrange or facilitate supplies between overseas buyers and sellers without supplying on own account.
  3. (c) Hiring of Means of Transport: Hiring of motor vehicles, trucks, or containers (other than aircraft and vessels) up to a period of one month.
📜 Circular No. 220/14/2024-GST • Custodial Services to FPIs

Question: When an Indian bank provides custodial and depository safekeeping services to Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs), does it fall under Section 13(8)(a)?
CBIC Clarification: Custodial services are specialized asset protection services provided to institutional investors and are NOT services provided to "ordinary account holders". Hence, Section 13(8)(a) does NOT apply. The Place of Supply falls under Section 13(2) (Location of Recipient / FPI abroad).

📜 Circular No. 232/26/2024-GST • Data Hosting Services & ITeS Back-Office

Question: Are Indian Data Hosting Service Providers (DHSPs) providing server infrastructure to foreign Cloud Computing Service Providers (CCSPs) treated as "Intermediaries"?
CBIC Clarification:

  • DHSPs provide data center hosting, cooling, bandwidth, and cybersecurity on a principal-to-principal basis on their own account. They do not broker or arrange transactions between CCSP and end-users.
  • Data hosting is neither an immovable property lease nor a supply of goods made physically available.
  • Therefore, DHSP services are governed by the default rule Section 13(2) $ ightarrow$ PoS is outside India $ ightarrow$ Eligible for Export of Services (0% GST)!

9. Determination of Nature of Supply: Inter-State, Intra-State & Territorial Waters

Once the Place of Supply (PoS) is determined using Sections 10 to 13, it must be cross-matched against the Location of the Supplier (LoS) to categorize the tax liability:

Statutory Category Core Legal Principles & Deemed Inclusions Tax Levied & Exceptions
Inter-State Supply
[Section 7 IGST Act]		 1. LoS and PoS are in two different States, two different UTs, or a State and a UT.
2. Import of goods into India till they cross customs frontiers.
3. Import of services.
4. Supply to or by a Special Economic Zone (SEZ) developer or unit [Sec 7(5)(b) — Deemed Inter-State regardless of physical location!].
5. Supply made in the taxable territory not being an intra-State supply. 		Integrated Tax (IGST).
Maximum statutory rate: 40%.
Intra-State Supply
[Section 8 IGST Act]		 Where the LoS and the PoS are in the same State or same Union Territory.
Non-Obstante Exclusions:
• Supply to/by an SEZ unit/developer.
• Import of goods.
• Supply to outbound international tourists [Sec 15]. 		CGST + SGST (or CGST + UTGST for Union Territories without legislature).
Supplies in Territorial Waters
[Section 9 IGST Act]		 Where the Location of the Supplier is in the territorial waters of India (up to 12 nautical miles from baseline), or the Place of Supply is in territorial waters:
$\longrightarrow$ Deemed to be in the coastal State or Union Territory where the nearest point of the appropriate baseline is located. 		Determined based on whether coastal baseline State matches recipient State.
⚠️ Special Economic Zone (SEZ) Absolute Rule [Section 7(5)(b)]

Any supply of goods or services made TO or BY an SEZ unit or SEZ developer is ALWAYS classified as an Inter-State Supply liable to IGST, even if the supplier's warehouse and the SEZ boundary are located in the very same city or across the street from each other within the same State!

11. Test Your Knowledge — All 12 ICAI Questions (With Full Solutions)

📋 Exam Methodology for PoS Questions
  • Step 1: Identify if supply involves Goods or Services.
  • Step 2: Identify the Location of Supplier (LoS) and Location of Recipient (LoR).
  • Step 3: Check if either party is outside India (Sec 13) or both in India (Sec 10, 11, 12).
  • Step 4: Apply the most specific applicable sub-section. Only if no specific rule fits, apply the general/default rule.
  • Step 5: Compare LoS vs PoS → Same State/UT = Intra-State (CGST+SGST); Different = Inter-State (IGST).
🎯 TYK Question 1 • Legal Services from UK Attorney (Cross-Border RCM)

Facts: XY Ltd. (registered in Rajasthan) received legal services from an attorney in UK (unrelated person). Payment of £8,000 was made.

Determine: Place of supply and whether XY Ltd. must pay GST under reverse charge.

🔑 Solution:

  • Supplier (UK Attorney) is OUTSIDE India → Section 13 applies.
  • Legal services do not fall under any specific clause of Sec 13(3) to 13(13).
  • Under Section 13(2) (Default Rule) → Place of Supply = Location of Recipient = Rajasthan, India.
  • Supplier is located in a non-taxable territory and recipient is in India → qualifies as Import of Services [Section 2(11)].
  • Under Reverse Charge Notification No. 10/2017-IT(R): When a service is supplied by a person in a non-taxable territory to a taxable territory person (other than Non-Taxable Online Recipient), the tax is payable by the recipient under RCM.
  • Conclusion: XY Ltd. must pay GST under Reverse Charge on £8,000. PoS is Rajasthan (India). Supply is Inter-State → IGST applies under RCM.

🎯 TYK Question 6 • Indian Company Supporting Foreign Buyer to Purchase from Indian Vendor (Intermediary?)

Facts: ABC Pvt. Ltd. (New Delhi) provides support services to foreign customers to help identify and evaluate Indian vendors. The foreign customer then independently places a purchase order on the Indian vendor. ABC charges US$1,00,000 for these identification/evaluation services.

🔑 Solution:

  • The key question: Is ABC an Intermediary [Sec 2(13)]?

  • Governed by Section 13(2) → PoS = Location of Foreign Recipient (Outside India) → Qualifies as Export of Services.
