2.1 Introduction & Dual GST Architecture

📌 Power to Levy Tax

Power to levy tax is drawn from the Constitution of India. To pave the way for Goods and Services Tax, the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016 inserted Article 246A, creating an enabling provision to levy GST concurrently on the supply of goods or services or both in India.

The very basis for the charge of tax in any taxing statute is the taxable event. In GST, the taxable event is SUPPLY (as studied in Chapter 1). India adopted a Dual GST Model where the Centre and the States concurrently levy tax on the same taxable supply:

Intra-State Supply

Where the location of the supplier and the place of supply are in the same State or Union Territory:
• CGST: Levied by the Central Government under Section 9 of the CGST Act.
• SGST / UTGST: Levied by the State Government or Union Territory.

Inter-State Supply

Where the location of the supplier and place of supply are in (i) two different States, (ii) two different UTs, or (iii) a State and a UT:
• IGST: Levied by the Central Government under Section 5 of the IGST Act (apportioned between Centre and States under Art 269A).

Section 9(1) & 5(1) Levy & Collection of CGST & IGST [ICAI Example 2]

Statutory Language: Section 9(1) CGST Act

"Subject to the provisions of sub-section (2), there shall be levied a tax called the central goods and services tax on all intra-State supplies of goods or services or both, except on the supply of alcoholic liquor for human consumption, on the value determined under section 15 and at such rates, not exceeding twenty per cent., as may be notified by the Government on the recommendations of the Council and collected in such manner as may be prescribed and shall be paid by the taxable person."

ICAI Example (2) • Intra-State Levy

A registered dealer in Bengaluru, Karnataka supplies industrial machinery to a buyer in Mysuru, Karnataka for ₹10,00,000. Since both location of supplier and place of supply are in Karnataka, it is an intra-State supply.
Tax Applicable: CGST @ 9% (₹90,000) and SGST @ 9% (₹90,000) under Section 9(1) of the CGST Act and Karnataka SGST Act.

Maximum Statutory Cap: CGST / SGST

The maximum rate of CGST is capped at 20% by Parliament.
Consequently, the maximum combined statutory rate for CGST + SGST is 40%.

Maximum Statutory Cap: IGST

Under Section 5(1) of the IGST Act, the rate of IGST is capped at 40%.
Standard notified slab rates in force: 0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28%.

Section 9(3) / 5(3) Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) — Concept & Statutory Rules

🔄 Forward Charge vs Reverse Charge

• Forward Charge (FCM): The normal mechanism where the supplier is liable to collect and deposit GST with the Government.
• Reverse Charge (RCM): The statutory mechanism where the recipient of goods or services is legally liable to pay GST directly to the Government.

⚡ Golden Rules of RCM (Essential for Exam Computations)
  1. Mandatory Registration: Under Section 24(iii), any person who is liable to pay tax under reverse charge must obtain compulsory registration under GST, irrespective of the threshold turnover limit!
  2. Payment in Cash ONLY: Under Section 49(4) read with Rule 85, tax payable under reverse charge CANNOT be paid by utilizing Input Tax Credit (ITC). It must be paid strictly in CASH through the Electronic Cash Ledger!
  3. Subsequent ITC Availability: Once the recipient pays GST under RCM in cash, the tax so paid is eligible as Input Tax Credit in the same month (subject to Section 16 & 17(5) rules).
  4. Invoicing by Recipient: Under Section 31(3)(f), a registered recipient receiving goods/services liable to RCM from an unregistered supplier must issue an invoice within 30 days. Under Section 31(3)(g), he must issue a Payment Voucher at the time of making payment.
  5. Composition Taxpayer: If a composition dealer receives supplies liable to RCM, he must pay tax at the normal applicable rate (not composition rate) in cash, and cannot claim ITC!

Option 1 (RCM Route): GTA does not pay GST. The specified recipient pays 5% GST under RCM in cash. Recipient can claim ITC. GTA gets NO ITC.

• Recipient is a Government Department registered only for TDS under Section 51 → Excluded from RCM → Forward Charge (Example 30)!

Section 9(5) / 5(5) Tax Liability on Electronic Commerce Operators (ECO) [ICAI Example 33]

Statutory Language: Section 9(5)

"The Government may notify categories of services the tax on intra-State supplies of which shall be paid by the electronic commerce operator if such services are supplied through it, and all the provisions of this Act shall apply to such electronic commerce operator as if he is the supplier liable for paying the tax."

ICAI Example (33) • Cab Booked Through RideNow ECO

Pranav books a taxi cab through RideNow mobile application. The cab service is provided by driver Rajesh.
Conclusion: Under Section 9(5), RideNow (the ECO) is deemed to be the supplier and is legally liable to pay GST on the ride fare!

Notified Service under Sec 9(5) Condition for ECO Liability Person Liable to Pay GST
1. Passenger Transportation (Omnibus, radio taxi, motor cab, maxi cab, motorcycle) Always liable, irrespective of whether the transport driver/operator is registered or unregistered. ECO is always liable (e.g. Uber, Ola, Rapido).
2. Hotel Accommodation Services (Inns, guest houses, clubs, campsites) Applicable ONLY IF the underlying hotel/inn is NOT liable to register under Section 22(1). If hotel is unregistered → ECO liable (MakeMyTrip, OYO).
If hotel is registered → Hotel itself pays GST.
3. Housekeeping Services (Plumbing, carpentering, cleaning) Applicable ONLY IF the service provider is NOT liable to register under Section 22(1). If plumber is unregistered → ECO liable (Urban Company).
If plumber is registered → Plumber pays GST.
4. Restaurant Services (Supplied through cloud kitchens or restaurants) Other than restaurant service provided at specified premises (hotel accommodation having declared tariff > ₹7,500/night). ECO is liable (Zomato, Swiggy). If supplied from a 5-star hotel (> ₹7,500 tariff) → Hotel pays GST under normal forward charge!

Section 10(1) & 10(2) Composition Levy — Eligibility Thresholds & Mechanism [ICAI Example 34]

📌 Simple & Hassle-Free Scheme for Small Taxpayers

The composition levy is an optional scheme designed for small businesses to reduce the compliance burden of maintaining elaborate accounts, filing detailed invoices, and submitting monthly returns. Tax is paid at a nominal flat percentage of turnover.

ICAI Example (34) • All-India PAN Aggregate Turnover Test

A footwear dealer ‘Prithviraj’ has offices in Maharashtra and Goa under the same PAN. He makes intra-State supplies from both offices. In the preceding FY, turnover of Maharashtra office was ₹120 Lakhs and Goa office was ₹25 Lakhs (Total ₹145 Lakhs).
Conclusion: Aggregate turnover on all-India basis is ₹145 Lakhs (which is ≤ ₹1.5 Crore). Prithviraj is eligible for composition scheme in both States!

⚠️ Crucial Distinction: Manufacturer vs Trader Turnover Base

• Manufacturer: Pays 1% on TOTAL turnover in State/UT (both taxable and exempt supplies are taxed!).
• Trader: Pays 1% on taxable turnover ONLY. Turnover of exempt goods is excluded from the tax computation (TYK Question 10).

Second Proviso to Sec 10(1) Marginal Services Limit for Goods Suppliers [ICAI Examples 37 & 38]

Statutory Cap on Supply of Marginal Services

Primarily, Section 10(1) is for suppliers of goods and restaurant services. However, small traders and manufacturers often provide incidental repair, installation, or warranty services.
Under the Second Proviso to Section 10(1), a person opting for composition levy may supply services (other than restaurant services) of value not exceeding:

Maximum Marginal Service Value = HIGHER of:
(a) 10% of Turnover in State/UT in the preceding Financial Year, OR
(b) ₹ 5,00,000
ICAI Example (37) • Electronic Product Dealer Repair Services

Ramsewak is engaged in supply of electronic products in Madhya Pradesh. His turnover in preceding FY was ₹60 Lakhs. In the current FY, he wishes to supply electronic repair and maintenance services along with products.
Permissible Service Limit: Higher of:
• 10% of ₹60 Lakhs = ₹6,00,000, OR
• ₹5,00,000
Conclusion: Ramsewak can supply repair services up to ₹6 Lakhs in the current FY without losing his Section 10(1) composition eligibility!

ICAI Example (38) • Interest on Loans/Deposits (Exclusion Rule)

Ms. Fatima runs a small readymade garments shop in Kochi, Kerala under composition scheme. Her turnover from garment sales in preceding FY was ₹40 Lakhs. During the year, she earned ₹80,000 as bank interest on fixed deposits.
Statutory Rule (Explanation to Sec 10(1)): For computing marginal service limit, aggregate turnover, and tax payment, the value of exempt services by way of extending deposits, loans or advances where consideration is interest or discount shall NOT be taken into account.
Conclusion: Fatima’s interest income of ₹80,000 will not disqualify her and will not count towards her ₹5,00,000 marginal service ceiling!

A hair stylist ‘Billoo Barber’ has salons in Delhi and Haryana. In preceding FY, his aggregate turnover was ₹45 Lakhs.
Conclusion: Since turnover is ≤ ₹50 Lakhs, Billoo Barber is eligible for composition scheme for services under Section 10(2A) in both Delhi and Haryana at the rate of 6% (3% CGST + 3% SGST).

A photographer ‘Champak’ commences photography services in Delhi on 1st April 2024. His turnover reaches ₹20 Lakhs on 1st October 2024 (when he becomes liable to register under Section 22). By 31st March 2025, his total turnover reaches ₹50 Lakhs. He opts for Section 10(2A).

• 1st October to 31st March (₹20 Lakhs to ₹50 Lakhs): Taxable under Section 10(2A) @ 6% on ₹30 Lakhs!

Conditions & Restrictions Statutory Disqualifications & Operational Rules

🚫 Persons Ineligible for Composition Levy under Section 10(1) & 10(2A):

  1. Non-Taxable Supplies: Any supplier engaged in supply of goods or services which are not leviable to tax under GST (e.g. serving alcoholic liquor for human consumption).
  2. Inter-State Outward Supplies: A person making any inter-State outward supply of goods or services is strictly prohibited! (Note: Inter-State inward procurement is allowed!).
  3. ECO Supplies: Supplying goods or services through an E-Commerce Operator who is required to collect TCS under Section 52.
  4. Manufacturer of Notified Goods: Any person engaged in the manufacture of:
    • Ice cream and other edible ice (with or without cocoa).
    • Pan masala.
    • Tobacco and manufactured tobacco substitutes.
    • Aerated water.
    • Fly ash bricks, building bricks, brick earth, roofing tiles.
    (Note: A trader of these notified goods is ELIGIBLE; only the manufacturer is barred!).
  5. Casual Taxable Person (CTP) & Non-Resident Taxable Person (NRTP): Ineligible.

📜 Operational Compliance Obligations:
• No Tax Invoices: Cannot issue a Tax Invoice; must issue a Bill of Supply containing the words: “composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies”.
• No Tax Collection: Cannot collect GST from customers. Tax is paid out of pocket.
• No ITC: Cannot claim any Input Tax Credit on inward supplies.
• Notice Board Display: Must display the words “Composition Taxable Person” on every notice, sign board, and at the entrance of every place of business.

Procedure Validity, Intimation & Withdrawal [ICAI Examples 39 & 40]

ICAI Example (39) • Mid-Year Threshold Breach

A person availing composition scheme during a financial year crosses the aggregate turnover limit of ₹1.5 Crore on 15th December.
Conclusion: The option availed lapses on the very day on which aggregate turnover exceeds ₹1.5 Crore (15th December). He must immediately shift to the regular scheme, start issuing tax invoices, and pay normal GST!

ICAI Example (40) • Common PAN All-or-None Rule

A dealer ‘Kishorilal & Sons’ has two offices in Delhi registered under the same PAN. He wants to opt for composition scheme for one office and normal scheme for the other office.
Conclusion: In terms of the Proviso to Section 10(2), where more than one registered person exists under the same PAN, the composition scheme can be availed only if all such registered persons opt together. A dealer cannot opt for composition for one unit and regular scheme for another!

TYK Solved ICAI Test Your Knowledge — Comprehensive Solutions (Q1 to Q10)

TYK Question 1 • Sponsorship of Sports Event

Case Facts: Panini Private Limited, Jaipur, agrees to sponsor a sports event organized by Pink City Club (an unincorporated association of persons). Determine who is liable to pay GST.

Statutory Solution:

Under Notification No. 13/2017-CT(R) (Entry 4), services provided by way of sponsorship to any body corporate or partnership firm located in the taxable territory are liable to GST under Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM).
Here, the recipient of sponsorship services is Panini Private Limited (a body corporate). Therefore, the tax on sponsorship services is legally payable by the recipient, Panini Private Limited under reverse charge.

TYK Question 2 • Executive Director Remuneration

Case Facts: Arpan Singhania is an Executive Director in Narayan Limited, Haryana. The company paid director remuneration of ₹1.5 Lakh for the month of January on which TDS was deducted under Section 192 of the Income-tax Act (Salaries). Examine the GST liability.

Statutory Solution:

As clarified by CBIC vide Circular No. 140/10/2020-GST:
• The part of director's remuneration which is declared as "Salaries" in the books of the company and subjected to TDS under Section 192 of the Income-tax Act represents consideration for services rendered by an employee to the employer in the course of employment.
• In terms of Paragraph 1 of Schedule III of the CGST Act, 2017, such services are treated as neither a supply of goods nor a supply of services.
Conclusion: The salary of ₹1.5 Lakh received by Arpan Singhania is NOT liable to GST!

TYK Question 3 • Common PAN Restriction for Trader and Interior Decorator

Case Facts: Mr. Rajbeer, registered in Delhi, sells goods with turnover of ₹80 Lakh in current FY (expected ₹130 Lakh). His son Karan wants to start an interior decoration service under composition scheme. Advise Mr. Rajbeer and his son Karan.

Statutory Solution:

1. If Karan starts business independently: Karan can obtain separate registration under his own PAN and opt for composition scheme for services under Section 10(2A) up to turnover of ₹50 Lakhs @ 6%.
2. If Karan joins Rajbeer’s business or uses same PAN: Under Section 10(1), a goods dealer can supply marginal services only up to 10% of turnover or ₹5 Lakhs (higher). Since interior decoration services would exceed ₹13 Lakhs (10% of ₹130L), Rajbeer would become ineligible for Section 10(1). Furthermore, under the proviso to Section 10(2), all registrations under the same PAN must opt for the same scheme. Hence, they must operate under separate PANs to allow Karan to avail Section 10(2A).

TYK Question 4 • Architect Firm Tax Computation under Sec 10(2A)

Case Facts: Varun & Arun Associates started a partnership firm of architects in Bhopal. Turnover: Apr-Jun ₹20L, Jul-Sep ₹30L, Oct-Dec ₹20L. Firm registered under Section 22 w.e.f. 1st July and opts for Section 10(2A). Compute tax liability (Rate 6% vs normal 18%).

Statutory Solution:

  • Turnover Apr-Jun (₹20 Lakh): Unregistered period before threshold of Section 22. Exempt from registration; No tax payable.
  • Turnover Jul-Sep (₹30 Lakh): Registered under Section 10(2A). Aggregate turnover reaches ₹50 Lakh on 30th September. Tax payable under Section 10(2A) @ 6% on ₹30 Lakh = ₹1,80,000 (CGST ₹90,000 + SGST ₹90,000).
  • Turnover Oct-Dec (₹20 Lakh): Threshold of ₹50 Lakh crossed on 30th September. Section 10(2A) lapses. Firm must pay normal tax @ 18% on ₹20 Lakh = ₹3,60,000 (CGST ₹1,80,000 + SGST ₹1,80,000).
TYK Question 5 • Composition Eligibility Across 4 Entities
  • (a) Technology Enterprises (Punjab - building bricks manufacturer): INELIGIBLE. Manufacturers of notified building bricks are barred from composition scheme.
  • (b) Siddharth & Sons (Delhi - restaurant serving alcohol): INELIGIBLE. Restaurant serving alcohol supplies non-taxable goods, which disqualifies under Section 10(2)(a).
  • (c) Sitaram Associates (Sikkim - garment trading, FY 24-25 turnover ₹73 Lakh): ELIGIBLE. Threshold in Special Category State of Sikkim is ₹75 Lakh. Since preceding FY turnover was ₹73 Lakh (≤ ₹75 Lakh), it is eligible for Section 10(1) @ 1%.
  • (d) Deepti Services Ltd (Uttarakhand - interior decorator, turnover ₹28 Lakh): ELIGIBLE under Section 10(2A) (≤ ₹50 Lakh). However, if it starts inter-State outward supplies, it becomes immediately disqualified!
TYK Question 6 • Wholesaler B & D Company Threshold Crossover

Case Facts: B & D Company, partnership firm in Nagpur, wholesale dealer in taxable product P (5%) and exempt product Q. Turnover Q1: ₹77.65L; Q2: ₹67L; Cumulative to 30th Sept: ₹144.65L. Crosses ₹1.5 Crore on 3rd October with Bill No. 2309 (cumulative reaches ₹1,50,48,000). Paid GTA freight ₹1,40,000 (RCM @ 5%). Compute tax liability.

Statutory Solution:

  • Composition Period (1st April to 2nd October):
    • Being a trader, tax @ 1% (0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST) is payable only on taxable turnover of product P!
    • Q1 Tax: 1% of ₹60,00,000 = ₹60,000.
    • Q2 Tax: 1% of ₹50,00,000 = ₹50,000.
    • 1st-2nd Oct Tax: 1% on ₹4,03,000 (Product P) = ₹4,030.
    • RCM on GTA Freight: 5% on ₹1,40,000 = ₹7,000 in cash.
  • Normal Tax Period (3rd October onwards):
    • From Bill No. 2309 onwards, firm must charge normal GST @ 5% on Product P!
TYK Question 7 • Restaurant Bank Interest Exclusion

Case Facts: Shubhlaxmi Foods, restaurant in Maharashtra. FY 24-25 turnover ₹140 Lakh (restaurant ₹130L, bank interest ₹10L). Can it opt for composition in FY 25-26? In FY 25-26, turnover is ₹130L restaurant + ₹10L interest. Compute GST.

Statutory Solution:

Under the Explanation to Section 10(1), interest income on deposits/loans is excluded from aggregate turnover for determining eligibility.
Preceding FY eligible turnover = ₹130 Lakh (≤ ₹1.5 Crore) → Eligible for composition!
GST liability in FY 25-26: 5% on restaurant turnover of ₹130 Lakh = ₹6,50,000 (CGST ₹3,25,000 + SGST ₹3,25,000). Bank interest is exempt.

TYK Question 8 • CA Firm Audit Services under Sec 10(2A)

Case Facts: Bansal & Chandiok, CA firm in Jaipur. Apr-Jun ₹10L, Jul-Sep ₹20L. Crosses ₹20L on 1st August. Opts for Section 10(2A).

Statutory Solution:

Turnover between 1st April and 31st July (₹20 Lakhs) is exempt from registration; no tax payable.
Turnover from 1st August to 30th September (₹10 Lakhs) is liable to tax under Section 10(2A) @ 6% = ₹60,000 (CGST ₹30,000 + SGST ₹30,000).

TYK Question 9 • Restaurant in Delhi

Case Facts: Mr. Prem, restaurant in New Delhi. Preceding FY turnover ₹130 Lakh. Eligible for composition? Rate applicable?

Statutory Solution:

Turnover limit in Delhi is ₹1.5 Crore. Since ₹130 Lakh ≤ ₹1.5 Crore, Mr. Prem is eligible for composition scheme.
Rate applicable: 5% (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST) of turnover in the State.

TYK Question 10 • Manufacturer vs Trader Tax Base Computation

Case Facts: M/s Heeralal and Sons (Karnataka): Taxable turnover within State ₹15,00,000; Exempt turnover within State ₹17,00,000 (Total ₹32,00,000). Compute tax liability under Rule 7 if (i) Manufacturer, and (ii) Trader.

Statutory Solution:

  • Case (i) If Manufacturer: Tax is 1% on turnover in the State (taxable + exempt supplies).
    • Tax = 1% of ₹32,00,000 = ₹32,000 (CGST ₹16,000 + SGST ₹16,000).
  • Case (ii) If Trader: Tax is 1% on taxable turnover ONLY.
    • Tax = 1% of ₹15,00,000 = ₹15,000 (CGST ₹7,50,000 + SGST ₹7,500).
    • Exempt turnover of ₹17,00,000 is excluded from tax payment!
